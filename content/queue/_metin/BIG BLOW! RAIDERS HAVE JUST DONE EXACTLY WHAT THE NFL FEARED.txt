## WHY HE SAID IT PUBLICLY, IN HIS OWN WORDS

Later in that press conference, somebody asked him directly: **Kirk is playing well, and you opened by instilling your confidence in him as QB1 — why is it important for you as a head coach to do that?**

**His answer: "He's the leader of the team. A guy that touches the ball every play. So it's important that the team knows where we're going."**

Raider Nation, **that's leadership 101 and I love it.** A locker room at 3-4 on a four-game skid does not need to spend a week reading speculation about who's taking the first snap in New York. **Uncertainty at quarterback poisons everything** — the receivers' timing, the line's communication, the play-caller's confidence.

**He removed the uncertainty before anybody could manufacture it. And by the way: Cousins is TIED FOR THE NFL LEAD with 14 touchdown passes.** This wasn't a loyalty move. **It's the obvious football answer.**

## AND THEN HE GAVE US THE MENDOZA TIMELINE

Now here's the part that will get clipped everywhere, and I want you to hear the whole quote, not the headline.

Asked what will go into his decision when he does turn to Fernando Mendoza as the starter:

**"Eventually Fernando is going to play football. We have a plan. We don't have a timeline for that. Kirk's playing really well, and WE'RE IN THE HUNT, and it's really important that we keep playing really well behind him and continue to develop Fernando. But Fernando's going to play at some point."**

**And then: "It's not going to be this week."**

Raider Nation, **there are four separate pieces of real news in that answer.**

**One: "Fernando is going to play football."** Not a maybe. A certainty.
**Two: "We have a plan."** They know what they're doing. They just aren't publishing it.
**Three: "We don't have a timeline."** So nobody circled a date in August.
**Four — and this is the one nobody will quote — "WE'RE IN THE HUNT."**

**That's a head coach at 3-4 telling you he does not consider this season over.** And he's not wrong. **The second half of this schedule is significantly easier than the first half, which was Kansas City, New England and Buffalo.** Beat the Jets and this team is 4-4 going into a bye.

**"It's not going to be this week." Translate that: Kirk Cousins starts in New York, and anybody telling you otherwise is guessing.**

## THE REVERSAL NOBODY EXPECTED: "IT IS GOING TO BE MY DECISION"

This exchange was fascinating and it took the reporter two tries to get it out.

**Back in the summer, Kubiak had said the quarterback decision would be "player-led" — that he'd let the team decide as a whole.** So the question was: is that still the process whenever that time comes?

**And listen to how he clarified it:**

**"When I say that, I think it becomes very evident to the players who's going to win a competition — whether it's quarterback or not. So at the end of the day, IT IS GOING TO BE MY DECISION. What's going to give the team the best opportunity to win games."**

Raider Nation, **that is a man taking the weight off his players' shoulders and putting it on his own.**

**And I think it's exactly right.** "Player-led" is a beautiful idea in July when everyone's 0-0 and friendly. **It's a terrible idea in Week 8 of a four-game losing streak**, when a locker room is frustrated and every vote becomes a referendum. **You cannot ask 53 men to decide who starts at quarterback while they're angry.**

**He owns it. Good.**

## AND NOW THE DIAGNOSIS OF THE SEASON

Raider Nation, this is the part I need you to really hear, because Kubiak just explained the entire 2026 Las Vegas Raiders offense in about four sentences, and it reframes everything.

He was asked how he keeps frustrations from mounting in a locker room when the run game and run defense keep failing. **And instead of a motivational answer, he gave a structural one:**

**"We're not going to run the ball until we STOP the run — until you make the game competitive. You can't be in the fourth quarter chasing points and expect to be a good run team. So you need ATTEMPTS. And in order to get those attempts, we have to stop the run and keep our opponent out of the end zone."**

**Read that again. "You need ATTEMPTS."**

Raider Nation, this is the single most useful thing he's said all year, and here's why.

**We have spent six weeks blaming the offensive line for 65 rushing yards and 3.2 a carry.** And the line deserves criticism. **But look at the actual mechanism he's describing:**

**The defense gives up points → the Raiders fall behind by multiple scores → you cannot run the ball from two scores down → so the carries disappear → and a running game with no volume never finds rhythm, never wears anybody down, never gets the fourth-quarter carries where backs break long runs.**

**It's not that the run game is failing in a vacuum. It's that the run game never gets to exist.**

**Four straight games allowing 30 or more points.** That's the root. **Everything downstream — the stacked boxes, the predictable second downs, the 2-for-5 red zone — grows out of trailing.**

## AND HE CONNECTED IT DIRECTLY TO THE MAN COVERAGE PROBLEM

Now watch him close the loop, because somebody asked him the exact question we built a whole video around: **why is this offense struggling against man coverage?**

**"The number one thing is — when teams can play that much man against you, it's because WE'RE NOT A THREAT RUNNING THE FOOTBALL right now."**

Raider Nation, **that's the chain closing on itself.**

The film analysts told us: **receivers aren't winning one-on-one → eight or nine in the box → no run game.** **And Kubiak just told us the other direction: no run threat → defenses feel free to play man all day → receivers have to win one-on-one.**

**They're both right. It's a loop, and it's strangling this offense.** You break a loop at its weakest link — **and the weakest link is a defense that has surrendered 30-plus points four weeks in a row.**

He also said something fair and specific: **"I like the strides our guys have made in practice. I've seen it in the games. I've seen how our RUNNERS ARE BREAKING TACKLES. I do like that."** Then the realism: **"But we have to call runs and be in competitive games in the second half."**

**The backs are running hard. They're just running at the wrong time, in the wrong game script, with the wrong number of carries.**

## WHERE ARE THE LEAKS IN THE RUN DEFENSE?

Asked that directly, Kubiak refused to point at anybody:

**"It's all team. It's all phase. It's tackling. It's at the front. It's putting them in situations to be successful as coaches. So WE'RE ALL ACCOUNTABLE."**

Raider Nation, **tackling, the front, and the coaching, all in one answer.** No scapegoat. And it lines up with everything else we've heard this week: **linebacker Quay Walker said the fix starts with grown men defeating blocks, winning individual matchups and making tackles.** The broadcast analyst said defenders stopped trusting each other and peeked into the backfield.

**Three different voices. Same disease. Losing at the point of attack.**

## "YOU CAN WORK ON IT WITHOUT BRINGING GUYS TO THE GROUND"

And then the follow-up I've been waiting weeks for: **can you actually improve tackling during the regular season? How do you address it this deep into a year?**

**"It is. Yeah. You CAN work on it without bringing guys to the ground. We're working on it."**

Raider Nation, **this is the head coach confirming what Rob Leonard told us.** Leonard stood at that podium and said his defense was **still drilling live tackling in Week 7** — angles, **contact entry**, same arm same leg, where you strike, where your eyes are, squeezing, finishing through — **with rugby tackling bags.** And the line that defined him: **"I don't care if they've got pads on or not, you can still get in a proper fit."**

**Most staffs in this league quit tackling drills in September. This one is doing it in late October without putting bodies on the ground.**

**That's not a quick fix. But it's the right fix, and they started it before the problem became a crisis.**

## AND NOW LET ME GIVE YOU THE GOOD STUFF, BECAUSE THERE WAS A LOT OF IT

Raider Nation, four straight losses makes everybody forget that real things are being built in that building. **Three of them came up Monday.**

**DJ GLAZE WAS A TEAM CAPTAIN THIS WEEK.**

Asked about Glaze's film from the Rams game, Kubiak said: **"DJ was a team captain for us this week. So that tells you how highly we believe, how high a level he's playing at, how good of a teammate he is, and how he's taken the next step in his career. And the thing about DJ is that he's NOT SATISFIED, and he's continuing to get better. Very self-motivated young man."**

**A young right tackle, in his third year, wearing the captain's patch on a team full of veterans.** Raider Nation, **that's a cornerstone.** We talked weeks ago about Glaze making a year-three leap, and now his head coach is calling him a captain. **Whatever happens at the deadline, that man is part of the future.**

**DYLAN LAUBE AND THE ART OF WEARING MANY HATS.**

Asked what he saw in Laube early that's showing up now, Kubiak gave a genuine philosophy of roster-building:

**"If you're not the starter at any position, then you have to provide value on multiple different parts of the team — on special teams, as a third-down back. You have to wear a lot of hats and do them well, and all the while be a talented teammate. And that means being accountable and having people COUNT ON YOU all the time. That's what Dylan is."**

Raider Nation, **that is exactly how a 53-man roster survives an injury-filled season.** It's the same philosophy that turned **Isaiah Pola-Mao** into a core special teams leader after he lost his defensive job, and the same one that has **Washington** going from one carry a game to a career-high 23 snaps. **Earn value somewhere, and the staff finds a place for you.**

**DARIEN PORTER, AND THE WORD "NON-LINEAR."**

A reporter noted that Porter was playing wide receiver in college just a couple of years ago and is now a corner — **not perfect, but you can see the makings of an NFL corner.**

**Kubiak: "It has not been linear, like anybody's growth. But like I told you last week — when you have talent and you have MENTAL TOUGHNESS, it provides a great foundation for a darn good football player. He has the mental toughness. He has the talent. And he just has to continue to improve."**

And remember what Rob Leonard said about Porter's **"thick skin"**: *"Emotional consistency. A guy that's been up, been down, and he's the same guy every day. He'll be one of the first guys out there at practice. That's what sticks out about great players — it's just the same."*

**A converted receiver learning the hardest position on defense in real time, against the best offenses in football. It is not going to be pretty every week. It isn't supposed to be.**

**AND THE SPECIAL TEAMS, AGAIN.**

Asked what Joe DeCamillis keeps bringing to this group, Kubiak's first word was perfect: **"GRIT."** Then: **"He's a veteran. He has a very talented staff with Maris and Tim. Hardworking, demanding, excellent football coach."**

Raider Nation, **this is the one unit on this team playing winning football**, and we traced exactly why last week: the coordinator diagnosed his group as **"a bunch of pleasers"** who were playing too carefully, told them to **"let it rip,"** and they were **exceptional** on Sunday. **Grit, from the top down.**

## THE HARD CONVERSATIONS AT HALFTIME

One more thing, and it matters.

A reporter relayed that one of the defensive players said there were **hard conversations in the locker room among the defensive players at halftime**, that he thought they responded a little better in the second half, and that those conversations will continue throughout the week. **Did Kubiak notice it, and does he want to see it?**

**"Absolutely. Yeah. And there's conversations like that every game, whether you're winning or losing. There's ALWAYS hard conversations at halftime. Making adjustments, holding people accountable. That's part of every team."**

Raider Nation, **this is the healthiest sign in the whole press conference.** A losing team either goes quiet and polite — everybody protecting themselves — or it gets loud and honest. **This defense got loud. And then it made the second half competitive.** Kubiak's own words about the Mendoza question were: **"We made it a competitive game in the third quarter."**

**The hard conversations worked. They just started 30 minutes too late.**

## AND HE TOOK THE BLAME AGAIN — THIS TIME FOR A TIMEOUT

Last exchange, and it's classic Kubiak.

A reporter pointed out there's been a different operational issue every single week: **last week it was 12 men on defense, this week it was after the FAKE PUNT** — and both times the Raiders had to burn timeouts early in halves. **How valuable are those timeouts, and is there a common thread?**

**"Yeah, those timeouts are critical. And we did a GREAT JOB of executing that punt — I've got to get us on the ball and ready to run and play faster. So that's on me as a head coach, making sure that operation... that timeout's on me."**

Raider Nation, **two things there.**

**One: THE RAIDERS RAN A FAKE PUNT AND EXECUTED IT.** In a game we lost by 14, this team successfully pulled off a fake punt. **That's the special teams unit again. That's gutsy coaching.**

**Two: and then they wasted a timeout because the offense couldn't get lined up fast enough afterward — and the head coach took that entirely on himself.** Not the punt team. Not the quarterback. **"That timeout's on me."**

That's five straight weeks of this man taking the bullet: the red zone **"starts with me,"** he did **"a really poor job offensively"** of starting the Rams game, and now the timeout. **At some point the accountability has to become correction — but you will never hear this coach hide.**
