Raider Nation, this is one of the strangest weeks I've ever seen following a win. Las Vegas opens the season with a convincing 27-13 victory — and somehow drops in ESPN's power rankings. An anonymous NFL coach is warning the Chargers that Klint Kubiak's rebuilt Raiders are no longer an easy opponent. The film is revealing coverage breakdowns that a better offense would have punished. And while all that is happening, analysts on national shows are already pushing the Bears and the Cowboys to make blockbuster offers for the best defensive player on our roster. Optimism and controversy, colliding in the same seven days. So today we sort it all out, honestly, piece by piece — and I'll tell you exactly what I believe and why.

I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we build toward 6,000 believers. This one gets heated. Let's go.

THE WARNING: "THE CHARGERS WILL HAVE THEIR HANDS FULL"

Let's start with the respect, because it came from an unexpected place. Speaking with The Athletic's Mike Sando, an anonymous NFL coach said the Chargers will have their hands full this Sunday, because Las Vegas looks organized, has a veteran quarterback, and brings a respectable defensive front. We covered that quote when it first surfaced, but I want to add context today, because the full arc of this franchise makes it land even harder.

Think about where this organization was nine months ago. The league's worst record. The first overall pick as the consolation prize. Pete Carroll fired after a year of front-office warfare. Geno Smith traded out the door. Another rebuild, the fourth or fifth in a decade depending on how you count. Then John Spytek spent aggressively in free agency, hired the former Seattle offensive coordinator who'd just won a Super Bowl to run the whole thing, and one game into the experiment, a rival coach is telling a playoff team to take us seriously. That's not a small shift. That's a reputation starting to turn.

And look at the other sideline. The Chargers reached the playoffs in 2025. They opened 2026 at home against an Arizona team that looked like a gift, and lost 26-4. Now they face a divisional opponent that has a real identity, with a stadium that fills up with silver and black every time we visit. The history still favors them — zero wins in four tries against Jim Harbaugh — but history is exactly what this new structure is supposed to change.

Here's the honest framing of the stakes. A win moves Las Vegas to 2-0, ends the Harbaugh drought, snaps the five-year streak without winning Weeks One and Two back to back, and forces the entire league to treat us as a real factor in the AFC West. A loss doesn't erase Miami. But it hands every skeptic the argument they're waiting to make: that Week One said more about the Dolphins than it did about the Raiders. That's why Sunday matters more than a normal Week Two game.

THE DISRESPECT: HOW DO YOU DROP AFTER A WIN?

Now let's talk about ESPN, because I know half of you are already typing in all caps. The Raiders entered the season ranked 27th in ESPN's power rankings. After winning by two touchdowns, with the offense and the defense both playing well, they dropped to 28th.

Let me lay out why that looks so strange on paper. Twelve teams that LOST their openers stayed ahead of Las Vegas. New Orleans lost and stayed above us. Washington lost and stayed above us. And the Jets jumped over us after beating Tennessee, a team most people consider one of the weakest in the league. So beating a weak opponent helped the Jets move up, but beating a weak opponent didn't help us at all. I've read it three times and I still can't find the logic.

But here's where I'm going to be fair, because this channel doesn't do blind outrage: we earned some of this skepticism. Last year, this franchise also won its opener, against New England, and then won just two more games the rest of the season. Two. That's the memory the national media is holding onto, and one afternoon against a Miami team that may finish near the bottom of the league isn't going to erase it. Respect in this league is built on consistency, not on single Sundays.

So how do you fix a ranking you think is unfair? You don't argue with it. You make it impossible to defend. Beat a divisional rival that made the playoffs last year and got heavy offseason praise, and the rankings move whether ESPN wants them to or not. Keep winning, and staying near the bottom becomes indefensible. That's the only reply that has ever worked.

THE REAL REASON FOR HOPE: KWITY PAYE

If you want one specific reason to believe the improvement is real, look at Kwity Paye. Las Vegas signed the former first-round defensive end in free agency mainly for his reputation as a strong run defender. His opening game suggested he's a lot more than that: two sacks, five pressures, and as many as five quarterback hits depending on whose charting you trust.

ESPN's Ryan McFadden singled him out as one of the biggest standouts of the game and made a point that every longtime Raiders fan will feel in their chest: this team has spent years searching for a second pass rusher who could take pressure off Maxx Crosby. Years. Rookies who never developed, free agents who never fit, trades that never landed. And in his first game under first-year coordinator Rob Leonard, whose whole philosophy is making quarterbacks uncomfortable, Paye looked like the answer.

Here's why that matters beyond one stat line. When a defense has only one great rusher, offenses slide their protection toward him every single snap and accept whatever happens on the other side. When the other side starts winning too, the whole structure breaks. Now add Nolan Smith Jr. to the rotation, and offensive coordinators have to pick which problem they want to deal with. That's not a good Week One. That's a defensive identity with staying power.

THE WARNING SIGNS: WHAT MIAMI EXPOSED

Now the honest part, because celebrating without self-scouting is how teams repeat last year. Levi Damien of Just Blog Baby made the point bluntly: the Raiders felt this exact same excitement after beating New England last year, and then won two of their remaining sixteen games while the Patriots went on to win the AFC. One good game doesn't prove a team is different. And the Miami tape, as encouraging as it was, showed two specific problems that better opponents will attack.

The first is on offense. Yes, Las Vegas scored 27 points without Brock Bowers, the centerpiece of Kubiak's passing game. Ashton Jeanty carried the load with 23 carries for 102 yards, six catches for 45 more, and two touchdowns, 147 total yards. Cousins moved the offense efficiently underneath and found Jeanty twice for scores. That's real, and it's encouraging. But Cousins struggled every time he tried to attack vertically, and both of his interceptions hung in the air long enough for Miami defenders to adjust and make a play. You know the split by now: nearly perfect short, one of seven deep.

Here's what happens next if nothing changes. Defenses stop respecting the deep ball. Safeties creep toward the line. Linebackers sit on the short routes. The timing of the quick passing game gets disrupted, and more bodies end up around Jeanty on every run. The formula that beat Miami gets strangled. That's the offensive challenge.

The second problem is on defense. The pass rush controlled that game, with Crosby and Paye combining for three and a half sacks and seven quarterback hits, repeatedly forcing Malik Willis to throw early or leave the pocket. But underneath that pressure, the secondary gave up three completions of more than twenty yards. Three different Miami receivers averaged over fifteen yards per catch. Rookie receiver Caleb Douglas caught five passes for 95 yards. Those are coverage breakdowns. Against Miami, they became field goals and punts. Against Justin Herbert, they become touchdowns.

And the timing makes it more urgent, because Darien Porter has been ruled out for Sunday. The secondary that needs to stay connected when the pass rush doesn't get home quickly will be doing it with a young corner stepping in. That's the matchup to watch.

So the Chargers game tests both weaknesses immediately. Cousins has to threaten enough of the field that the defense can't crowd the short game. And the secondary has to stay tight in coverage on the plays when Crosby and Paye don't arrive in time. If Kubiak can fix those two things, this team is legitimately different. If he can't, the encouraging start could turn into another familiar collapse.

THE TRADE SHADOW: CROSBY TO CHICAGO OR DALLAS?

And now the topic that makes every Raiders fan tense up. Maxx Crosby came back from his knee surgery and immediately reminded the league why his name shows up whenever a contender needs a pass rusher. Against Miami he played 45 snaps and earned a 90.5 grade from Pro Football Focus. He registered a sack and a half, eight pressures, five hurries, and passed seventy career sacks. The torn meniscus and the long rehab didn't take anything away from him.

The problem is, instead of ending the trade speculation, that performance gave outside analysts a new reason to talk about it. Former NFL offensive lineman Mitchell Schwartz named the Chicago Bears as a possible deadline destination on the Jim Rome Show. His reasoning: Chicago scored 59 points against Carolina but allowed 37, and a team that expects to play with big leads needs someone who can pressure the quarterback and close out games. In his view, Crosby changes both the run defense and the pass rush from day one.

Then the Cowboys entered the conversation after losing their opener to the Giants. CBS Sports analyst Pete Prisco said Dallas clearly lacks a pass rusher on Crosby's level, and pointed out that in the key moment, the Cowboys couldn't get a sack or a stop to give their offense one more chance. His suggestion: Dallas may need a major move before the deadline.

Now let me be very clear about what this is and what it isn't. This is analyst speculation. It is not reporting. Nobody has said John Spytek is shopping Crosby, and nobody has said Crosby wants to leave. In fact, Schwartz himself admitted his scenario only works if the season falls apart, something like two wins and six losses, at which point he wonders whether Crosby would want to join a contender again after nearly being traded in the spring. And Crosby has said publicly that the Raiders feel like home again.

So here's my position, and it's the same one I've held since the Baltimore trade collapsed: winning is the only thing that kills this rumor. If Las Vegas stays competitive, Crosby is the foundation of everything this defense is building, and no team is going to pry him loose. If the season collapses the way last year's did, the phone calls from Chicago and Dallas will get louder, and the media noise will be very hard to ignore. The front office controls the roster, but the scoreboard controls the conversation.

And notice the irony in the Schwartz scenario. His whole case rests on Crosby's value being at its peak because he's healthy and dominant again. But the same thing that makes Crosby attractive to Chicago and Dallas is exactly why Vegas can't afford to move him while this team is winning. A healthy Crosby is what turned Paye into a two-sack player, because protections were sliding toward number 98 all afternoon. Take him out and the whole pass rush we just praised gets easier to block.

THE ULTIMATE PROVING GROUND

So here's where we are, Raider Nation. One win changed the conversation, but it didn't settle anything. An opposing coach is warning the Chargers. ESPN is still ranking us like one of the worst teams in football. Paye and Crosby have given this defense a real pass-rushing identity. Cousins and the secondary have to fix what Miami exposed. And as analysts connect Crosby to Chicago and Dallas, the best protection against another trade debate is the simplest one: keep winning.

Sunday at SoFi answers a lot of questions at once. Is the offense more than a short-passing machine? Can a young secondary hold up against Herbert without Porter? Is the Harbaugh drought over? And does ESPN have to rewrite its rankings? I said 24-17 Raiders, and I'm sticking with it.
