## FIRST: DYLAN LAUBE BROKE A 248-GAME DROUGHT

Raider Nation, put this one in a frame.

**Dylan Laube returned a kickoff 95 YARDS FOR A TOUCHDOWN — the Raiders' FIRST kickoff return score in 248 GAMES.**

**Two hundred and forty-eight games.** That is more than fourteen seasons of football without a kickoff return touchdown. **And in a game decided by five points, that score was the margin.**

**But here's the part I love even more, because it happened before he ever touched the ball.**

**Laube entered Sunday among the league leaders in kickoff return yards.** And after New York opened the game with a field goal, **the Jets sent the ensuing kickoff OUT OF BOUNDS** — handing Las Vegas the football at its own 40-yard line.

**The analysis credits Laube's return threat with influencing that mistake. A kicker tried so hard not to kick it to him that he gave us 15 free yards.**

**And the offense made that field position count.** **Jalen Nailor caught two passes for 24 yards on a 10-play, 60-yard touchdown drive.**

Raider Nation, **that's two scores his presence created on the same afternoon — one with his legs, one with his reputation.**

## AND LET ME CONNECT THE DOTS, BECAUSE THIS WAS BUILT

This did not come out of nowhere. **We watched it get built on this channel, week by week.**

**Three weeks ago**, the special teams coordinator stood at that podium and gave the most honest audit of the season. He said the unit was learning a brand-new system — **different verbiage, different assignments.** He said the return game was already solid, around **fourth and third in the league** in punt and kickoff return. And then he diagnosed the psychology: **"We have a group of pleasers here. Sometimes, especially the younger guys, they don't just LET IT RIP."**

**His message that week: "Just let it rip, go out and have fun and play ball."**

**The next Sunday, Kubiak's special teams were called "EXCEPTIONAL" in a game they lost by 14.**

**And this Sunday, they broke a 248-game drought and won the football game.**

**Raider Nation, that is coaching. A coach found a psychological problem, named it publicly, fixed it in five days, and three weeks later it won a game.**

**And Kubiak made Dylan Laube a captain. TWICE.** A man who isn't a starter, because — in the head coach's own words — **"if you can play four core special teams AND be a producer on your side of the ball, then you've got VALUE in this league."**

**That value just showed up as six points.**

## AND JALEN NAILOR'S RETURN MATTERED MORE THAN THE BOX SCORE SHOWS

Two catches for 24 yards doesn't look like much. **But those catches came on a 10-play, 60-yard touchdown drive** — and that's the whole point about him.

**His absence genuinely strained this offense in Week 6, and his return helped open things back up.** And remember what Kubiak said on Friday: **Nailor IS getting separation. He IS getting open down the field.** The coach pushed back on the national narrative that this offense can't separate, and said it was **"time on task"** before the connection hit.

**This week it started hitting.**

## NOW THE THIRD QUARTER — AND THIS IS THE MOST IMPROVED THING ON THIS TEAM

Raider Nation, sit down for these numbers, because this is the stat of the week and nobody is talking about it.

**Entering Sunday, the Raiders had scored THREE POINTS across their previous FOUR third quarters COMBINED.**

**Three points. Four third quarters.**

**Across their first seven games, they had scored 26 third-quarter points total.**

**Against the Jets alone, they scored FOURTEEN in that period.**

**Their most productive third quarter of the entire season.**

**And it gets better.** Washington accounted for half the Raiders' yardage on the opening possession after halftime, which ended in a touchdown — **the FIRST TIME ALL SEASON that this team scored on its first offensive drive of the second half.**

Raider Nation, **we have hammered this every single day for a month.** Minus-26 in the third quarter, worst in the NFL. Kubiak said there was a practice plan. He said he **"liked the response."** After the Rams loss he said, **"We made it a competitive game in the third quarter."**

**And Sunday the dam broke. Fourteen points, and a score on the opening drive of the half for the first time in eight games.**

**That is a real, measurable fix. Give that coaching staff its credit.**

## AND THE OFFENSIVE LINE FACT THAT CORRECTS OUR OWN NARRATIVE

Raider Nation, I owe this group a more honest framing than I've been giving them.

**Despite all the criticism surrounding that unit — including plenty from me — the Raiders' offensive line entered this week ranked around TOP 10 TO 15 in several statistical categories, with pass protection generally stronger than run blocking.**

**Top 10 to 15. Not 30th. Not broken.**

And against New York, **it helped BOTH phases** — giving Cousins chances to throw or move, **and letting the ground game operate more effectively than it had in weeks.**

**So let me recalibrate what I've been telling you: the pass protection has been genuinely good all along** — remember 41 attempts and zero sacks against Buffalo. **The run blocking is where the problem lives.** And Kubiak said it precisely on Wednesday: **"we're going to keep shaking it up until we find the right mix."** **Sunday the mix worked better.**

**And Brock Bowers continued to stand out, which reinforces the argument we've made for weeks: his receiving ability makes him far more than a traditional tight end.**

## NOW KIRK COUSINS — AND HE IS NOW THE NFL'S TOUCHDOWN LEADER

**Cousins finished with THREE PASSING TOUCHDOWNS AND ONE RUSHING TOUCHDOWN. Four total scores in a comeback win.**

**And his rushing score was his FIRST OF THE SEASON.**

**He entered Week 8 leading the National Football League with 16 touchdown passes. His three against New York pushed him to NINETEEN — still the most in the league.**

**Nineteen touchdown passes. League leader. At 38 years old. On a team that finished last season with the worst record in football.**

Raider Nation, **take a moment with that.** A man signed late in free agency as a one-year bridge — **$20 million fully guaranteed, with Atlanta covering most of it** — is leading the NFL in touchdown passes in Week 8.

Raider Nation, **think about what that actually tells you.** The 38-year-old veteran quarterback, the special teams captain who just broke a 248-game drought, and the best tight end in football **had an inside joke going, and they held each other to it in the middle of a five-point road win.**

**That is a locker room that just survived four straight losses and did NOT fracture.** Every coach in that building told us this room was hardening instead of breaking. **The wellness celebration is the proof.**

## NOW THE CONTROVERSY. ASHTON JEANTY GOT TWO CARRIES.

Raider Nation, here it is — the question that follows this team out of New York.

**Ashton Jeanty finished with TWO CARRIES. The lowest rushing workload of his professional career.**

**At halftime he had ONE carry, one target, and one reception.**

**Two carries. For a man selected SIXTH OVERALL just a year ago.**

**And Mike Washington Jr. instead received 25 CARRIES, gaining 83 YARDS — an average of 3.3 per attempt.**

## AND HERE'S WHAT KUBIAK SAID ABOUT IT

Asked about the decision afterward, **Klint Kubiak accepted responsibility and offered a limited explanation:**

**"That's my call. And Ashton is still going to be a big part of our team."**

**And critically — Kubiak said Jeanty's pregame comments about the Jets' tackling were NOT a factor in Washington's promotion.**

Raider Nation, **that matters, and I want to be precise about it: that rules out presenting those two carries as a confirmed PUNISHMENT for what he said.** The head coach was asked directly and said no.

**What it does NOT do is fully explain how this backfield operates next week.**

## SO LET ME GIVE YOU THE HONEST ANALYSIS, BECAUSE THE NUMBERS COMPLICATE EVERYTHING

Here's the part that keeps me from declaring the running game fixed.

**Washington's 25 carries produced 3.3 yards per attempt.**

**Ashton Jeanty entered the matchup averaging 3.1 yards per carry.**

**Three-point-three versus three-point-one.**

Raider Nation, **that is not a solution. That is a rounding error.**

**The offensive line has been blamed for most of this ground game's difficulty — and Washington's average was NOT dramatically different from what Jeanty had been producing.** Which means **the problem was never primarily the man taking the handoff.** It's what's in front of him. **I've said it for weeks and the Jets game just proved it with a different back.**

**And his first half was modest too: seven carries for 24 yards.** In fact, **Las Vegas managed only 97 TOTAL OFFENSIVE YARDS and ONE offensive touchdown before halftime.**

**Ninety-seven yards in a half. In a game they won by five.**

## BUT HERE IS WHERE WASHINGTON'S PROMOTION GENUINELY HELPED

Now let me argue the other side properly, because the value was real — it just wasn't about yards per carry.

**Washington's workload gave this offense a more CONSISTENT ground presence, which allowed Cousins to operate through the short-to-intermediate passing game instead of carrying every possession himself.**

**That's the point.** Twenty-five carries, even at 3.3, keeps a defense honest, keeps the clock moving, and keeps your 38-year-old quarterback out of third-and-long. **Volume has value independent of average.** It's exactly what Kubiak meant when he said **"you need ATTEMPTS"** and **"quit throwing it so much."**

**And there's a second, sneakier benefit: the LIMITED FILM of Washington in that role gave New York a different preparation problem.** They'd barely seen him as a primary back all season. **You can't scout what doesn't exist.**

**And it worked when it mattered most: the rookie accounted for HALF the Raiders' yardage on the opening possession after halftime — the drive that produced the first second-half-opening score of the entire season.**

## AND THERE MAY BE A PHYSICAL REASON FOR THE JEANTY DECISION

Raider Nation, this is important and I want to flag the uncertainty clearly.

**One analysis interpreted Jeanty's usage as an effective REST OPPORTUNITY** — particularly given **the injury he suffered shortly before the season**, and concerns that **he had not fully recovered when the Raiders faced New England.**

**And remember what we learned on Friday: Jeanty has dealt with an ANKLE ISSUE since before the season even started.**

**But I have to be honest with you: that is an INTERPRETATION of the workload, not a confirmed explanation from the coaching staff.** Kubiak didn't say that. He said **"that's my call."**

**And one more thing that argues against a total benching: keeping Jeanty active still preserved his RECEIVING ability — an area where Washington does not offer the same effectiveness.**

**Because look at what Jeanty actually did: THREE CATCHES FOR 64 YARDS.**

**Sixty-four receiving yards.** Raider Nation, **that matters enormously when you're evaluating whether this was a removal from the offense or a change in HOW the staff wanted to use him.** He was the third-leading contributor to this passing game. **He just wasn't running it.**

## NOW REX RYAN, AND I HAVE TO READ THIS TO YOU STRAIGHT

Raider Nation, Jeanty's pregame comments had already made this matchup personal — **he'd suggested a couple of New York defenders didn't really want to tackle, and declined to name them.**

**And former Jets head coach Rex Ryan responded on ESPN's Sunday NFL Countdown. Sharply.**

**Raider Nation, those are REX RYAN'S assessments, and the severity of them should stay attached to him, not to me.** I'm reporting what a national analyst said about our player. **I am not endorsing one word of it.**

## AND HERE IS MY ANSWER TO IT

**"Worst running back draft pick of the last 20 years" is an absurd statement about a man in his second professional season.**

Let me give you the facts Rex Ryan skipped.

**This offense has faced the second-most stacked boxes in the National Football League.** The film analysts said it out loud on national television after the Rams game: **nobody separates against man coverage, so defenses put eight and nine men in the box.** **Kubiak said it himself: "Teams can play that much man against us because we're not a threat running the football."** And: **"It's a team thing — running backs, fullbacks, offensive line."**

**This team was held under 75 rushing yards in five straight games.** **The Raiders have been among the league's least productive rushing attacks for FOUR CONSECUTIVE SEASONS** — three coaching staffs before Jeanty ever arrived.

**And the final piece of evidence? A different back carried it 25 times on Sunday and averaged 3.3.**

**If Ashton Jeanty is the problem, explain Mike Washington's 3.3.**

**Now — was Jeanty wrong to say what he said? Yes.** I said so before the game and I'll say it again. **You don't hand a desperate team a bulletin board, especially when a national panel can point out that Vegas has more missed tackles than the Jets do.** **And his two carries didn't give him much chance to answer any of it on the ground.**

**But his receiving production helped win the football game, and the Raiders left New Jersey with the W.**

**The useful question now is much bigger than an exchange of insults: can Kubiak combine Washington's rushing workload with Jeanty's receiving value in a way that improves the ENTIRE backfield?**

**A greater split is the obvious answer — Washington carrying it, Jeanty catching it, both on the field.** That's not a demotion. **That's a division of labor.**

## NOW THE DEFENSE — AND WE CANNOT PRETEND ONE WIN FIXED IT

Raider Nation, I'm not going to let a victory buy silence. **The defense supplied the clearest reason this team cannot assume one win solves anything.**

**They allowed 33 points.**

**And by my count, that is the FIFTH STRAIGHT GAME this defense has allowed 30 or more.** We went into Sunday with four — **30, 45, 33 and 35** — and tying a franchise mark that hadn't been touched since 1999. **Now it's five.**

**We won anyway. That is not a sustainable formula.**

**And there were specific problems.**

**Darien Porter left after only ONE PLAY.** One play. **A converted college receiver who Rob Leonard praised for his "thick skin" and his **"emotional consistency"** — gone on the first snap.

**But the secondary's problems extended well beyond his absence.**

**Eric Stokes made two important third-down stops that helped limit New York to field goals — including on a possession that reached FIRST-AND-GOAL AT THE RAIDERS' 5-YARD LINE.** Raider Nation, **that's a huge sequence. First-and-goal at the five and they got three points.** Those stops are part of why this team won.

**But the Jets repeatedly challenged him, particularly in short-yardage passing situations, and overall it was a disappointing outing.**

**And a rookie corner struggled on New York's first touchdown, and could have defended the run better on their first touchdown after halftime.**

## BUT HERE IS THE TRADE-OFF, AND I THINK IT WAS THE RIGHT CALL

This is the strategic point I want you to understand, because it explains the whole afternoon.

**Las Vegas committed to improving its RUN DEFENSE — which left its defensive backs in difficult individual matchups.**

Raider Nation, **that's a deliberate choice, and I think it was correct.** When you commit extra bodies to stopping the run, you are mathematically asking your corners to win alone. **And the analysis I read put it perfectly: that trade-off was MORE UNDERSTANDABLE than continuing to struggle against BOTH phases.**

**We were 30th against the run, allowing 143.6 yards a game at 5.0 a carry, second worst in football.** You cannot fix everything at once. **So they picked the bigger problem, accepted the exposure behind it, and won a game.**

**And it closed well: they held the Jets to TEN second-half points.** Ten. **After giving up more than that in a half for most of October.**

**But the honest verdict: the win did not erase those individual concerns. And the analysis argues the Raiders need to address the defensive backfield AGGRESSIVELY in the offseason.**

**I agree. Add it to the list — behind the interior defensive line, which is still the disease.**
