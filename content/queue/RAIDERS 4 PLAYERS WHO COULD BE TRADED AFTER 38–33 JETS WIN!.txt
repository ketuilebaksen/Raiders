# RAIDERS: 4 PLAYERS IN THE TRADE CONVERSATION AFTER THE 38-33 JETS WIN — And After This Film, I'd Keep ALL FOUR 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, the Raiders went into New York, beat the Jets, and got back to **4-4.**

**And here's the honest truth about today's video: nobody reported a single trade.** There is no list from inside that building. **What there IS — and what I'm going to give you — is four Raiders whose names are genuinely in the deadline conversation right now, my own ranking of them, and the film from Sunday that changed my mind about all of it.**

**Because I watched one play in the fourth quarter that should end at least one of these trade debates forever.**

**Second and eleven. From our own 42. Down two points.** And what Klint Kubiak called, and who he called it for, **completely reframed the biggest controversy on this football team.**

Plus: the fourth-down call that bothered me, the 121-yard first half that nearly buried us, the phantom holding call that erased a Maxx Crosby sack, **and the position I'd spend a premium pick on instead of a receiver.**

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're pushing for **6,000 believers** — hit that like button, drop **"I AM A RAIDER"** in the comments, and let's go.

## FIRST, HOW CLOSE THIS CAME TO BEING A DISASTER

Raider Nation, **we were down 23-14 at halftime — and I was GLAD we were that close.**

**It took a kickoff return just to stay within nine.**

Go back to the opening drive, because it set the tone. **Three snaps in, the Jets were already at our 2-yard line.** **Garrett Wilson got 31 yards on the very first play.** **Braelon Allen ripped off consecutive 15-yard runs**, and a flag moved them even closer.

**I was ready to spot New York seven points before I'd finished my coffee.**

**Then Eric Stokes broke up a throw to Wilson. A rookie safety got a hand on the next one. And we got off the field down THREE.**

Raider Nation, **those four points we saved felt enormous by the end of the afternoon.** In a game decided by five, a red zone stand on the opening drive was the difference between a win and a loss. **And it's exactly what Kubiak defended on Monday when he said Stokes "made some really big plays for us, especially in the red zone."**

## BUT BRAELON ALLEN WAS A NIGHTMARE

**He had 121 YARDS BEFORE HALFTIME.**

**One hundred twenty-one. In a half.**

And the comparison in the breakdown I watched was brutal and accurate: **watching our defensive backs try to bring him down once he got going was like watching people try to tackle Derrick Henry.**

**Meanwhile Geno Smith could look downfield behind that line all day. And New York held the ball for NINETEEN MINUTES AND SEVENTEEN SECONDS in the first half.**

**Nineteen minutes of a thirty-minute half.** Raider Nation, our defense was on the field so long that our offense barely existed — **97 total yards in that first half.**

**And New York scored on its FIRST SIX POSSESSIONS.**

## AND THE ONE ANSWER THEY COULDN'T DO ANYTHING ABOUT

**Dylan Laube took a kickoff 95 yards down the sideline.**

**And for a minute there — with our offense having scored exactly once — WE ACTUALLY LED 14-13.**

**"You could hear Raider Nation in that stadium."**

Raider Nation, that's exactly what Kubiak said on Sunday: **"The silver and black was out. We HEARD them. They were loud."** On the road. At a 10 a.m. West Coast kickoff. **For a team that hadn't won in a month.**

**A 248-game drought, broken in front of Laube's own family, and it gave this team a lead it had no business having.**

## NOW THE CALL THAT BOTHERED ME

Second quarter. Fourth down. **And the Raiders put CONNOR HAYWARD behind center for the push.**

**He took the snap. The Jets stopped him.**

And Raider Nation, here's the sharpest piece of analysis I heard about the whole game, and I completely agree with it:

**"I understand the appeal — Seattle made it work with A.J. Barner. But our line likes to get defenders moving SIDEWAYS and give the back a lane to CUT THROUGH. When everybody's packed in tight, where's he supposed to cut?"**

**That is the problem in one sentence.** This offensive line is built on movement — **zone principles, displacement, creating a seam that a back reads on the fly.** **A quarterback-push play is the opposite: everybody collapses into a pile and you need raw vertical force.**

**It is the one short-yardage concept this specific line is least equipped to run.** And remember, **Tyler Linderbaum has drawn a flag in that exact situation three separate times this season.**

**The suggested fix, which I love: "I'd give MICHAEL MAYER a shot at that push in practice."** A 250-pound tight end with leverage and leg drive. **"Until we can move somebody, I'd want another call on game day."**

**Agreed. Completely. Stop running the one play your line can't block.**

## THE SECOND HALF, AND THE COMEBACK

Give these guys credit for how they came out after halftime.

**Kirk Cousins found Brock Bowers for his SECOND touchdown.** The Jets answered with **a 21-yard Allen run.** **And Cousins took the next one in himself** — his first rushing touchdown of the season, and the one that produced the "wellness celebration" his teammates invented for him.

**Down 30-28, our defense finally forced a punt** — after New York had scored on **six straight possessions.**

**Now we had the ball back. And by the end of the quarter we'd worked it out to our own 42. Then a run lost a yard.**

**Second and eleven.**

## AND HERE IS THE PLAY THAT SHOULD END THE JEANTY TRADE DEBATE

Raider Nation, think about who had been getting the handoffs all afternoon. **Mike Washington Jr. finished with 25 carries for 83 yards. Ashton Jeanty got TWO for NINE.**

**If you were scanning the stat sheet looking for the man who could swing this football game, those numbers would have sent you looking in the wrong place entirely.**

**Klint Kubiak called a SCREEN.**

**Cousins flipped it left. And Ashton Jeanty followed his blockers all the way to the Jets' 13-YARD LINE.**

**FORTY-FIVE YARDS. On a throw our quarterback could get out quickly.**

**And then — this is the part I love — the very next snap, Kubiak handed it RIGHT BACK to him. And Jeanty went EIGHT YARDS to the five.**

**One carry accounted for EIGHT of his NINE rushing yards on the day. And it came immediately after his biggest catch.**

**Jalen Nailor finished the drive with a two-yard touchdown — his first touchdown catch of the season.**

Raider Nation, **that little stretch is why I still want Ashton Jeanty running the football.**

**Imagine watching a man put your team in scoring position TWICE IN A ROW — forty-five yards and then eight more — and coming away convinced you ought to trade him.**

## BUT I'LL BE FAIR ABOUT HOW WE GOT TO THIS CONVERSATION

Because I understand it, and I'm not going to pretend the criticism came from nowhere.

**I remember Jeanty making the first defender miss as a rookie. He forced 71 MISSED TACKLES that season.**

**Seventy-one.**

**And this year, I've watched him run into traffic and get wrapped up before he can get his feet under him.** That's the honest difference. **Richie Incognito questioned how well he was finding the holes.** And before kickoff, **Rex Ryan called him soft** — after Jeanty had questioned whether some Jets defenders wanted to tackle.

**Fair enough to bring the comments up. But I'm far more interested in what his own coach actually told him.**

**And here's the piece that settles it: Jeanty said he KNEW GOING IN that Washington would be featured.**

**He was told. In advance. Like a professional.**

**Afterward, Kubiak said the split was HIS call, dismissed the idea that he was punishing Jeanty for talking about the Jets, and said he still sees a big role for him in the run game.** And on Monday he went further: **"Ashton made huge plays for us in the pass game, IN PROTECTION. PHENOMENAL in protection — like he's always been."** And: **"We're going to need Mike, we're going to need Ashton, we're going to need Dylan. All THREE of those guys."**

## SO HERE IS THE SPLIT I ACTUALLY WANT AGAINST SAN FRANCISCO

**I'd start Washington against the 49ers too. He earned it.**

**But at 3.3 yards a carry against a two-win team, I'd lean closer to 15 TOUCHES for Washington and 10 OR 12 FOR JEANTY — including throws.**

**And here's the specific design I want to see, because this is where it gets fun:**

**Send BOTH backs into the huddle. Keep Washington behind Cousins. And MOTION JEANTY INTO THE SLOT.**

**Now watch who goes with him.**

**If a LINEBACKER walks out there — I want Jeanty to sell the inside route and break away from him.** A linebacker cannot cover a top-ten running back in space. **That's a mismatch you created for free.**

**If a DEFENSIVE BACK widens to cover him and the middle opens up — hand it to Washington.** You just removed a body from the box.

**Raider Nation, THAT is how you unstack a box without making a trade. You make the defense choose.**

**And Jeanty caught three or four targets for 64 yards on Sunday.** **What I'm genuinely curious about now is whether he can get open BEFORE the catch — when the design leaves him one defender to beat.**

## AND THINK ABOUT IT FROM THE QUARTERBACK'S SIDE FOR A SECOND

This is the detail I keep coming back to.

**I've had my questions about Kirk Cousins' arm. But I LIKED how little he had to ask of it on that screen.**

**The blockers got out. The throw hit Jeanty in stride. And our back got to pick where he wanted to run.**

Raider Nation, **that's the perfect play for a 38-year-old quarterback with limited mobility** — zero risk, zero arm strength required, and all the yardage comes after the catch. **It's the same philosophy as those three-tight-end designs that seal the edge so he can get out safely.**

**This staff is building an offense AROUND its quarterback's limitations instead of pretending they don't exist. That's good coaching.**

**And one more thing: if the Niners see both backs come out and start pointing at Jeanty — FINE BY ME.** Because now **Cousins gets to see how they intend to cover him BEFORE anybody snaps the ball.**

**I'd want that information even on a play where we never throw it his way.**

## AND HERE'S A WILD IDEA I'VE COME AROUND ON

Raider Nation, stay with me on this one.

**I wouldn't be shocked if Ashton Jeanty eventually earns snaps as a WIDE RECEIVER on a regular basis.**

**And there's real history here. Charley Taylor won Rookie of the Year as a RUNNING BACK before switching to receiver — and made the Hall of Fame. Bobby Mitchell went from halfback to Pro Bowl receiver in Washington.**

**I especially like the Taylor example, because he'd ALREADY SHOWN HE COULD RUN.**

**I like Jeanty as a runner and I'd love to find out what else he can do — while we've got another back who can handle the carries.**

**And think about what that solves.** This team has spent two months looking for a receiver who can separate against man coverage. **What if one of the answers is already on the roster, taking handoffs?**

## NOW — THE FOUR NAMES IN THE TRADE CONVERSATION, AND MY ANSWER ON EACH

Raider Nation, here's my list. **These are the four Raiders whose names are genuinely being discussed, and I want to be clear: NONE of this is reported. Nobody has broken a deal. This is the conversation, and my answer to it.**

## NUMBER FOUR: ASHTON JEANTY — ABSOLUTELY NOT

**I just spent ten minutes explaining why.** Forty-five yards on a screen, eight more on the next carry, **phenomenal in protection according to his own head coach**, 71 forced missed tackles as a rookie, and **64 receiving yards on a day he got two carries.**

**He's 22 years old on a rookie deal with a Hall of Fame positional precedent for exactly the versatility this offense needs.**

**Rex Ryan called him the worst running back pick in twenty years. His coach called him phenomenal. One of those men watches the film every day.**

**Keep him. This isn't close.**

## NUMBER THREE: JACKSON POWERS-JOHNSON OR CALEB ROGERS — HOLD BOTH

Here's the squeeze that's real.

**Trey Zuhn III started at right guard on Sunday. And Caleb Rogers AND Jackson Powers-Johnson had BOTH started there earlier this season.**

**Three men, one job.** And Kubiak said Zuhn earned more opportunities — then immediately added: **"He has to STEAL ANOTHER GUY'S JOB. You've got to LEAPFROG somebody."**

**So somebody's roster value is sitting on a bench right now, and that's exactly when teams call.**

**But I'd hold both, and here's my reasoning.** **I liked stretches of what Rogers gave us against Buffalo, then had concerns against the Rams. I'm giving Zuhn that same second look before I decide anything.** One start is not an evaluation.

**And the depth matters more than the pick would.** Kubiak himself listed how much has changed: **"They've been changing at guard. They've changed up front defensively. Changed at corner. That's the NFL."** **You don't sell interior line depth in November when your run game just started working.**

**And the character piece is real: Rogers and JPJ helped Zuhn prepare to take their snaps.** Kubiak made a point of saying it publicly. **That's the kind of room you protect.**

## NUMBER TWO: KIRK COUSINS — KEEP HIM, OBVIOUSLY

**Nineteen touchdown passes. The most in the National Football League.** Four total scores against the Jets. **Ian Rapoport reported the Raiders don't want Fernando Mendoza playing anytime soon** because the organization believes Cousins **was never the cause of the losing streak.**

**Jon Gruden floated a second-round pick on a podcast. That was his opinion, not a report, and the answer is no.**

**And the contract already answers the long-term question: the roster bonus and the option trigger in March tell you this was always a one-year bridge.** **You don't need to trade a bridge. You just walk across it.**

**Keep him and go win games.**

## NUMBER ONE: MAXX CROSBY — AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS

Raider Nation, this is where the real conversation is, and the Jets film gave me the single best argument I've heard for keeping him.

**"I hear the trade talk around Maxx — and I wonder WHO DRAWS THAT EXTRA BLOCKER IF HE GOES."**

**Who draws the extra blocker.**

**That's the whole thing.** We have spent a month talking about Crosby seeing **the highest rate of chips and double teams of his career — sixteen of them in the Buffalo game alone.** Everybody frames that as a problem.

**It isn't only a problem. It's a SERVICE.** Every chip a tight end gives him is a tight end not running a route. **Every slide a guard makes toward him is a guard not blocking somebody else.** **Crosby absorbs two bodies on every single snap, and that's the only reason anybody else on that front has a chance.**

**Trade him and you don't just lose a pass rusher. You hand four blockers back to the offense.**

**And look at what he still did on Sunday: second-and-20, Maxx Crosby got Geno Smith down for a TWELVE-YARD LOSS.** **Third-and-32 was coming. Game basically over.**

**Except there was DEFENSIVE HOLDING. The sack came off the board. And Adonai Mitchell picked up 47 yards on the very next snap.**

**Raider Nation, that's football. Your best player makes the play of the game and a flag erases it.** Holding them to a field goal from there is what kept the lead in our hands. **But do not tell me Maxx Crosby isn't producing. He produced a 12-yard sack on second-and-20 and the officials took it.**

**Keep him. Two late first-round picks don't replace the man who occupies two blockers.**

## AND JEREMY CHINN GOT TO CLEAN UP HIS OWN MISTAKE

One more moment from that game that I love, because it's so human.

**Jeremy Chinn drew a flag for a LATE HIT on Garrett Wilson. And then, ONE SNAP LATER, he intercepted Geno Smith in the end zone.**

**He made the mistake and then he erased it himself, in the span of two plays, to seal a 38-33 win.**

**And Kubiak — a former safety — called it "a BIG TIME catch" and said Chinn was "extremely AGGRESSIVE. He wanted to END THE GAME right there, and got his feet in bounds."**

Raider Nation, **that's the whole philosophy this staff keeps preaching, in one sequence.** *"When a defensive back gets beat, everybody knows about it. You've got to have thick skin. You've got to be able to move on to the next down."*

**He moved on to the next down. And the next down won the football game.**

## NOW THE HONEST CAVEATS, BECAUSE I OWE YOU THEM

Raider Nation, before anybody crowns this offensive line, two things.

**One: the Jets were missing edge defenders David Bailey and Joseph Ossai, and a tackle was on injured reserve.** **That matters.** You cannot credit a protection breakthrough without noting who wasn't rushing.

**Two: Eric Stokes had a rough afternoon overall, despite those two early breakups.** And that Jets receiving group **can hurt you several different ways** — though interestingly, **Omar Cooper Jr. didn't draw a single target** all day.

**But here's what I will absolutely credit: this offense went from ONE touchdown in the first half to THREE in the second.**

**And the defense held Braelon Allen to 26 RUSHING YARDS AFTER HALFTIME — including that 21-yard score.** So take the score away and it's **five yards on the rest of his second-half carries.** **After 121 in the first half.** **And they forced another three-and-out right after Nailor put us ahead.**

**I'll take that response every single week.**

## AND HERE IS WHERE I'D ACTUALLY SPEND THE MONEY

Raider Nation, this is the most important part of this video, so stay with me.

**The position I would spend a PREMIUM PICK on is DEFENSIVE TACKLE.**

**Not receiver. Defensive tackle.**

And here's the reasoning, and it's airtight. **This front office has added good players in free agency — Jeremy Chinn, Eric Stokes, and the starting linebacker duo of Nico Dean and Quay Walker.** **So give your first-year coordinator somebody who can BEAT A GUARD, too.**

**Because remember the diagnosis that came out of the film panel after this very game: "Our interior guys need to do a better job of beating their one-on-one blocks and KEEPING THOSE INTERIOR GUYS OFF OUR LINEBACKERS."**

**You can draft all the linebackers you want. If a guard climbs to the second level untouched, your linebacker is blocked before he can read the play.**

**And I'd add this: I'd also give MALCOLM KOONCE more work opposite Crosby and see how that rotation looks with Kwity Paye.** We've spent weeks saying snaps should follow production — **Koonce has led this team in sacks on fewer snaps, and Paye finally got home against Buffalo.**

**And one more thing for Rob Leonard: I'd still find chances to BLITZ. I don't want one bad experience against Kansas City dictating how much time we give everybody else.** Raider Nation, Leonard said it himself: **"Can we rush with four and get home?"** **If the answer keeps being no, you have to send a fifth.**

## AND HERE'S WHY I TRUST KUBIAK TO FIND A RECEIVER WITHOUT A TRADE

This is the argument that actually changed my mind, and it's a good one.

**"If there's a receiver we love on draft night, I get the argument. But watching how Kubiak found touches for Bowers, Mayer, and a back who barely carried the ball — I TRUST HIM to get a pass catcher free."**

**"I have a harder time asking Leonard to manufacture that same advantage INSIDE, when an offense can just turn its protection toward Crosby."**

Raider Nation, **that's the cleanest way anybody has put it.**

**Kubiak proved on Sunday that he can scheme a man open.** He got **twelve catches and 130 yards out of two tight ends.** He got **45 yards out of a running back on a screen.** He threw a **corner route to Bowers in the red zone** after passing on the same look a week earlier. **He can create touches out of nothing.**

**But nobody can scheme a defensive tackle into winning a one-on-one against a guard. That's a talent purchase. You either have the man or you don't.**

**So: don't pay a premium for a receiver. Pay it for the interior.** **And on draft history — we moved back to 38 and took Stokes while Houston moved up to 36. We could have stayed at 37 in 2025 and had a shot at Luther Burden III, who Chicago took at 39, long before we picked Jack Bech at 58.** I can appreciate what Stokes gave us on that opening drive **and still want help inside.**

**And I'd rather have a class like the one that gave us Brock Bowers, DJ Glaze and Dylan Laube than a pile of extra picks.** We counted extra picks for years. **It didn't work.**

## SO WHERE ARE WE?

**Four and four. We've beaten Miami, the Chargers, New Orleans and the Jets.**

**And honestly? Eight to ten wins would feel like progress to me — even if we missed the playoffs.** **That's the honest bar for a first-year staff that inherited the worst record in football.**

**But San Francisco comes first. And the deadline falls right after it.**

**Win there and you're 5-4, you're a buyer, and Spytek should be calling about an interior defensive lineman — DeForest Buckner at roughly $6.5 million against the cap is still sitting right there.**

**Lose and you're 4-5 and these four conversations get a lot louder.**

## WHAT I'M WATCHING AGAINST SAN FRANCISCO

**One: Jeanty's role.** Next week, when Washington goes in, **I'm watching Ashton.** Does he get the slot motion? Does he get 10 to 12 touches?

**Two: short yardage.** If we see another quarterback-push play, **I'm going to lose my mind.** Give it to a tight end or run what this line actually blocks.

**Three: the first-quarter run defense.** One hundred twenty-one yards to one back in a half is not survivable against San Francisco.

**Four: Tonka Hemingway's stinger.** Thirteen snaps on Sunday. **The interior is the whole disease.**

**Five: does Leonard blitz?** One bad week against Kansas City shouldn't define a season.

Raider Nation, four names are in the conversation and I would keep every single one of them. **Because the answer to this roster was never subtraction — it's one interior defender who can beat a guard.**
