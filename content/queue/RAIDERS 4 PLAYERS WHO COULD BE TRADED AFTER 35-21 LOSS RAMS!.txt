## FIRST: WHY THIS LIST MATTERS MORE TODAY THAN IT DID YESTERDAY

Raider Nation, a week ago this would have been fun speculation. **Today it's a live conversation, and here's why.**

**The Raiders lost 35-21 to the Los Angeles Rams at Allegiant Stadium.** A 2-4 football team came into our building missing Davante Adams, missing Myles Garrett, missing Poona Ford, **with their left tackle Alaric Jackson serving a two-game suspension and a kicker they picked up off the street** — and they beat us by 14.

**That's four straight losses. This team is 3-4.** Four straight games allowing 30 or more points. **A red zone that went 0-for-3 in the first half while the Rams went 4-for-4.**

**And then Klint Kubiak stood at that podium and said the words that turned this video from a thought experiment into a scouting report:**

**"At this point, we're thinking about EVERYTHING. How to fix our problems, our issues in the red zone, in the third quarter — and PERSONNEL, SCHEME. We've got to talk about all of it."**

**Personnel. Scheme. All of it.** His words, not mine.

**He also said: "No one's coming to help us."** So let's talk about what this front office might do by itself.

## AND THE PRICE TAG THAT CHANGED THE WHOLE MARKET

Before the names, you need to know this, because every general manager in football noticed it.

**The Arizona Cardinals sent receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. to the Houston Texans earlier this month. The return: a 2027 SECOND-round pick and a SIXTH-round pick — with Houston also getting a seventh-rounder back in the deal.**

Raider Nation, sit with that. **A former FOURTH OVERALL PICK who was barely being used in the desert fetched a second-round pick.**

**That sets a bar.** And when desperate teams start paying real prices, **sellers with something to sell suddenly feel very good about their phone numbers.**

**That one trade is the reason this list exists.** Now let's count it down, from the Raider least likely to be moved to the man most likely to get a postcard from a new city.

## NUMBER FOUR: SAFETY ISAIAH POLA-MAO

He's at the bottom for a simple reason: **it takes two to tango, and I'm honestly not sure how many dance partners he has.**

**Here's the case.** He's **set to be a free agent after this season**, and the Raiders have stocked that safety room with younger bodies. **Trey'on Stokes has been the free safety. Jeremy Chinn is at strong safety. And Dalton Johnson and Tristin McCollum are waiting in the wings.**

**When a depth chart gets crowded, somebody becomes the extra chair at the table.**

And I'll be honest about the rest: **Pola-Mao has had a rough run in the eyes of a lot of fans and analysts.** He took a beating in the Week 3 postgame conversation, and **the criticism about his coverage has followed him around like a bad smell at a barbecue.**

**But here's the fair argument for him, and I want to make it properly.** Used as a **BOX SAFETY** — a man who plays closer to the line and plays physical football — **he has real value.** That's a legitimate NFL skill set. **The question is whether the Raiders want to use him that way, and whether another team will pay for it.**

Financially it's simple: **move him and somebody else absorbs the rest of his deal, freeing up a few million.** That's not life-changing money in this league, **but it's the kind of cap flexibility front offices like to hoard.**

## AND THERE IS EXACTLY ONE TEAM THAT MAKES SENSE

And Raider Nation, this one is a beautiful little piece of football soap opera.

**The Pittsburgh Steelers.**

**Why?** Two reasons, and they're both delicious. **First, Isaiah Pola-Mao is famously related to the legendary Steelers safety Troy Polamalu.** Family ties to that franchise's history. **Second — and this is the real football reason — he played his best football as an undrafted free agent under a defensive mind named Patrick Graham, who is now running the defense in Pittsburgh.**

**Family ties. A familiar coordinator. And a team that can use a safety who plays near the line.** Ding, ding, ding.

**The hypothetical floating around:** Pola-Mao to Pittsburgh for a **2027 sixth-round pick**, with the Raiders **adding a 2028 seventh to sweeten the pot.** And why a 2028 seventh? **Because the Raiders don't own a seventh-rounder this cycle, so a clean pick swap isn't on the menu.**

**Is that a lot? No.** But if it clears a roster spot and puts a pick in the pocket, **that's the sort of unglamorous business that deadlines are actually made of.**

## BUT HERE IS WHY I WOULD NOT DO IT

Raider Nation, I have to push back hard on this one, because something happened this week that this whole argument ignores.

**On Friday, Kubiak was asked about Pola-Mao earning a team honor despite having lost his starting job on defense. His answer: "Isaiah is a CORE PLAYER on teams. He has a lot of leadership qualities in the way he produces on the field and how accountable he is."**

**And then: "I'm just really glad he's a Raider."**

**And then on Sunday — in a game where almost nothing went right — a reporter told Kubiak his special teams were EXCEPTIONAL.** Kubiak's reply: **"That's been a standard throughout the year. That's a phase we continue to rely on."**

**So you want to trade a core special teams leader, for a sixth-round pick, out of the one unit on this football team that's actually playing well?** With a head coach who publicly said he's glad the man is here?

**Number four, folks. Least likely. And in my opinion, it should stay that way.**

## NUMBER THREE: TAKE A DEEP BREATH. KIRK COUSINS.

Yes. **That Kirk Cousins.** The man currently starting under center for the Las Vegas Raiders. **I can hear the keyboards warming up. Stay with me, because the contract is genuinely fascinating.**

**Cousins signed with the Raiders in April on a deal that LOOKED like a five-year, $172 million monster — but was really a one-year, fully guaranteed $20 million bridge.** And the structure is wild: **the Atlanta Falcons are covering roughly $8.7 million of it, while the Raiders' own cash outlay this season is about $1.3 million.**

**And then comes the twist that tells you how this ends.**

**A fully guaranteed $10 MILLION roster bonus is due on the third day of the new league year. And if Cousins is still on the roster on the FIFTH day — which lands around mid-March — a two-year, $80 MILLION option becomes FULLY GUARANTEED.**

Raider Nation, let that sink in. **Unless this franchise plans to make a surprising long-term financial commitment to a 38-year-old quarterback, the final chapter of this story is already written in a spreadsheet.**

**This is a bridge. Everybody knows it — including Kirk.**

## AND REMEMBER WHO IS WAITING

**Fernando Mendoza — a national champion out of Indiana and the FIRST OVERALL PICK in the 2026 draft — has been learning this league from the sideline.** And behind him, **Aidan O'Connell, a man who has started NFL games before, is the third quarterback on the depth chart.**

**That third arm matters more than people realize.** It means the Raiders are **not forced to hand a rookie the keys the moment a veteran leaves.** There's a safety net.

**Now here's the spicy logic, and it's smart.** If Las Vegas ever decides Mendoza's time has come, there are exactly two ways to do it:

**Option one: you BENCH the veteran, hand over the offense, and watch Cousins gather dust — and you have ZERO leverage to get anything back.**

**Option two: you TRADE the veteran, get something real for him, and THEN hand over the offense.**

**If the Raiders go down that road, option two is obviously the smart play.** **A third-round pick for a quarterback you already know won't be back is the kind of gift most front offices dream about.** Imagine turning that pick into a wide receiver, a defensive tackle, or an interior offensive lineman. **That's enormous upside for a man who was only ever here for the bridge.**

**The hypothetical on the table: a third-round pick to Las Vegas, with Cousins going to a contender — and Minnesota, where he spent six seasons and made his name, is the proposed landing spot.** A reunion with real sentimental flavor. **And remember, Jon Gruden went on a podcast this week and said if he were Philadelphia or Minnesota, he'd offer the Raiders a SECOND-round pick. That's his opinion, not a report — but the number is now in the air.**

## BUT HERE'S THE CATCH, AND IT'S A BIG ONE

**The Raiders would only trade Kirk Cousins if the season goes sideways.** If they keep winning, **you don't touch the quarterback.** If the price is poor, **you don't touch the quarterback.**

**And on the football side, the man has been doing his job.** He has **started every game this season.** He's **tied for the NFL lead with 14 touchdown passes.** Against Buffalo he threw for **313 yards and two touchdowns on 23 of 41 with one interception**, put up **six scoring drives**, and **was never sacked on 41 attempts** — and the Raiders still came up short 33-30. **That's the picture of a team that's competitive but not convincing. Close enough to dream, far enough to wonder.**

**He's also been a steadying presence for a first-year head coach who knows him intimately from their Minnesota years, 2019 through 2021.** And there's a real argument that **a veteran quarterback helps the DEFENSE** by keeping the offense out of chaos. **Ripping that away mid-season is a genuine risk.**

**And the leadership isn't theoretical.** Andrew Janocko told us this month that Cousins **"thrives on accountability, wants to be pushed, wants to be coached,"** and that when young players see a veteran be the first to say *"I've got to step up,"* **"that's the type of stuff young guys should mimic."** Even **Aaron Donald said of him: "He looks good. He's playing well."**

**So where do I land? Honestly, where I landed yesterday: I'm not saying it happens. I'm saying that if a team calls with a strong enough offer, ignoring it would be foolish.** Four straight losses makes that call more likely, not less.

**Number three: the quarterback of the moment, with a contract that practically has an expiration date stamped on it.**

## NUMBER TWO: AND THIS IS A REAL HEAD-TURNER. KOLTON MILLER.

Raider Nation, a **Raiders lifer. A first-round pick back in 2018.** A man who has protected a revolving door of quarterbacks through coaching change after coaching change and stayed standing through all of it.

**And yet here he is on the list.**

**In fact, one popular argument says Miller might be MORE likely to be traded than Cousins — because his situation doesn't depend on whether the Raiders win or lose.** It's about **the contract, the calendar, and the injury history.**

**Let's talk about the money, because this is the part that drives everything.**

**Miller signed a three-year, $66 million extension in the summer of 2025, running through the 2028 season.** And here's the detail that matters: **$17.5 MILLION of his 2027 salary is GUARANTEED FOR INJURY.**

Raider Nation, do you see the trap? **If he suffers a serious injury this season, the Raiders could be on the hook for a lot of money next year for a player who may not be able to help them.**

**And Miller has had his share of bad luck.** A **high ankle sprain in Week 4 last season** sent him to injured reserve and ended his year early. **At 31 years old, with a body that has taken a pounding for eight-plus seasons**, that's a risk the front office has to price — every single Sunday he plays.

## AND THERE'S A FOOTBALL ARGUMENT TOO, AS MUCH AS IT HURTS

**Miller has been one of the most consistent left tackles in this league for years** — and that is no small feat given the quarterback carousel and the coaching turnover he's endured. **He deserves enormous respect for that.**

**But the film in recent seasons has shown some slippage.** **In 2024, he allowed the most pressures and sacks since his rookie year.** And the honest question is whether **the player who was once a top-ten left tackle is still that player today.**

**His run blocking in particular has become a topic of conversation.** And Raider Nation — **for a team trying to build a rushing attack around Ashton Jeanty, that matters enormously.** We just watched Jeanty run 10 times for 22 yards against Buffalo, and a Rams defense hold this offense to 21 points at home.

**And then there's the depth. Charles Grant, a third-round pick from 2025, is already sitting behind him.** We know **a report out of The Athletic said Grant took at least one starting rep at left tackle in warm-ups before the Buffalo game.** **Trey Zuhn came in as a 2026 third-rounder.** **DJ Glaze is holding down the right side.** And the Raiders just added **Amari Kight and Darian Kinnard** to the practice squad.

**That's a lot of tackles in one building.**

## WHERE I STAND ON KOLTON MILLER

**I'd listen. I would not initiate.**

**You do not hand a 38-year-old quarterback a brand new blind side in Week 8** while your receivers can't separate against man coverage and your head coach is already saying he did a poor job getting the offense going. **That's not a plan, that's a panic.**

**But if somebody offers a second-round pick?** With $17.5 million of injury guarantee sitting there like an unexploded shell, and three young tackles on the roster? **Raider Nation, I understand why that call would be hard to hang up on.** I wouldn't like it. **I'd understand it.**

## AND NUMBER ONE — HERE'S WHERE I HAVE TO BE HONEST WITH YOU

Raider Nation, the countdown I was working from **never actually revealed its number one.** It got to Kolton Miller and ran out of road. **I'm not going to invent somebody's analysis and put it in their mouth.**

**So number one is MINE, and I'll give you my receipts.**

**Number one is Jackson Powers-Johnson.**

**And here's the case, with everything we've learned in the last ten days.**

**He got benched.** Against Buffalo, Kubiak started second-year lineman **Caleb Rogers at right guard**, and **JPJ took ZERO offensive snaps.** Not a rotation. Zero.

**And it didn't even go well for the man who replaced him.** Rogers **allowed a couple of pressures, took a penalty, and whiffed on a block at the goal line that stopped Ashton Jeanty for a loss in the fourth quarter** — and JPJ *still* didn't play.

**Then there's the structural problem, and it's the one that always decides these things: Klint Kubiak didn't draft him. John Spytek didn't draft him.** He was a high pick under the previous regime. **A new staff makes its own decisions about where players fit, and being somebody else's second-round pick buys you nothing.**

**And look at how crowded that interior has become.** **Tyler Linderbaum's arrival changed the entire picture at center.** They added **Spencer Burford**, who — according to the data from the Buffalo game — **allowed zero pressures on 47 pass plays with an 85.6 pass-blocking grade, the best by any Raiders lineman all season.** They drafted **Trey Zuhn.** They brought in **Bryce Cabeldue.**

**And he has real value to somebody. He won the RIMINGTON TROPHY as the best center in college football.** That's not a fringe player. **That's a man another team will believe in.**

**Fit-wise? Seattle, where Kubiak has history** — and that history is proven, because the Raiders just signed Amari Kight off exactly that connection. **Plus the Giants, Texans, Saints and Patriots as teams that could use a guard.**

## BUT I AM GOING TO ARGUE WITH MYSELF, BECAUSE SOMETHING CHANGED

And this is why I love doing this honestly.

**On Friday, unprompted, Kubiak said this: "A lot of credit to Jackson. He's got a great attitude. He's still working to get better, and he knows his best football can be in front of him. Just keep working."**

**Nobody asked him to say that.** A staff that had completely moved on doesn't volunteer that line.

**And I'll tell you my real position: I hate the idea of trading him for a fifth-round pick. I think he's worth more than that.** Strip away the injuries and the inconsistency and **he's a talented young player who could still become a quality starter — maybe even at center.**

**But here's the cold truth of a 3-4 football team fifteen days from a deadline: assets sitting on the bench do nothing for a team trying to win games.** If he isn't in the plan, **you cash him in and you turn that pick into a receiver who can separate.**

**Number one. The man I think is most likely to be wearing different colors in November.**

## NOW LET ME TELL YOU WHO I WOULD NOT TRADE

Because a list of four names makes it sound like everything's for sale, **and it isn't.**

**Brock Bowers.** Don't even pick up the phone. The film analysts were brutal about his lack of separation against Rams man coverage — **and they were right** — but the reason is that **this team is using the best tight end in football as a number one WIDE RECEIVER because it doesn't have one.** That's not his failure. **That's a roster hole wearing his jersey.**

**Ashton Jeanty.** He's running into eight and nine men in the box because nobody out wide makes a defense back off. **You don't sell the back. You fix the box.**

**Maxx Crosby.** He saw **sixteen chips and double teams in the Buffalo game alone** — the highest rate of his career. **You don't trade the man who's being tripled. You buy him help.**

**Tonka Hemingway, Tre Tucker, Michael Mayer, Dont'e Thornton Jr., Fernando Mendoza.** Young, cheap, developing. **This is the core.**

Raider Nation, here's my honest verdict on all of it.

**I don't want a fire sale. I want one phone call.**

**The diagnosis has never been clearer.** National film analysts laid out the chain on television this week: **receivers don't win one-on-one against man coverage → defenses put eight or nine in the box on Jeanty → no run game → no balance → and in Kubiak's own words, "you can't win in this league having to throw it as much as we're throwing it."**
