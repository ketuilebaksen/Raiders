# RAIDERS JUST FOUND 2 SURPRISE WEAPONS AFTER THE CRAZY 49ERS GAME! 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, I know we lost 38-17. I know Kirk Cousins threw three picks. I know we fumbled twice at the goal line.

**But I went back through that tape and I found TWO PLAYERS who came out of that afternoon looking like real, legitimate, long-term weapons for this football team — and neither of them was supposed to be the story.**

**SURPRISE WEAPON NUMBER ONE: rookie safety Traden Stokes. FIVE TACKLES and an INTERCEPTION where he stayed with Brock Purdy's eyes long enough to get in FRONT of a ball meant for GEORGE KITTLE.**

**SURPRISE WEAPON NUMBER TWO: Fonz Faticasi. Fourth and one, Brock Purdy tries to sneak it — and Faticasi MEETS HIM IN THE MIDDLE and our offense gets the ball back.**

**Raider Nation, in a game where everything went wrong, a rookie safety out-read a 69-percent passer and a defensive tackle stoned a quarterback sneak on fourth down. Those are two building blocks. On the worst Sunday of the season.**

**And Nakobe Dean finished with EIGHTEEN TACKLES of his own, which deserves its own paragraph and is going to get one.**

But Raider Nation, I also have to tell you the truth about this game, **because somebody has to explain to me how we scored on TWO OF OUR THREE first-half possessions and still walked into that locker room down DOUBLE DIGITS.**

**And it's not the five turnovers. The Niners had already exposed two mistakes before any of that — and one of them goes all the way back to DRAFT WEEKEND.**

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing **6,000 believers** — hit that like button, drop **"I AM A RAIDER"** in the comments, and let's break this thing down.

## FIRST: THE MATH THAT BROKE MY BRAIN

Raider Nation, follow this with me, because this is the single most important thing about the 38-17 loss.

**We scored on TWO of our THREE first-half possessions. And we were down 21-10 at the break.**

**How?**

**Because San Francisco opened the game with THREE STRAIGHT TOUCHDOWN DRIVES.**

**And every one of those touchdown drives lasted AT LEAST TWELVE PLAYS.**

**So we only got THREE possessions in an entire half of football.**

**When Kirk sneaked into the end zone to make it 14-7, I was doing the usual fan math — get one stop, get the ball back, we've got a chance to tie this thing. And then I watched San Francisco march right back down the field. By the time Matt Gay kicked that field goal it was 21-10 and WE HAD USED UP OUR ENTIRE HALF.**

**Three possessions. Two scores. Down eleven.**

**Raider Nation, you cannot out-score a team that only lets you touch the ball three times.**

## AND HERE'S THE CLOCK, AND IT'S OBSCENE

**San Francisco had the ball for TWENTY MINUTES AND THIRTY-FIVE SECONDS.**

**We had it for NINE MINUTES AND CHANGE.**

**They picked up EIGHTEEN FIRST DOWNS on THIRTY-EIGHT PLAYS.**

**We picked up FIVE.**

**By halftime they'd outgained us 240 YARDS TO 75.**

Raider Nation, **that's the loss. Right there. That's the whole thing.** **Klint Kubiak said it himself at the podium: "You look at just the play count, and it was like 38 to 17. That's the exact opposite of how you want time of possession to go in a first half."**

**Eighteen first downs to five. That is not a game. That is a possession.**

**And THAT is what bothers me about blaming a 38-17 loss on five turnovers. The turnovers were real and they were fatal. But the game was already structurally lost before the second half ever started.**

## MISTAKE NUMBER ONE: OUR LINEBACKERS ON SECOND DOWN

Raider Nation, this is the thing that wore me out watching it.

**When the offense only needs A YARD on second down — are you really going to TURN YOUR BACK ON THE HANDOFF?**

**Because that's what we kept doing.**

**And here's the trap: take ONE STEP toward the line and Brock Purdy throws into the space you just left.**

**Think about what that buys a play caller, Raider Nation.** **He doesn't have to out-scheme you. He just has to show you a run look and watch you talk yourself out of defending it.**

**On one early drive, we actually DID make them face third and long — and Purdy SCRAMBLED FOR 18 YARDS.**

**Raider Nation, that one hurt the most.** **I'm watching our guys finally EARN the down they want, and then their quarterback just RUNS PAST THE STICKS.**

**And when that happens, the next first down feels completely different.** **Because now somebody has to keep an eye on the QUARTERBACK too. That's ONE MORE DEFENDER who has to hesitate while the handoff is coming STRAIGHT AT US.**

**That's how a defense gets paralyzed. Not by talent. By accumulated hesitation.**

## AND THEN THE RAMS GAME CAME RIGHT BACK TO ME

Raider Nation, this is where I got a very familiar sick feeling.

**San Francisco could walk TWO BACKS onto the field, give us a BUNCH of possible places to look — and then just HAND IT OFF BETWEEN THE CENTER AND THE GUARD.**

**On the opening drive, Christian McCaffrey went UP THE MIDDLE for ELEVEN. Kyle Juszczyk went UP THE MIDDLE on the NEXT SNAP and got them to the ONE.**

**All that work figuring out who might get the ball — and we still had to MOVE THE GUY STANDING ACROSS FROM US.**

Raider Nation, **that is the exact same thing the Rams did to us. The exact same thing.** **Heavy personnel, a dozen possible answers pre-snap, and then the most basic run in football straight up the gut.**

**Because the fancy formation isn't the point. The fancy formation is there to make you THINK. And while you're thinking, a 320-pound guard is already moving your defensive tackle backwards.**

**So the question is simple, Raider Nation: WHO DID WE EXPECT TO WIN THOSE BLOCKS?**

## AND THAT QUESTION GOES BACK TO DRAFT WEEKEND

Raider Nation, this is my first real issue, and I want to be fair about it.

**We waited until the SEVENTH ROUND to draft a defensive tackle — Brandon Cleveland at pick 229.**

**We also brought in Fonz Faticasi, so the position DID get some attention. I'm not pretending it was ignored.**

**I just think we BET TOO MUCH on that group being enough.**

**After watching last year's offensive line, I wanted us to spend an EARLIER PICK inside — or look HARDER AT A TRADE.**

**And here's the receipt from Sunday: the Niners ran for 125 YARDS BEFORE HALFTIME. FIVE YARDS A CARRY.**

**Raider Nation, how many ways do you need to dress up a handoff when that's what it gets you? You don't. You just run it again.**

**That's a long afternoon to spend HOPING SOMEBODY WINS AN INDIVIDUAL MATCHUP.**

## BUT — AND HERE IS SURPRISE WEAPON NUMBER TWO — FATICASI SHOWED US WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE

Raider Nation, to be completely fair, **Fonz Faticasi gave us a pretty good picture of what WINNING one of those snaps actually looks like.**

**Later in the game, Purdy tried to SNEAK IT on FOURTH AND ONE.**

**FATICASI MET HIM IN THE MIDDLE. And our offense got the ball.**

**I LOVED that play.**

Raider Nation, **a quarterback sneak on fourth and one is supposed to be a free yard in the modern NFL. It is the highest-percentage play in football.** **And our defensive tackle went and took it away.**

**That's the proof of concept. That's the answer to "who's going to win that block?" — HIM. He did it. On the biggest short-yardage snap of the game.**

**And here's my only complaint: I'd have loved it even MORE to see that kind of push on an EARLY DOWN, when their ENTIRE PLAYBOOK was open.** **Because fourth and one is the easy one to get up for. First and ten in the second quarter when nobody's watching — that's where seasons are decided.**

**Raider Nation, if Fonz Faticasi can do that on fourth and one, he can do it on first and ten. We just have to ask him to.**

## MISTAKE NUMBER TWO: THE FRONT WE STUCK WITH

Raider Nation, put yourself in Rob Leonard's shoes for a second. **You can't go back and change the draft. But you CAN decide who lines up on the edge.**

**He went with a BASE LOOK and walked QUAY WALKER UP as the FIFTH MAN.**

**And I get why. Walker CAN rush. And you'd like to keep one of your better athletes on the field.**

**My second issue isn't the call. It's STICKING WITH THAT ANSWER WHILE THE RUN GAME KEPT WORKING.**

**I wanted ANOTHER DEFENSIVE LINEMAN taking on a blocker, and WALKER FREE TO RUN TO THE BALL.**

Raider Nation, **that's a completely different job for Quay Walker even though the front looks almost identical from the stands.** **As the fifth man on the line, he's got to defeat a blocker. Free behind the line, he's a 240-pound athlete running sideline to sideline, arriving unblocked.**

**One of those uses his best trait. The other one wastes it.**

**And remember what we said before kickoff — the pregame plan was literally: "use Quay Walker closer to the line of scrimmage and let Nakobe Dean blitz more, to get pressure from places the Niners don't expect." We had the idea. We just ran out of patience with it.**

## AND YOU COULD FEEL THE COST OF CHASING A STOP

Raider Nation, this part is the saddest.

**On San Francisco's THIRD touchdown, WE SENT PRESSURE — and Purdy found McCaffrey WIDE OPEN FOR TWELVE YARDS.**

**Earlier in the year, I LIKED how aggressive those calls felt. On Sunday, I was asking: WHO HAD THE BACK IF THE RUSH DIDN'T GET THERE?**

**It reminded me of the preseason game against Arizona, when every bad series made me want to try ANOTHER CALL.**

**And eventually, as a fan, I have to ask myself: WHICH CALL DO I ACTUALLY TRUST?**

Raider Nation, **that's the honest emotional truth of being a fan of this defense right now.** **We've cycled through so many answers that I don't know which one I believe in anymore. And I suspect that's how it feels in the huddle too.**

**And remember what Kubiak said Monday about that exact kind of play — the one where Tonka Hemingway ended up covering Christian McCaffrey: "We did NOT PEEL ON THE HALFBACK. That was NOT THOSE GUYS' JOB."**

**So it's a blitz where somebody didn't peel on the back. Which is exactly the question I asked watching it live: who had McCaffrey?**

## SO LET'S TALK ABOUT ROB LEONARD HONESTLY

Raider Nation, I understand the heat on him. Let me give you the numbers and then my actual position.

**Opponents made THIRTY-FOUR RED ZONE TRIPS through our first eight games. San Francisco added FOUR MORE.**

**We've given up at least THIRTY POINTS IN SIX STRAIGHT GAMES.**

**We sit at 4-5.**

**But I also remember getting FOUR FOURTH-QUARTER TAKEAWAYS AGAINST THE SAINTS to win that football game. That was this same coordinator. That was this same defense.**

**So here's where I land: I'd give a FIRST-YEAR COORDINATOR ROOM TO LEARN. And Klint Kubiak just made it clear on Monday he's doing exactly that — "stand behind Rob, stand behind our defensive staff, stand behind our players."**

**But I'd still try SOMETHING DIFFERENT on Sunday.**

## AND HERE ARE THE FOUR THINGS I'D ACTUALLY TRY

Raider Nation, this is the constructive part, so stay with me.

**ONE: LET HIM SEE THE GAME FROM THE BOOTH.**

Sometimes a coordinator calls a better game from upstairs than from the sideline. **You see the whole field. You see the structure instead of the collision.** **It costs nothing and it's been the fix for plenty of coaches.**

**TWO: GIVE JOE WOODS A LOOK AT CALLING PLAYS — or change who gets the call in the huddle if COMMUNICATION is the issue.**

**Raider Nation, notice what that is and isn't. That's not firing anybody. That's handing one specific responsibility to a veteran coach for one week to see if the signal gets through cleaner.** **If the problem is that the call arrives late and the defense lines up unsettled, that's a fixable logistics problem, not a scheme problem.**

**THREE: START WITH A HEAVIER FRONT ON RUNNING DOWNS.**

**And then — on obvious passing downs — MOVE MAXX CROSBY INSIDE OCCASIONALLY and make a GUARD handle him without the same CHIP HELP he sees outside.**

Raider Nation, **I love this idea and here's why.** **Every single week, offenses dedicate a tight end or a back to chipping Maxx Crosby on the edge. We counted SIXTEEN chips and doubles on him in one game this season.** **But that help is built into the OUTSIDE rush. Line him up over a guard and that chip isn't there — the tight end is standing twelve yards away from where the problem now is.**

**One snap. One alignment change. A free one-on-one for the best defensive player on our roster.**

**FOUR: FIVE GUYS UP FRONT WITH ONE LINEBACKER and EXTRA DEFENSIVE BACKS behind him.**

**Because here's the logic, Raider Nation: if we're going to ask a SAFETY to come downhill and tackle, GIVE HIM A CLEAN VIEW OF THE BACK.** **Don't make him diagnose through a pile. Five linemen occupy five blockers, one linebacker fills, and the safety arrives with his eyes already on the ball.**

**Let's find out whether that's something our guys can execute TWICE IN A ROW.**

## AND HERE'S THE QUESTION I'D ASK BEFORE CHASING A NAME

Raider Nation, this is the most important paragraph in this whole breakdown, so please hear me on it.

**For anybody already looking up other coordinators: WHAT EXACTLY WOULD YOU WANT THE NEXT GUY TO DO WITH THESE SAME TACKLES?**

**That's the question I'd ask before getting caught up in a name.**

**I want to know WHOSE JOB GETS EASIER WITH THE CHANGE.**

**If the answer is "Quay Walker gets to chase from behind the line" or "Maxx Crosby gets a clean rush on a guard" — okay. NOW we're talking about something I can PICTURE.**

**But if the answer is just "a new voice," Raider Nation, then we've fired a first-year coordinator and handed the same personnel to a stranger in Week 10. That's not a plan. That's a press release.**

**And that, honestly, is the strongest argument FOR what Kubiak did Monday.**

## NOW SURPRISE WEAPON NUMBER ONE: TRADEN STOKES IS THE REAL DEAL

Raider Nation, let's get to the good part, because this kid has earned it.

**One guy I'd trust coming downhill OR playing deep is Traden Stokes.**

**Our rookie safety had FIVE TACKLES. And on his INTERCEPTION, he STAYED WITH PURDY'S EYES long enough to get IN FRONT OF A BALL MEANT FOR GEORGE KITTLE.**

Raider Nation, **I need you to appreciate how hard that is.**

**Brock Purdy is completing 69 percent of his passes — THIRD BEST IN THE ENTIRE LEAGUE. George Kittle is one of the best tight ends of this generation.**

**And a ROOKIE SAFETY read the quarterback's eyes, trusted what he saw, and beat Kittle to the spot.**

**That's not athleticism. That's PROCESSING. And processing is the thing you can't teach — which is exactly why Klint Kubiak keeps using the same two words about him: "PLAYMAKER" and "INSTINCTIVE."**

**He called him "a SHOT OF ADRENALINE to our operation." He said "he's been a BRIGHT SPOT THROUGHOUT THE SEASON." Two press conferences in a row.**

**Raider Nation, I can sit here wishing we'd picked a defensive tackle earlier AND still be absolutely thrilled we took Stokes. Those two things live comfortably in the same sentence.**

## AND NAKOBE DEAN HAD EIGHTEEN TACKLES

Raider Nation, this number deserves more attention than it's getting.

**EIGHTEEN TACKLES.**

**Now, I'll be honest with you about what a number like that means: eighteen tackles from a linebacker is simultaneously a compliment and an indictment.** **It means he was flying around making plays. It ALSO means there were an awful lot of runners to tackle, and most of them had already gotten past the line of scrimmage.**

**But Raider Nation, when the front isn't holding up, your linebacker can either disappear or he can make eighteen tackles. He made eighteen tackles.**

**That's a man who did not quit on a 38-17 football game. Remember that.**

## NOW THE TURNOVERS — AND THE ORDER MATTERS ENORMOUSLY

Raider Nation, this is the part that's going to hurt, because Stokes' interception gave us the ball down eleven, **and it was the SECOND of THREE times we had possession with that exact deficit.**

**Three separate chances. Down eleven. And the ORDER matters.**

**CHANCE ONE — the second half kickoff.** Kirk LOST CONTROL of the ball, gathered it, and tried to reach Tre Tucker. **Jaden Duggar took it back 35 yards for the touchdown.**

**Raider Nation, once that ball hit the ground I was ready to live with second and long. Fall on it. Eat it. Kubiak said the same thing: "You've got to EAT IT and go to the next down."**

**But then Kirk came right back and hit BROCK BOWERS FOR A 21-YARD TOUCHDOWN. That got us to 28-17.**

**Which is exactly why the Stokes interception felt like such a BREAK. We had SURVIVED the worst throw of the day and we had the ball with a chance to pull closer.**

**CHANCE TWO — and this is where it broke me.** **FRED WARNER PUNCHED IT OUT OF BOWERS' HANDS and DRE GREENLAW RECOVERED.**

**CHANCE THREE — after that fourth-down stop by Faticasi.** **Michael Mayer caught one over the middle, gained 24 YARDS, and got ALL THE WAY TO THE ONE — before UPTON STOUT KNOCKED IT LOOSE. WARNER RECOVERED IN THE END ZONE.**

**Raider Nation, ONE MORE YARD on that play and I'm sitting here talking about how to WIN this football game. Instead I'm watching San Francisco take over at the 20.**

## AND I HAVE TO GIVE SAN FRANCISCO CREDIT ON THOSE TWO FUMBLES

Raider Nation, this is the professional observation, and it's important.

**BOTH TIGHT ENDS had the ball secured well enough that I thought the play was OVER.**

**These were not careless, sloppy, swinging-the-ball-around fumbles. These were two good players who thought they'd made the play.**

**And then watch Warner ATTACK THE BALL on the first one — and then watch WHERE HE ENDS UP on the second one. He's there to RECOVER what SOMEBODY ELSE knocked loose.**

**Raider Nation, THAT IS A DEFENSE FINISHING THE PLAY TOGETHER.**

**That's the continuity Kirk Cousins warned us about. That's what "a lot of continuity and it shows in the tape" actually looks like on the field — eleven men who all know the ball is still live.**

**And Kubiak gave them their due on Monday: "Credit to San Francisco. 54 and I think 20 did a GREAT JOB of forcing some balls out." And then he put it on himself: "WE'VE GOT TO COACH BALL SECURITY BETTER."**

**And he defended them: "Those guys are HARDER ON THEMSELVES than anybody. Those are TWO OF OUR BEST PLAYERS that we really COUNT ON."**

**He's right. Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer are not the problem with this football team.**

## AND THE LAST TWO POSSESSIONS FINISHED THE JOB

**We got down to the ONE again — and OFFENSIVE PASS INTERFERENCE wiped out a MAYER TOUCHDOWN, and RONALDO GREEN picked off the throw for Tucker in the END ZONE.**

**The last interception went to MALIK MUSTAFA on a DEEP FOURTH DOWN SHOT.**

**So that's how our FINAL FOUR POSSESSIONS ended, Raider Nation: TWO FUMBLES, then TWO PICKS.**

**And this is the stat that complicates everything: THE LINE ALLOWED ZERO SACKS.**

**Zero sacks against Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner. That's a real accomplishment and it deserves to be said.**

**But I still want to know whether Kirk had ROOM TO STEP INTO A THROW.** **Because "no sacks" and "a clean platform" are two different things entirely — and Kubiak himself said the red zone pick happened "where we DID NOT GIVE HIM PROTECTION to make a good decision."**

**And when Kirk DOES have time, Raider Nation, I expect him to make a better decision than he made on Sunday. He said so himself: "the ball just needs to be thrown into the first row of the stands."**

## AND NOW LOOK AT WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WORKING

Raider Nation, this is the blueprint for Seattle, so write it down.

**BROCK BOWERS FOUND OPENINGS IN THE ZONE AND CAUGHT SEVEN BALLS FOR 111 YARDS.**

**Seven for 111 against a top-five defense. Plus a 21-yard touchdown.**

**The man is a problem for everybody and he was a problem on Sunday too.**

**But the UNDERNEATH THROWS TO OUR BACKS were a completely different story, and these numbers are brutal:**

**ASHTON JEANTY: FIVE CATCHES FOR MINUS FIVE YARDS.**

**MIKE WASHINGTON JR.: SIX CARRIES FOR MINUS THREE YARDS.**

**Raider Nation, negative yardage. On eleven touches.** *(Quick note on the numbers: Kubiak said at the podium that the two backs combined for 17 touches and 17 yards, which includes Washington's receiving work — so the rushing-and-receiving splits land a little differently depending on how you cut it. Either way, the headline is the same: nothing worked on the ground.)*

**And here's WHY it didn't work: WHEN FRED WARNER CAN TAKE HIS FIRST STEP DOWNHILL, A SWING PASS MIGHT AS WELL TELL HIM WHERE TO MEET THE RUNNER.**

**Raider Nation, that's the perfect explanation.** **A swing pass is a slow-developing play that goes sideways. If the linebacker isn't held by anything, he just runs to the spot and arrives before the ball does.** **Negative five yards on five catches isn't Ashton Jeanty failing. That's a play design meeting the best linebacker in football with nothing in his way.**

**So that's why I'd KEEP WORKING THROUGH THE TIGHT ENDS against Seattle. At least those throws made SOMEBODY TURN AROUND AND COVER GROUND.**

**Make a defender run backwards. That's the whole idea.**
