Raider Nation, there's a date you need to circle: November 10th. That's the NFL trade deadline, and for the first time in years, it's a date that actually matters to this football team.

Think about what that means. For the last several seasons, the only question around the deadline in Las Vegas was which of our good players another team might want. This year, at 2-0, with the offense scoring at least 26 points in both games, the question flips completely. Should the Raiders be buyers?

And one name has emerged in that conversation: Cleveland Browns receiver Jerry Jeudy.

Let me be crystal clear right now, because this channel always separates a report from a scenario. There is no reported trade between Las Vegas and Cleveland. Nobody has said Spytek called Andrew Berry. This is a proposed scenario laid out by an outlet looking at both rosters, and nothing more.

But the circumstances behind it are real, and the price being floated is so low it made me sit up. So today: the players who created this conversation, the two problems that haven't gone away, and the full Jeudy file, including why our head coach already knows him.

I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we build toward 6,000 believers. Let's get into it.

THE MEN WHO MADE THIS A BUYER'S CONVERSATION

You don't talk about adding pieces unless the pieces you already have are worth adding to. So let's start there, quickly.

Tre Tucker was electric at SoFi. Seven targets, five catches, 119 yards and a touchdown. And I want you to understand why that touchdown mattered so much: it wasn't a blown coverage. He ran right past the Chargers' secondary and won with speed. There was also another ball where he slipped and fell, which means his afternoon could have been even bigger. At this point the honest question is whether Tucker is becoming this team's number one receiver.

Kirk Cousins finished 19 of 29 for 253 yards, three touchdowns, one interception, and a passer rating of 113.1. And that interception came on the play where Tucker slipped while wide open, so it's not a decision you hang on the quarterback. He's also now the first Raiders quarterback since Kenny Stabler to open a season with three touchdown passes in each of the first two games.

Hezekiah Masses, the fifth-round rookie, delivered the kind of day scouts dream about: two interceptions, 29 return yards, two pass deflections, a tackle for loss, and five total tackles. He was known coming out of Cal for his ball awareness, and that's exactly what showed up. He read the quarterback, reacted fast, and attacked the football. Nobody expects two interceptions every week. But if he plays well enough that this staff is comfortable leaving him out there, that's a major development for this defense.

Matt Gay went one for one on field goals, and that one was a career-best 59-yarder, plus all three extra points. In a road division game, three points from 59 yards is enormous.

And Cody White, who was elevated from the practice squad on Saturday, caught two touchdowns. The first was contested with a defender all over him. The second came wide open. He contributed on special teams too. That is exactly how a practice squad player forces a conversation about a permanent roster spot.

THE TWO PROBLEMS THAT DIDN'T GO AWAY

Now the honest part, because 2-0 doesn't mean clean.

First, right tackle. DJ Glaze had a rough afternoon: he allowed pressure, gave up a sack, and missed an assignment in the run game. I'll note that the film reviews aren't unanimous here, because one breakdown I went through thought he was the best of a struggling offensive line group even with that sack. Both things can be true, and neither is good: if your best lineman gave up a sack and missed a run assignment, the whole unit had a bad day. With a 38-year-old quarterback operating this offense, protection at that spot has to be better.

Second, Ashton Jeanty. This wasn't the performance we all wanted: 21 carries for 48 yards, 2.3 a carry, with a long run of nine yards. Plus three drops in the passing game, including one that would have converted a first down.

But let's keep perspective, and I mean that sincerely. One bad game doesn't erase what this young back can become. He put up more than 100 yards and two touchdowns in Week One. The season is three weeks old. And his next chance comes on Sunday against New Orleans, at home, where he gets to answer.

WHY WIDE RECEIVER IS THE POSITION

So why does the deadline conversation land on a receiver instead of the offensive line?

Look at the target distribution. Tucker is emerging. Cody White came out of nowhere. Jack Bech is developing in the slot. And Jalen Nailor had one catch for nine yards on three targets. Brock Bowers is close to returning, and when he does, this passing game gains its best weapon.

But here's the thing about Bowers: he's coming back from a knee procedure, and no smart staff is going to hand him a full workload immediately. Which means for the next several weeks, this offense still needs somebody on the outside that defenses have to plan for. That's the hole. Not a superstar. A reliable, experienced professional who can win a matchup and hold a safety's attention.

And that's exactly the player Jeudy used to be.

THE JEUDY FILE

Let's go through his story honestly, the good and the bad.

He was the 15th overall pick in the draft, and Cleveland acquired him from Denver in 2024. His first season with the Browns was excellent: 90 catches, 1,229 yards, four touchdowns, a Pro Bowl selection, and sixth in the entire NFL in receiving yards. That production earned him a three-year extension worth $52.5 million.

Then 2025 went the other way. Fifty catches, 602 yards, and ten drops charged to him. That's a massive step backward in every category that matters.

And this season, his role has shrunk even further. Two catches for 26 yards in Week One. In Week Two, no receptions on a single target.

Part of that is what's happening around him. Cleveland drafted a receiver in the first round of the 2026 draft and another in the second, and both rookies are already getting real opportunities, with one making an immediate impact. That creates a crowded room. And at 27 years old, on a veteran contract, Jeudy becomes the obvious candidate to get squeezed out as a franchise prioritizes the young players it just invested premium picks in.

So the situation is straightforward: a talented veteran, on a real contract, watching his role disappear on a team that has moved on to the next generation.

THE CONNECTION NOBODY ELSE MENTIONS

Here's the part that makes this more than a random name on a list. Klint Kubiak already knows Jerry Jeudy. Kubiak was on the Denver staff in 2022, when Jeudy was still there.

If that sounds familiar, it should. We talked about this exact dynamic with Cody White, who spent time with Kubiak in Seattle last season and then scored two touchdowns on seven snaps. There's a pattern with this head coach: he trusts players he already knows, and he knows exactly how to use them. Familiarity shortens the learning curve, and in the middle of a season, the learning curve is everything. A receiver who already understands the concepts can contribute in week one with the team. A stranger takes a month.

That's why the Jeudy idea is more believable than most midseason receiver rumors.

THE PRICE, AND WHY IT'S THE WHOLE STORY

Now the part that got my attention. The framework being floated would send Jeudy to Las Vegas for a 2027 fourth-round pick, which could become a third-round pick if he plays at least 65 percent of the Raiders' offensive snaps for the rest of the regular season.

A conditional fourth. For a former Pro Bowl receiver who finished sixth in the league in receiving yards two seasons ago.

Now, I have to add the caveats, because that report added them too. The Browns could ask for a straight third-round pick instead. There are salary cap questions on Cleveland's side of any deal. And again, none of this is a negotiation, it's a proposed structure.

But compare this with the other receiver conversation we covered last week. Stephen A. Smith spent a whole segment telling the Raiders to trade for Justin Jefferson, which would cost multiple first-round picks and roughly $35 million a year against a cap where this team had about $8.26 million of room. I said then that the phone call is worth making but the price probably isn't.

This is the realistic version of that dream. Not the best receiver on the planet. A proven veteran with a Pro Bowl on his résumé, for a late-round pick, on a contract that's already been signed.

WHAT HE'D ACTUALLY DO FOR THIS OFFENSE

Let me paint the picture on the field, because that's what really decides whether a trade is worth it.

Right now, when defenses line up against this offense, they see a fast slot-style threat in Tucker, a physical slot receiver in Bech, a tight end in Michael Mayer who blocks like a sixth lineman, and a running back who catches passes. That's a good group, but it's a group of specialists. There's no true outside receiver who forces a cornerback to play honest for sixty minutes.

Jeudy's whole game is built on that job. He's a route runner who separates on the perimeter, and at his best he's the kind of player who wins the individual matchup that lets everything else function. Put him on one side, Tucker on the other, Bech in the slot, Bowers and Mayer in two-tight-end sets, and now a defensive coordinator can't simply bracket one player and dare the rest to beat him.

And remember the problem we keep coming back to. Jeanty averaged 2.3 yards a carry because the Chargers loaded up to stop the run and this line couldn't move them. Every extra receiver a defense has to respect is one fewer body in that box. A receiver addition is also, indirectly, a run-game fix.

THE COUNTERARGUMENT

Let me give you the other side, because it's legitimate.

Ten drops in 2025 is a real number, and we have our own drop problem right now: three from Jeanty alone on Sunday, including a first-down drop. Adding a receiver who struggled with the same issue doesn't automatically fix anything. Sometimes a player's production falls off for reasons that follow him to the next city.

There's also the development argument. Somebody on this roster has to get those targets. If Jeudy arrives and plays 65 percent of the snaps, that comes out of the growth of Bech, Tucker, and Cody White, three young players who have all earned chances. You can't find out what you have if you keep replacing them.

And there's a third point. The clearest weakness on this offense isn't the receiver room. It's the interior of the offensive line, where Linderbaum, Burford, and Powers-Johnson all had rough games and the run game produced 2.3 yards a carry. If Spytek has one deadline move in him, you could argue it should go up front, not on the perimeter.

WHERE I LAND

So here's my honest verdict.

At a conditional fourth-round pick, I make that call in a heartbeat. That's a lottery ticket price for a player who has produced at a high level in this league, who already knows the head coach's system, and who would give Cousins one more established option while Bowers works his way back. If it doesn't work, you've lost a pick that probably becomes a backup somewhere. If it works, your passing game has another real threat for the stretch run.

If Cleveland insists on a third, I'd hesitate. And if the price climbs from there, I'd walk and spend that capital on the offensive line instead.

But here's what has to happen first, and this is the part I want you to remember: none of this matters if the Raiders don't keep winning. Buyers at the deadline are teams in contention. Beat New Orleans on Sunday and go to 3-0, and a name like Jeudy stops being a scenario on a website and starts being a real conversation inside that building. Lose a couple, and the whole thing evaporates.

That's what makes this season so much fun already. Tucker is emerging. Cousins is efficient. A rookie corner is picking off Pro Bowl quarterbacks. A practice squad receiver is scoring touchdowns. And the front office gets to spend the next few weeks deciding whether this team is worth adding to.

Sunday starts the answer.
