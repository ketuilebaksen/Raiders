**But why did we make that decision in the first place?**

And here's the thing I have to say straight, because you've earned it: **I am NOT going to tell you this is a huge new weapon.** **Cody White was already here.** **We already know how he helps.** **The immediate benefit is keeping a useful contributor available before a very difficult matchup — and that's the honest size of it.**

**What this reversal really leaves is one unanswered question: WHAT CHANGED OVERNIGHT?**

Plus: **the 49ers injury report could give our defense a genuine opening**, there's **a possible return that makes life a whole lot tougher for Kirk Cousins**, there's a **report about Ashton Jeanty that directly contradicts what his head coach said**, and on Sunday **Klint Kubiak coaches against his own brother.**

## FIRST: HERE IS EXACTLY WHAT HAPPENED

Raider Nation, let's get the sequence right, because the sequence IS the story.

**On November 4th, the Raiders RELEASED Cody White and ACTIVATED Carter Renan from injured reserve.**

**The NEXT DAY, they WAIVED Renan and SIGNED WHITE BACK to the active roster.**

**One day. One decision. And then almost the complete opposite.**

**And seeing White back makes me happy, because he gave this team something when we needed it.**

**THREE TOUCHDOWN CATCHES IN SEPTEMBER.**

Raider Nation, **those shouldn't disappear from the conversation just because he isn't one of the biggest names in this offense.** **Those were real contributions during our winning start** — the 3-0 run against Miami, the Chargers and New Orleans. **And his special teams work matters too**, on a unit that just broke a 248-game drought and won us a football game.

## BUT THAT IS EXACTLY WHY I QUESTIONED RELEASING HIM

**If we're moving on from somebody who has HELPED us, I want to understand what we're GAINING.**

**Now — getting another player healthy genuinely creates a decision.** That's real. **A man coming off injured reserve has to go somewhere, and somebody has to come off the 53.** **That doesn't automatically mean the guy making room has stopped being useful.**

**But bringing White straight back the very next day makes the question worth asking.**

**And Raider Nation, I don't know what changed behind closed doors. I genuinely don't.**

**Maybe another possibility disappeared.** A trade that didn't materialize, a claim that got beaten, a physical that didn't pass. **Maybe the team simply reconsidered the value of keeping him** once they watched the practice field without him.

**I can ask those questions without pretending either explanation is confirmed. Nobody reported a reason.**

**What we KNOW is the transaction sequence. And to me, it's completely fair to LIKE THE RESULT while QUESTIONING HOW WE GOT THERE.**

## AND CARTER RENAN IS THE OTHER SIDE OF THIS — AND IT'S ROUGH

Raider Nation, spare a thought for this young man, because his week was brutal.

**He finally came off injured reserve — and then LOST his roster spot the following day.** **After missing the entire preseason. After waiting through the whole injury process.**

**One day on the active roster. That's a rough turnaround.**

**And we never got to see what role he could have earned in this offense this season.**

**And what honestly interested me was his BACKGROUND, because it's genuinely unusual.**

**Carter Renan played OFFENSIVE LINE at Division III Shenandoah — before moving to TIGHT END, and then continuing his college career at Towson.** **And he dropped significant weight along the way.**

Raider Nation, **think about that path.** A Division III offensive lineman who **changed his body and changed his position** to chase a professional career. **That's not a prospect. That's a decision somebody made about his own life.**

**And that experience gives me a real reason to be curious about how his BLOCKING BACKGROUND and his RECEIVING ABILITY could fit together.** A tight end who learned to block as a lineman first? **That's exactly the player who turns a four-yard run into a twelve-yard run.**

**Plus his 9.38 RELATIVE ATHLETIC SCORE supports the idea that there was real physical upside to develop.**

**Nine-point-three-eight. That's near the top of the scale.**

**That doesn't guarantee a productive NFL player, obviously.** Athletic scores don't block anybody. **But I wanted to SEE more.**

**And personally? I would have preferred keeping that development opportunity over Dont'e Thornton Jr.**

**Now let me be careful: that's a ROSTER PREFERENCE. It is not proof the coaches evaluated either player incorrectly.** They watch both men every single day. **I watch Sundays.** **But Thornton has 10 catches for 135 yards in his NFL career, and I'd have been curious about the lineman-turned-tight-end with a 9.38.**

## NOW THE RAIDERS INJURY REPORT — AND ONE UPDATE WORRIES ME

Let's go through it, because this is where Sunday actually gets decided.

**DARIEN PORTER went from NO PRACTICE Wednesday to LIMITED work Thursday. That's encouraging.**

Raider Nation, **we need options in that secondary badly** — Porter played exactly ONE SNAP against the Jets before the groin injury, and **Germad McCoy and Hezekiah Masses have been getting the reps since.** **Getting him back onto a field is progress.**

**But limited practice does not tell us his final Sunday status.** **I'm happy about the DIRECTION without treating it as a guarantee.**

**DONT'E THORNTON JR. was LIMITED with a BACK issue.** So his participation deserves context too. **A man who missed the first seven games with a hamstring, finally got activated, and is now managing a back — that's a tough stretch for a young receiver.**

**And here's the one that genuinely concerns me: THOMAS BOOKER IV went from LIMITED Wednesday to MISSING THURSDAY with an ANKLE injury.**

Raider Nation, **that is INSIDE. That is the interior defensive line. Against San Francisco.**

**We cannot afford to overlook what happens in the trenches.** We are **30th in the league against the run at 146 yards a game.** Tonka Hemingway is managing a stinger after playing 13 snaps. **And now Booker's ankle.**

**That rotation is Adam Butler, Folorunso Fatukasi, Hemingway, Booker, JJ Pegues — and it's getting thinner by the day.**

## WHICH IS WHY I WANT TIM KEENAN III BACK UP

**If we elevate Tim Keenan III again, I'd like to see him get real work against that running game.**

Raider Nation, the man **got three pressures and his first NFL sack on 19 snaps against the Jets**, and **earned a 90.1 PFF run defense grade at Alabama in 2024.** **He's the one young interior player on this roster whose college strength is the exact thing this defense is worst at.**

**But he REVERTED to the practice squad after the Jets game, so another appearance requires a roster step first.** That's the precise situation. **He isn't on the 53.**

**With Booker dealing with that ankle, it makes complete sense to think through our options instead of assuming everybody will be available.**

## AND NOW THE 49ERS INJURY REPORT — AND THIS IS A REAL OPENING

Raider Nation, this is substantial, so let's go through it carefully.

**Mike Evans is UNLIKELY to play, with John Lynch discussing an ACHILLES TENDINITIS problem.**

**Deebo Samuel Sr. also ISN'T expected back.**

**And Christian Kirk MISSED Thursday's practice with a KNEE issue.** That adds another concern to their receiving group.

**Demarcus Robinson returning to practice gives them a possible boost — but he still needs ACTIVATION from injured reserve before he can play.** And Raider Nation, **getting back onto the practice field and being ready for Sunday are NOT the same thing.** We just watched that exact distinction with Jack Bech.

**Now — they still have receivers such as Brandin Cooks and Jacob Cowing.**

**And I am NOT looking at those names and assuming our secondary gets an easy afternoon.** Brandin Cooks has been torching NFL defenses for a decade. **What I'm saying is that San Francisco could have FEWER ESTABLISHED OPTIONS available — and our defense needs to make that limitation SHOW UP during the game.**

## AND LET ME BE CLEAR ABOUT HOW I TALK ABOUT THIS

Raider Nation, **I don't celebrate injuries. I won't do it.**

**But I also don't expect anybody to apologize when OUR team is short-handed.** Nobody sent us a card when Hemingway was in concussion protocol or when Thornton missed seven games.

**So if this matchup gives us an opening — TAKE ADVANTAGE OF IT.**

**And that means something specific: TIGHT COVERAGE. GOOD TACKLING. And avoiding the mistakes that allow REPLACEMENT PLAYERS to settle in and start believing they can control the afternoon.**

Raider Nation, **that last part is the whole thing.** A backup receiver who catches two easy ones early starts playing like a star. **A missed tackle in space turns a role player into a highlight.** **You beat a short-handed team by refusing to let anybody get comfortable.**

**And remember what happened TWO WEEKS AGO: the Rams came into Allegiant without Davante Adams, without Myles Garrett, without Poona Ford — and ran for 199 yards and beat us by 14.** **We have done the "they're banged up" thing before and it blew up in our faces.**

## AND THE BRANDON AIYUK SITUATION IS INTERESTING — BUT IT ISN'T FOOTBALL

Here's the wildcard, and I'm going to handle it responsibly.

**Brandon Aiyuk submitted paperwork seeking REINSTATEMENT. And his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said he wanted to return. A meeting was planned for FRIDAY.**

**Now — that does NOT mean the relationship is repaired. And it certainly does not mean we should count him into Sunday's lineup.**

Raider Nation, **the awkwardness around that situation is genuinely interesting to me as a football story.** **But I'm not building an upset prediction around it.**

**And here's my favorite line about the whole thing: a difficult conversation at their facility doesn't tackle anybody for us.**

**That's exactly right.** Drama in somebody else's building does not win us a football game. **The useful football question is: WHICH receivers actually dress, WHAT can they do, and whether OUR defense forces them into uncomfortable situations.**

**Everything else is a podcast topic.**

## BUT CHRISTIAN McCAFFREY CHANGES EVERYTHING

And here's why I'm not getting carried away with any of it.

**Christian McCaffrey went from MISSING Wednesday's practice to LIMITED work Thursday with a HAMSTRING issue.**

**We need to prepare for him without pretending his availability is settled.**

**Because Raider Nation — if he's out there, losing receivers does NOT stop San Francisco from attacking us UNDERNEATH or ON THE GROUND.**

**And that is the exact nightmare for this defense.** We allow **146 rushing yards a game, 30th in the league.** We gave up **140 in a half to the Jets** and **199 to the Rams.** **A healthy Christian McCaffrey against a 30th-ranked run defense, with a thin interior and Booker's ankle in question?**

**That's not an opening. That's an ambush.**

**Receivers being out matters a lot less when the other team can simply hand it off and throw screens to the best dual-threat back of his generation.**

## AND NICK BOSA IS PUSHING TO RETURN

Raider Nation, the injury news does NOT all favor us.

**Nick Bosa is pushing to return — and we have to prepare for that possibility.**

**His last appearance was WEEK TWO against Miami, and he recorded NO SACKS in those early appearances.**

**And that number does not make me comfortable.**

Raider Nation, **let me explain why zero sacks in two games is not good news.** **A pass rusher coming back from a long absence with no production on his ledger is a man with something to prove, playing at home, in a rivalry game, against an offensive line that has started THREE different men at right guard this season.**

**His potential return completely changes the protection challenge.**

**And here's the sentence I want every single person in Raider Nation to remember: I like having Kirk Cousins leading this offense — BUT CONFIDENCE IN THE QUARTERBACK ISN'T A PROTECTION PLAN.**

**That's it. That's the whole thing.** Nineteen touchdown passes don't block anybody. **A 38-year-old quarterback with limited mobility against a returning Nick Bosa is a schematic problem, not a faith problem.**

**If Bosa plays, we need to handle that pressure WITHOUT letting it dictate every possession.** **Give Cousins a chance to work. Keep the offense ORGANIZED. And don't make every important down feel like a RESCUE MISSION.**

**And remember how they protected him against the Jets: screens that asked nothing of his arm, three-tight-end sets that sealed the edge so he could get out safely, and Ashton Jeanty being "phenomenal in protection."** **All of that gets more important, not less, if Bosa is out there.**

## AND AS FOR FERNANDO MENDOZA

**I'm comfortable letting him wait while Cousins gives us a chance to compete.**

Raider Nation, **a returning Nick Bosa in a road rivalry game is the single worst environment imaginable for a rookie's first NFL start.** **Rapoport reported the Raiders don't want him playing anytime soon, and Wednesday's first-team warm-up reps were about READINESS, not a change.**

**"Three more years of reps or one more day of reps — he has to be ready."** That's the plan. **Not this week.**

## NOW THE ROB LEONARD CHALLENGE — AND THIS IS WHERE OPPORTUNITY BECOMES RESPONSIBILITY

Raider Nation, this is the part I feel most strongly about.

**We beat the Jets and I'm happy about that. But winning did NOT make the defensive problems disappear.**

**If San Francisco comes in missing key receiving options, I need to see us make their offense WORK HARDER.**

**And honestly? I think Rob Leonard needs a better performance here.**

Let me be specific about what I mean, because "needs to be better" is lazy criticism. **Here's the actual complaint:**

**We cannot keep letting opponents move the ball and then DEPEND ON A LATE ANSWER from our offense.**

**Look at the pattern.** Against the Jets: **140 rushing yards allowed in the first half**, New York scoring on its **first six possessions**, down nine in the third quarter — **and then we needed a comeback, a 95-yard kickoff return, and a toe-tap interception in the back of the end zone to survive a 2-5 team.**

**That is not a formula. That's a rescue.**

**I want better work BEFORE drives reach scoring territory.** **Give our offense some possessions where it isn't immediately trying to REPAIR DAMAGE from the previous series.**

Raider Nation, **that's the cleanest way anybody has described what this defense actually owes this team.** Not shutouts. **Just stop handing the offense a deficit to climb out of every single week.**

**And Leonard himself has the right diagnosis — he's been drilling space tackling with rugby bags since Week 7, and his own question was "can we rush with four and get home?"** **Against a receiving room this thin, four should be enough. If it isn't, send five.**

## AND NOW A REPORT THAT CONTRADICTS WHAT KUBIAK SAID — AND I HAVE TO FLAG IT

Raider Nation, this is important and I'm going to be very careful here.

**ESPN's Dan Graziano reported that the Raiders HOPED the reduced workload would serve as a WAKE-UP CALL for Ashton Jeanty.**

**That's the reporting. I'm passing it along exactly as it was reported.**

**But Raider Nation, you need to hear it against what the head coach said publicly — twice.**

**Kubiak was asked directly whether Jeanty's lack of carries had anything to do with his confidence in him. His answer: "NO, IT DID NOT. IT DID NOT."** Said it twice. **And he separately said Jeanty's pregame comments about the Jets' tackling were NOT a factor.** And: **"That's my call, and Ashton's still going to be a HUGE part of our team."** And on Wednesday, asked if Jeanty can still be a 25-carry bellcow: **"OH, ABSOLUTELY."**

**So we have a national report saying "wake-up call," and a head coach saying it wasn't about confidence or punishment.**

**I'm not going to tell you which one is true. I wasn't in the building.** **A "wake-up call" and "not a loss of confidence" can technically both be true** — you can want a young player to feel urgency without having given up on him. **But they are clearly in tension, and you deserve to know both exist.**

**What I'll say is this: rewarding Mike Washington makes complete sense IF he's earning the work. But we still need to USE what Jeanty offers — instead of treating this as an argument with only one winner.**

## AND HERE'S THE PLAN I WANT IN THE BACKFIELD

**Washington finished with 83 rushing yards, about 3.3 per carry. Jeanty contributed 64 RECEIVING yards.**

**So I can absolutely see an approach where Washington gets more carries and Jeanty stays involved THROUGH THE AIR.**

**And that's a possible DIRECTION — not a permanent arrangement. The team has announced their roles can still move around**, and Kubiak confirmed Wednesday that the split is **week to week: "I do. I do. Yes."**

**And Raider Nation, keeping both involved also gives this whole conversation some perspective: FEWER CARRIES DON'T HAVE TO MEAN A PLAYER HAS NOTHING LEFT TO OFFER.**

**Receiving work and protection STILL MATTER — especially if the pass rush becomes a problem.** **And with Nick Bosa possibly returning, protection just became the most valuable thing a running back can do.**

**Remember: Jeanty leads ALL running backs in the NFL in receptions and receiving yards** — 37 catches, 376 yards, three touchdowns. **And Kubiak called him "phenomenal in protection, like he's always been."**

**I want our best options HELPING EACH OTHER — because winning this matchup matters far more than declaring somebody the permanent starter right now.**

**And Kubiak has made that flexibility clear. He praised Washington's development while emphasizing that this offense still needs its BACKS — plural — against San Francisco.**

**I want decisions that help us SUSTAIN DRIVES. If one approach stalls, ADJUST. We shouldn't become so committed to proving a point that we ignore what the game actually needs.**
