## WEAPON ONE: TIM KEENAN III — AND WE'VE NEEDED HIM SINCE CHRISTIAN WILKINS LEFT

Let's start with the defensive front, because this is the oldest wound on this roster.

**We have needed more help inside since CHRISTIAN WILKINS LEFT.**

Raider Nation, that's the honest origin of this whole problem. **One hundred forty-six rushing yards allowed per game, one of the worst in football. One hundred forty in a half against the Jets. Thirtieth in the league against the run.** It all traces back to a hole in the middle that never got filled.

**And Maxx Crosby cannot be the answer to every problem.** Sixteen chips and double teams in one game against Buffalo. **Somebody else has to take advantage of their opportunities.**

**And against the Jets, Tim Keenan III gave me a reason to watch him much more closely.**

**He stayed PATIENT, got to Geno Smith, and FINISHED the sack.**

**For a man trying to establish himself in this league, that's a pretty good way to get noticed. And honestly, his path makes that moment far more satisfying.**

## BECAUSE RAIDER NATION, LOOK AT WHAT THIS MAN HAS BEEN THROUGH

**The Rams drafted him in the SEVENTH ROUND. Then waived him. Then brought him back to their practice squad. And then released him AGAIN.**

**The Raiders signed him to their practice squad afterward.**

**Drafted, waived, re-signed, released, and claimed by somebody else — before he ever got a chance to establish himself.**

**That is a LOT of uncertainty for a young man to carry. But he stayed READY. And when the opportunity finally arrived, he gave this coaching staff something useful.**

**And I'll say this plainly: I enjoyed the celebration.** When a man has bounced around that much trying to find a place, **I don't mind one bit seeing him enjoy a moment he earned.**

## AND THERE WAS ALREADY A REASON TO BELIEVE HE COULD HELP AGAINST THE RUN

Here's the background that makes this more than a one-game story.

**At Alabama in 2024, Tim Keenan III earned a 90.1 PFF RUN DEFENSE GRADE.**

**Ninety point one. Against the run.**

Raider Nation, **that doesn't guarantee a thing on Sundays, obviously.** College grades are not NFL production. **But it gives his emergence real BACKGROUND.**

**We are not talking about somebody who had never shown a useful skill before. We're talking about a young defensive tackle trying to turn a genuine college strength into a place in this rotation** — and that strength happens to be **precisely the thing this defense is worst at.**

## AND NOW LOOK AT THE ACTUAL WORKLOAD, BECAUSE IT'S GROWING

**He played 19 DEFENSIVE SNAPS against the Jets — after playing 19 against the Rams.**

**And against New York he added SEVEN SPECIAL TEAMS SNAPS and finished with THREE PRESSURES and his FIRST NFL SACK.**

Raider Nation, **nineteen snaps twice in a row is not a cameo. That's a rotation.** And **three pressures on nineteen snaps is a genuinely good rate** — that's a man affecting the quarterback roughly once every six plays he's on the field.

**And the seven special teams snaps matter too.** Remember Kubiak's philosophy on Dylan Laube: **"If you can play four core special teams AND be a producer on your side of the ball — then you've got VALUE in this league."** **Keenan is doing both.**

**And Kubiak has praised his ATTITUDE, his EFFORT, and his PRACTICE WORK.** His words: **"He's got WINNING ATTRIBUTES. He's a good teammate. He's got good energy. He's playing hard. He's EARNING his opportunities."**

Raider Nation, **I care about that a great deal, because coaches see everything leading into those game snaps.** We only see Sunday. **They see Wednesday.** **If the preparation matches what we're starting to see on game day, there's a much stronger reason to expand his role.**

**What I'm watching next: can he keep affecting plays WHILE handling more of the work that comes with being part of an NFL defensive front?** Nineteen snaps is one thing. **Thirty-five is a different job.**

## AND THE INTERIOR COMPETITION IS THE REAL STORY BEFORE THE DEADLINE

Here's the full picture of who's fighting for those snaps.

**And Tonka Hemingway is already in it — he PLAYED against the Jets, so this isn't about waiting for him to come back.** He went 13 snaps with a stinger, and Kubiak said they're continuing to evaluate him. **The question is what he and Keenan could give us TOGETHER with more opportunities.**

## WEAPON TWO: MIKE WASHINGTON JR. — AND LET'S GIVE IT THE RIGHT CONTEXT

Now to the offense, where finding a contributor creates a completely different kind of question.

**We need somebody who can take carries and keep us from putting everything on the passing game.** **But when that player starts getting MOST of the rushing work, it affects somebody we invested very heavily in.**

**That's why Mike Washington Jr. is more than a nice surprise right now. His role against the Jets changed how I look at this entire backfield heading into San Francisco.**

**So let's be honest about the production first: 25 CARRIES for 83 YARDS, with a LONGEST RUN OF 15 YARDS. That's about 3.3 yards per carry.**

**I am not calling that a dominant rushing afternoon. It wasn't.** **A longest run of 15 yards on 25 attempts means there was no explosive play in there at all.**

**What stood out was the RESPONSIBILITY. The staff kept GOING BACK TO HIM.**

Raider Nation, **that's the real story.** Twenty-five carries isn't a stat line — **it's a statement of trust in the middle of a game they were losing at halftime.** **I liked seeing him find openings and FINISH FORWARD.** **But I also want those opportunities to turn into more CONSISTENT gains as this offense develops.**

## AND THERE IS A REASON TO BE INTERESTED IN HIS UPSIDE BEYOND ONE GAME

Here's the draft context, and it's genuinely interesting.

**Brian Baldinger suggested after the draft that Mike Washington Jr. might be the SECOND-BEST RUNNING BACK IN THE CLASS.**

**Now — that's HIS evaluation. It is not a ranking everybody has to accept, and I'm presenting it as one respected analyst's opinion.**

**But look at the context: JEREMIAH LOVE went THIRD OVERALL to Arizona. And Washington arrived in the FOURTH ROUND.**

**Third overall versus the fourth round.** Raider Nation, **you can absolutely understand the excitement about potential VALUE there.** If Baldinger was anywhere close to right, **the Raiders got a top-of-the-draft talent on a fourth-round contract.**

**I like the possibility. But his NEXT performances will matter a lot more to me than draft praise.** **Twenty-five carries for 3.3 against a two-win team is an audition, not a verdict.**

**And yes — I understand why fantasy managers are suddenly paying attention. A back who gets that workload out of nowhere is going to attract interest.**

**But for me the bigger point is what KUBIAK ACTUALLY DID. He TRUSTED Washington with the football instead of merely TALKING about giving him opportunities.**

**That's a coaching decision we can evaluate directly. Words are free. Twenty-five carries cost something.**

## NOW THE SNAP COUNTS — AND THEY TELL THE REAL STORY

Raider Nation, here are the numbers that make this official, and they're more balanced than the carry split suggests.

**Mike Washington Jr. played 38 OFFENSIVE SNAPS — roughly 60%.**
**Ashton Jeanty played 25 — roughly 40%.**
**And Dylan Laube had ONE offensive snap.**

**Sixty-forty.**

**Raider Nation, that is NOT a benching.** Everybody looked at "two carries" and concluded Jeanty had been removed from the offense. **He played FORTY PERCENT OF THE SNAPS.** He was on that field for two out of every five plays.

**Kubiak described the workload as something that can CHANGE based on who is playing best** — and he confirmed Wednesday that the split will be **week to week: "I do. I do. Yes."**

**I can live with that — PROVIDED each player's role gives him a REAL CHANCE to contribute.**

## AND HERE IS THE STATISTIC THAT ENDS THE JEANTY DEBATE

Raider Nation, I need you to sit down for this, because I did not have this number until today and it is remarkable.

**Through eight games, Ashton Jeanty has 37 CATCHES for 376 YARDS and THREE RECEIVING TOUCHDOWNS.**

**And here's what that means: HE LEADS ALL RUNNING BACKS IN THE NFL IN RECEPTIONS AND RECEIVING YARDS — AND HE IS TIED FOR FIRST IN RECEIVING TOUCHDOWNS.**

**The LEADING receiving back in the National Football League.**

Raider Nation. **Rex Ryan went on national television and called this man "soft as char" and approximately the worst running back draft pick of the last twenty years.**

**And the man leads every running back in football in catches, in receiving yards, and is tied for the lead in receiving touchdowns.**

**That is VALUABLE PRODUCTION. And I do not want frustration with his rushing numbers to make anybody overlook it.**

**And remember what the coaches have actually said about him.** Kubiak: **"Ashton made huge plays for us in the pass game, IN PROTECTION. PHENOMENAL in protection — like he's always been."** And on Wednesday, asked whether he still believes Jeanty can be a 25-carry bellcow: **"OH, ABSOLUTELY."**

**And the 45-yard screen that set up the go-ahead touchdown drive against the Jets.** **And the eight-yard carry on the very next snap.**

**If we can get that contribution while Washington handles more of the carries — Raider Nation, there's something genuinely worth developing here.**

## NOW LET ME DEAL WITH THE DRAFT ARGUMENT, CAREFULLY

Raider Nation, I know why this still bothers people, and I'm not going to pretend it doesn't exist.

**Jeanty was the SIXTH OVERALL PICK, and expectations come with that investment. The debate about taking a running back that high did not disappear just because another back started producing.**

**And there's reporting on how that pick happened — but it's genuinely contested, so I'm going to lay it out properly and let you decide.**

**Tony Pauline reported that Jeanty was a PETE CARROLL pick, and that there was PUSHBACK from people inside the building who preferred a LINEMAN.**

**But former Raiders staffer RYAN PAGANETTI subsequently CHALLENGED the idea that Carroll was the person driving that selection.**

**So I would NOT assign responsibility as though that argument is settled. It isn't.**

**Pauline also reported that John Spytek had LIMITED SAY in the hiring of Carroll — but Raider Nation, that does not establish who controlled every draft decision.** Those are two different things. **A general manager who didn't pick the coach can still run a draft room.**

**And I understand why Carroll's history with Marshawn Lynch makes people connect those dots.** I also understand questioning the investment **when you think about Spytek, about Tom Brady, and about teams that have succeeded using MULTIPLE BACKS instead of one expensive one.**

**But those comparisons are REASONS TO ASK QUESTIONS. They are not PROOF of what happened in that room.**

**Personally? I can see the argument for Tetairoa McMillan, or for one of the top offensive linemen in that class, given the needs we had.** A receiver who separates or a guard who moves bodies would both look awfully useful right now.

**But we are working with THIS roster, and I want us getting something from BOTH backs.**

## SO HERE IS HOW I'D ACTUALLY RUN THIS BACKFIELD

**Give Washington opportunities to build RHYTHM on early downs.** Volume is his whole value right now — the staff going back to him 25 times is what kept this offense balanced.

**Keep Jeanty involved as a RECEIVER AND A RUNNER — instead of making his presence PREDICTABLE.**

Raider Nation, **that second half of the sentence is everything.** We talked about this trap yesterday: **if Washington entering means run and Jeanty entering means pass, an opposing coordinator doesn't need to scout you — he just watches who jogs onto the field.** **Play action off the Washington look. A draw off the Jeanty look.** **Make them pay for guessing.**

**And sharing the work could help PRESERVE him, too.**

That's not a small thing. **Jeanty has dealt with an ankle issue since before the season even started**, and there were concerns he hadn't fully recovered by the time we played New England. **A 40% snap share with 37 catches is how you keep a 22-year-old healthy for December.**

**And that's the part of the Christian McCaffrey comparison I find useful** — **the VERSATILITY, without pretending they're identical players.** **A back who leads the league's running backs in receiving is being used the way the best modern backs get used.** Not as a battering ram. **As a mismatch.**

## AND NOW THINK ABOUT WHAT THIS DOES FOR BROCK BOWERS

Raider Nation, this is the strategic payoff, and it's beautiful.

**Brock Bowers already demands attention from every defense he faces.** Twelve catches and 130 yards between him and Michael Mayer against the Jets. A red zone corner route nobody could stop. **Every defensive coordinator in America builds his week around that man.**

**Now: if BOTH backs give the 49ers something else to account for, Kirk Cousins has more ways to attack.**

**That's exactly how I want this offense to function.** **A tight end they have to bracket. A back who leads all running backs in receiving. Another back taking 25 carries. And 19 touchdown passes from a quarterback who's leading the league.**

**You can't double everybody.**

## WEAPON THREE: AND THIS ONE IS MINE — TREY ZUHN III

Raider Nation, here's the honesty: **the breakdown I was working from set up three weapons, covered the first two in detail, and ran out of road before it got to the third.** The third was described as a man who **"just stepped into a spot we've struggled to get right."**

**And there's only one man that can be. So this pick is mine, and here's the case.**

**TREY ZUHN III made his first career start at RIGHT GUARD against the Jets. And the Raiders won.**

Raider Nation, **think about how long we chased this spot.** Caleb Rogers started there and whiffed on a goal-line block that stopped Jeanty for a loss. Jackson Powers-Johnson started there and then took zero offensive snaps against Buffalo. Kubiak promised to **"keep SHAKING IT UP until we find the right mix."** He said **"yes"** when asked if Zuhn would get reps. He called him a **"Swiss Army knife"** who could play tackle, guard or center — **"he might be able to play quarterback, I don't know, I haven't let him try yet."**

**Then we learned the delay was an INJURY a few weeks ago that he battled back from. Then he took first-team reps. Then "could be." Then he started.**

**And Kubiak's review: "Trey was CALM in there. I thought he was WELL PREPARED. That was NOT A SURPRISE — but I thought he played well."**

**And on whether he's earned more: "I BELIEVE SO. YES."**

**And this offense went from ONE touchdown in the first half to THREE in the second, with 25 carries for a back running behind that line.**

**That's a weapon, Raider Nation — because a right guard you don't have to think about is the cheapest upgrade in football.**
