**Four and four doesn't guarantee anything. But it gives us a reason to keep pushing.**

**We finally ended that four-game losing streak — and we are NINTH in the AFC playoff picture.**

**Ninth.** Raider Nation, with **Buffalo, New England, Kansas City, Denver and Jacksonville** all involved, and **teams in the North still fighting**, that is a live race. **Not a good position. A LIVE one.**

**I look at that picture and I see an opportunity — even if we haven't proven we belong with the strongest teams yet.**

## SO LET ME BE HONEST ABOUT THIS TEAM

Raider Nation, here's the sober version, and I owe it to you.

**We PUSHED the Chiefs and the Bills — both three-point losses.** That's real. **But the Rams and the Patriots beat us CONVINCINGLY.** Forty-five to seventeen. Thirty-five to twenty-one. **Those weren't bad luck. Those were beatings.**

**And against the Jets — a two-win football team — we were down NINE in the third quarter and didn't take the lead until the FOURTH.**

**But I am not treating one comeback as proof that an expensive trade makes us a championship threat.**

## NOW THE BIG ONE: MY ASKING PRICE ON KIRK COUSINS IS A FIRST-ROUND PICK

Raider Nation, this is the number, and I want to explain it properly.

**A FIRST. Not a second.**

**And I know how that sounds.** A second-round pick sounds perfectly fair for a 38-year-old quarterback — **until you actually consider what we'd lose.**

**He is giving this offense TOUCHDOWN PRODUCTION and COMPOSURE.** **Nineteen touchdown passes — the most in the National Football League.** Four total scores against the Jets. **The greatest comeback in NFL history on his résumé — the one he produced against the Colts while he was in Minnesota.**

**If another team wants to take all of that away from us WHILE WE'RE STILL COMPETING, I want a return that makes the decision worth even considering.**

**Otherwise? I'm completely comfortable keeping him.**

**And his familiarity with Klint Kubiak matters enormously.** There is a history between those two from their Minnesota years, 2019 through 2021. **I like having a quarterback who understands what this offense needs** — and you can see it in the design: the screens that ask nothing of his arm, the three-tight-end sets that seal the edge so he can get out safely, the red zone corner routes to Bowers.

**That offense is built around what this specific man can do. You don't hand that away for a second-rounder.**

## AND HERE IS WHO I'D LISTEN TO — AND WHAT I'D SAY

**If MINNESOTA believes quarterback is the thing separating it from a deeper playoff run — then I want its offer to REFLECT THAT BELIEF.**

**If BALTIMORE calls, I'd listen too.**

**But I am NOT lowering my price because somebody else needs help.** Raider Nation, that's the whole discipline of a deadline. **Desperation is the other team's problem, not ours.**

**And Baltimore brings something back to the surface, doesn't it.**

**Remember the failed Maxx Crosby deal — the one where the Raiders AGREED to send him to the Ravens in March for TWO FIRST-ROUND PICKS, before it collapsed on a meniscus physical.**

**Now, I'm not going to pretend the Ravens owe us compensation from a trade that never went through. That's not how this works.**

**But I certainly wouldn't feel OBLIGATED to do them a favor either.**

**That's the right posture. No grudge. No discount.**

## AND THE LOCKER ROOM ARGUMENT IS REAL

Raider Nation, this is the part the spreadsheet never captures.

**Those players are fighting to win NOW.**

Maxx Crosby is the first man in the building and one of the last to leave, pushing himself on special teams at 4-4. Jeremy Chinn toe-tapped a game away in the back of an end zone. **If we remove the quarterback helping them do all of that — the return had better justify the decision.**

## BUT KEEPING HIM RAISES THE MENDOZA QUESTION — AND THERE'S A PERFECT PRECEDENT

Here's the fair pushback: what if Cousins keeps playing well NEXT season too? Doesn't that block the first overall pick?

**And I'd be comfortable evaluating that when we get there — because there's a precedent that proves these two things can coexist.**

**Buffalo moved from Tyrod Taylor to Josh Allen AFTER reaching the playoffs.**

Raider Nation, **read that again, because it kills the entire false choice.** The Bills made the postseason with a veteran, and then handed the job to their young quarterback. **Competing and planning a quarterback transition are not opposites. They happened in the same building, in consecutive seasons, and it worked out pretty well for Buffalo.**

**I want Fernando Mendoza playing when he's READY — not because we're impatient to see him.**

**And that's exactly what Rapoport reported: the Raiders don't want Mendoza playing anytime soon, because they believe Cousins has performed well and the ORIGINAL PLAN — the rookie learning behind him — IS WORKING.**

**Learn in the meeting room, so you don't learn the hard way on a Sunday. Kubiak said it himself about Mike Washington.**

## AND HERE IS THE UNCOMFORTABLE HYPOTHETICAL NOBODY WANTS TO SAY OUT LOUD

**I'm not predicting that. But you can absolutely understand why it would be uncomfortable — and why, if Minnesota starts looking for an answer, I'd evaluate the opportunity.**

**I just wouldn't let another team's urgency convince me to give away something valuable.**

## NOW — KEEPING THE CORE DOESN'T MEAN CLOSING THE DOOR

Raider Nation, this is important: **"keep the core" is not the same as "do nothing."**

**Wide receiver, defensive tackle and guard all deserve attention.**

**If Brian Thomas Jr. became available for a SECOND-ROUND PICK, I'd consider it.** A 24-year-old who made the Pro Bowl as a rookie, buried on his own depth chart in Jacksonville with nine catches. **A second, not a first. That's the number.**

**And if BALTIMORE or PHILADELPHIA offered a useful offensive or defensive lineman, I'd absolutely take a look.** Philadelphia is the building where **Moro Ojomo** sits — 25 years old, headed for free agency, exactly the young interior piece this defense needs.

**But here's my requirement: I need somebody who improves a SPECIFIC WEAKNESS, at a price that leaves us room to KEEP BUILDING.**

**Not a name. A fix.**

## AND NOW THE EXACT AMMUNITION — THIS IS MORE PRECISE THAN I'VE HAD IT BEFORE

Raider Nation, I've been telling you this front office has "picks in every round but the seventh." **Here's the precise inventory, and it's better than I described.**

**The Raiders have CAP FLEXIBILITY and SEVEN PICKS next year: one in each of the first FIVE rounds, TWO SIXTHS, and no seventh.**

**Seven picks. Five of them in the top five rounds. Plus cap room.**

**That gives us options** — and it's why Spytek has never had to panic while columnists screamed at him. **Compare it to Indianapolis, which doesn't even have a first-round pick in 2027.**

**But I'm going to say the unpopular part too: DRAFT PICKS DON'T AUTOMATICALLY BECOME GOOD PLAYERS.**

Raider Nation, **we lived that.** We counted extra picks for years in this building and it didn't produce a winner. **A pick is a lottery ticket with a salary attached.** The point of having seven isn't to hoard them — **it's to be able to spend one or two without flinching.**

## AND HERE IS WHERE I'D PUSH BACK ON MY OWN FAN BASE

**I would NOT chase a receiver just because that's the exciting move.**

**We are already finding ways to score.** **Twenty-seven points a game, sixth in the league through Week 6.** Twelve catches and 130 yards from two tight ends against the Jets. A 45-yard screen to a running back. Three second-half touchdowns.

**Better protection, or a STRONGER RUN DEFENSE, might make a bigger difference to the actual results.**

Raider Nation, **I've spent six weeks on this channel arguing for a receiver.** And I still think a separator helps. **But look at the scoreboard math honestly: a top-six scoring offense and a defense allowing 146 rushing yards a game, one of the worst in football.**

**One of those is a wish. The other is an emergency.**

## AND HERE IS WHAT I WOULD NOT SELL

Let me go name by name on the veterans, because this is where front offices make expensive mistakes.

**KOLTON MILLER: moving him means REPLACING A STARTING TACKLE. So I'd need a convincing return.** Not a flyer, not a "we'll figure it out." **You do not hand a 38-year-old quarterback a brand-new blind side in November.**

**JACKSON POWERS-JOHNSON and ISAIAH POLA-MAO: I'd weigh their current roles against any offer.**

**And here's the principle that applies to all three: a late pick can help — but it does NOT automatically outweigh useful depth.**

Raider Nation, **we just learned this lesson the hard way.** We waived **Jonah Laulu** and **six teams put in claims, including the Jets**, and Tennessee got him for free. **You cannot get paid for depth you've already given away.**

**And on Pola-Mao specifically — Kubiak called him "a core player on teams" and said "I'm just really glad he's a Raider," and then special teams won us a football game with a 95-yard return.** **You don't sell from the one unit that's winning.**

**I want each decision judged on what we GAIN and what we LEAVE BEHIND. And my preference is to let the MARKET give us a reason to act.**

**That's the whole plan in one sentence. Don't initiate. Answer the phone.**

Raider Nation, this is the part that genuinely changes the deadline math, and almost nobody connects it.

**A young player earning a bigger role could change how urgently we need to spend a pick at his position.**

**And honestly? I appreciate a staff willing to FOLLOW THROUGH on competition.**

Raider Nation, **that sentence is the whole Kubiak-Spytek era.** *"You can't sit in those chairs if you're not an accountable human."* *"He has to steal another guy's job — you've got to leapfrog somebody."* *"You're one of eleven."*

**Draft position buys you a first look. Nothing else.**

## AND THE SAME IDEA EXPLAINS MIKE WASHINGTON'S WORKLOAD

**His PREPARATION earned the opportunity. And I thought his VISION gave the running game a lift.**

**That's the message I want every young player on this roster hearing: prepare properly, handle your assignments, and you can earn meaningful work.**

**Now — 83 yards on 25 carries is 3.3 per attempt. But that number needs CONTEXT.**

**Some of those attempts came NEAR THE GOAL LINE, where I thought the BLOCKING let him down.**

Raider Nation, **this is the honest footnote I owe you after yesterday.** I told you 3.3 versus Jeanty's 3.1 was "a rounding error" and that changing backs solved nothing. **That's still true — but it's incomplete.** A back's average gets destroyed by goal-line carries into a wall. **Those aren't running plays; they're collisions.**

**Changing running backs won't automatically fix those situations.**

**The line and the tight ends helped us establish balance elsewhere — and that was real.** **But I still need the SHORT-YARDAGE EXECUTION to improve when the field gets crowded.** And remember that failed fourth down with a backup behind center that the Jets stopped cold. **That's the same disease.**

## AND NONE OF THAT ERASES ASHTON JEANTY

## BUT HERE IS THE TRAP I'M WATCHING FOR — AND IT'S THE SMARTEST POINT IN THIS WHOLE VIDEO

Raider Nation, stay with me, because this is where a three-back rotation either becomes a weapon or becomes a liability.

**If defenders start treating WASHINGTON'S PRESENCE as a RUN SIGNAL — I'd love to see us challenge that assumption with PLAY ACTION.**

**And if they expect a PASS when JEANTY enters — then a DRAW could become very useful.**

**That's the chess match.** Because here's the danger: **every substitution is information you give the defense for free.** If Washington means run and Jeanty means pass, **then an opposing defensive coordinator doesn't need to scout you — he just needs to watch who's jogging onto the field.**

**I want us using the WHOLE BACKFIELD INTELLIGENTLY instead of making every substitution an EASY CLUE.**

Raider Nation, this is exactly the "cat-and-mouse" problem Kubiak described about the pass rush weeks ago: **"the more reps you get, the more time they have to see your scheme."** **Tendencies are the price of success.** And against San Francisco, Seattle and Denver, **those tendencies get exploited.**

**Play action off the Washington look. A draw off the Jeanty look. That's how you make a strength out of a tell.**

## AND THE BACKS NEED HELP FROM THE RECEIVERS TOO

**Jack Bech's return gives us another player willing to do the BLOCKING WORK that supports this offense.** And Kubiak said it showed up specifically **in the run game.**

**I value that toughness enormously. Because a receiver can help a drive WITHOUT CATCHING THE BALL** — and those contributions deserve attention when we're judging this group.

**I want the run game supported ACROSS THE FORMATION, especially when defenses know we need a few yards.**

**And Jalen Nailor made his opportunity count with that touchdown against the Jets.** **Kubiak credited his PRACTICE WORK** — *"He's earned it in practice too. I'm not surprised by the game he had."* **I want those chances to keep following preparation.**

**And a functioning run game creates openings for him.** It all connects.

## NOW THE DEFENSE — AND THIS IS WHERE I NEED MORE

Raider Nation, here's my honest complaint, and it survived the win.

**The Jets moved the ball on the ground EARLY — 140 rushing yards in the first half, 121 from one back before halftime — and we cannot keep waiting until later to settle down.**

**That's why DEFENSIVE TACKLE remains at the top of my list of possible upgrades.**

**And there's a bright spot: TIM KEENAN III earning opportunities and recording his FIRST REGULAR-SEASON SACK is genuinely encouraging.** Kubiak said he's got **"winning attributes"** and good practice habits. **I want that development to continue — alongside better execution from the rest of that front.**

## AND THE SECONDARY IS NOW A FULL-ON COMPETITION

Here's the clearest picture we've had of that back end.

**Darien Porter's GROIN INJURY creates another question. His progress needs monitoring.**

**And in the meantime, GERMAD McCOY and HEZEKIAH MASSES are getting opportunities.**

**McCoy entered FIRST after Porter went out — and Kubiak said he wants that EXPOSURE to continue.** His words: *"We wanted to get him some exposure, and that's going to continue."*

**And Masses contributed a TIPPED PASS** — the play Kubiak singled out.

Raider Nation, **I EXPECT mistakes from young corners.** That's the deal. **But those snaps give the coaches a chance to identify who can handle more responsibility** — and that's exactly what the back half of a season is for.

**And ERIC STOKES had difficult moments — but he also made important plays NEAR THE END ZONE**, including the third-down stops that held the Jets to a field goal after they reached first-and-goal at our 5.

**I can criticize the coverage without ignoring his response.** And that's Kubiak's own standard: *"When a defensive back gets beat, everybody knows about it. You've got to have thick skin and move on to the next down."*

**And that applies to the whole defense. It kept competing. It produced stops. And it finished with Jeremy Chinn's interception.**

**I respect that finish. I just want us making FEWER MISTAKES EARLIER — so we don't need another desperate stand to protect a lead.**
