## FIRST: HOW SPYTEK OPENED, BECAUSE IT MATTERED

Raider Nation, he didn't open with the compensation. He didn't open with Mendoza. **He opened with a thank you, and he took his time with it.**

**"First of all, Klint and I just want to thank the entire Raiders organization — wants to thank KIRK COUSINS. He's a CONSUMMATE PROFESSIONAL. I have the UTMOST RESPECT for him. I think most importantly AS A HUMAN."**

**"He gave EVERYTHING HE HAD to this organization for the time that he was here. He PLAYED GREAT FOOTBALL. He led not only the QUARTERBACK ROOM but the ENTIRE TEAM TOO."**

**"So thank you to Kirk, to JULIE, to the ENTIRE COUSINS FAMILY for what they gave this organization. We're very GRATEFUL for that."**

**And then — Raider Nation, this is the part that got me — he passed along a message from the owner:**

**"MD sends his MOST HEARTFELT THANK YOU as well. And he wants Kirk to know: ONCE A RAIDER, ALWAYS A RAIDER."**

**ONCE A RAIDER, ALWAYS A RAIDER.**

Raider Nation, **Mark Davis made sure that sentence got said out loud in a press conference.** **That's not a formality. That's the owner of this franchise making certain that a man who gave us eight starts and a locker room culture walks out the door knowing exactly where he stands with us forever.**

**That's who we are. That's the Raider way. We don't throw people out the back door.**

## AND KLINT KUBIAK'S TRIBUTE WAS PERSONAL, BECAUSE IT IS PERSONAL

Raider Nation, remember — these two have been together for **FOUR YEARS.** Kubiak coached him in Minnesota. **This isn't a coach losing a quarterback. This is a coach losing a friend from the building.**

**"He's IGNITED OUR BUILDING since April, since he's been here — as a LEADER, as a GREAT TEAMMATE. Helping us INSTALL THIS CULTURE."**

**"I have a SPECIAL RELATIONSHIP with him, working with him for FOUR YEARS now. We COULDN'T HAVE PICKED A BETTER GUY to kick us off this season."**

**"He EARNED the right to be in this conversation, and his PRODUCTION put him in this position."**

**And then: "We're going to MISS KIRK. I just LOVE coming to work every day with him and love the TEAMMATE that he is and love the PERSON that he is."**

**"He's one of the MOST UNSELFISH GUYS I'VE EVER BEEN AROUND. And one of the GREATEST TEAMMATES I've ever been able to coach."**

**And the sentence that actually matters most: "His INFLUENCE WILL REMAIN ON OUR TEAM throughout this season. And it remains with FERNANDO. His influence on Fernando in our quarterback room has been TREMENDOUS."**

Raider Nation, **"his production put him in this position" is the most honest thing in that whole answer.** **Kirk Cousins played SO WELL that he became a tradeable asset that another team would pay real draft capital for.** **He didn't get traded because he failed. He got traded because he succeeded.**

## NOW THE COMPENSATION — AND LET'S BE HONEST ABOUT IT

Raider Nation, here's what we got.

**A MINNESOTA THIRD ROUND PICK.**

**And they clarified it specifically, because the Vikings have TWO third-rounders: it is the VIKINGS' OWN THIRD, not the Eagles' third.**

Raider Nation, **I'm going to be straight with you: as a pure football trade, a third-round pick for the NFL's touchdown leader feels light.** It does. **I'm not going to insult you by pretending otherwise.**

**But asked why what Minnesota offered was enough to move on, Spytek gave an answer that reframes the entire thing:**

**"We had to make a HARD DECISION. We all kind of STARE THE FUTURE IN THE FACE here with FERNANDO. And Kirk's been VERY OPEN with that — was very open with that when we got him."**

**"We had to basically decide: do we want to GO FORWARD HERE OVER THE LAST EIGHT GAMES, or do we want to TAKE WHAT MINNESOTA'S OFFERING RIGHT NOW? Ultimately we decided to do that."**

**And Raider Nation, this is the sentence I want you to hold onto: "KIRK'S BEEN VERY OPEN WITH THAT WHEN WE GOT HIM."**

**Kirk Cousins KNEW. From day one. He signed up knowing a first-overall-pick quarterback was coming and that his job was to hold the wheel and teach the kid. He never pretended otherwise. He never campaigned against it.**

**That's why Kubiak called him one of the most unselfish men he's ever coached.**

## AND NOW THE DETAIL THAT CHANGES HOW YOU SEE THIS TRADE

Raider Nation, THIS is the part nobody saw coming, and it completely changes the math.

A reporter asked about **Tom Delaney** and the contract structure he negotiated for the Raiders last year. **And Spytek's answer revealed the hidden number in this deal.**

**"It was a UNIQUE NEGOTIATION with Kirk last year — given the contract he was coming off of in Atlanta and what we were trying to get accomplished."**

**"We found a MIDDLE GROUND to make it work, where SOME OF THE COMPENSATION WOULD BE DUE NEXT YEAR — HAD HE FINISHED TODAY ON OUR ROSTER."**

**"It was a TEN MILLION DOLLAR we were willing to pay. The OWNERSHIP was willing to pay it, because we felt like this is the KIND OF PLAYER we were signing and what he could bring to our organization."**

**"And then TODAY things changed. And now THAT'S MINNESOTA'S CONTRACT."**

**Raider Nation. Do you understand what just happened?**

**There was a TEN MILLION DOLLAR trigger. If Kirk Cousins had finished TODAY on the Raiders roster, we owed ten million dollars next year.**

**So this trade isn't a third-round pick. This trade is a THIRD-ROUND PICK PLUS TEN MILLION DOLLARS OFF NEXT YEAR'S BOOKS.**

**That's a completely different transaction. That's real money for a franchise that's going to be shopping for interior defensive linemen and guards in March.**

**And Raider Nation, give Tom Delaney his flowers today, because that structure was negotiated MONTHS AGO. Nobody knew it would matter on this exact Tuesday. A contract negotiator built an exit ramp into a deal in the spring, and in November it turned a light return into a smart one.**

**That's the unglamorous, invisible work that separates well-run franchises from the other kind.**

## AND HERE'S HOW FAST IT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

Raider Nation, the timeline is wild.

Asked when the process began and to walk through the discussions with Minnesota:

**"I don't want to get too far into that, but really THIS MORNING, Nolan and I spoke for a while and we figured out kind of the FRAMEWORK of it — and whether it made sense for both teams. We ultimately agreed that it did."**

**"I talked to a lot of the guys around the league for a long time — but I would say THIS MORNING."**

**And then the question of the day: were you actively SHOPPING Kirk Cousins, or waiting for someone to call?**

**"I NEVER MADE ONE CALL ON KIRK COUSINS. I got a FEW phone calls — but ONE REAL ONE THIS MORNING."**

**I never made one call on Kirk Cousins.**

Raider Nation, **that is an enormously important sentence, and I want you to sit with it.**

**John Spytek did not put his starting quarterback on the market. He did not leak his name. He did not shop him for leverage. He answered the phone when it rang.**

**And later he described the window: "All of a sudden you wake up in the morning and you've got a decision to make, and you've got SIX HOURS TO DO IT."**

**SIX HOURS.**

**"We spent a lot of time together this morning and we made what we felt was the BEST DECISION FOR THE RAIDERS GOING FORWARD."**

**Raider Nation, that's a general manager and a head coach locked in a room on deadline morning, with a franchise-altering choice and a clock running out. And they came out together and announced it together.**

## AND THE PLAN WAS ALWAYS THERE — THEY JUST DIDN'T KNOW THE DATE

Raider Nation, this is the answer that proves this wasn't panic.

Asked how much Fernando Mendoza factored into the decision:

**"We had a PLAN that we talked about AT LENGTH starting with his INTERVIEW — and it kind of led us all the way up to today."**

**"And it was really to PLAY KIRK UNTIL SOMETHING CHANGED. It could have been the LEVEL OF PLAY, the TEAM, FERNANDO — all those things went into it."**

**Starting with his interview.**

Raider Nation, **that means this plan predates the season. It predates the draft. It was discussed in Klint Kubiak's JOB INTERVIEW.**

**Play the veteran until something changes. That was always the plan. Today something changed — a phone call, a record of 4-5, and a rookie who got too good to keep on the bench.**

**And Kubiak said the same thing from his side: "We KNEW THIS DAY WAS COMING. We DIDN'T KNOW WHEN it was going to be. But he's continued to GROW since he's been here."**

## AND THEY WERE CAREFUL ABOUT HOW THEY SAID MENDOZA EARNED IT

Raider Nation, listen to the precision here, because I found it fascinating.

**"We did want to say and CONFIRM that FERNANDO WILL BE THE STARTING QUARTERBACK GOING FORWARD. It's something he's worked VERY HARD FOR since the moment he walked into this building. He PASSED EVERY TEST."**

**And then: "I want to STOP SHORT OF SAYING HE'S EARNED IT — but he PUT US IN A POSITION where we had to make a REALLY HARD DECISION TODAY, with his growth as a PERSON, as a TEAMMATE, as a LEADER, and as a QUARTERBACK."**

**Raider Nation, "I want to stop short of saying he's earned it" is a remarkable piece of honesty from a general manager on the day he hands a rookie the keys.**

**He's saying: this young man has done everything we asked in PRACTICE. He hasn't earned anything in a GAME yet. Nobody has. That's Sunday's job.**

**And that's exactly the right message to send to a 22-year-old on the day he becomes the face of the franchise. No coronation. A job.**

**He said it twice, too — "he performed at a level in practice that was HARD TO IGNORE, IMPOSSIBLE TO IGNORE, and he put us in a position to make a really hard decision today."**

**Impossible to ignore. That's the phrase.**

## AND KUBIAK TOLD US EXACTLY WHAT HE'S SEEN IMPROVE

Raider Nation, this is the football substance, and it's genuinely encouraging.

**"Fernando has done everything we've asked him to do in practice. I think he's EXCEEDED those expectations. And we all know that LIVE BULLET SUNDAY is FAR DIFFERENT from practice."**

**"However — he's shown us that he's ready, NOT ONLY PHYSICALLY BUT MENTALLY. He's up there STUDYING RIGHT NOW like he would any other game. He puts a LOT of work in. HIS TEAMMATES KNOW HOW PREPARED HE IS. It's NOT JUST LIP SERVICE — he has SHOWN IT ON THE PRACTICE FIELD."**

**And then asked where specifically he's made strides since the regular season began, Kubiak gave the answer that should make you sit up:**

**"Every day he's going against a LIVE PASS RUSH — with MAXX, with PEGUES, with QUAY WALKER, and NUMBER 17 COMING UP THE A GAP. So he's got a lot of GAME SPEED REPS."**

Raider Nation, **think about that for a second.**

**This rookie's daily practice environment has been Maxx Crosby coming off the edge at him. Every single day. Since April.**

**That's not a scout-team quarterback playing catch. That's a young man who has spent six months looking at the best defensive player in football coming at his face.**

**"And I see him progress in his ACCURACY, in his TOTAL COMMAND of our system, his ability to GET IN AND OUT OF A HUDDLE, operate TWO-MINUTE DRILLS, SITUATIONAL FOOTBALL."**

**"But his MOST IMPORTANT piece of production is his ability to THROW A FOOTBALL ACCURATELY and MAKE GOOD DECISIONS. And I've seen those TWO THINGS REALLY JUMP OUT."**

**Accuracy and decision-making. Raider Nation, those are the only two things that actually matter at that position. Everything else is garnish.**

**The level at which he operates the SCOUT TEAM. And the UNSELFISHNESS.**

**Raider Nation, a lot of first-overall picks would sulk through that. He attacked it. And both men brought it up unprompted.**

**"We could go on and on. But there's A LOT MORE TO PLAYING THE POSITION than just what your coaches tell you in your meeting room."**

**And then the two items that are worth the whole trade:**

**"It's an ENDLESS LIST of all positive things."**

**What buttons to push on your teammates when things are going badly.**

**Raider Nation, that's the "comparison is the thief of joy" speech. That's the In-N-Out trip. That's walking out of the stadium the same person after a two-catch day. Fernando Mendoza watched a man do that for nine weeks from four feet away.**

**And Kubiak's line stands: "His influence will REMAIN on our team throughout this season. And it remains with Fernando."**

**Kirk Cousins is on a plane to Minnesota. What he installed in that building is staying.**

## AND KUBIAK WAS HONEST ABOUT THE TRANSITION — HE DIDN'T SELL US A FANTASY

Raider Nation, this is why I trust this coach.

Asked how he anticipates making the transition SEAMLESS:

**"You know, I anticipate IT BEING A TRANSITION. We're going to FIND OUT TOGETHER."**

**He refused the word "seamless." Raider Nation, that's the honesty I want.**

**"But we have spent a LOT of time on the practice field together — obviously a lot of time away from the cameras of game day. He has PLENTY OF REPS with his teammates: HANDING IT OFF, THROWING IT TO THE ELIGIBLES, and BEING A LEADER IN THE LOCKER ROOM."**

**"So he's got REPS WITH THESE GUYS. Now he just gets to go do it in a GAME ENVIRONMENT."**

**And on the rookie learning curve — how do you square the inevitable ups and downs with still being on the hunt?**

**"You DON'T PUT A GOVERNOR ON THEM. We have EXPECTATIONS for him to PLAY AT A HIGH LEVEL. Yes — you play your first NFL game, there's going to be UPS AND DOWNS. But that's why he's ONE-ELEVENTH of the operation. He's got to RELY ON HIS TEAMMATES and he's got to DO HIS JOB."**

**ONE-ELEVENTH OF THE OPERATION.**

Raider Nation, **that is exactly the right thing to say and I want to applaud it.** **You don't hand a 22-year-old the whole franchise and say "save us." You tell him: do YOUR job. There are ten other men in the huddle. Be one-eleventh.**

**That's how you keep a rookie from pressing. And remember, pressing is EXACTLY what beat us in San Francisco — Kirk Cousins himself said it: "sometimes when you get behind, you try to get it all back in one play."**

## AND THE PLAYBOOK QUESTION — THE "POINT GUARD" ANSWER

Raider Nation, this was the sharpest football answer of the day.

Asked how much this changes the playbook, given that there are things Fernando does physically that Kirk doesn't, and things Kirk knows mentally that Fernando doesn't:

**"We installed an offense in APRIL and we've been improving it since that day. So we're going to do WHAT THE WHOLE TEAM DOES WELL."**

**"Obviously there are parts to his game that we might look to do A LITTLE BIT DIFFERENTLY. However — it's about THE OTHER TEN PLAYERS IN THE HUDDLE TOO, and what THEY ALL DO BEST."**

**"And the QUARTERBACK IS THE ULTIMATE POINT GUARD. So he's got to BE the point guard. He's got to be the LEAD MAN."**

**"We've got to go PRACTICE this week and get our BEST PLAYS out there — and things that HE does best, ALONGSIDE his teammates."**

Raider Nation, **"the quarterback is the ultimate point guard" is a beautiful way to put it.** **He's not the scorer. He's the distributor. Get Brock Bowers the ball. Get Michael Mayer the ball. Hand it to the backs. Run the operation.**

**And notice what he did NOT promise: a brand new offense built around a mobile rookie. He said the offense stays, with adjustments for what Fernando does well.**

**That's correct. Raider Nation, you do not rewrite a scheme in four days for a quarterback making his first start against the defending champions.**

**And one small thing I loved: when the reporter asked about the physical differences, the room LAUGHED. Even Kubiak. Because yes — the 22-year-old can do things athletically that the 38-year-old could not, and everybody in that room knows it. That's the upside. That's the whole reason he was the first pick.**
