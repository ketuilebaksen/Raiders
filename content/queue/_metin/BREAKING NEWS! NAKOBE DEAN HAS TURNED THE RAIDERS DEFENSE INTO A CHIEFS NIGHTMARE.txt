FIRST, THE STAKES

Start with a simple fact that should make every Raiders fan smile.

The Raiders are 3-0. The Chiefs are 3-0. Sunday's winner sits alone on top of the AFC West.

That's not a typo. That's not a dream. That's not a video game save file. That's real life in October of 2026, and the silver and black are right in the middle of it.

THE STAT SHEET IS ALMOST SILLY

Let me show you what kind of season this man is having, because the list is ridiculous.

On this Raiders defense, Dean ranks first in total tackles. First in solo tackles. First in tackles for loss. First in forced fumbles. He is tied for third on the team in sacks, and he is fourth in quarterback hits.

Read that list again, slowly.

A middle linebacker, the man wearing the green dot, the guy responsible for relaying every call, is showing up in the pass-rush categories right next to the edge rushers. That is not normal. Most green-dot linebackers are organizers first and producers second. Dean is leading this defense in the categories that measure violence and in the categories that measure disruption, at the same time.

And he's doing it while never leaving the field. We're talking about roughly 98 percent of the defensive snaps, one of the highest rates on the entire team. He doesn't rotate. He doesn't get a breather on third down. He plays every snap, and when he's out there, something happens. A tackle for loss, a hit on the quarterback, a ball on the ground. Pick your poison.

THE ANALYTICS CROWD LOVES HIM TOO

The numbers people agree, and that matters because the eye test can lie.

According to Pro Football Focus, Dean is sitting on a grade in the mid-80s, which ranks third among all linebackers in the league. He also has one of the highest run stop totals in the entire NFL.

And there's a claim going around that I want to handle carefully, because I always try to be straight with you. The claim is that he's the only linebacker in the league with a top-15 grade in run defense, pass rush, and coverage at the same time. That comes from the analysis that fueled this breakdown, so take it as a strong talking point rather than gospel carved into stone.

But even if you give that claim some wiggle room, the underlying point survives: Dean does everything. He stops the run. He gets after the quarterback. He drops into coverage without getting exposed. And he does it sideline to sideline. He's a one-man version of a defensive coordinator's wish list, which is exactly why Rob Leonard can call the aggressive stuff he calls.

THE FOURTH QUARTER IS WHERE THIS GETS SPICY

Here's where the story turns from impressive to historic.

Through three games, the Raiders are the only team in the entire NFL that has not allowed a single point in the fourth quarter. Not one. Zero.

Think about how wild that is for this specific franchise. We have all watched this team for years, and the fourth quarter used to be where hope went to die. Leads evaporated. Fans stared at their phones. Somebody somewhere threw a remote at a wall.

Now the Raiders are shutting doors in the final fifteen minutes like a bouncer at closing time.

And in the middle of all of it, again and again, is number 17. Dean has forced a fumble in the fourth quarter of every single game this season. Three games, three fourth-quarter forced fumbles, and all three directly helped win the game.

Now let me give you the stat that I think is the single most important number in this entire video. During his four years with the Philadelphia Eagles, Dean forced three fumbles total. Three, in four seasons.

In three weeks with the Raiders, he has already matched that number.

If that doesn't make you want to run through a brick wall wearing a Raiders jersey, I don't know what will.

Let's walk through all three, because each one is its own little movie.

Week One against Miami. The Raiders eventually win 27-13, but a huge swing happens late in the third quarter. The Dolphins face third-and-five at the Raiders' 13-yard line. That's a field goal at worst, a touchdown at best, and a one-score game either way.

Malik Willis scrambles, and Dean chases him down like a heat-seeking missile for the sack. The ball pops loose and Jeremy Chinn recovers it.

That's a drive killer. That's points off the board. That's the kind of play that doesn't always make a highlight package but absolutely shows up in the final score, because the difference between a 27-13 win and a 27-20 nail-biter is exactly one sequence like that one.

PLAY TWO: WEEK TWO, THE SHORT-MEMORY PLAY

Week Two against the Chargers. This is the one that showed me what this defense has become.

Las Vegas had just been flagged for having 12 men on the field on fourth-and-one. A silly, avoidable mistake. They had to burn a timeout.

Now, in previous years, you know exactly what happens next. That is precisely the moment where this defense folds like a cheap lawn chair, the opponent converts, the drive continues, and the broadcast crew starts talking about how the Raiders beat themselves.

Instead, Dean and that group said short memory, let's go. On the very next play, Chargers running back Omarion Hampton takes the handoff, the Raiders punch the ball out, and Las Vegas recovers. The Raiders score, go up by double digits, and the game is essentially decided.

The swing from self-inflicted mistake to game-changing takeaway took about fifteen seconds.

That's leadership. That's composure. That is exactly what a green-dot linebacker is supposed to provide, and it's the thing you cannot measure in a box score.

Week Three in New Orleans. This one is the dagger.

The Raiders lead 35-27 with about a minute left. The Saints have the ball near their own 40, and they're still within one score, in their own building, with a quarterback who has been closing games since last November. Every Raiders fan watching is chewing their fingernails down to the bone.

SO HOW DOES THIS HAPPEN?

Here's the fair question: how does a linebacker go from three forced fumbles in four years to three forced fumbles in three weeks? Is it luck? Is it talent? Is it the water in Las Vegas?

I think it's three things working together.

First, health. Dean has dealt with injuries in his career, and the thing holding him back has never been talent. It's been availability. When he's on the field, he's a problem, and right now he's on the field for 98 percent of the snaps.

Second, scheme. Rob Leonard has built a fast, aggressive, do-not-blink defense, and it lets his linebackers attack instead of hesitate. A linebacker who is reading and reacting is a step slow. A linebacker who knows exactly what the call wants him to do is a missile.

Third, mentality. Dean said after the Saints game that the team knew it had to rally back, start playing its type of football, and that their style is taking the ball away.

That last phrase is the key. "Our style is taking the ball away." That's not a statistic, that's an identity, and identities produce repeatable behavior. When a coaching staff lets players play free, you get what we've been watching: guys sprinting across an open field with no hesitation at all, and then bang, forced fumble.

THE DEFENSE'S NUMBERS BACK IT UP

And this isn't one player's highlight reel, it's a unit.

The Raiders defense has forced nine takeaways through three games, which leads the league. They're allowing around 18 points per game, which ranks inside the top ten in the NFL. And they're in the top half of the league in yards allowed as well.

Now, think about the last time the Raiders had a top-ten defense. You have to go all the way back to 2002, the year this franchise went to the Super Bowl. And here's the detail that gives me chills: 2002 is also the last time the Raiders started a season 3-0.

Does that mean we're going to the Super Bowl this year? I'm not going to say that, because I'm not that reckless and you'd rightly roast me for it in the comments. But when a defense produces numbers this franchise hasn't seen in more than twenty years, you pay attention.

THE MOST UNDERRATED PART: THE COMMUNICATION

Now let's talk about the part of this story that I think is the most underrated. It isn't the plays. It's the talking.

When you wear the green dot on your helmet, you are the quarterback of the defense. You relay the calls, you get everyone lined up, you adjust on the fly, and you make sure ten other men are on the same page before the snap.

Now consider what this defense actually looks like compared to last season. A new defensive coordinator. A new scheme. And a flood of new faces: Dean himself, Quay Walker, Kwity Paye, Trayden Stokes, Darien Porter, Hezekiah Masses, Eric Stokes back in the mix, Maxx Crosby back from knee surgery, and now Nolan Smith joining the edge rotation.

Almost nothing about this group is the same as last year.

When you have that much turnover, the most valuable commodity in the world is somebody who can talk. Somebody who can organize chaos. Somebody who makes everyone around him better. That's the reason this defense looked organized in Week One instead of in Week Eight, and organization in September is the hardest thing to find in this league.

And here's the quote that sums the man up. Asked about it in a press conference this week, Dean talked about his superpower, and said it's trying to make those around him better day in and day out, doing it his own way rather than anybody else's, while striving to be the best version of himself every day.

I love that. That's what leadership actually sounds like. Not loud, not performative. Just real.

It's also no accident that under Klint Kubiak this team names captains based on performance every single week, and Dean has earned that honor again for the Chiefs game, right alongside Brock Bowers and Tommy Eichenberg. Kubiak has gone further than that, calling Dean the heartbeat of this defense. And at the podium this week, asked about him directly, Kubiak said they signed him to be a leader, that the great thing is he doesn't do it alone, that he brings the best out of other people.

When your head coach says that, you're not just a good player. You're a franchise cornerstone.

THE CULTURE PART THAT DOESN'T SHOW UP ON A STAT SHEET

Now let's talk about culture, because this is the part of the rebuild I think matters most over three years instead of three weeks.

When Klint Kubiak was hired, the biggest thing this team needed was a culture change, on and off the field. So look at who they brought in to build it.

Dean is a national champion at Georgia, a program that has been about as consistent as any in college football over the last several years. He is a Super Bowl champion with the Philadelphia Eagles. This is a player who has been in the biggest moments, who has seen up close what winning actually looks like, and who knows what it costs.

That matters more than we admit. We spend all our time talking about talent, but championship habits are real. Players who have been there before don't shrivel when the lights get bright. They get excited. They say, all right, let's rock.

And he didn't arrive alone, in spirit or in fact. Dean and Quay Walker were teammates at Georgia. When Dean signed with the Raiders in free agency, he told the story of how neither man knew the other was coming. They had talked about playing on the same team someday but never really thought it would happen. When Dean told Walker he was joining the Raiders, Walker basically said, you're lying. Turns out Walker was signing too. Dean called it a blessing to be back with somebody he considers a brother.

Honestly, that's the thing I love most about this roster. These aren't mercenaries collecting checks in the desert. These are players who like each other, who play for each other, who have each other's backs. When Max talks about foxhole guys, that's what he means. And the two of them now lead this team with 27 and 24 tackles.

THE CONTRACT, AND THE BEST SIGNING ARGUMENT

Let's talk money, because the investment was real.

Dean signed a three-year contract worth $36 million with $23 million guaranteed. And right now, the Raiders look like geniuses.

Let me put it plainly. When Dean hit free agency, plenty of people questioned whether this franchise should spend that kind of money on a linebacker with an injury history. It was a legitimate concern. Linebacker is not a premium position, and availability had been his issue.

Now he might be the best signing of the entire offseason. And I'll go further, as bold as it sounds: when you combine his on-field production, the culture he's helped build, and the leadership he provides, I don't think there is a better signing on this roster.

Raiders fans have been burned at this position before, and we all know the names. The Cory Littleton contract was a disaster. And remember how much we all loved Robert Spillane? Spillane is a good football player and I'll never speak ill of him, but he isn't in the same conversation as what Dean is doing right now. People have simply stopped bringing him up, because Dean has been that good.

And you know who else appears to have noticed? Philadelphia. There has been chatter coming out of that building that the Eagles defense misses Dean. Now, I want to be clear that this is chatter and not an official verdict, and I'm not going to repeat the unflattering comparisons being thrown around over there about other players, because that's gossip and it isn't my business.

But isn't it wild to imagine that a Super Bowl champion organization is already looking back at the linebacker it let walk and wondering what it did?

WHY THIS IS A NIGHTMARE MATCHUP FOR KANSAS CITY

Which brings us to the Chiefs, and the reason I called this a nightmare.

Think about what Kansas City wants to do. They've added Kenneth Walker III, the league's leading rusher, and their offense now runs through what Walker gives them. If you stop him, you stop everything, because the Chiefs become one-dimensional, and then this pass rush gets to pin its ears back with Crosby, Paye, Koonce and now Nolan Smith.

And who is the Raiders' best run defender? Dean. According to Pro Football Focus, he has the third-most run stops in the entire NFL.

When Rob Leonard was asked about the challenge of stopping the league's leading rusher, he talked about the linebacker duo of Dean and Quay Walker. And Dean himself called Kenneth Walker versatile and dynamic, which tells you he isn't underestimating anybody. He also said the team has to trust the game plan, handle its business, and play to its level.

That's just the run game. Now add Travis Kelce.

Dean's ability to cover tight ends, to drop into coverage and not get exposed, may be the single thing that makes this defense more dangerous than any Raiders unit in years. And it matters even more this week, because we all watched Tyler Shough throw four touchdown passes to tight ends in New Orleans with Trayden Stokes out of the lineup.

Stokes is back now, cleared from the protocol. But the middle of the field still starts with the linebacker. Dean is fast, he's smart, and he's physical. If the Raiders can disrupt the timing between Mahomes and Kelce, that changes this entire football game.

And one more layer on the game plan: Klint Kubiak was Seattle's offensive coordinator last season, when Kenneth Walker was his running back on the team that won Super Bowl 60. He spent a year designing ways to get that man the football. Now he's spent a week designing ways to take it away.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE BALL, AND THE INJURY THAT HURTS

Let me give you the offensive picture too, because it's not all defense.

Maybe this offense isn't putting up video game numbers, but it has what it needs. Kirk Cousins has been steady: nine touchdown passes, no turnover problem, and the first quarterback in NFL history to throw three touchdowns in each of his first three games with a new team. Brock Bowers is back and dominant after winning AFC Offensive Player of the Week with 13 targets, 10 catches, 116 yards and a touchdown. And Ashton Jeanty and Mike Washington Jr. now give this ground game a one-two punch, with Washington averaging 6.8 yards a carry.

But there's a real injury to flag, and it's a painful one: Jackson Powers-Johnson has been ruled out for Sunday. That's a genuine hit to an interior offensive line that was already the weakest part of this roster, and it's exactly why the Cesar Ruiz addition matters so much, even if a lineman walking in the door this week can't fix everything by kickoff.

On defense, the news keeps getting better. Stokes is back at safety, and the communication in that secondary only improves with him on the field next to Masses and Porter.

THE MINDSET SHIFT

Here's another thing I love, and it's the hardest thing to explain to someone who doesn't watch this team every week.

The old Raiders played scared. You could feel it through the television. They played not to lose. You'd see this team up by a touchdown in the fourth quarter and instinctively know it was going to end badly.

There is a massive difference between a team trying to win a football game and a team trying not to lose one.

Look at the Saints game again. The Raiders were trying to beat the Saints. The Saints, late, were trying not to lose to the Raiders. That's a complete flip of the last three years, and it starts with leaders like Dean. When the lights are brightest, a lot of players shrink. Dean says let's go.

NOW THE HONEST OTHER SIDE

I promised I'd keep it real with you, so here's the part a homer channel would skip.

The Chiefs are 3-0 for a reason. Mahomes has won a lot of big games and he is never going to panic, no matter what this crowd does. Reid is a master at making adjustments, and he will have an answer for whatever works in the first quarter. The Raiders have not beaten this franchise in a long time, and one great linebacker cannot by himself change the history of a rivalry.

If the Raiders turn the ball over, if the offensive line struggles without Powers-Johnson, if Walker breaks off a couple of long runs early, this can get ugly. Nobody is telling you this is a guaranteed win.

What I am telling you is that the Raiders have a legitimate shot, and the biggest reason is that their defense is finally good enough to give them one.

It's also worth saying clearly, because the national media is still catching up: this Raiders defense is, in my opinion, the biggest surprise from any team in the league this season. Not another team's offense. Not anybody's special teams. This unit, with nine takeaways and zero fourth-quarter points allowed, is the reason the Raiders are undefeated. That isn't smoke and mirrors and it isn't luck. It's a group that flies around the field, plays for each other, and believes in the man in the middle.

SO LET ME DEFINE THE CLAIM

I said at the top that I believe Dean is the best linebacker in the NFL right now, and I know that sounds crazy to some people. I got laughed at on social media when I said it. That's fine.

Let me be precise about what I'm claiming. I'm not saying he's the most talented linebacker in football. I'm not saying he has the biggest name or the longest résumé. I'm saying that when you combine production, versatility, leadership, and impact on the actual scoreboard, nobody is doing a harder job better than he is right now.

Is Fred Warner of the 49ers in that conversation? Of course he is. Warner is the standard at this position and I'll never pretend otherwise.

But the fact that we're even saying Nakobe Dean's name in the same breath as Fred Warner should fire up every single person in Raider Nation. A few years ago that was unthinkable. Now it's an honest debate.
