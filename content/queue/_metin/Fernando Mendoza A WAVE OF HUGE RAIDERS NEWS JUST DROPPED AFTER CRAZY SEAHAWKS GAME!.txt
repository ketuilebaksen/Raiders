# A WAVE OF RAIDERS NEWS AFTER THE CRAZY SEAHAWKS GAME — AND A WARNING ABOUT DENVER 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, I'm telling you — Fernando Mendoza's first NFL start against Seattle left me with **more questions than answers.**

**Because for a while, I genuinely thought we were watching THE BEGINNING OF SOMETHING SPECIAL.**

**He looked COMFORTABLE. The offense had LIFE. I was actually feeling pretty good about what I was seeing.**

**He opened the game SEVEN FOR SEVEN. And he finished the first half ELEVEN OF THIRTEEN.**

**And our defense held the defending Super Bowl champions to FOUR FIRST DOWNS in the first half. FOUR.**

**But then the game took a turn — and what happened afterward should have every Raiders fan paying attention.**

**And honestly, Raider Nation? I'M NOT EVEN SURE MENDOZA IS THE GUY WE SHOULD BE QUESTIONING THE MOST.**

**Especially with DENVER coming up — off a BYE WEEK — there's a warning from this game that we simply cannot ignore. Because Seattle just published the instruction manual on how to break our rookie, and Denver has had two weeks to read it.**

So let me explain. I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing **6,000 believers** — hit that like button, drop **"I AM A RAIDER"** in the comments, and let's break this whole thing down.

## FIRST: WE PLAYED CLEAN FOOTBALL AND STILL LOST

Raider Nation, that 21-13 loss was frustrating because **we had a real chance to win.**

**Our defense came out READY. And QUAY WALKER'S FUMBLE RECOVERY gave us the ball at SEATTLE'S 46-YARD LINE.**

**We DIDN'T COMMIT A PENALTY. We DIDN'T TURN THE FOOTBALL OVER.**

**Raider Nation, think about that for one second.**

**ZERO penalties. ZERO turnovers. One week after FIVE TURNOVERS in a single half in San Francisco.**

**WE PLAYED CLEAN FOOTBALL — AND STILL COULDN'T SCORE ENOUGH.**

**And Klint Kubiak opened his Monday press conference with exactly that: "When you PROTECT THE FOOTBALL and you have NO PENALTIES, that GIVES YOU A CHANCE TO WIN THE GAME."**

**So Raider Nation, I am NOT going to look at the final score and tell you Fernando Mendoza was the problem.**

**I saw rookie mistakes. ABSOLUTELY. But I ALSO saw an offense that BARELY HELPED HIM once Seattle adjusted.**

## AND THE OPENING SCRIPT WAS GENUINELY BEAUTIFUL

Raider Nation, go back and watch those first few drives, because Klint Kubiak had a plan that **actually made sense.**

**He used PLAY ACTION. He got Mendoza OUTSIDE THE POCKET. And he let him make COMFORTABLE THROWS.**

**MENDOZA OPENED SEVEN FOR SEVEN.**

**He finished the first half ELEVEN OF THIRTEEN FOR 90 YARDS.**

**And we had BALANCE, too: AROUND TWENTY RUSHING ATTEMPTS COMPARED WITH THIRTEEN PASSES.**

**Raider Nation, WHEN DID WE LAST LOOK THAT ORGANIZED?**

**Twenty runs to thirteen passes. In a HALF. Against the best defense in football. That's the balance Klint Kubiak has been begging for since September — "the game HAS TO BE BALANCED. You can be a running and throwing team — when you're throwing it EVERY TIME? THAT'S NOT EASY."**

**He finally got it. And it worked.**

**Those plays gave Mendoza a RHYTHM and made Seattle DEFEND MORE THAN ONE THING.**

## AND HIS MOVEMENT WAS EVEN BETTER THAN I HOPED

Raider Nation, this is the part that excited me most.

**Mendoza isn't the type of quarterback who's going to break off a 50-yard run every Sunday. But HE CAN ESCAPE PRESSURE and PUNISH A DEFENSE THAT LEAVES HIM SPACE.**

**He finished with SIX CARRIES FOR 22 YARDS AND A RUSHING TOUCHDOWN.**

**And that MOBILITY FORCES DEFENDERS TO CONTAIN HIM INSTEAD OF CHARGING STRAIGHT AHEAD.**

Raider Nation, **that last sentence is the whole value of a mobile quarterback and most people miss it.** **It's not about the rushing yards. It's that a pass rusher who has to WORRY about containment rushes with less abandon. He hesitates. He stays in his lane. He arrives a half-second late.**

**And Kubiak confirmed the value of it: "YES, THAT HELPS. HIS MOBILITY HELPS. IT GIVES US A DIFFERENT DIMENSION."**

**A different dimension. That's the thing Kirk Cousins could never give this team.**

**And that's exactly why I was so disappointed when the offense STOPPED CREATING THOSE CHANCES later on. Because once Seattle changed the picture, things got UGLY.**

## NOW THE WARNING: SIX SACKS, AND IT WASN'T A BLITZ

Raider Nation, this is the most important analysis in this whole breakdown, so stay with me — because what Seattle did is going to get copied.

**SIX SACKS in a rookie's first start is a HUGE warning. And I'm NOT blaming all of them on the line.**

**There was trouble with protection — including a play where DEVON WITHERSPOON CAME FREE as we struggled to account for him and NICK EMMANWORI.**

**But Mendoza ALSO HELD THE BALL when he needed to get rid of it. He averaged around 2.81 SECONDS TO THROW.**

**Sometimes he WAITED FOR HIS FIRST READ, then LOOKED ELSEWHERE when a defender was ALREADY IN HIS FACE. You simply cannot survive doing that all afternoon.**

**AND HERE'S THE PART THAT SHOULD SCARE YOU:**

**WHAT MADE SEATTLE SO EFFECTIVE WASN'T A NON-STOP BLITZ.**

**They brought extra pressure on roughly TWENTY-FIVE PERCENT of his dropbacks — CLOSE TO THEIR NORMAL RATE OF AROUND TWENTY-TWO PERCENT.**

Raider Nation, **READ THAT AGAIN. They barely blitzed more than usual.**

**Twenty-five percent versus a normal twenty-two percent. That's a THREE PERCENT increase. That's nothing.**

**MIKE MACDONALD DIDN'T NEED TO SEND EVERYBODY.**

**HE MADE MENDOZA THINK THEY WERE COMING FROM ONE DIRECTION — AND THEN ATTACKED FROM ANOTHER.**

**Sometimes defenders SHOWED PRESSURE OPPOSITE THE TIGHT END, only for A DEFENSIVE BACK TO RUSH FROM THE OTHER SIDE.**

**Mendoza TRIED CHANGING THE PROTECTION — BUT SEATTLE STAYED AHEAD OF HIM.**

Raider Nation, **and the kid described this himself at the podium with total honesty:**

**"They were showing a FOUR-WEAK BLITZ TO THE BOUNDARY. So I made a CHECK, SLID THE PROTECTION THAT WAY — AND THEY DROPPED THAT SIDE AND CAME THE OTHER SIDE."**

**"COACH MACDONALD WAS PLAYING CHESS. HE FOOLED ME. HE GOT ME A COUPLE OF TIMES."**

**That's a TOUGH LESSON in your first NFL start. And Raider Nation, it's the kind of lesson that only hurts once — if you learn it.**

## AND WE SHOULD HAVE EXPECTED SOME OF IT

Raider Nation, let me be fair to everybody here.

**We saw in COLLEGE that Mendoza could HOLD ON TO THE BALL TOO LONG — particularly when he DIDN'T IDENTIFY PRESSURE BEHIND HIM quickly enough.**

**And the national analysts warned about exactly this before kickoff: "there were moments, and THIS GOES BACK TO COLLEGE AS WELL, where HE HOLDS ON TO THE FOOTBALL A LITTLE BIT TOO LONG and TAKES TOO MANY SACKS. That is NOT what you want to do against a Seahawks defense."**

**They called it. It happened.**

**HE'LL LEARN. BUT LEARNING CANNOT MEAN TAKING SIX HARD HITS EVERY WEEK.**

**He has to FIND HIS HOT RECEIVER, RELEASE THE BALL, and KNOW WHEN A PLAY IS OVER.**

**Raider Nation, and the kid said the same words himself: "I NEED TO UNDERSTAND MY HOT ROUTES — WHICH I NEED TO DO BETTER. And we need to ESSENTIALLY BE A MASTER AT THE PROTECTION SCHEME."**

**And Kubiak's version: a VETERAN QUARTERBACK "KNOWS THE SAFE PLACE TO DITCH THE BALL. There's a part of his game that FERNANDO WILL LEARN."**

**I LOVE that he wants to MAKE SOMETHING HAPPEN. I just don't want that CONFIDENCE TURNING INTO UNNECESSARY SACKS while we're supposed to be DEVELOPING him.**

**And Raider Nation, this roster has TWO QUARTERBACKS and a Plan C the head coach won't describe. Six hard hits a week is not a development plan. It's a risk.**

## AND THEN HE LOST HIS SAFETY VALVE

Raider Nation, now imagine handling all of that pressure while losing your most dependable target.

**BROCK BOWERS WENT OUT WITH AN ANKLE INJURY — AND THE WHOLE PASSING GAME FELT DIFFERENT.**

**Bowers was the guy who could GIVE HIM AN EASY ANSWER. Without him, Seattle had FEWER THREATS TO WORRY ABOUT — and our receivers WEREN'T MAKING THEM PAY.**

**And Kubiak confirmed it Monday: "WHEN WE LOST BROCK, YOU DEFINITELY SAW A BIG CHANGE IN PRODUCTION."**

**Raider Nation, the good news from Monday: the ankle is "NOT AS BAD AS WE THOUGHT" and he's DAY-TO-DAY — though Kubiak also said it's "STILL A SIGNIFICANT INJURY." No speculation from me. We monitor.**

**But I'm JUST AS CONCERNED that THE OFFENSE DIDN'T HAVE A CONVINCING BACKUP PLAN.**

**MICHAEL MAYER can catch short passes, FIGHT THROUGH TACKLES, and HELP UNDERNEATH. WHY WEREN'T WE FINDING MORE WAYS TO USE HIM?**

**Raider Nation, when your best tight end goes down, the obvious answer is YOUR OTHER TIGHT END. Remember what Kirk Cousins told us weeks ago: this team has "the kind of TIGHT END ROOM that forces a defense to PICK ITS POISON." We had the antidote on the roster and barely used it.**

## AND NOW THE RECEIVER NUMBERS — AND RAIDER NATION, BRACE YOURSELF

Raider Nation, just look at this wide receiver production. All of it.

**TRE TUCKER: ONE CATCH FOR NINE YARDS.**

**JAYLEN NAILOR: ONE CATCH FOR MINUS ONE YARD.**

**JACK BECH: TWO FOR TEN.**

**CODY WHITE: ONE FOR TWENTY-TWO.**

**THAT'S FIVE CATCHES COMBINED. FROM THE ENTIRE WIDE RECEIVER ROOM. FOR FORTY YARDS.**

**Mendoza completed SEVENTEEN OF TWENTY-NINE PASSES overall — and OUR RECEIVERS BARELY MADE AN IMPACT.**

**And I keep thinking about that MISSED THIRD DOWN OPPORTUNITY involving Nailor.**

Raider Nation, **WHEN RECEIVERS CAN'T SEPARATE, THE QUARTERBACK LOOKS WORSE THAN HE SHOULD.**

**That's the sentence I want you to carry into Sunday. Six sacks looks like a quarterback problem and an offensive line problem. But Kubiak himself said it's THREE THINGS: "That's GETTING OPEN FAST. That's PROTECTING BETTER. And that's HIM GETTING RID OF THE BALL."**

**GETTING OPEN FAST was first on his list. And five catches for forty yards from the whole receiver room tells you nobody got open fast.**

**Raider Nation, we have been saying since October that this team is STILL LOOKING FOR A CLEAR NUMBER ONE RECEIVER. Sunday was the bill arriving.**

## AND THE RUN GAME WASN'T HELPING EITHER

Raider Nation, here's the other half.

**ASHTON JEANTY FINISHED AS OUR THIRD-LEADING RUSHER WITH NINE CARRIES FOR EIGHTEEN YARDS.**

**THIRD-LEADING RUSHER. The sixth overall pick. Behind two other men.**

**Raider Nation, SERIOUSLY — HOW ARE WE SUPPOSED TO BUILD A BALANCED ATTACK LIKE THAT?**

**And to his credit — AND THIS IS IMPORTANT — HE LED US IN RECEIVING WITH FOUR CATCHES FOR FORTY-SIX YARDS.**

**Raider Nation, forty-six receiving yards. One week after five catches for MINUS FIVE in San Francisco. That's a real bounce-back and he deserves credit for it.**

**But DEFENDERS STILL WEREN'T WORRIED ENOUGH ABOUT THE HANDOFF.**

**And that's the whole Jeanty argument we made last week: ONE YARD BEFORE CONTACT, dead last in the league. In the only two games of his career where he got normal blocking, he averaged 5.86 a carry — second best among all running backs. The man needs a hole.**

**And the season number: 2.9 YARDS PER CARRY — THE WORST IN THE LEAGUE.**

## AND HERE IS THE STAT THAT SHOWS HOW BADLY WE GOT SQUEEZED

Raider Nation, this number is genuinely shocking.

**WE FINISHED WITH ONLY 163 TOTAL YARDS.**

**Mendoza threw for 143 — BUT SIX SACKS COST US FORTY-FIVE YARDS, leaving just NINETY-EIGHT NET PASSING YARDS.**

**Ninety-eight net passing yards. For a team that averages 259 passing yards a game, FIFTH BEST IN THE LEAGUE.**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S HOW BADLY SEATTLE SQUEEZED THIS OFFENSE.**

**And remember what that defense is: the FEWEST YARDS PER PLAY IN THE NFL at 4.1, only 257 yards a game allowed, and — by yards allowed per game — the BEST DEFENSE SINCE 1999. Better than the 2002 Bucs. Better than the 2000 Ravens.**

**163 total yards against THAT is not a scandal. But it's not a competitive offense either.**

## AND THIS BRINGS ME RIGHT BACK TO KLINT KUBIAK

Raider Nation, here's my honest criticism, and it's the heart of this whole breakdown.

**I LOVED the opening script. I DIDN'T LOVE what happened once Seattle figured it out.**

**WE WENT SEVEN OF TEN ON THIRD DOWN BEFORE HALFTIME — AND ZERO OF SEVEN AFTERWARD.**

**Raider Nation. SEVEN OF TEN. AND THEN ZERO OF SEVEN.**

**That is one of the most dramatic half-to-half collapses you will ever see in a football game.**

**And Kubiak himself called it what it is: "What happened in the second half to go ZERO FOR SEVEN ON THIRD DOWN IS UNACCEPTABLE. THERE'S NO REASON THAT WE SHOULD DO THAT."**

**And here's the number that explains WHY: MENDOZA'S AVERAGE TARGET DEPTH IN THE FIRST HALF WAS ABOUT 4.64 YARDS.**

**Four and a half yards. Raider Nation, that's not a passing game. That's an extended handoff.**

**FINE — TAKE THE EASY COMPLETIONS. That's how you settle a rookie down, and seven-for-seven proves it worked.**

**BUT YOU CAN'T KEEP THROWING UNDERNEATH WHEN DEFENDERS ARE ALREADY WAITING THERE.**

**Because once Mike Macdonald saw four-and-a-half-yard target depth on film in real time, he moved everybody up. And then there was nowhere to go.**

## AND HERE IS THE STAT THAT PROVES WE LEFT A WEAPON UNUSED

Raider Nation, this is the one that frustrates me most.

**MENDOZA COMPLETED 52.7 PERCENT OF PASSES THROWN AT LEAST TWENTY YARDS DOWNFIELD IN HIS FINAL COLLEGE SEASON.**

**FIFTY-TWO POINT SEVEN PERCENT ON DEEP BALLS. That is an ELITE number. That's a man who can throw it over your head.**

**SO WHY WEREN'T OUR DEEP SHOTS BETTER TIMED?**

**Raider Nation, I'M NOT SAYING THROW INTO DOUBLE COVERAGE. I'm saying GIVE TRE TUCKER A CHANCE TO STRETCH THE DEFENSE and FORCE THOSE SAFETIES TO RESPECT SOMETHING BEYOND ANOTHER SHORT PASS.**

**Now, in fairness, Kubiak gave a real explanation: "That's what makes this defense SO GREAT. They PLAY SHELL COVERAGE and TAKE AWAY THE DEEP BALL. AND THEY DID THAT TODAY."**

**And he admitted he underused Tucker: "I COULD HAVE DONE A BETTER JOB OF GETTING TREY MORE INVOLVED YESTERDAY."**

**And one more time: Tucker finished with ONE CATCH FOR NINE YARDS. A man whose whole value is speed got one target's worth of production.**

## AND WHY WASN'T THE SCREEN GAME WORKING?

Raider Nation, this is the simplest fix available and we didn't use it.

**WHEN YOU'RE STRUGGLING TO RUN AND YOUR QUARTERBACK IS TAKING HITS, YOU HAVE TO MANUFACTURE SOME YARDS.**

**GET JEANTY THE BALL WITH BLOCKERS IN FRONT OF HIM.**

**GIVE TUCKER ROOM TO USE HIS SPEED.**

**LET MAYER CATCH SOMETHING UNDERNEATH AND RUN THROUGH A TACKLE.**

**WE REALLY NEEDED THOSE PLAYS TO SLOW DOWN THE RUSH.**

Raider Nation, **here's why a screen is the perfect answer to an aggressive pass rush: it punishes effort.** **A defensive lineman sprinting upfield at a quarterback runs himself right out of the play. One completed screen for fifteen yards makes a pass rusher hesitate for the rest of the afternoon.**

**And Jeanty caught four passes for 46 yards — the team lead! So the screen game CAN work. We just needed more of it when the pocket was collapsing.**

## AND I UNDERSTAND THE SITUATION, BUT THE HANDCUFFS HAVE TO COME OFF

Raider Nation, let me be balanced here, because Klint Kubiak deserves that.

**I UNDERSTAND he's LEARNING AS A FIRST-YEAR HEAD COACH. And he DOESN'T HAVE A LOADED ROSTER. He said it himself about his own job: "that's part of my job as I'M LEARNING AS I GROW INTO THE JOB."**

**BUT PROTECTING A ROOKIE SHOULDN'T MEAN KEEPING THE HANDCUFFS ON HIM UNTIL THE DEFENSE KNOWS EXACTLY WHAT'S COMING.**

**Raider Nation, that's the paradox of the "nothing peeled back" promise. He said the playbook stays full — and then the actual target depth was four and a half yards.**

**Full playbook on paper. Handcuffs in practice. Pick one.**

**And he owned it Monday, to his credit: "I LOOK AT MYSELF. I have to do a MUCH BETTER JOB of MOVING THE BALL and SPREADING THE BALL AROUND." And: "I'VE GOT TO HELP HIM BETTER IN THE SECOND HALF." And: "I PUT IT ON MYSELF. THERE'S NO ONE ELSE TO BLAME BUT ME."**

## AND I WANT TO SEE SOME NEW FACES

Raider Nation, two names.

**MALIK BENSON was INACTIVE against Seattle. WHY NOT GIVE HIM AN OPPORTUNITY DOWN THE ROAD?**

**Same with DONT'E THORNTON JR. once he's healthy. He was INACTIVE with a BACK INJURY — and WE COULD USE ANOTHER DOWNFIELD THREAT.**

**Raider Nation, I'm NOT claiming those guys will suddenly fix the whole offense. I just want to see us TRY SOMETHING DIFFERENT instead of watching THE SAME SHORT ROUTES GET SMOTHERED.**

**Five catches for forty yards from the entire receiver room. At some point you shuffle the deck.**

## AND NOW THE DEFENSE — AND THE FIRST HALF WAS MAGNIFICENT

Raider Nation, this deserves to be the lead story and nobody's covering it.

**OUR DEFENSE HELD SEATTLE TO FOUR FIRST DOWNS IN THE FIRST HALF.**

**FOUR.**

**Raider Nation, FOUR FIRST DOWNS. Against the DEFENDING SUPER BOWL CHAMPIONS. From a unit that was the FIFTH TEAM IN NFL HISTORY to allow thirty-plus points in six straight games.**

**THAT'S EXACTLY THE KIND OF HELP YOU WANT TO GIVE A ROOKIE MAKING HIS FIRST START.**

**And Kubiak gave the credit where it belonged: "I thought ROB did a REALLY GOOD JOB THIS WEEK of GETTING OUR GUYS READY TO PLAY, GETTING THEM READY TO START FAST, GETTING TURNOVERS, EXECUTING THE GAME PLAN."**

**Raider Nation, remember what Rob Leonard promised on Thursday, in the week everybody wanted him fired: "Especially to START THE GAME, we've got to START FASTER. SENSE OF URGENCY TO GET LINED UP. PLAYING WITH PHYSICALITY. EVERYBODY RUN TO THE BALL. AND IT'LL HAPPEN."**

**HE SAID IT AND THEN HIS UNIT DID IT. Four first downs in a half. The thirty-point streak is dead.**

## BUT THEN SEATTLE ADJUSTED — AND THIS IS THE SCARY PART

Raider Nation, here's how a four-first-down half became a 21-13 loss.

**SEATTLE ADJUSTED, AND DREW LOCK STARTED EXTENDING PLAYS OUTSIDE THE POCKET.**

**OUR DEFENDERS HAD TO COVER FOR WHAT FELT LIKE FOREVER — AND THE PASSING GAME EXPLODED.**

**JAXON SMITH-NJIGBA HAD ONE CATCH FOR ELEVEN YARDS BEFORE HALFTIME.**

**AND THEN PILED UP 144 YARDS IN THE THIRD QUARTER ALONE.**

**ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-FOUR YARDS. IN ONE QUARTER.**

Raider Nation, **that's the whole game. Eleven yards in two quarters, then 144 in fifteen minutes.**

**And COOPER KUPP also benefited WHEN PLAYS BROKE DOWN. WE WERE CHASING RECEIVERS EVERYWHERE.**

**And Kubiak named it precisely on Monday: "really TWO BIG OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS that we gave up DEFENSIVELY FOR TWO TOUCHDOWNS that really hurt us." And on JSN: "I thought they made some GREAT OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS. That was a BIG PART OF IT."**

**Raider Nation, off-script. That's the word. It wasn't the scheme. It was a quarterback escaping the pocket and eleven men covering for eight seconds instead of three.**

**And remember the warning Rob Leonard gave us all week: "FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL. WHEN YOU'RE ON THE FIELD A LONG TIME, MISTAKES TEND TO HAPPEN MORE OFTEN."**

**And what was the offense doing in that same stretch? ZERO FOR SEVEN ON THIRD DOWN. Four or five straight three-and-outs, by Kubiak's own count.**

**Raider Nation, that defense played a magnificent half and then got left on the field to drown. The offense did that to them.**

## AND HERE'S WHAT ELSE SEATTLE DID THAT WE HAVE TO MATCH

Raider Nation, this is a coaching point for our own staff.

**Seattle MOVED SMITH-NJIGBA AROUND and ATTACKED UNDERNEATH AREAS WHERE OUR SECOND-LEVEL DEFENDERS HAVE STRUGGLED.**

**And THEY ALSO WON MORE OFTEN ON FIRST DOWN — WHICH OPENED UP PLAY ACTION and KEPT THEM AWAY FROM DIFFICULT THIRD DOWNS.**

**THAT'S AN ADJUSTMENT WE NEEDED TO MATCH.**

Raider Nation, **look at that symmetry and weep. Seattle won on first down, which unlocked play action, which kept them out of third-and-long.**

**And Kubiak described our version of the exact same mechanism in reverse: "If you're NOT GOOD ON FIRST DOWN, you put yourself in a DROP BACK POSITION on SECOND AND LONG. And then you're THIRD AND LONG. When you're NOT HAVING SUCCESS ON FIRST AND TEN, THAT AFFECTS IT A LOT."**

**Same game. Same mechanism. One team won first down and the other didn't.**

**MEANWHILE, WE KEPT ASKING MENDOZA TO OPERATE FROM A POCKET THAT WASN'T HOLDING UP.**

**WE SAW WHAT HE COULD DO ON THE MOVE EARLY. WHY ABANDON THAT WHEN IT WAS BECOMING CLEAR HE NEEDED IT?**

## AND THAT'S WHAT DENVER WILL BE STUDYING

Raider Nation, here is the warning, and please take it seriously.

**DENVER IS COMING OFF A BYE WEEK. And they have a CLEAR EXAMPLE of how to disrupt him:**

**SHOW PRESSURE. TAKE AWAY HIS FIRST READ. KEEP HIM CONTAINED. AND FORCE HIM TO SOLVE EVERYTHING WHILE THE POCKET CLOSES.**

**Raider Nation, that's four steps. It's written down. It's on tape. And Denver has had TWO WEEKS to install it.**

**And remember — Seattle didn't even need to blitz. Twenty-five percent against a normal twenty-two. You don't need exotic pressure to copy this. You need discipline and a good disguise.**

**SO BEFORE WE START DEMANDING PERFECT QUARTERBACK PLAY, I NEED TO SEE KUBAK RESPOND.**

**MOVE MENDOZA AROUND. GIVE HIM QUICK ANSWERS. AND TAKE A SHOT DOWNFIELD WHEN IT'S THERE.**

**THE GAME PLAN CAN'T STOP WORKING THE MOMENT THOSE FIRST SCRIPTED PLAYS ARE FINISHED.**

**Raider Nation, that's the real test of this coaching staff. The first fifteen plays were excellent. The next forty-five were not. In the NFL, everybody can script an opener. The job is the ADJUSTMENT.**

## AND ON THE KIRK COUSINS COMPARISONS — LET'S BE FAIR

Raider Nation, I know some of you are comparing him to Kirk Cousins. Let me put that in perspective.

**AN EXPERIENCED QUARTERBACK CAN HIDE WEAKNESSES BY RECOGNIZING PRESSURE AND RELEASING QUICKLY.**

**Look at how PATRICK MAHOMES handles those situations. HE RARELY GIVES A FREE RUSHER THE CLEAN HIT THEY'RE LOOKING FOR — BECAUSE HE SEES WHAT'S COMING.**

**MENDOZA ISN'T AT THAT POINT YET. AND NOBODY SHOULD EXPECT HIM TO BE AFTER ONE START.**

**And Kubiak said the identical thing: "a VETERAN QUARTERBACK KNOWS THE SAFE PLACE TO DITCH THE BALL. There's a part of his game that FERNANDO WILL LEARN."**

**Raider Nation, here's the honest summary of this franchise right now:**

**OUR HEAD COACH IS LEARNING. OUR QUARTERBACK IS LEARNING. AND OUR SECOND-YEAR GENERAL MANAGER STILL HAS WORK TO DO BUILDING THIS ROSTER.**

**All three things are true at once. And all three are fixable.**

**MENDOZA IMPROVED THROUGHOUT HIS COLLEGE CAREER — and I want THAT SAME GROWTH HERE. 134th-ranked recruit, rejected as a walk-on at Miami, third string, 4,712 yards at Cal, and then 41 touchdowns and a Heisman at Indiana. That's a career-long upward line.**

**EVERY PRACTICE AND FILM SESSION SHOULD HELP HIM SPOT THOSE PRESSURE LOOKS BEFORE THEY TURN INTO ANOTHER SACK.**

**PATIENCE MATTERS. BUT SO DOES SUPPORTING HIM.**
