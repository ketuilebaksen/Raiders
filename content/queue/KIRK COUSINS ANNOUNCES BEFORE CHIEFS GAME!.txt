Raider Nation, here's a sentence nobody would have believed in August: the national media is running out of ways to ignore the Las Vegas Raiders.

Three games, three wins, a share of first place in the AFC West, and a home date with the Kansas City Chiefs that suddenly feels like the biggest regular season game this franchise has hosted in years.

So today we're skipping the panic, ignoring the doom-scrollers, and counting the good news, because there's a lot of it. Five pieces, in order, including one that I think almost everybody is underselling and one that I'm going to be completely honest about, because good news only means something when you admit the bad parts too.

I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we build toward 6,000 believers. Let's count them.

GOOD NEWS ONE: THE SAINTS ARE ACTUALLY GOOD

Start here, because this is the piece everybody is skipping.

Do not let the final score or the four turnovers fool you. New Orleans is a good football team, and the win is better than it looks.

Look at their season. In Week One they trailed the Detroit Lions 21-0, then came storming back with 30 unanswered points, tied the game with barely a minute left in regulation, and pushed it to overtime, where a two-point conversion to win it fell incomplete in the back of the end zone. Tyler Shough threw for 410 yards that night. In Week Two, they went into Baltimore, erased a deficit, and beat the Ravens, with Shough punching in the go-ahead touchdown himself.

That's a team that has been in three straight fights and come out swinging every time. Saints fans have been more excited about their franchise than at any point since the Drew Brees days, and they had every reason to be.

And on Sunday, in their home opener, that team did everything a good team does. They built a lead. They took a punch. They landed a bigger one. Shough threw a career-high four touchdown passes. Chris Olave caught nine balls for 107 yards. New Orleans outgained the Raiders 381 to 340 and held the ball nearly five minutes longer.

For three quarters, that was their football game.

And the Raiders still walked into a hostile building, trailed by 11, and left with a win. That's what good teams do to other good teams: they survive, and they find a way. When we look back at this season in December, I think this is the game that told us what this group is made of.

GOOD NEWS TWO: BOWERS IS EXACTLY WHAT WE HOPED

Ten catches on thirteen targets, 116 yards and a touchdown, in his first game back from a knee procedure.

Sit with that target number for a second. Kirk Cousins threw the football 33 times on Sunday, and roughly four out of every ten of those throws went to number 89. In his first game. Off an injury.

Calling Brock Bowers a tight end undersells what he is. He lines up in the slot. He lines up outside. He wins contested catches. He runs routes like a receiver, and he moves the chains on third down like a machine. He isn't just the best tight end in this league; he's one of the best pass catchers in football, period.

And remember who's calling the plays. Klint Kubiak came here from a Seattle offense built around feeding one alpha target, making the ball go to him even when the whole stadium knew it was coming. That's exactly the role Bowers is filling.

The ripple effect showed up all afternoon. Tre Tucker had four catches for 43 yards working as the gadget piece. Ashton Jeanty caught all three of his targets. And Michael Mayer caught three balls for 32 yards and a touchdown, which is precisely why this front office handed him a three-year, $45 million extension weeks before the season.

That contract was a statement of intent, and Sunday was the preview: two tight ends on the field at the same time, creating matchup problems that defenses simply can't solve with their base personnel. Safeties can't cheat toward the run. Linebackers can't sit in the middle. Everybody else gets more room.

GOOD NEWS THREE: COUSINS KEEPS GETTING BETTER

Twenty-two of 33 for 248 yards, three touchdowns, no interceptions.

That's three touchdown passes in three straight games to open a season, and no Raiders quarterback had ever done that. He's tied for the league lead with nine touchdown passes. He's completing about two-thirds of his throws. He's protecting the football. And he's doing the thing good veteran quarterbacks do, which is making an offense look organized while everything around it moves at a hundred miles an hour.

Look at the progression. Week One, he did what he needed to do to win. Week Two, he made a couple of mistakes and still delivered three touchdowns. Week Three, he got his best weapon back and it showed immediately.

And here's the fun part. All of this is happening with the number one overall pick standing on the sideline in his helmet, listening to the play calls and learning. Imagine this offense in a year or two, with Fernando Mendoza putting the ball down the seam to Bowers on a rope.

That future is exciting. It doesn't need to arrive today. And that might be the best news of all: this franchise isn't shoving its young quarterback into a fire, because the veteran in front of him is winning games. That's how you build a quarterback properly.

GOOD NEWS FOUR: THE RUN GAME PROBLEM HAS AN EASY FIX

Now the honest one, because good news isn't believable without it.

The running game had a rough day. Ashton Jeanty carried 19 times for 56 yards, about three yards a pop. Through three games he has 206 rushing yards at just over three a carry, and he's still looking for his first rushing touchdown of the season.

I'll say what needs saying in his defense. He's getting his touches, over 20 total in every game. He's a real part of the passing game, and he's been good in pass protection, which matters more than most fans realize because it keeps a 38-year-old quarterback upright.

But the back doing the most damage in open space right now is Mike Washington Jr., whose 36-yard touchdown run in the third quarter was the single biggest play of the game.

Here's the good news inside that. The Raiders won anyway. You can take away this team's run game and it still scores 35 points on the road. That's a balanced attack that doesn't depend on any one thing.

And the fix is simple. Maybe Jeanty carries it 15 or 16 times and Washington gets eight to 10. Both backs stay fresh across a long season, the offense gets more explosive plays, and Jeanty is preserved for the situations where he's most valuable, in the passing game and in protection. The offensive line is still finding itself, but a run game you can improve by handing the ball to a player who's already breaking big plays is the kind of problem every team in this league wants.

GOOD NEWS FIVE: THIS DEFENSE IS A TURNOVER MACHINE

Four takeaways in the fourth quarter alone. Let's go through exactly who did it.

Malcolm Koonce got home for the sack that jarred the ball loose, and Maxx Crosby fell on it. Hezekiah Masses jumped in front of a throw for an interception and returned it to the four-yard line. Jeremy Chinn strip-sacked Tyler Shough on fourth down. And Nakobe Dean knocked the football out of Juwan Johnson's hands in the final minute to seal it.

Turnovers are the most valuable statistic in football, and creating them at this rate tells you three things are happening at once: the pass rush is getting home, the secondary is playing the football instead of just the receiver, and the whole group is playing with a hunger you can't fake.

And I want to spend a moment on Dean, because what he's done in three weeks is remarkable. He was a third-round pick by the Eagles in 2022, a Butkus Award winner at Georgia, and he spent much of his time in Philadelphia fighting injuries and trying to hold a starting job. He won a Super Bowl ring there. Then this spring the Raiders signed him in free agency and paired him with his former Georgia teammate Quay Walker.

Since he arrived, he's become the tone setter, the man wearing the green dot, the guy flying to the football on every snap. He's been so good that it's fair to ask whether he's the best free agent signing this franchise has made in years.

Add Masses, a fifth-round rookie who has now made game-changing plays two weeks in a row, and Koonce delivering in the biggest moments, and you have a defense full of players who play like they have something to prove.

And they did all of that on Sunday shorthanded. Trayden Stokes, the second-round rookie safety who's been one of the bright spots of this secondary, was ruled out with a concussion. Isaiah Pola-Mao stepped in and, to be fair, had a difficult afternoon. That's not a shot at a player who has started plenty of games here; it's simply that Stokes is the more versatile piece, the center fielder who covers ground on the back end, and he was missed against a quarterback throwing all over the yard.

Getting him back for Kansas City, if the protocol allows it, is a very big deal. Patrick Mahomes does not hand out free passes.

THE BIGGER PICTURE: WHY 3-0 CHANGES THE WHOLE SEASON

Now zoom out, because this is the part I love.

Here's a simple truth about this football team. Even if they lose their next three games, they're 3-3. That means they are almost guaranteed to be playing meaningful football in October, and if they stay in the mix, meaningful football in November and December too.

That's what every one of us actually wanted from this season. Not a parade. Not a guarantee. Just a team worth caring about all the way through the fall. Three wins in the bank buys exactly that.

And who knows how far it goes. Look at what happened last season: New England, written off before the year began, went all the way to the Super Bowl. Denver, another team nobody picked, reached the AFC Championship game and lost by a field goal. Surprise teams happen every single year in this league.

I'm not confusing hope with prediction. But it's a perfectly reasonable question to ask: why not the Raiders?

The foundation is right there. A general manager in John Spytek building with a clear vision. A head coach in Klint Kubiak who has this locker room believing every week. A calm veteran quarterback making plays. A young, hungry defense with an edge. A generational tight end who changes the structure of the entire offense. And a rookie quarterback of the future learning quietly behind a winner.

You could not draw up a better foundation for a franchise turning the corner, and nobody could have predicted it would click this fast.

THE ONE THING THAT HAS TO GET FIXED

Before we celebrate all the way to Sunday, one warning, and it comes straight out of the same box score.

All four of Shough's touchdown passes went to tight ends. Two to Juwan Johnson, two to Noah Fant. That's not bad luck and it isn't a coincidence. With Stokes out, the middle of the field belonged to New Orleans, and a team with a struggling run game still put 27 points on the board because of it.

Now think about the opponent walking into Allegiant. Kansas City has been attacking that exact area of the field with the best quarterback of this generation for a decade. If the coverage over the middle looks the same on Sunday as it did in the Superdome, this won't be a one-score game.

So the assignment for this week is clear. Get Stokes back if the doctors allow it. Ask the linebackers to carry tight ends up the seam instead of sitting underneath. And above all, keep winning with four rushers, because the best coverage this defense has is a quarterback who doesn't have time to look.
