# KLINT KUBIAK'S SHOCKING DECISION FOR WEEK 10 — "WE'VE GOT TO GO TO PLAN C" 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, Klint Kubiak walked into that Friday press conference and admitted something I did not expect him to say out loud two days before a rookie's first NFL start.

**He only has TWO quarterbacks on the roster. And he has already built a PLAN C.**

Asked what his plan would be for an **EMERGENCY QUARTERBACK** on Sunday:

**"Yeah, WE HAVE A PLAN. We've got AIDAN — and then WE'VE GOT TO GO TO PLAN C. So, NOT GOING TO TELL YOU PLAN C."**

**"But plan C is part of the gig — PREPARING FOR THAT WHEN YOU ONLY HAVE TWO GUYS AVAILABLE."**

**And then came the real news, Raider Nation. Asked if he has a plan to BRING IN ANOTHER QUARTERBACK at some point:**

**"DEFINITELY IN DISCUSSION WITH THAT. YES."**

**Raider Nation, that's your head coach confirming the Raiders are actively discussing adding a quarterback — two days before the first overall pick makes his first career start against the defending world champions.**

**Think about what that means. Fernando Mendoza takes the first snap. Aidan O'Connell is the only quarterback behind him. And if BOTH of them go down, the Raiders have a secret emergency plan involving a man who is not a quarterback.**

**THAT'S the shocking decision. He's starting a rookie with no net underneath him.**

Plus: two injury updates that matter enormously for Sunday. What he said about **Brock Bowers demanding the best corners every week.** The **"emotionally stable"** line about Mendoza. How he teaches a mobile rookie NOT to run. And the special teams coordinator revealing the punt numbers that are **"unbelievably elite."**

## FIRST, THE INJURY REPORT — AND BOTH OF THESE HURT

Raider Nation, he opened with it.

**"THOMAS BOOKER IS QUESTIONABLE."**

**And then, asked about Dont'e Thornton not being out there: "QUESTIONABLE AS WELL. GOT A BACK."**

Raider Nation, **those are the two worst names you could read on a Friday report this week.**

**Thomas Booker is a DEFENSIVE TACKLE. He was OUT last week with an ankle injury, and this defense just became the fifth team in NFL history to allow thirty-plus points in six straight games while giving up 190 rushing yards to San Francisco.**

**Raider Nation, we need interior defensive linemen. We have needed them since September. Rob Leonard just told us "FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL" — and you fight fatigue with BODIES. Losing a rotational tackle again against a physical running team is exactly the wrong injury at exactly the wrong time.**

**And Dont'e Thornton with a BACK issue — Raider Nation, no speculation from me on anybody's back, that's between him and the medical staff. But remember: Thornton finished last week with FULL PARTICIPATION, and this fan base has been waiting all season for him to have a real impact game.**

**And look at the timing: a rookie quarterback making his first start, in a receiver room that — in my own words last week — is STILL LOOKING FOR THAT CLEAR NUMBER ONE. And now our vertical threat is questionable with a back.**

**Watch both of those names right up until kickoff.**

## AND NOW THE BIG ONE — THE QUARTERBACK ROOM IS DOWN TO TWO

Raider Nation, let's go back to that Plan C answer, because I think people are going to gloss over it and they shouldn't.

**Kirk Cousins got traded on Tuesday. That didn't just change the starter. IT EMPTIED THE ROOM.**

**On Wednesday, Mendoza talked about the quarterback room like it was a family: "ALL THREE of us prepared LIKE THE STARTER. We were all in there doing our routine, watching film together, going over the game plan together." And Kubiak said: "To be in a quarterback room with THOSE THREE GUYS studying together — THEY ALL MAKE EACH OTHER BETTER."**

**Three became two. And now the head coach is on the record saying he's "DEFINITELY IN DISCUSSION" about adding another.**

Raider Nation, **here's why that's smart and why it's also a little alarming.**

**It's SMART because you cannot go into the back half of a season with two quarterbacks and a rookie starter. One bad hit and you're handing the football to a wide receiver in the fourth quarter. Every well-run team carries three for exactly this reason.**

**It's ALARMING because it tells you how fast Tuesday happened. Six hours, per Spytek. They traded a starting quarterback at 4-5 and they are STILL sorting out the room three days later. That's not a criticism — that's just the reality of a deadline deal that materialized on deadline morning.**

**And "not going to tell you Plan C" — Raider Nation, I respect that. You don't broadcast your emergency quarterback to the defending champions. But somewhere on this roster there's a man who's been taking secret snaps this week, and I would love to know who it is.**

**My ask: sign a veteran quarterback this week. Not to play. To make sure a 22-year-old never has to play hurt.**

## AND HE WOULD NOT LET ANYBODY MAKE THIS WEEK EMOTIONAL

Raider Nation, this is the Seattle week subtext — because Klint Kubiak won a Super Bowl with these people last year.

Asked how he makes sure it's just another game, for himself, the staff, and the players who were there last year:

**"It's EASY. Because the more EXPERIENCE you have in the NFL, the more you TREAT THEM ALL THE SAME."**

**"And it's going to be an important game BECAUSE IT'S THE NEXT GAME FOR US."**

**And then the human part: "Obviously I'll see a lot of people with A LOT OF EXTREMELY STRONG BONDS that I'll have FOREVER, that I'm SO GRATEFUL to see and SAY HELLO TO."**

**"AND THEN IT'S GAME TIME."**

Raider Nation, **"and then it's game time" is as good a line as he's given all year.**

**Hug them in the tunnel. And then take their season.**

**And remember what he told us about that place: "ORGANIZATIONALLY, it's an ALL-FOOTBALL BUILDING. From JOHN SCHNEIDER to COACH MACDONALD — extremely INTELLIGENT PEOPLE who TREAT PEOPLE THE RIGHT WAY."** **And when they asked about his ring: "I DID get it. It's in the SAFETY DEPOSIT BOX."**

**Locked away. He's going for another one.**

## AND HIS ANSWER ABOUT THE "CHESS MATCH" TELLS YOU WHO HE IS

Raider Nation, a reporter asked how much he loves the chess-match element of coaching on game day — the kind of question a coach uses to show off.

**And Kubiak completely refused it: "My FAVORITE PART of game day is just SEEING THE PLAYERS go out there and PUT THEIR BODIES ON THE LINE and PLAY WITH EACH OTHER."**

**"I know that is a part of the game, but — just to see THINGS THAT YOU'VE WORKED ON COME TO LIFE IN THE GAME. See GUYS BATTLE THROUGH ADVERSITY."**

**"That's the JOY of getting to be on the sidelines and coaching."**

Raider Nation, **he was handed the chance to talk about how clever he is, and he talked about his players instead.**

**Nine games in, this is just who the man is. The thumb-pointing. "Stand behind Rob." "That was not those guys' job." "It starts with me." And now: my favorite part is watching them put their bodies on the line.**

## AND THE BROCK BOWERS ANSWER WAS SHORT AND PERFECT

Raider Nation, asked what advantages come with having Brock Bowers matched up on CORNERS the way he has been this year:

**"Brock DEMANDS EXCELLENT COVERAGE FROM THE BEST PLAYERS EVERY WEEK. AND HE'S EARNED IT."**

**Raider Nation, read that again, because it's a scouting report disguised as a compliment.**

**Teams are no longer covering Brock Bowers with linebackers and safeties. They're putting CORNERBACKS on him — which means defensive coordinators have decided he's a receiver, not a tight end.**

**And here's why that's actually GOOD news for a rookie quarterback: if a corner travels with Bowers, that's one fewer corner for the outside receivers. If they put a SAFETY in the slot to help, that's one fewer safety in the deep middle.**

**And remember what Rob Leonard said about facing a number-one target: "YOU START WITH HIM DAY ONE. Early down, third down, red zone. And NOT JUST COVERAGE-WISE."** **That's what Mike Macdonald is doing to Bowers this week. Which means Michael Mayer, Tre Tucker and Jack Bech get real opportunities.**

**Seven catches, 111 yards and a touchdown against San Francisco. The point guard's best option.**

## AND HE SAID THE SINGLE BEST THING ABOUT MENDOZA ALL WEEK

Raider Nation, this answer is the one I'm going to remember.

Asked whether he saw Fernando turn it up a notch in practice this week now that he's the starter:

**"I DIDN'T. And I think that's WHAT YOU WANT from coaches you work with and the players you work with — THAT THERE'S NO SURPRISES AND PEOPLE ARE THE SAME ALL THE TIME."**

**"Obviously INTENSITY GETS NOTCHED UP. But you want to go see WHAT THEY'VE DONE IN PRACTICE and see HOW IT SHOWS UP IN THE GAME."**

**"And usually it's those EMOTIONALLY STABLE INDIVIDUALS that are able to TREAT PRACTICE AND THE GAMES THE SAME WAY."**

**EMOTIONALLY STABLE INDIVIDUALS.**

Raider Nation, **that is the highest compliment this coach has paid anybody this season, and almost nobody will notice it.**

**Because think about what it means: the kid did NOT come out this week trying to prove he deserved the job. He didn't show off. He didn't press. He practiced exactly like he practiced in October, when his plan was to sit until the season was mathematically dead.**

**And remember Kubiak's answer on Wednesday when asked for a standout practice moment: "There's NOT ONE — AND THAT'S A GOOD THING. IT'S CONSISTENCY."**

**No standout moment. No notch turned up. Same man all the time. Raider Nation, THAT'S the profile of a quarterback who doesn't melt in the fourth quarter.**

**And remember the kid's own words about Kirk: he learned "HOW TO MAKE NO MOMENT TOO BIG."**

## AND HE WAS ASKED HOW HE'S ENJOYING HIS ROOKIE YEAR AS A HEAD COACH

Raider Nation, this answer was unexpectedly moving and I want to share all of it.

Asked how he's enjoying the minutia of being a head coach halfway through his first season:

**"It's such a BLESSING to get to work in this league — WHATEVER POSITION YOU ARE. Position coach, or you're QUALITY CONTROL. YOU'RE ONE OF THIRTY-TWO."**

**"And I think you realize how SPECIAL and how UNIQUE that is — AND HOW IT'S NOT FOR LONG EITHER."**

**"The NFL WILL CHEW YOU UP AND SPIT YOU OUT. SO YOU BETTER MAKE SURE YOU'RE ENJOYING IT ON THE WAY."**

Raider Nation, **"the NFL will chew you up and spit you out, so you better make sure you're enjoying it on the way" — from a 4-5 first-year head coach, in the week after a franchise-record sixth straight thirty-point game, days after trading his starting quarterback.**

**And he mentioned QUALITY CONTROL coaches. The anonymous young men grinding film at two in the morning for almost no money. He said THEY'RE one of thirty-two too.**

**And Raider Nation, do you hear the echo? Kirk Cousins stood at that same podium two weeks ago and said "COMPARISON IS THE THIEF OF JOY. This league can steal your joy really quick if you let it — and IT'S DONE IT TO ME MANY TIMES. DON'T LET THIS LEAGUE STEAL YOUR JOY."**

**The quarterback said it. Now the head coach says the league will chew you up, so enjoy it on the way.**

**Same message. Different man. That's a CULTURE, Raider Nation. That's not a coincidence.**

## AND HE EXPLAINED THE DANGER OF KNOWING THE OPPONENT TOO WELL

Raider Nation, this is the smartest tactical answer he gave.

Asked about the logistical challenge — you know them, they know you from a system standpoint — and whether there's a tendency to OVERTHINK it:

**"ABSOLUTELY. Those are the discussions you have on GAME PLAN NIGHTS. And then you BREAK YOUR MEETING, you're ON THE SAME PAGE, and NOW IT'S ABOUT THE PLAYERS. How do we GET THEM READY TO GO?"**

**"And I think you TAKE THAT PIECE OUT OF IT."**

**"There's a lot of discussion in game planning every week: HEY, WHAT HAVE WE PUT ON TAPE? WHAT DO WE THINK THEY'RE SEEING? WHAT ARE THEY GOING TO DO? And then YOU HAVE TO MAKE A DECISION. Then you GET YOUR BUTT OUT ON THE PRACTICE FIELD and MAKE SURE THE PLAYERS ARE EXECUTING."**

Raider Nation, **"you take that piece out of it" is the mature version of this week.**

**Because the trap in a game like this is obvious: you start trying to out-guess people who know your tendencies, you call things you haven't practiced, and your eleven men look confused. And Rob Leonard warned about exactly that: "IF ANYTHING MESSES WITH THAT, WE'LL TAKE IT OUT. WE WON'T DO IT. I want their confidence EXTREMELY HIGH AT ALL TIMES."**

**Both coordinators and the head coach are saying the same thing: decide on Wednesday, then stop being clever and go execute.**

**And asked if he'd talked to Rob about what he knows about Seattle's offensive players from last year: "YES, DEFINITELY. Discussions about PERSONNEL AND SCHEME. That's definitely part of the process this week."**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S the real edge. Our head coach spent last season scheming against this roster every day in practice. If that intel is worth anything, it shows up in the first fifteen plays on defense.**

## AND NOW THE RUN GAME — AND HE DID NOT HIDE

Raider Nation, this was the toughest question of the day and he took it square.

Asked what he can do DIFFERENTLY to get the running game on track, after half a season of trying:

**"No, it's a FAIR QUESTION. Those are things we're working on. We work on it ALL THE TIME in practice. I DEFINITELY HAVE SEEN STRIDES. I know IT DIDN'T SHOW UP LAST WEEK."**

**"And there's THINGS THAT I CAN DO BETTER. There's THINGS OUR PLAYERS CAN DO BETTER."**

**And then, asked whether the offense's success ultimately comes down to Ashton Jeanty running the football effectively:**

**"TEAM SUCCESS WILL COME WHEN THAT AREA IMPROVES — ON OFFENSE AND ON DEFENSE."**

Raider Nation, **fifteen carries for twenty-four yards last week. 1.6 yards a carry. FIVE tackles for loss. And a combined seventeen touches for seventeen yards between Jeanty and Mike Washington Jr.**

**And he said it again: "things that I can do better." That's the fourth straight press conference where he's put the run game on himself first. On Monday it was "it STARTS WITH ME — making sure we're OPENING RUN LANES."**

**But Raider Nation, I have to be honest with you about Sunday: Seattle allowed 89 rushing yards a game and held Bijan Robinson to 63 on 20 carries. This is the worst possible week for the run game to still be broken.**

**And a rookie quarterback NEEDS it more than a veteran does. Second-and-five is where rookies learn. Second-and-ten is where they get hurt.**

## AND HIS ANSWER ON TEACHING A MOBILE ROOKIE NOT TO RUN WAS A MASTERCLASS

Raider Nation, this was the best football answer of the week and I want to go through it carefully.

Asked how hard it is to teach a young man with Fernando's mobility — who's used to taking off — to trust his eyes, stay in the pocket, and run as a LAST RESORT:

**"It is just your FILM STUDY, and the things you're COACHING WITHIN EACH PLAY. It's a PROCESS — from now UNTIL THE END OF HIS CAREER."**

**"And you learn WAY MORE FROM FULL SPEED PRACTICE REPS. So he GETS THOSE REPS."**

**"But hey — GO THROUGH YOUR PROGRESSION. And obviously IF IT'S NOT THERE, YOU'VE GOT TO GO RUN THE FOOTBALL."**

**And then the part that actually matters most: "And then WHEN you're running the football — HOW DO YOU PROTECT YOURSELF so that you can GET US TO THE NEXT DOWN? Because WE NEED YOU AVAILABLE FOR THE WHOLE SEASON."**

**"YOU'RE NOT TRYING TO SELL OUT FOR A YARD ON FIRST AND TEN."**

Raider Nation, **"you're not trying to sell out for a yard on first and ten" is the single most important sentence anybody has said to Fernando Mendoza this week.**

**Because here's what's going to happen on Sunday. He's 22. He ran for 240 yards and six touchdowns in college. The defending champions are going to have the pocket collapsing. And every instinct in his body is going to tell him to lower his shoulder and fight for a yard.**

**And a rookie quarterback who takes twelve big hits in his first start is a rookie quarterback who misses games. And we just learned this team has TWO quarterbacks and a Plan C.**

**Raider Nation, GET DOWN. Get out of bounds. Live to Monday.**

**And then he said the thing that tells you why they drafted him: "That's also why, when you're SELECTING MEN IN THE DRAFT, you IDENTIFY THEIR INSTINCTS. And I LIKE FERNANDO'S INSTINCTS. I liked them in college."**

**"And it's HIS FIRST NFL GAME. So WE'RE ALL GOING TO SEE. But I'M EXCITED FOR HIS OPPORTUNITY — and for THE WHOLE OFFENSE TO GO PLAY TOGETHER."**

**"We're all going to see." Raider Nation, that's honest. No guarantee. No hype. We're all going to see.**

## NOW THE SPECIAL TEAMS SEGMENT — AND IT'S THE BEST PART

Raider Nation, this is where the real Raiders football talk happened, and I love that somebody asked it.

A reporter said: everyone sees Dylan Laube's great runs, and not to minimize that — **but when I went back and watched the film in DETAIL, there were some CRUSHING BLOCKS. Can you get into the blocking and the SELLOUT you're getting from those guys? Because I don't think they're getting enough credit.**

**And the coordinator said: "NO, YOU NAILED IT."**

**"Usually when there are big runs like that, it's holes opening BECAUSE GUYS ARE DOING A GOOD JOB OF BLOCKING. And I think our guys have done an OUTSTANDING JOB of that FOR THE MOST PART DURING THE YEAR."**

**"A lot of the credit has to go to MARIS and TIMMY McCONNELL — they do a good job of trying to SCRIPT EVERYTHING DURING THE WEEK. And I think it's been VERY COLLABORATIVE."**

Raider Nation, **a 95-yard touchdown that broke a 248-game drought. A 54-yard return the next week. Seven returns averaging 31.7 YARDS against San Francisco.**

**And the man in charge of it immediately handed the credit to two assistant coaches and eleven blockers.**

**And remember Dylan Laube does the exact same thing. And Kubiak said it too: "HE DOESN'T DO IT ALONE. You've got to look at the COACHING on special teams and the PLAYERS AROUND HIM SETTING UP BLOCKS."**

**Nobody in this building takes credit alone. That's three different men refusing it about the same unit.**

**And then the warning: "WE'VE GOT A HELL OF A CHALLENGE in front of us this week, because THESE GUYS THIS WEEK ARE REAL."**

## AND THEN THE NUMBERS — AND THEY ARE GENUINELY ELITE

Raider Nation, a reporter had clearly been timing punts with a stopwatch, and I respect that enormously.

He said the player **"who shall not be named"** had a couple of snaps last week that hit **6.5 SECONDS**, and one that hit **6.4** on his watch — **"unbelievably elite."**

**And the coordinator played along: "Yeah, HE'S DONE A GOOD JOB FOR US ALL YEAR — as you keep trying to just PULL IT OUT OF ME. We HOPE THAT CONTINUES."**

Raider Nation, **let me explain why 6.4 and 6.5 seconds matter, because this is the hidden gear of a football team.**

**Punt HANG TIME is the whole ballgame on coverage. League average is somewhere around 4.5 seconds. SIX-PLUS seconds of air is the difference between your coverage unit arriving as the returner catches it and a returner getting ten free yards before anybody touches him.**

**Against a team with — in the coordinator's own words — "an UNBELIEVABLE RETURNER," hang time isn't a stat. It's a defense.**

**And then he revealed what he's actually most proud of: "The biggest thing I'm MOST EXCITED about with him is THE FIELD GOAL OPERATION. Because that to me is THE HARDEST THING TO GET — where LACES and LOCATION and all that kind of stuff has been SO GOOD. And he's done a GOOD JOB with that."**

Raider Nation, **LACES AND LOCATION.** **That's the holder spinning the laces away from the kicker's foot and placing the ball on the exact spot, in under a second and a half, every single time, with 300-pound men colliding in front of him.**

**And in a game where this offense may struggle to find the end zone against the best defense in football, EVERY FIELD GOAL MATTERS. Matt Gay hit a 50-yarder last week. That operation has to be perfect on Sunday.**

## AND HERE IS THE GAME PLAN IN ONE SENTENCE

Raider Nation, the last question was the best one: how important is it to TRULY FLIP THE FIELD against a team like Seattle, where their defense is so good you may be punting from deep in your own territory?

**"OH, THERE'S NO QUESTION."**

**"When you look at what they did LAST YEAR — I said a couple of weeks ago about the Jets being first... they were FIRST and the Jets were SECOND last year. And THEY REALLY HAVEN'T FALLEN OFF."**

**"They've got an UNBELIEVABLE RETURNER. And the PUNTER is a BIG-TIME GUY also for them. HE CAN FLIP THE FIELD, does a GREAT JOB there."**

**"They just did a great job last year PLAYING COMPLIMENTARY FOOTBALL, and IT'S CONTINUED THIS YEAR. THEY KNOW WHAT KIND OF TEAM THEY ARE. THEY KNOW WHAT'S IMPORTANT TO THEM."**

**And then — Raider Nation, write this down, because it's the entire blueprint:**

**"WE'VE GOT TO MAKE IT A LONG FIELD FOR THEM AND A SHORT FIELD FOR US. THAT'S THE BIGGEST KEY."**

**"And that starts with the PUNTER and the KICKER — but WE'VE GOT TO DO A GREAT JOB IN ALL FACETS OF THE GAME this week, because it's going to be A HECK OF A CHALLENGE."**

Raider Nation, **"a long field for them and a short field for us" is how you win a football game with a rookie quarterback.**

**Because every yard the return unit gains is a yard Fernando Mendoza doesn't have to throw for against the best defense in the NFL. Starting at the 40 instead of the 20 turns a twelve-play drive into a seven-play drive. It cuts the number of chances to make a mistake nearly in half.**

**And every yard the punt unit pins Seattle back is a yard our exhausted defensive line doesn't have to defend. Rob Leonard: FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL.**

**Raider Nation, special teams isn't the sideshow this Sunday. It might be the whole plan.**

**And the coordinator also confirmed they're actively scheming to get Laube touches: asked how hard it is to keep coming up with new WRINKLES because teams are trying to keep it out of his hands — "OH YEAH, NO QUESTION."** **Expect something designed on Sunday. And expect Darien to get looks too.**
