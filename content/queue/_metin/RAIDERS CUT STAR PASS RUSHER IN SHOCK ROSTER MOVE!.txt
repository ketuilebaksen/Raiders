THE LINEBACKER MOVE KUBIAK PUSHED FOR

First, let's give proper context to the linebacker addition, because this didn't happen in a vacuum. Rewind the tape with me: Cam McGrone — one of our special teams leaders — released at cut-down. Then that preseason finale, where the linebacker room's tackling flat-out scared the coaching staff: Cody Lindenberg with his worst outing of August, two missed tackles in twenty snaps; Tommy Eichenberg missing two more of his own. In a Rob Leonard defense that asks its second level to clean up everything the front funnels to it, missed tackles aren't a detail — they're a season-killer. We watched Arizona gash this team for 150-plus rushing yards in the opener for exactly that reason.

So when Dont'e Thornton's move to injured reserve opened a spot on the 53, Kubiak and the staff didn't spend it on the thin receiver room. They spent it on linebacker Seun Olubi — taking that room from four bodies to five. At the time, some of you asked why. THIS is why. When a head coach watches his second level miss tackles for three straight weeks, he demands bodies, competition, and insurance — and the front office answered. It's not a headline-grabbing name, but it's the same pattern we've documented all summer: this staff identifies a weakness on tape and attacks it within DAYS. Now the question is whether Olubi holds that spot — because as you're about to see, the market is full of alternatives.

THE SHOPPING LIST: SIX PLAYERS WHO COULD FALL INTO VEGAS' LAP

Roster cuts around the league mean talent is floating free — and with the claim priority and open flexibility this front office is sitting on, Spytek needs to be READY. Six names to watch closely.

One: Davis Allen, tight end, Rams. Not the biggest name on this list, but maybe the most logical. Be honest about our tight end room behind the stars: Ian Thomas struggled badly through the preseason — the drops, the pass-protection issues we broke down after the finale. Allen is buried in a loaded Rams room, but he blocks well in the run game, contributes as a receiver, and actually produced last season. If Los Angeles waives him, Las Vegas should pounce. Cheap, useful, and he fixes a real weakness behind Bowers and Mayer.

Two: Darius Slayton, receiver, Giants. The contract situation is complicated, so New York may keep him — but if he shakes loose, you make the call. Slayton isn't the deep-threat terror he was early in his career, but the man has quietly stayed productive: more than 525 receiving yards in EACH of the last two seasons. Read that again and then look at our receiver room, where nobody outside of Bowers has ever strung two seasons like that together. He doesn't need to be a number one here. He needs to be a reliable grown-up who catches the ball when his number is called — which is precisely what Kubiak publicly demanded from this position.

Three: Christian Kirk, receiver, 49ers. Kirk could get squeezed out of a young, deep San Francisco room. No, he's not the 2022-2023 Jacksonville version anymore — but there's still real value in a veteran who simply knows how to get open. Slot experience, third-down reliability, professionalism for a young room. If he's available, at minimum you find out the price.

Four: Kris Jenkins Jr., defensive tackle, Bengals. Now we're talking about my favorite name on this list. Jenkins has been inconsistent in his young career, but the flashes are real and the pedigree is Day Two. Cincinnati could move on after loading up their defensive front — and here's the connection that makes this spicy: Jenkins is a Michigan man, and John Spytek's Michigan ties run DEEP. Front offices draft and claim what they know. With our interior line leaning hard on Hemingway, Butler, and Fatukasi, another young body with upside is exactly the kind of swing worth taking. If Jenkins hits the wire, I'd bet real money Vegas takes a serious look.

Five: Rayshawn Benny, defensive tackle, Ravens. Another Michigan Wolverine — sensing a theme? A seventh-round pick this spring who played WELL in preseason but isn't a lock for Baltimore's 53. Nobody's selling him as a future superstar, and that's fine. This is bottom-of-roster construction: low risk, real depth, developmental upside. The kind of unglamorous move that wins Decembers.

Six: Cole Wisniewski, safety, Eagles. We've had this name circled for a week, and the logic only grows. Philadelphia has safety depth to spare; the Raiders are thin on the back end — remember, we lost Anusiem for the year, and the secondary behind Chinn, Pola-Mao, and the rising Trayden Stokes is young and unproven. Wisniewski brings size, versatility, an intriguing college skill set, and special teams value. If the Eagles move on, Vegas has a legitimate case to move in.

The point isn't signing all six, Raider Nation. The point is that cut season creates windows, and great front offices are standing in them when they open. We've watched Spytek do it all summer — the Fatukasi strike, the Luter claim, the disciplined patience. These are the quiet moves that make a roster deep enough to survive eighteen weeks.

THE BIG ONE: THE 49ERS AND MAXX CROSBY

And now the story that's going to divide this fan base — because the rumor mill says the San Francisco 49ers remain interested in trading for Maxx Crosby.

Deep breath. Let's handle this the way we always do: facts first, panic never. There is NO indication a trade is imminent. There's no real evidence the Raiders are actively shopping Crosby. But per Grant Cohn of Sports Illustrated, San Francisco is one of several teams whose interest is still alive — and that's not nothing, because we all remember March. The Raiders DID agree to trade Crosby once, to Baltimore, before the deal collapsed. And the reason it collapsed matters more than ever: Crosby underwent meniscus repair surgery on January 7th, and concerns about that recovery reportedly drove the Ravens to back out after the physical. That history hangs over every future conversation — and it's exactly why the Raiders may prefer to wait until Crosby proves, on the field, in real games, that he's ALL the way back before anyone talks numbers again. Remember: he didn't take a single preseason snap. The league hasn't seen healthy Maxx since January.

Now the rumored package, because this is where it gets interesting. One theoretical framework Cohn mentioned: a FIRST-round pick plus Mykel Williams — the 11th overall pick in the 2025 draft, just 21 years old... and coming off a torn ACL. The alternative version: a second-round pick instead of Williams. So the real question hits your gut immediately: is a first-rounder plus a talented-but-injured 21-year-old enough for the face of your defense?

Let's put Crosby's résumé on the table so we're arguing with facts: 69.5 career sacks since 2019. Ten sacks LAST season — on a torn meniscus, on a 3–14 team, with 935 snaps of relentless effort. Forty-four and a half sacks over the past four years. This is not "a defensive end." This is one of the defining Raiders of his era — the guy who filmed a farewell video in March, got un-traded by a failed physical, and came back to work like a professional anyway. And think about what the other side of this trade means: Crosby lining up opposite a healthy Nick Bosa would instantly become one of the most terrifying pass-rush duos in football, in an NFC West that's already loaded. Strengthening San Francisco while gutting our own defensive identity? That's a bitter, bitter pill.

And let me play out the other side fairly, because my job is to give you the whole board, not just the comfortable half. Say the Raiders DID take that package. A first-round pick in 2027 — a draft we've already talked about on this channel, loaded at receiver with names like Cam Coleman and Jeremiah Smith at the top. Suddenly you're sitting on two first-rounders in a class that could hand Fernando Mendoza his true WR1 for the next decade. Add Mykel Williams — a top-eleven talent who, at 21, is YOUNGER than some of the rookies we just drafted, with the exact length-and-power profile teams covet on the edge. If his ACL recovery goes clean, you've essentially swapped six prime years of Williams plus a premium pick for what remains of Crosby's prime. That's the cold, actuarial argument — the one a portfolio-manager GM is paid to at least run the numbers on. I hate typing it. But pretending the argument doesn't exist would be lying to you, and that's not what this channel does.

Here's my honest framework, and it's the same one I gave you in August: this entire conversation is a hostage to the standings. If the Raiders come out competitive — if Cousins is steady, if the defense holds, if we're hanging around .500 in October — trading Crosby is indefensible and the phone stays unanswered. But if the losses stack early and the front office quietly decides this is a longer rebuild than advertised... then turning a 29-year-old edge rusher into a first-rounder plus a 21-year-old former top-15 pick becomes a very different math problem, especially for a GM who's shown us all summer that he manages this roster like a portfolio. I'm not ready to say Maxx Crosby is going anywhere — and I don't WANT him going anywhere. But I am telling you to watch this space, because the surest way to kill this rumor forever is the same as it's always been: WIN. Win early, win often, and nobody in silver and black ever has to read a Crosby trade tweet again.

And one more layer the national media keeps missing: the Crawford factor. Our edge room is down to four healthy bodies after that Achilles — Crosby, Paye, Koonce, Patrick Johnson, with Sloan waiting on the practice squad. In THAT context, trading Crosby before the season isn't just emotionally difficult, it's structurally almost impossible. You'd be tearing the load-bearing wall out of a defense that just lost its rookie reinforcement for the year. Which is one more reason I believe this rumor stays a rumor through September at minimum: the timing simply doesn't work for Las Vegas, and Spytek has shown us again and again that he doesn't move on anyone else's clock. If this conversation ever gets real, it gets real at the October trade deadline — with two months of Crosby health tape and two months of standings evidence in hand. Mark this timestamp, Raider Nation.

The waiver market still has six names that could quietly make this roster better — Allen for the tight end depth, Slayton or Kirk for veteran hands, the Michigan duo for the interior, Wisniewski for the back end. And the Crosby situation sits exactly where it's sat all offseason: no deal, real interest, and everything depending on what this team shows in September and October.
