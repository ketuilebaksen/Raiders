## NOW THE PART I CANNOT STOP THINKING ABOUT: THE MAHOMES SECRET

Okay, Raider Nation. Put your coffee down for this one, because this is the most interesting football thing I've heard in weeks, **and it explains a game that still hurts.**

Orlovsky said he's fascinated by something about Patrick Mahomes and the Chiefs, and here it is:

**Mahomes is the ONLY quarterback in football who, when he's under center to hand the ball off, REVERSES OUT instead of opening up.**

Let me explain what that means in plain English, because it's beautiful and it's evil.

**Normally, a quarterback taking a snap under center and handing off to his left will simply open his left hip and turn that way.** Short, direct, efficient. **Every quarterback in America does it that way.**

**Mahomes does the opposite. He opens RIGHT first — and then spins all the way around, almost a full 360, to hand the ball off to his LEFT.**

**And here's what it does to a defense.** The defense's eyes see **"quarterback opening right."** Their instinct — the thing they've been coached since they were nine years old — says the run is going that way. **So the defenders on the other side pause.** And by the time he's completed that spin, **the ball is completely hidden from the backside defender.** Jason McCourty described it perfectly: first you pause, **and then you can't even see the ball.**

**And here's the kicker, the number that made me rewind it three times:**

**"Maybe a 0.1 second hesitation. And that's the difference between four yards and twelve yards in the run game."**

**ONE TENTH OF A SECOND. Four yards versus twelve.**

Orlovsky noted that **Aaron Rodgers would do it occasionally, but Mahomes is the only man doing it consistently** — and that it's part of why Kansas City is having so much success running the football. **He also teased that Mahomes is using nonverbal signals with Kenneth Walker that he finds even more interesting.**

## WHY EVERY RAIDERS FAN SHOULD CARE ABOUT THIS

Raider Nation, **this isn't trivia. This is a scouting report on a team we lost to by three points.**

**We lost 30-27 to Kansas City.** And remember what Rob Leonard confessed at that podium this week? **He named that exact game:**

**"I keep thinking of the Chiefs week. We played great run defense — and then a 76-yard explosive. It's hard to win doing that."**

**Seventy-six yards.** In a three-point loss. And now ESPN is explaining, on the air, **the mechanism that turns four-yard runs into twelve-yard runs against defenses that are playing it right.**

**A tenth of a second of backside hesitation is how a well-defended run becomes an explosive.** That's not a scheme failure. **That's a magic trick.**

**And here's the honest part of this, the thing Orlovsky himself raised: can you even game-plan it?** You can tell your backside linebacker all week long. **But as he said — your eyes are what you're taught. Even if you KNOW he does it, your initial instinct takes over.**

**That's what makes it devastating.** You cannot coach a man out of his own eyeballs in five days.

**So file this away for the rematch**, because we see Kansas City again. **And when you watch Leonard drill space tackling with rugby bags in Week 7, understand what he's actually building: a defense that finishes the play even when its eyes have been lied to.**

Raider Nation, **sit with that for a second.** **That's the team that put 45 points and 536 yards on us.** The team that embarrassed this franchise in Week 5. **And their big splash — a 2028 first-round pick — has done NOTHING.**

**Two lessons here, and they both matter for us in the next three weeks.**

**Lesson one: a famous name is not a plan.** I said it two days ago when we talked about trading for a receiver, and I'll say it again — **you cannot judge a trade without knowing how the player fits.** New England spent a future first on a star receiver and still can't get him the ball. **That's the nightmare scenario for every deadline buyer, and it should make John Spytek careful, not timid.**
