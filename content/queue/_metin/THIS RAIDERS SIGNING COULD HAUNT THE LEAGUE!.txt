## OPTION ONE: JAUAN JENNINGS, AND THE KUBIAK CONNECTION

According to Austin Boyd, Bleacher Report's **Gary Davenport** believes Las Vegas could be the **best destination** in football for Minnesota Vikings wide receiver Jauan Jennings should he become available.

Not *a* destination. The best one. And the reason is sitting in our own head coach's office.

**Jennings worked with Klint Kubiak in San Francisco in 2023, when both were part of the 49ers organization.**

Raider Nation, this is the fourth time this pattern has appeared on this channel, and at some point it stops being a coincidence and becomes a strategy. Cody White came from Kubiak's Seattle staff. Dariq Young came from Seattle. Jerry Jeudy was in Denver in 2022 when Kubiak ran that passing game. Raheem Mostert spent years in the Shanahan system that Kubiak's offense descends from.

**This head coach keeps betting on players he has personally coached.** And it keeps working — a practice squad receiver from his Seattle days has five catches and three touchdowns this season.

## THE HONEST STATE OF JENNINGS RIGHT NOW

Now the uncomfortable numbers, because I'm not going to dress this up.

Jennings signed a one-year deal with Minnesota **after spending weeks on the market** — that's a detail worth remembering. And his playing time has been extremely limited: **two receptions for nine yards this season**, with no significant role carved out in that offense.

Two catches. Nine yards.

Davenport's argument is that the Raiders could offer him a far better opportunity, and that part is obviously true. Our receiving corps remains one of the weakest in the NFL — Tre Tucker, Brock Bowers and a group that desperately needs reliable support. Adding an experienced target matters, **especially if Fernando Mendoza eventually takes over as the starter**, because a rookie quarterback needs receivers who get to the right spot at the right depth every single time.

And the scheme fit is real. Davenport points out that Kubiak's system incorporates elements of the Shanahan-style West Coast offense, which suits Jennings' skill set — he's a physical, tough-catch, middle-of-the-field receiver, which is exactly the archetype that offense is built to feed.

**The price: a late day-three pick might be enough to start negotiations.**

## BUT HERE'S THE RED FLAG NOBODY WILL MENTION

And now the caution that I think is the most important sentence in this entire story, because most channels are going to skip right past it.

**The Raiders had the chance to sign Jauan Jennings before he went to Minnesota — and they didn't make a major push for him.**

Raider Nation, sit with that.

This front office already evaluated this player, in free agency, when he was available for nothing but money and no draft pick at all. Kubiak had coached him personally. They knew exactly what he was. **And they passed.**

So any version of this story that treats Jennings as an obvious solution has to answer one question: what changed? Either the staff's evaluation was wrong in the spring, or the staff's evaluation was right and the desperation of October is now doing the talking.

And his limited production in Minnesota might not be a matter of waiting for a better opportunity. It might suggest he's struggling to adapt to that offense — which is a very different problem, and one a new playbook in week seven doesn't fix.

**One more wrinkle, and it's a genuinely important one.** Boyd's article also raises the possibility that Jennings could end up **released** if Minnesota decides he doesn't fit their plans. Which means Las Vegas has to weigh the cost of a trade against the chance of acquiring the same player for free a few weeks later.

That's the whole calculation: do you pay a sixth-round pick today, or do you wait and risk another team claiming him tomorrow? Personally, with a receiver this team already declined once, **I wait.** If he's cut, you sign him with no pick attached and Kubiak gets his guy. If somebody else takes him, you've lost a player you didn't want badly enough to sign in March.

## OPTION TWO: CHRIS GODWIN, AND THE SPYTEK CONNECTION

Now the bigger name, and the one that actually fits the title of this video.

According to an October 15th report by Austin Boyd, Tampa Bay Buccaneers receiver **Chris Godwin** could become a trade target — because of his connection with Raiders general manager **John Spytek, who previously worked as an assistant GM in Tampa Bay.**

Raider Nation, there it is again. Not a coach connection this time. **A front office connection.**

And this is the thesis of today's video, so let me state it plainly. Every team at this deadline is looking at the same stat sheets and the same film. What separates buyers is *conviction* — knowing whether a player's bad season is circumstance or decline. **Spytek doesn't have to guess about Chris Godwin's work habits, his professionalism, or how he handles a locker room. He was in the building.** Kubiak doesn't have to guess about Jauan Jennings. He coached him.

That's the edge. Not picks. Information.

## WHY THE NEED IS DESPERATE — ONE STAT

And the need could not be clearer. Following that Week Five loss to New England, here's the number that tells the whole story:

**Tre Tucker and Cody White are the ONLY wide receivers on this roster with more than 100 receiving yards this season.** And Jalen Nailor has recorded just 52 yards in four games.

Two receivers over a hundred yards. In six weeks. One of them was on the practice squad in September.

Las Vegas explored Marvin Harrison Jr., who went to Houston. Brian Thomas Jr. has been mentioned too. But younger receivers command a much higher price tag — that's why I've refused to pay a second-round pick all month.

## THE GODWIN CASE, BOTH SIDES

So let me give you the full picture.

**In his favor:** he earned **All-Pro honors in 2019** and has posted **four seasons with over 1,000 receiving yards.** That's a genuinely accomplished professional, not a reclamation project. He knows how to win inside and outside, he's as reliable a route runner as there is on this market, and he would not need six weeks to learn where to line up.

**Against him:** his last 1,000-yard season was 2023, and the injuries have become a serious concern. According to Boyd, **Godwin played no more than nine games in either of the previous two seasons.** This year he's played in all five — which is encouraging — but he's recorded only **19 receptions for 179 yards.**

Now, there's real context for that. Tampa Bay's quarterback availability issues and broader offensive struggles may be affecting his production. A receiver can only catch what gets thrown to him by whoever is healthy enough to throw it.

**A change of scenery could help — but there are no guarantees.** If the Buccaneers will accept a late day-three pick, Las Vegas could pursue an affordable deal. But his age, injury history and recent numbers make it a calculated gamble.

## MY VERDICT ON BOTH

Here's where I land, and I'm going to be specific because vague opinions are useless.

**Chris Godwin at a sixth or seventh-round pick: yes, immediately.** He's the best available combination of proven production, immediate scheme readiness and professional reliability at a price that costs this rebuild nothing. Nineteen catches for 179 yards from a man playing in a broken offense is not the same as 19 catches from a man who can't play anymore — and Spytek is uniquely positioned to know which one this is.

**Jauan Jennings at any pick: wait.** The team already declined him once at a lower price. Watch whether Minnesota releases him, then sign him for free.

And remember how I've ranked this board all month: **Jeudy at a fourth is still the best value, Ridley at a sixth is close to free, and Brian Thomas Jr. at a second is a no.** Godwin now slots in right beside Ridley — a cheap, proven, immediate contributor. That's exactly the profile I've been telling you to wait for.

**And that's the signing that could haunt this league.** Not a blockbuster. A 1,000-yard professional, bought with a pick nobody will remember, from a general manager who knows precisely what he's buying — and dropped into an offense that already scores 28 a game with nobody to throw to.

## BUT SHOULD THEY BUY AT ALL?

Now the honest hard question, and this is the part a real analyst has to ask.

Should the Raiders be seeking reinforcements at all?

The recent losses have exposed significant weaknesses, and the upcoming games against **Buffalo and the Los Angeles Rams** could determine this team's entire trajectory. **Dropping both would leave Las Vegas at 3-4** — and at 3-4, a long-term rebuild becomes far more appealing than an immediate playoff push.

Holding draft picks would let the Raiders address several shortcomings next offseason and build the roster properly around Fernando Mendoza. That's not pessimism; that's the plan this organization has actually been executing.

And this is why the *price* matters more than the *player*. At a day-three pick, you can buy a receiver and still rebuild — a sixth-rounder is not the difference between a good offseason and a bad one. At a second-round pick, you're making a declaration about this season that a 3-2 team hasn't earned.

**So my answer: buy small, and only small.** If Spytek believes Godwin can make an immediate impact, a low-cost trade brings valuable experience to an offense that needs it. Anything above that, and the reward stops justifying the risk.

## AND NOW THE RUMOR NOBODY EXPECTED: KIRK COUSINS

Let's shift, because there's a story here that runs in the opposite direction entirely.

According to an October 16th report by Austin Boyd, **NFL.com's Kevin Patra believes Kirk Cousins could attract interest from playoff-contending teams** should a starting quarterback suffer an injury before the November 10th deadline.

And I want to lead with the number that makes this a real conversation rather than clickbait:

**Cousins has thrown 12 touchdown passes this season — the second-highest mark in the NFL — at age 38.**

Second in the league. Twelve touchdowns. For a team that's 3-2 with the weakest receiver room in football.

Patra's logic is sound: a contender whose quarterback goes down would view Cousins as a valuable short-term solution — an experienced starter who can keep a title window open instead of watching a season collapse. That's a premium commodity, and there are only a handful of men in the world who can do it.

**According to Boyd, the Raiders could seek a fourth-round pick. No trade has been confirmed.**

## THE THREE DESTINATIONS

And the specific landing spots are fascinating.

**Minnesota.** Cousins spent six seasons there and knows that organization inside out. If the Vikings face further issues regarding Kyler Murray's availability, they might consider bringing their former quarterback back. The familiarity cuts the learning curve to almost nothing — he could run that building from memory.

**Baltimore.** The Ravens might need another quarterback if concerns about Lamar Jackson's injury worsen. But negotiations could be complicated by history, Raider Nation — **this is the same franchise whose Maxx Crosby trade with Las Vegas collapsed at the medical stage in March.** That's a bruise that hasn't fully healed, and it would require the return to justify strengthening a potential rival.

**Denver.** Bo Nix has struggled this season despite real talent around him. If the Raiders fall out of contention, Denver could theoretically consider a short-term move. But trading a quarterback **to a division rival** would be an enormous decision, and Las Vegas would demand significant compensation — the same division tax we discussed with Khalil Mack, running the other direction.

## MY TAKE, AND IT'S A FIRM NO

Now let me tell you exactly what I think, because this is the most consequential question in the video.

First, the honest framing: **there are no reports that the Raiders are actively trying to trade Kirk Cousins.** This is an insider explaining a market condition, not a transaction. Anybody telling you he's on the block is inventing it.

Second, the football. **A fourth-round pick for the quarterback who is second in the NFL in touchdown passes is not close to enough.** And it isn't about the pick's value — it's about what you'd be doing to your own building.

Think about what this team would actually be after that trade. You'd be handing a rookie quarterback to an offensive line that allowed pressure on **44.8 percent of dropbacks** in New England, with a receiver room where two players have cracked a hundred yards, and a run game averaging 2.1 yards a carry in its last outing. That's not developing Mendoza. **That's damaging him** — and I made this exact argument last week: fix the protection first, then have the quarterback conversation.

And there's the locker room, which matters more than people admit. We spent this entire week listening to Cousins mentor the rookie from the sideline, defend his head coach at the podium, praise Jeanty's attitude and say "they point the thumb, not the finger." **You don't trade the man holding a 3-2 team together for a Saturday pick.**

**The scenario where I'd listen:** if Las Vegas loses to Buffalo and the Rams, falls to 3-4, and the season genuinely dies — and somebody offers a second-round pick because their starter went down in November. That's a different conversation. A fourth is not.

His 12 touchdown passes prove his value. And his experience stays important even when Mendoza eventually takes over, because the best thing that can happen to that rookie is another year of watching a professional do the job correctly.

## WHY A CHEAP VETERAN RECEIVER HELPS MORE THAN YOU THINK

Before we close, let me make the football case for why a Godwin-type addition matters more than his stat line suggests — because this connects to everything we've broken down for a month.

We've established that the Raiders have faced the second-most stacked boxes in football. We've established that defenses do that because they aren't afraid of the players on the field. And on Wednesday, Klint Kubiak gave us the counter: slants and rub concepts against man coverage.

**Here's the thing about rub routes and slants: they require receivers who are precise, physical and fearless over the middle.** Not fast. Not tall. Precise. A rub concept dies if one receiver runs his crossing route a yard too deep. A slant dies if the receiver drifts instead of driving flat.

That is exactly the profile of a 1,000-yard veteran who has run those routes for a decade — and exactly what you cannot ask of a rookie who's been on the roster three weeks.

So the cheap signing isn't a luxury. **It's the personnel that makes the scheme fix possible.** You can draw up all the mesh concepts you want; somebody has to run them at the right depth on the first possession against Buffalo.

And the same logic applies to Jennings, which is why Davenport's point about the Shanahan-style West Coast elements matters. Those offenses live on timing and contact-balance catches in traffic. Both of these men have made a career out of that specific skill.

## THE OTHER HALF OF SUNDAY: STOPPING JOSH ALLEN

One more thing, because the receiver conversation isn't the only challenge on this team's desk.

Maxx Crosby has been discussing the other one: **stopping Josh Allen.**

And we know the diagnosis from Kubiak's own podium answer — Allen is an elite athlete and an elite thrower with great instincts for when to run, and between him and James Cook, Buffalo has two players who can hurt you with their legs.

Raider Nation, that's why I keep coming back to the same point about this deadline. **A receiver trade doesn't stop Josh Allen.** What stops Josh Allen is keeping him off the field — first downs, sustained drives, a run game that works, and a defense that gets off the field on third down.

Which is exactly why Kubiak said the key to this game is "not being one-dimensional," and exactly why a cheap receiver who can run a slant on third-and-six is worth more to this defense than it sounds. **The best thing the offense can do for Rob Leonard's unit is stay on the field.**

## THE WHOLE PICTURE

So here's where this deadline actually stands for Las Vegas.

Two receivers are available at day-three prices, and this front office has personal history with both — Kubiak coached Jennings in San Francisco in 2023, and Spytek was an assistant GM in Tampa Bay while Godwin was becoming an All-Pro. **That's a competitive advantage that doesn't show up in any draft capital chart.**

Godwin is the better bet and the better value: four 1,000-yard seasons, All-Pro honors, available for a late pick, with a real injury caveat. Jennings is the cheaper dream with a red flag attached, because this team already passed on him once and he might be released anyway.

Meanwhile the quarterback is second in the league in touchdowns, which has contenders quietly watching him — and the Raiders would be foolish to answer that phone for a fourth-round pick.
