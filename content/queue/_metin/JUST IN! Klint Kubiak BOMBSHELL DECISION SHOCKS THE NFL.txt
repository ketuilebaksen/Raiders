INSIDE THE PODIUM: Kubiak Calls Masses "Our Starting Corner," Bech Goes Down, and Cousins Compares Bowers to Justin Jefferson 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, the podium after a 3-0 start sounds very different from the podium after a 3-14 season, and this week it gave us a lot to work with.

Klint Kubiak announced an injury that changes the receiver room, confirmed that a fifth-round rookie is now simply "our starting corner," said a backup running back has earned more opportunities, and delivered a line about the trenches that tells you exactly what this staff is still worried about. Then Kirk Cousins compared building chemistry with Brock Bowers to what he experienced with Justin Jefferson. And Bowers himself finally explained what he was dealing with all through camp.

So today we go quote by quote, and after each one I'll tell you what it actually means for where this team is going. Because every one of these answers is really about the future.

I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we build toward 6,000 believers. Let's get into the podium.

THE INJURY: "JACK'S GOING TO BE OUT FOR A LITTLE BIT"

Kubiak opened with the news: "Obviously Jack's going to be out for a little bit. We have not made a roster decision yet, so that'll be TBD."

Then he explained how it happened: Cousins was throwing the ball away, and a defender hit Jack Bech in the forearm. In his words, "he got blowed up," and he added that they were lucky it wasn't a concussion, before saying, "but he'll be back. We'll get him back."

Here's what that means. Bech was the power slot in this offense, the receiver who ran nearly 70 percent of his routes from inside and caught that opening-drive touchdown against Miami. Losing him thins a receiver room that was already the least-invested part of this roster.

But notice the phrase "we have not made a roster decision yet." That's a move coming. And remember who's sitting right there: Cody White, elevated from the practice squad, with three touchdowns in two weeks. If Bech lands on injured reserve, that's the spot that finally makes White a permanent Raider. It also makes the deadline conversation louder, whether that's Jerry Jeudy at a conditional fourth-round pick or somebody else.

"WE EXPECT STOKES TO BE BACK THIS WEEK"

On the injury front, two more updates. Jackson Powers-Johnson is still being evaluated with a soreness issue, and, in Kubiak's words, "we expect Stokes to be back this week."

That's the single most important piece of medical news for Sunday. Trayden Stokes is the center fielder on the back end, the rookie safety whose range and communication were badly missed in New Orleans. And we all watched what happened without him: Tyler Shough threw four touchdown passes and every single one went to a tight end.

Now Kansas City comes to Allegiant. If you want one reason to feel better about that matchup than you did on Sunday night, it's that sentence from the head coach.

Kubiak also confirmed he anticipates Aidan O'Connell being back with the team at some point this week, after the passing of his father. Football second on that one. Welcome back, Aidan.

THE LINE THAT MADE MY WEEK: "HE'S OUR STARTING CORNER"

Somebody asked how valuable it was for Hezekiah Masses to spend Sunday matched up against Chris Olave, one of the best receivers in the NFC.

Kubiak's answer was seven words that changed the way I look at this defense: "He's our starting corner, so he's going to be getting their best every week."

Think about the context. Darien Porter, the corner Masses replaced, is healthy again and practicing. The conventional move was to slide the veteran back in. Instead the head coach stood at a microphone and called a fifth-round rookie the starting corner, with no qualifiers.

That's a message to the whole locker room: produce here and the job is yours, whatever round you came from. And it means the Raiders are developing a starting cornerback the hardest possible way, by handing him the other team's best receiver every Sunday. Some weeks that will hurt. Long-term, that's how you build a secondary.

"MIKE DEFINITELY EARNED MORE OPPORTUNITIES"

On Mike Washington Jr. and that 36-yard touchdown run, Kubiak said he "made a really big impact on the game" and "definitely earned more opportunities."

That's a coach telling you the backfield is about to be a rotation, and it's exactly what we argued for on this channel. Ashton Jeanty is carrying it around 19 or 20 times a game with just over three yards a carry and no rushing touchdown yet. Washington is breaking the long ones. Giving Jeanty 15 or 16 carries and Washington eight to 10 keeps both fresh, adds explosive plays, and preserves Jeanty for the passing game and pass protection where he's genuinely valuable.

And the timing matters for the future. This team has Kansas City, then a brutal stretch with New England, Buffalo and the Rams. Fresh legs in October are wins in December.

"WE DON'T WIN THE GAME WITHOUT THOSE FOUR TURNOVERS"

Asked about a defense that leads the league in takeaways, Kubiak didn't dress it up: "We don't win the game without those four turnovers in the fourth quarter. That was impressive, how they kept getting the ball back for us on offense, and it just really changed the whole game. Protecting the ball and taking the ball away is a big part of winning and losing."

Then the part I loved most: "Three games into it, long season to go. Every week's a new challenge, and we're starting from scratch now."

Starting from scratch. That's a head coach refusing to let a 3-0 team read its own headlines. He backed it up by describing the locker room as a mature, goal-oriented group with strong leadership that is "not satisfied with what's been done for three games," and said the only way to stay successful is to get better every day and have a great Wednesday, Thursday and Friday practice. For a franchise that spent years searching for an identity, that answer is the identity. Not swagger. Just Wednesday, Thursday, Friday.

THE SEATTLE PIPELINE: CODY WHITE AND DARIQ YOUNG

Kubiak was asked how much he expected from Cody White and Dariq Young when he brought them over from Seattle, since both have now contributed in the passing game and on special teams, with special teams tackles on Sunday.

His answer: he had experience with those guys and knew they could do both jobs well, and that familiarity was the reason for bringing them here. Then he went further: "they've surpassed expectations with how hard they're playing and how great of teammates they are, and we count on them to do that every week."

This is the pattern I keep pointing out, and it matters for the future of this roster. Kubiak brings players he already knows, trusts them immediately, and uses them where their specific skills matter. White spent time with him in Seattle and has three touchdowns in two weeks. Jaxon Smith-Njigba took a leap with that Seattle staff. It's also the reason a Jeudy trade would make sense, because Kubiak coached him in Denver in 2022. When a head coach finds production in players other teams treat as depth, the cap sheet stops being a ceiling.

"HE BRINGS THE BEST OUT OF OTHER PEOPLE"

On Nakobe Dean, Kubiak said: "He's a leader. It's why we signed him, to be a leader, to help us win. And the great thing about him is that he doesn't do it alone. He brings the best out of other people. He's a great communicator. He's making us a better team."

That's the sentence that explains why this defense plays faster than its talent level. Dean wears the green dot, gets everybody lined up, and the tape shows players reacting instead of hesitating. Pair that with a former Butkus Award winner's instincts and you get a man leading the team in tackles almost every week and forcing fourth-quarter fumbles in back-to-back games.

THE CROSBY ANSWER THAT SHOWS REAL MATURITY

Somebody asked how well Maxx Crosby has taken to sitting out snaps during a game so the defense is fresher late. Kubiak's answer: he knows it's about winning and about being available for the whole season, and "Max is about winning, so he understands that."

Appreciate how big that is. Crosby played 935 snaps on a torn meniscus last season because this team had no other choice. Now he's being managed, he's accepting it, and the payoff showed up in the fourth quarter in New Orleans when a fresh front took the football away four times. That's a franchise finally treating its best player like a long-term asset instead of an emergency generator.

THE WARNING: "WE GOT TO CONTINUE TO KEEP GETTING BETTER"

Asked about the Saints' defensive line and the struggles in the run game, Kubiak called New Orleans a really well-coached team that played hard, and then said the run game "is something we got to continue to keep getting better at."

Asked how much credit the two lines deserve, he said it plainly: to be a good team, you have to be really good in the trenches. He credited Malcolm Koonce for a huge turnover and a huge third-down stop, and the offensive line for protection and for finding lanes.

There's the season in one answer. The head coach knows where the ceiling is, and it's up front. Three yards a carry won't survive the schedule ahead, which is why I still believe the interior offensive line has the strongest claim on any deadline move. On Jeanty getting both ankles taped mid-game, Kubiak kept it simple: everybody's sore, nobody's 100 percent, that's the NFL, and Jeanty has played through it well.

COUSINS: "RESILIENT, PLAYING THROUGH THE SMOKE"

Now the quarterback, and his opening summary was excellent.

He called it a great win on the road in a tough environment against a good football team that made it challenging all day, and said, "I thought we were resilient just playing through the smoke, which inevitably is going to happen in this league."

Playing through the smoke. That's the phrase of the week. And listen to what he praised: the operation in the noise, handling the play clock, the motions, the play calls, guys listening and executing. That's the least glamorous compliment a quarterback can give, and it's the one that separates road wins from road losses.

He also credited the defense for short fields in the second half and said they capitalized on most of them, called Washington's run huge, and then immediately went to the critique: "got to be a little bit better in the red zone early in the game."

And the closing line was perfect: "They don't get any easier from here. We just got to keep building and try to go 1-0 each week."

THE JEFFERSON COMPARISON

And then he gave us the quote of the week.

Asked whether the chemistry he built with Bowers in training camp carried over, Cousins said they tried to build a foundation through OTAs and camp, but that there's nothing like real game experience, and that the chemistry is still building rather than something already achieved.

Then this: "I had this with Justin Jefferson where early on you don't realize just how good he was as a player, and then you play with him longer and you start to realize, oh, okay, practice didn't really show just how good he is."

Sit with that. Cousins is telling you Bowers belongs in the category of player whose greatness you only fully grasp after real games together, and that more games with Brock will keep building the rapport. The man who threw passes to Justin Jefferson just told you the ceiling here is higher than ten catches for 116 yards and a touchdown.

He also said something bigger: it's a privilege to be part of building something that can outlast him, so that someday he's at home watching the next generation knowing he helped build this. That's not a rental quarterback.

BOWERS: "THOUGHT IT WAS NOTHING"

Finally, the man himself, who explained the whole saga for the first time.

He said it was tough dealing with the knee during camp, that he thought it was nothing and expected it to go away by the start of the season, but eventually, "just had to get it done and get it right before the long stretch of the season."

That's the most important sentence of the entire week, and it validates every bit of patience this staff showed. The procedure wasn't a setback. It was an investment in having him for December and January, and it's exactly the opposite of what happened with that same knee last season, when he tried to play through it and it lingered for months.

On Cousins, he said it helps a lot having an experienced guy back there who keeps everyone calm, cool and collected and gets them in the right play. On the huddle message while trailing by two scores: have faith in what you're doing, accept that there are ups and downs, stay consistent with what you worked on all week, and good things happen.

And asked about the key to his own success, he wouldn't take it, pointing at the defense getting the ball back, at Cousins throwing him a great ball, at the line protecting. "It's a whole team game." Ten catches in his first game back, and he spent the interview handing out credit. That's your best player, at 23 years old.

WHAT IT ALL ADDS UP TO

Put the podium together and the direction is clear. The staff is developing a young starting corner on purpose. The backfield is about to become a rotation. The best defensive player is being managed for the long haul. The head coach's stated ceiling is the trenches, which tells you where the front office will look before November 10th. The receiver room needs a body, and a roster decision is coming. And the quarterback thinks his chemistry with a generational tight end is still in its early stages.

All of that, at 3-0, with Kansas City coming to Allegiant.
