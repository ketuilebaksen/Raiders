## FIRST, WHY I'M EVEN SAYING THE NAME

Let's start with the problem, because the solution only makes sense if you understand the disease.

**The Las Vegas Raiders face the second-most stacked boxes in the National Football League.**

I have said that sentence on this channel more times than I can count, and I'm going to keep saying it until somebody in that building fixes it. Every single week, defenses walk an extra body down into the box, dare this offense to throw it over their heads, and then tackle Ashton Jeanty two yards past the line of scrimmage.

**And it's working. Look at the numbers.**

Jeanty has been under 3.0 yards per carry in four of six games. Against Buffalo the Raiders ran it **20 times for 65 yards** — 3.2 a crack. **Two-for-five in the red zone.** A fourth-and-one at the Buffalo 13 that turned into a false start and a field goal, in a game they lost by three.

Raider Nation, that is not a running back problem. **Ashton Jeanty is the sixth overall pick and he runs like his hair's on fire.** That's not a scheme problem either — we went through the film and the zone-gap split is close to even, and every starter on that line grades better in zone.

**That's a MATH problem.** Eleven defenders, and six or seven of them are standing within five yards of the ball, because nobody out wide is making them pay for it.

## SO WHAT DOES TYREEK HILL ACTUALLY FIX?

Here's the part I want you to really sit with.

**Tyreek Hill is the single most box-unstacking human being in professional football.** Not the best receiver in football — that's a different argument. **The most terrifying.** There is no safety alive who plays a single coverage with Tyreek Hill on the field, because the first time you do it, you lose the game on one play.

So what happens the moment he lines up in Las Vegas?

**That extra defender in the box walks back out.** He has to. **You cannot play Cover 1 with an eighth man in the box against Tyreek Hill, Brock Bowers and Tre Tucker.** The safety goes deep, the corner gives cushion, the linebacker widens to cover the wheel — **and suddenly Ashton Jeanty is running at six defenders instead of eight.**

Raider Nation, that is the whole thesis of this channel in one sentence: **the fastest way to fix the Raiders' run game is to sign a wide receiver.**

And listen to what Kubiak himself said Wednesday when he was asked about throwing it down the field: *"Anytime you can make that part of your offense, it's going to make the whole team successful."*

## THE FERNANDO MENDOZA SHORT-YARDAGE QUESTION — I LOVED THIS

A reporter asked it carefully, and I want to repeat his framing because he deserves credit: **he explicitly said there's no quarterback controversy and he wasn't implying one.** His question was about short yardage — **have the Raiders thought about using Fernando Mendoza in those situations, specifically because of what he can do with the football in his hands?**

Raider Nation, this is a GREAT question, because the Raiders have been terrible in short yardage. Fourth-and-one at the 13. Three trips to that situation with a flag each time.

**And Kubiak's answer was the most interesting thing he said all day.**

He called it a great point. Then he explained the hesitation: *"There's something to coming out off the field cold on the most important down of the drive. That's probably maybe why that doesn't happen more."* **And then he said it: "It's definitely a valid point."**

Let me translate that, because it matters.

**The head coach of the Las Vegas Raiders just publicly admitted he's thinking about putting the number one overall pick on the field in short yardage.** He gave the honest reason it hasn't happened — a quarterback running in cold on third-and-one is being asked to execute the highest-pressure snap of a drive without a single warm-up rep — **but he did not close the door. He opened it.**

**My take: I'd do it.** The downside of a cold quarterback on one sneak is small. The downside of going two-for-five in the red zone every single week is **losing football games by three points.** And Mendoza is a big, athletic kid who moves — this is the one role where you get his legs involved without touching Cousins' job.

Watch that space. If the Raiders get stuffed on another fourth-and-one, I think you see number one under center before November's out.

## THE THIRD QUARTER: HE CALLED IT MENTAL, AND THERE'S A PRACTICE PLAN

We have hammered this all week, so here's the update straight from the source.

Kubiak was asked how you coach a mental problem coming out of halftime. **His answer: it's a lot of energy, too.** He said the coaching staff has to make sure the players come out of that second half with more energy, that it's been discussed at length, and that **it's a point of emphasis.** Starting the game matters — **and starting the third quarter matters just as much.** He flatly called it an area the team has to improve.

Then he got asked whether they actually simulate it in practice, and he said **yes — they have a plan in practice for it, and they have to get better there.**

Raider Nation, I want to be fair here. **That's the right answer.** He didn't deflect, he didn't blame the players, he named it and said there's a drill for it.

**But I'm going to hold him to it, because the number is brutal:** this team has been **outscored by 26 points in the third quarter — the worst margin in the National Football League.** Against Buffalo they came out of the half with a three-and-out, then threw an interception to linebacker Dorian Williams on a ball meant for Michael Mayer, and Buffalo turned it into a touchdown.

**Energy is a real thing in football. But at some point, "we come out flat" stops being an energy problem and becomes a halftime process problem.** Sunday against the Rams, the very first play of the third quarter is the most important play of the Raiders' season. I'll be watching it like a hawk.

## DONT'E THORNTON JR. — AND THIS IS THE REAL ROSTER NEWS

Here's the one that got my attention.

Asked about his expectations for Dont'e Thornton Jr. this week, Kubiak said **he's going out there to practice to show them that he's ready.** He's been building up — **"a really good first week, a good second week"** — and **"he's got his legs underneath him."** Then the kicker: **"He's got to go show us something."**

And when a reporter asked straight out whether there's a chance he could be activated, Kubiak said: **"Yeah. Yes."**

Raider Nation, **we have been tracking this activation for weeks.** We've watched him get held out when we wanted him up. And now the head coach is saying out loud that activation is on the table.

**And look at the timing.** I just spent ten minutes explaining that this offense needs somebody who makes defenses back off the box — and the Raiders have a big, fast, vertical receiver in the building who's getting his legs back **right now.**

**Is Dont'e Thornton Tyreek Hill? No. Nobody is.** But he is a free swing at the exact problem, he costs nothing, and he's already in the system. **If he shows them something this week, he should be out there Sunday.**

## JACKSON POWERS-JOHNSON, AND HOW A PRO HANDLES A DEMOTION

This one tells you about the locker room.

Kubiak was asked how Jackson Powers-Johnson has handled stepping down from his role and still competing. We covered this on Tuesday — **JPJ took zero offensive snaps against Buffalo while Caleb Rogers played every single one.** That's a hard thing for a young player who was drafted to be a starter.

Kubiak's answer: **guys are obviously disappointed, but you keep competing, you're going to get a chance again, and you make sure you're ready when it comes. "That's what a professional does."** And he pointed to a veteran in that room who's already lived it as the example to follow.

Raider Nation, **that's the sentence that decides seasons.** A 3-3 team with three straight losses either fractures into guys protecting their own stats, or it hardens into a group that keeps grinding. Kubiak's answer tells you which direction this room is going — **and he said something similar about the rookies: you have a good game, then you get beat, and you just keep competing. "It's the most competitive business I know. That's what makes it the most rewarding."**

And on the offensive line specifically: when asked if the group is set for this week or if there's still open competition, he said **"We're going to keep them competing."** So nothing is locked. The competition is the point.

## THE PASS RUSH PROBLEM NOBODY WAS TALKING ABOUT

Now this question was genuinely sharp, and I want to give the reporter his flowers.

He pointed out that the Raiders are **still generating pressures and sacks — but their TIME to pressure is long.** It's taking this defense a while to get home.

**Kubiak's answer was refreshingly honest:** the deeper you get into a season, **teams know what you do.** The more reps on tape, the more time an opponent has to study your scheme. So it's an evolving scheme — **it's about getting the players home, and then when you do get home, making sure you get the ball out. "That's that cat-and-mouse game of the NFL."**

Raider Nation, **connect this to the stat we talked about yesterday**, because they are the same story: **ten takeaways in the first three games, one in the three losses since.**

**That's the cat-and-mouse game being lost.** Early in the year, defenses are a mystery and quarterbacks hold the ball. By Week 6, the league has your tape — **so pressure arrives half a second late, the quarterback gets the ball out, and the takeaways dry up.**

**The fix isn't effort. It's disguise.** New looks, different pressure angles, simulated pressures, somebody unexpected coming off the edge. Kubiak said the scheme is evolving. **Against a Rams offense this good, it had better evolve by Sunday.**

## WHY IS KIRK COUSINS PLAYING THIS WELL AT 38?

Somebody asked the question we've all been asking, and Kubiak's answer was pure coach — and I mean that as a compliment.

**His answer: go back to April.**

He said if you look at any player playing well right now, look at what their April looked like. **They had a good April. They had a good training camp. You just keep stacking days** — and whether it's the quarterback, a safety or a guard, **stacking days builds confidence.**

And when he was asked what he saw in Cousins' film at the end of last season in Atlanta that he projected into this plan, Kubiak gave a one-liner with a laugh: **"Four and oh."**

That's it. **Four and oh.** The man finished last season winning football games, and this coaching staff believed what they saw.

Raider Nation, there's a lesson in there for everybody screaming about a quarterback change. **The 38-year-old is top of the league in multiple categories because he did the boring work in April when nobody was filming.** And that's also, by the way, exactly the standard this staff is holding Mendoza to.

## WHICH BRINGS US TO FERNANDO MENDOZA — AND THIS STORY IS GREAT

Asked how he feels about where Mendoza is and how he's developing behind the scenes, Kubiak said he's **very, very pleased.** Then the honest caveat: **none of us really know until he goes in there and plays.** But **he's doing everything they ask — and more. "That's just his character."**

A reporter pushed: what does "more than asked" actually mean?

**And Kubiak's answer was my favorite moment of the whole press conference.**

He said it's as simple as him walking down the hall to go get in the steam room when everybody's gone — **and Mendoza is out on the field by himself.** He added, to his credit, that **Mendoza isn't the only guy doing that** — but he's putting in the extra time.

Raider Nation, **that's the number one overall pick, alone on an empty practice field, after the building has cleared out.**

You cannot coach that. You can't draft that. **And that's exactly the "stack days in April" thing Kubiak was talking about with Cousins — the rookie is already doing it.** Whenever that day comes, the man is going to be ready.

## AND CREDIT TO THE ROOKIE CLASS — ALL SIXTEEN OF THEM

A reporter noted something I hadn't fully clocked: between the active roster and the practice squad, **the Raiders have roughly SIXTEEN rookies in this building**, and the class is drawing real praise for its contributions.

Kubiak agreed: **a lot of key contributors, and guys they're still counting on.** He said getting your young guys to play early is a big part of being successful in the NFL — **and he gave direct credit to John Spytek for the young men he's brought in, "not only as good players, but guys that are team-oriented individuals, guys that are about the Raider way."**

Then the realism: **young guys have setbacks too.** Good game, then you get beat, then you keep competing.

**Sixteen rookies, playing meaningful snaps, at 3-3 against the hardest part of the schedule.** Raider Nation, whatever you think about this season's record — **that is what a foundation looks like.**

## NOW THE RAMS, AND THIS IS A SCARY WEEK

Here's the part that should straighten your spine.

Asked about Los Angeles' heavy use of **13 personnel** — that's one back and THREE tight ends — and the versatility of their tight end group, Kubiak didn't hedge even slightly:

**"They're probably the best offense in the NFL."**

Very well coached. They utilize their tight ends. **"They are a very impressive group."** He said it's going to be a really important week of preparation, called them a very good offense and a very good operation, and said **they have a lot of respect for them.**

Raider Nation, let me tell you what 13 personnel actually does to a defense, because this is the whole chess match Sunday.

**Three tight ends on the field screams RUN.** So your instinct is to answer with your big defense — bring in extra linebackers, get heavy, stack the box. **And the second you do that, those "tight ends" split out and run routes, and now you've got a linebacker trying to cover a 250-pound athlete in space.**

**It's a personnel trap.** Stay light and they run it down your throat. Go heavy and they throw it over your head. **And it is maddening to defend, especially for a defense that just admitted its time-to-pressure is slow.**

**So here's the Raiders' nightmare scenario:** a slow pass rush against the best offense in football, with a defense forced into guessing games on personnel, while the Raiders' own offense goes two-for-five in the red zone and gets outscored in the third quarter again.

**And here's the dream scenario:** Hemingway clears protocol and locks the middle, the pressure package evolves, Thornton gets activated and stretches the field, Mendoza takes a short-yardage snap or two, and this offense finally finishes drives.

**That's the game. Right there.**

## THE DEEP BALL, AND HOW IT TIES BACK TO EVERYTHING

Last thing from the presser, and it brings us full circle.

Asked whether there are more opportunities to throw it downfield — the Rams had success doing it and missed a few other shots — Kubiak said **"I do."** Anytime you can make that part of your offense, it makes the whole team successful.

**But then he protected his quarterback, and he was right to.** He said he really loves Cousins' decision-making, and that this isn't something where you just **"go throw it up in the air and hope something good happens."** You have to get the right look, with the right guy running the route. **"You've got to read the defense. That's the most important thing."**

Raider Nation, **do you hear the loop?**

The Raiders need deep shots to unstack the box. **To take deep shots you need the right look, the right route and the right guy.** The quarterback is good enough. The reads are good enough. **What's missing is the guy** — the receiver who makes that safety choose.

**That is why I spent the first half of this video arguing for Tyreek Hill.** Not because I want fireworks. Because **the head coach, the quarterback, the numbers and the film are all pointing at the same empty seat.**

Maybe it's Hill. Maybe it's a smaller deadline move. Maybe it's Dont'e Thornton Jr. earning it this week. **But somebody has to fill that seat before November 10th, or we spend the rest of this season watching Ashton Jeanty run into eight defenders.**
