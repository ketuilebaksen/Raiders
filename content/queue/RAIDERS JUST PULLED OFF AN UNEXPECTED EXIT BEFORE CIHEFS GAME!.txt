WINNER: MALCOLM KOONCE

Koonce may have been the best defensive player on the field in New Orleans, and that includes everybody wearing black and gold.

The strip sack that flipped the game. A tackle for loss. Three quarterback hits. And pressure on several other snaps that never showed up in the box score.

Here's the context that makes it matter. The Saints were always going to devote protection to Maxx Crosby. That's what every opponent does. And the entire theory of a defense built this way is that when they slide help toward 98, somebody else has to make them pay for it.

Koonce made them pay. That's exactly the role, and it's the role we argued he should have three weeks ago when we broke down how he was being misused on early downs.

WINNER: BROCK BOWERS

Ten catches, 116 yards, a touchdown, and a reminder of why he's one of the most dangerous tight ends in football in his first game back from a knee procedure.

The play I keep rewinding is the third-down back-shoulder throw from Kirk Cousins. That's not a scheme completion. That's two players trusting each other: a quarterback putting the ball where only his guy can get it, and a tight end knowing it's coming.

He wasn't flawless, and he'd tell you that himself. But the connection between Cousins and Bowers is quickly becoming the single biggest strength of this offense, and remember what Cousins said at the podium: the chemistry is still building.

WINNER: HEZEKIAH MASSES

Here's a compliment that sounds strange until you think about it: Masses was barely mentioned during the broadcast, and for a young cornerback, that's wonderful.

He wasn't getting attacked relentlessly, which tells you New Orleans looked at the matchup and decided to go elsewhere. He played tight coverage. And he still came away with another interception.

That's three picks in three games for a fifth-round rookie. Kubiak called him "our starting corner" at the podium this week with Darien Porter healthy, and performances like this are exactly why.

WINNER: CODY WHITE

Elevated from the practice squad again. In the end zone again.

Three receiving touchdowns in three weeks, with scores in each of the last two games, including two against the Chargers. Every single time this staff gives him an opportunity, he produces, and at this point he's made himself impossible to ignore.

Hold that thought, because it's about to become the most important roster question of the week.

WINNER: JEREMY CHINN

The game-winning sack late in the fourth quarter came from Chinn, and it was one of the main reasons this team closed the game out instead of surviving it.

What I liked just as much was how Rob Leonard used him. He moved Chinn into the box, created confusion for the Saints, and turned a safety into a pressure player at exactly the moment New Orleans needed to protect the quarterback. Chinn finished with eight tackles, the second-highest total on the team.

That's coaching and talent arriving at the same time, and it's becoming this defense's signature.

WINNER: MIKE WASHINGTON JR.

Ashton Jeanty couldn't get going on the ground, so a rookie provided the spark instead.

And look at the timing of his big touchdown run. It came immediately after New Orleans had scored, at the exact moment the building was at its loudest and the momentum was about to get away. That's the kind of answer that keeps a road team alive.

Kubiak said this week that Washington "definitely earned more opportunities." That's a rotation forming in real time.

WINNER: MICHAEL MAYER

There's been a lot of debate about Mayer and whether he's more than an elite blocking tight end. Against New Orleans he answered it: three catches for 32 yards and a touchdown.

That's the whole point of the three-year, $45 million extension this front office handed him before the season. It wasn't just to pay a blocker. It was to put two tight ends on the field at the same time and force defenses to choose. On Sunday, with Bowers back, we finally saw what that looks like.

WINNER: KIRK COUSINS

Three and oh as the Raiders' starter, with three touchdown passes in each of his first three games. Nine touchdown passes through three weeks leads the league.

What matters most isn't the raw number, though. It's how he's running this offense: efficient, accurate, careful with the football, and lethal on third down to Bowers.

I'll be honest about the critique too, because it's real. He isn't pushing the ball downfield as much as some of you want, and there will be games where that matters. But right now the results are there, he isn't turning the ball over, and he's winning. That's the job.

WINNER: NAKOBE DEAN

The forced fumble on the sideline late in the game essentially ended it, but his afternoon was about so much more than one play.

He was excellent against the run and finished with a team-high 12 tackles. He wears the green dot and gets everybody lined up. And Kubiak's description of him this week was perfect: they signed him to be a leader, and the great thing is that he doesn't do it alone, he brings the best out of other people.

That's exactly what you need from your linebacker.

NOW THE LOSERS, BECAUSE 3-0 DOESN'T MEAN PERFECT

Here's where this channel earns your trust, because these mistakes almost decided the game.

Dylan Laube. His special teams fumble was a major error, and New Orleans turned it directly into points and the lead. That cannot happen, especially from a player whose role on this roster is built largely around special teams. Against a better opponent, a mistake like that decides the afternoon. And Kansas City is a better opponent.

Isaiah Pola-Mao. With Trayden Stokes out in the concussion protocol, he had to play more coverage than this staff probably wanted. The Saints' first touchdown came after he missed his assignment and ended up roughly ten yards off at the goal line. It wasn't a strong day overall, and it showed exactly how much this defense missed Stokes. The good news, as we covered this week: Stokes has cleared the protocol and will play against the Chiefs.

Kolton Miller. He gave up a sack on a play that nearly became a game-changing fumble by Cousins, and Connor Hayward being in the right place is the only reason it didn't get worse. He allowed several pressures and looked shaky for long stretches.

Spencer Burford. He could have been flagged for holding multiple times, and his play was one of the reasons this team couldn't run the football consistently on the left side.

Put those last two together and you have the honest answer to why Jeanty is averaging barely three yards a carry. There's plenty for that offensive line to clean up, and the head coach said it himself this week: to be a good team, you have to be really good in the trenches.

THE BAD NEWS: JACK BECH FRACTURED HIS FOREARM

And then, after the victory, the news that actually changes this week's game plan. Jack Bech fractured his forearm.

That's a significant loss, and I want to explain why, because Bech's value doesn't live in the stat sheet. He's a strong run blocker from the slot. He's effective on intermediate routes. And most importantly, he takes pressure off Brock Bowers, Jalen Nailor and Tre Tucker by occupying a defender who would otherwise be helping somewhere else.

Losing a complementary piece hurts more than people think, and it hurts most against a defense with a week to design coverage for your remaining weapons.

The Raiders responded quickly. On Monday they released Ronnie Bell from the practice squad and signed wide receiver Tyler Scott. Scott was a fourth-round pick by the Chicago Bears in the 2023 draft. He's played 28 career games with four starts in 2023, and across that time he has 18 catches on 33 targets for 173 yards, no touchdowns, and ten first downs. He didn't play in 2025.

So let's set expectations honestly: he's practice squad depth, and nobody should expect him to replace what Bech brings. He's a body who can contribute on special teams and potentially be elevated once he learns the offense. He also has a Seattle connection that overlaps with Kubiak's past, which, as we've seen three times already this season, is how this head coach likes to shop.

THE REAL STORY: CODY WHITE'S MOMENT

But the bigger storyline is the one standing right there with three touchdowns in three weeks.

Bech's injury gives Cody White an even bigger opportunity. The Raiders can either bring him onto the 53-man roster full time while Bech is out, or keep elevating him from the practice squad week to week. Kubiak said at the podium that the roster decision is still to be determined.

My view hasn't changed: sign him. A receiver who scores every time you give him a chance, who already knows this head coach from Seattle, and who contributes on special teams, is exactly the kind of player you don't risk losing on a technicality. And with Bech out, the snaps are there.

WHAT BECH'S ABSENCE ACTUALLY CHANGES ON THE FIELD

Let me get specific about the football, because "complementary piece" is a phrase that hides a lot of detail.

Against Miami, Bech ran about 69 percent of his routes from the slot and caught the opening-drive touchdown. That's a power slot: a bigger body who can win an in-breaking route against a safety and then turn around and block on the edge in two-back personnel. It's one of the reasons this offense averaged 6.4 yards a play out of heavy formations in Week One.

Without him, two things happen. First, somebody else plays the slot, and the most likely candidates are Tre Tucker moving inside or Bowers aligning there more often. Both of those are fine answers, but both take a player away from where he's most dangerous. Second, the two-tight-end package becomes even more central, which means Michael Mayer's workload goes up in a week when he also has to block one of the better fronts in football.

So the plan almost writes itself: more Bowers and Mayer on the field together, more Cody White on the outside, and Jeanty and Washington staying involved as receivers out of the backfield. If Kansas City wants to take the middle of the field away from Bowers, the answers have to come from the names nobody expected in August.

THE PATTERN THAT DEFINES THIS TEAM

Step back and look at what all of these names have in common, because it's the real story of this 3-0 start.

Koonce makes plays when offenses focus on Crosby. Masses produces interceptions as a fifth-round rookie. Cody White keeps finding the end zone as a practice squad elevation. Washington provides a spark when Jeanty can't find room. Mayer proves he's more than a blocker. Chinn makes the game-winning play from a position that isn't supposed to rush the passer. Dean is everywhere. And Cousins gives this offense the one thing it has been missing for years: consistency.

That's not luck. That's a roster where opportunity keeps finding players who are ready for it, and a coaching staff that keeps putting people in spots where they can win.

AND NOW, THE MEASURING STICK

Week Four brings Kansas City, and this isn't just another game. It's for sole possession of first place in the AFC West.

So here's the checklist. If Cody White keeps making plays, if Bowers stays uncoverable, if Cousins keeps protecting the football, if Koonce keeps making offenses pay for respecting Crosby, if that offensive line cleans up the left side, and if the defense fixes the middle of the field with Stokes back, then this team gets to prove that three weeks were more than three weeks.

The Raiders do it without Jack Bech, which is a real challenge. But every one of these games has been a challenge, and they're 3-0.
