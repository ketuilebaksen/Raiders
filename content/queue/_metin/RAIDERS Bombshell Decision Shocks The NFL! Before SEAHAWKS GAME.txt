## FIRST: WHAT ROB LEONARD ACTUALLY SAID

Raider Nation, here's what started the whole argument.

**Rob Leonard was talking about why his defense keeps having those inconsistent stretches, and he brought up FATIGUE.**

**He said MISTAKES BECOME MORE COMMON WHEN PLAYERS STAY ON THE FIELD TOO LONG. His exact phrase was "FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL."**

**And I get why fans immediately looked toward the OFFENSE. We've seen stalled drives. We've seen quick possessions.**

**BUT DID HE ACTUALLY BLAME THE OFFENSE? NO.**

**And I'm not letting him off the hook either. I just want to look at the FOOTBALL before deciding who's blaming whom.**

Raider Nation, **let me give the offense its defense first, because it deserves one.**

**The offense has absolutely had its problems. Those quick possessions don't help anybody. And the 49ers game was rough — Kirk Cousins threw interceptions, teammates lost fumbles, and FIVE SECOND-HALF TURNOVERS put this team in impossible spots.**

**But I don't think it's fair to act like this offense is INCAPABLE of putting together scoring drives. We've seen enough to compete when the defense makes stops. This offense has averaged 27.6 points a game. SIXTH in the league in scoring.**

**And that Jets game is the perfect reminder: WE WON 38-33, and DESPITE ALLOWING 439 YARDS, the defense made CRUCIAL LATE STOPS.**

**So both units can do it. Just never on the same afternoon.**

## BUT HERE IS THE NUMBER THAT ENDS THE ARGUMENT

Raider Nation, this is the stat that settles whose fault the fatigue is, and it is absolutely damning.

**What bothers me is how often our defense CREATES A DECENT SITUATION and then COMPLETELY WASTES IT.**

**THIRD AND FIVE. THIRD AND SIX. Raider Nation, THAT'S when you're supposed to pressure the quarterback and end the drive.**

**Instead, the opponent converts, the drive continues — and suddenly everyone's talking about how exhausted the defense is.**

**SAN FRANCISCO CONVERTED EIGHT OF THIRTEEN THIRD DOWNS AGAINST US.**

**EIGHT OF THIRTEEN. Sixty-two percent.**

**Raider Nation, SOMETIMES YOU'RE EXHAUSTED BECAUSE YOU COULDN'T FINISH THE JOB. You had the chance to get off the field and you didn't take it.**

**That's why I DON'T hear Rob Leonard's comments as a shot at the offense. I hear a coach DESCRIBING THE RESULT OF A PROBLEM HIS OWN UNIT NEEDS TO FIX.**

**And honestly? He said as much himself. "It's ALL ABOUT US. We've got to EXECUTE BETTER, ESPECIALLY TO START THE GAME. And that's REALLY ALL ABOUT US."** **And he admitted the weight: "There's a WEIGHT YOU FEEL THAT YOU CARRY, especially WHEN THE OFFENSE IS SCORING, that WE NEED TO DO OUR PART. That DOESN'T FEEL GREAT."**

**Raider Nation, that's not finger-pointing. That's thumb-pointing. Same as his head coach.**

**Eight of thirteen third downs. San Francisco never punted. Thirty-eight minutes and eighteen seconds of possession. You don't get tired because your offense went three-and-out. You get tired because you let a 62-percent third-down conversion rate keep you on the grass.**

## AND IF YOU'VE WATCHED THIS DEFENSIVE LINE, YOU KNOW WHERE IT STARTS

Raider Nation, let's go man by man, because this is the honest inventory.

**MAXX CROSBY brings the kind of ENERGY AND DISRUPTION you want — but there's NOT ENOUGH CONSISTENCY AROUND HIM.**

**ADAM BUTLER can rush the passer — but I don't love what happens when teams CHALLENGE HIM AGAINST THE RUN.**

**TONKA HEMINGWAY has EXPLOSIVENESS — but he still has WORK TO DO WITH HIS PASS RUSH MOVES.**

**FONZ FATICASI brings more value as a RUN DEFENDER. And JJ PEGUES can help in that area too.**

**BUT RAIDER NATION — WHERE'S THE GUY YOU TRUST ON EVERY DOWN?**

**That's the question. That's the whole thing. There isn't one.**

**And this is exactly the complaint I've been making for weeks: when an offense spends all afternoon chipping and doubling Maxx Crosby, SOMEBODY ELSE gets a one-on-one on every single snap. And those one-on-ones aren't getting won.**

## AND NOW LET ME SHOW YOU EXACTLY HOW TEAMS EXPLOIT IT

Raider Nation, this is the most important tactical explanation I can give you all season. Follow me closely.

**Say an opponent RUNS THE BALL on SECOND DOWN and sets up THIRD AND SIX.**

**Our RUN-FOCUSED defensive tackles are STILL ON THE FIELD.**

**And if the offense DOESN'T CHANGE PERSONNEL, it can HURRY TO THE LINE without giving us a comfortable chance to substitute.**

**So now we've got to defend a PASSING DOWN with the WRONG COMBINATION OF PLAYERS — or risk SCRAMBLING THROUGH A LATE SUBSTITUTION.**

Raider Nation, **do you understand how devastating that is? That's not a scheme failure. That's a RULES exploit.**

**In the NFL, the defense only gets to substitute if the offense substitutes. So if Seattle keeps the same eleven men on the field and sprints to the line, our two run-stuffing defensive tackles are STUCK out there on third and six against a passing play.**

**And that's how teams exploit us when WE DON'T HAVE DEPENDABLE THREE-DOWN LINEMEN.**

**And Raider Nation, Seattle is more than capable of seeing that weakness. Mike Macdonald is one of the best defensive minds in football and Klint Kubiak just told us their building is full of "EXTREMELY INTELLIGENT PEOPLE." They've watched the tape.**

**And here's the ugly compounding effect: the men stuck on the field on third and six are the ones who just played a 12-play drive. Fatigue makes cowards of us all — AND the wrong personnel. At the same time.**

## SO DOES ROB LEONARD GET A FREE PASS? NO.

Raider Nation, let me be balanced here, because this is the honest position.

**NO, he doesn't. He's RESPONSIBLE FOR FINDING ANSWERS WITH THESE PLAYERS.**

**BUT I ALSO DON'T THINK FIRING HIM AUTOMATICALLY FIXES A TALENT PROBLEM.**

**And I've LIKED parts of his approach. Some of those blitzes DO create confusion — especially when the LINEBACKERS get involved and the offense has to figure out who's coming.**

**But I NEED THAT TO HAPPEN CONSISTENTLY.**

**I need to see this group STOP THE RUN EARLY, GET A FAVORABLE DOWN, and ACTUALLY FINISH THE POSSESSION.**

**Otherwise, Raider Nation, ALL THE CREATIVE PRESSURE IN THE WORLD DOESN'T MATTER.**

**And that's exactly what Klint Kubiak meant when he refused to fire him: "I think the WORLD WANTS SOMEONE'S HEAD ON A PLATTER every time things don't go right. But we BROUGHT PEOPLE HERE FOR A REASON." And: "I SEE IT WORK. JUST NOT FOR FOUR QUARTERS."**

**And Leonard's response to that backing was telling: "IT MEANS A LOT. NOT TOO MANY PEOPLE IN THIS PROFESSION DO THINGS LIKE THAT."**

## AND I HAVE ONE MORE QUESTION I CAN'T IGNORE

Raider Nation, this one's uncomfortable but it's fair.

**ERIC STOKES has had MOMENTS. But I wonder whether the Raiders eventually need a DIFFERENT TYPE OF CORNER to fit what they're trying to do.**

**ONE REPLACEMENT WON'T FIX EVERYTHING. But guys have to FIT THE SCHEME. YOU CAN'T KEEP HIDING EVERY WEAKNESS.**

**Raider Nation, that's the key phrase: you can't keep hiding every weakness. Every snap you spend protecting one player is a snap you're not using to attack the offense.**

**So for me, the BIGGER OFFSEASON PRIORITY would be ADDING A COUPLE OF RELIABLE DEFENSIVE LINEMEN and FINDING ANOTHER STRONG FIT ON THE BACK END.**

**But Raider Nation, the offseason is not Sunday. UNTIL THEN, ROB LEONARD HAS TO MAKE THIS DEFENSE BETTER WITH WHAT HE'S GOT — STARTING AGAINST THE SEAHAWKS.**

**And to his credit, he told us the lever he'd pull: "WHAT ELEVEN DO WE WANT TO PUT OUT THERE TO START THE GAME? And then HAVE A PLAN TO GET TO A DIFFERENT ONE LATER."**

## NOW THE GOOD NEWS — TREY ZUHN III IS CLEARED

Raider Nation, here's the update that genuinely lifted my Saturday.

**TREY ZUHN III WAS DEALING WITH A CONCUSSION — BUT HE WAS A FULL PARTICIPANT IN FRIDAY'S PRACTICE AND DOESN'T CARRY A GAME DESIGNATION.**

**And if you're asking me who that emergency player might be? CONNOR HEYWARD is one name I would consider. He's PLAYED QUARTERBACK BEFORE, going back to high school, and he's FAMILIAR WITH HANDLING THE FOOTBALL IN DIFFERENT WAYS.**

**But Raider Nation, let me be crystal clear: THAT IS MY SPECULATION. NOT SOMETHING THE RAIDERS HAVE CONFIRMED. I'm guessing at a name, and you should treat it as a guess.**

**My preference is simple: PROTECT FERNANDO MENDOZA AND HAVE AIDAN O'CONNELL READY. I know Kubiak has a backup plan, BUT I'D FEEL SAFER WITH A DEDICATED THIRD QUARTERBACK.**

**And that's exactly why Kubiak's advice to his rookie this week matters so much: "WHEN you're running the football — HOW DO YOU PROTECT YOURSELF so that you can GET US TO THE NEXT DOWN? Because WE NEED YOU AVAILABLE FOR THE WHOLE SEASON. You're NOT TRYING TO SELL OUT FOR A YARD ON FIRST AND TEN."**

**Raider Nation, with two quarterbacks on the roster, that's not coaching advice. That's survival.**

**Lock has been MANAGING ELBOW AND OBLIQUE ISSUES HIMSELF, BUT HE'S CLEARED TO START — with JALEN MILROE BACKING HIM UP.**

**And remember what Klint Kubiak said about him — a man who knows him personally: "To me, DREW IS A STARTING NFL QUARTERBACK. I have SO MUCH RESPECT for Drew and HOW GOOD OF A PLAYER HE IS. He's TWO AND OH this year. And DREW IS WINNING FOOTBALL GAMES."**

**Raider Nation, 2-0. On the defending champions. With the best defense in the NFL behind him. He doesn't have to be spectacular. He just has to not lose it.**

**Make his afternoon hard. Third and seven with somebody in his face. That's the plan.**

## AND SEATTLE'S GROUND GAME JUST GOT MORE DANGEROUS

Raider Nation, this is the part that scares me most, and you already know why.

**ZACH CHARBONNET AND JAYDON PRICE HAVE BOTH RETURNED. And they've ALREADY PLAYED TOGETHER IN THE LAST TWO GAMES.**

**Raider Nation, that's IMPORTANT — because we know exactly how much trouble this team has had stopping the run.**

**One hundred fifty-plus rushing yards allowed in FIVE OF SIX WEEKS. 190 yards to San Francisco on 43 carries. Thirtieth in the league. 146 a game.**

**And IF SEATTLE CAN PICK UP STEADY YARDS EARLY, IT WON'T NEED TO ASK DREW LOCK TO CARRY THE ENTIRE OFFENSE.**

**That's the whole game right there. If they run it successfully, Drew Lock never has to make a difficult throw. And if they don't, he has to beat us by himself — and that's the version of this game we can win.**

**And PRICE CAN CONTRIBUTE AS A RECEIVER TOO. So the backfield isn't just a run threat.**

**So here's my ask of this defense: I WANT TO SEE OUR LINEBACKERS TACKLE CLEANLY. OUR INTERIOR GUYS HOLD THEIR GROUND. AND SOMEBODY MAKE A PLAY BEFORE SEATTLE GETS COMFORTABLE MOVING THE CHAINS.**

**Raider Nation, that's the same prescription we've had for two months: KEEP THE INTERIOR LINEMEN OFF OUR LINEBACKERS. Nothing has changed about the medicine.**

## BUT THERE ARE REAL OPENINGS — AND THEY ARE BIG

Raider Nation, this is why I think we have a chance on Sunday.

**SEATTLE HAS RULED OUT CHARLES CROSS, QUENTON NELSON AND LEONARD WILLIAMS — ALONG WITH SAM DARNOLD.**

**THOSE ARE SIGNIFICANT ABSENCES.**

**JOSH JONES CAN STEP IN AT LEFT TACKLE, while GREY ZABEL and ANTHONY BRADFORD remain part of that line.**

Raider Nation, **think about what that means.** **They're starting a backup quarterback behind a line missing its starting left tackle and an interior starter. That is exactly the recipe for pressure — IF you attack it correctly.**

**So IF I'M ROB LEONARD, I'M LOOKING FOR WAYS TO ATTACK THOSE ABSENCES.**

**NOT WITH RECKLESS BLITZES ON EVERY SNAP — but with DISCIPLINED PRESSURE AND BETTER RUN FITS.**

**Raider Nation, that distinction is everything. Remember the McCaffrey touchdown: we sent pressure, nobody peeled on the halfback, and Purdy found him wide open for twelve. Kubiak confirmed it: "WE DID NOT PEEL ON THE HALFBACK."**

**Reckless blitzing against a backup quarterback behind a patched-up line is how you turn a sack opportunity into a touchdown. DISCIPLINED pressure — four men winning, or five with everybody accounted for — is how you end a season.**

**I WANT TO SEE OUR DEFENSE ADJUST WHEN SEATTLE ISN'T AT FULL STRENGTH. That's the test of a coordinator. Not surviving a great offense — PUNISHING a short-handed one.**

Raider Nation, don't get comfortable. Look at this group.

**JAXON SMITH-NJIGBA IS THE GUY WHO WORRIES ME MOST. HIS ROUTE RUNNING IS OUTSTANDING AND HE CAN PUNISH A CORNER WHO GIVES HIM EVEN A LITTLE ROOM.**

**And Rob Leonard confirmed exactly how seriously they're taking him: "He's UNBELIEVABLE. He's NUMBER ONE IN THE LEAGUE in all things RECEPTIONS. YOU START WITH HIM DAY ONE. Early down, third down, red zone. And NOT JUST COVERAGE-WISE."**

**RASHID SHAHEED IS READY TO RETURN. COOPER KUPP gives that offense another EXPERIENCED TARGET. And AJ BARNER can make plays at TIGHT END.**

**Raider Nation, Rashid Shaheed is also — per our own special teams coordinator — part of why "THESE GUYS THIS WEEK ARE REAL" on special teams. He called their returner "UNBELIEVABLE."**

**And here's the bind: WE CAN'T JUST STACK THE BOX. YOU'VE GOT TO TACKLE THE BACKS WITHOUT LEAVING THE RECEIVERS RUNNING FREE.**

**AND IF OUR PASS RUSH DOESN'T GET THERE, THOSE COVERAGE ASSIGNMENTS BECOME EVEN HARDER.**

**Raider Nation, that's the exact same dilemma we had in San Francisco: bring the safety down to stop the run and Kittle runs through the vacated space. Only this time it's Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Cooper Kupp.**

**There's no free answer. The only real answer is the front four winning on their own.**

**THEY WORKED TOGETHER IN SEATTLE. And Klint Kubiak KNOWS PLENTY about how Mike Macdonald likes to STRUCTURE HIS DEFENSE.**

**He understands the PERSONNEL PACKAGES, the MOVEMENT BEFORE THE SNAP, and the way Seattle tries to DICTATE WHAT AN OFFENSE CAN DO.**

**BUT MIKE MACDONALD KNOWS HIM TOO.**

**So here's the question: CAN KLINT KUBIAK MAKE SEATTLE REACT TO OUR FORMATIONS INSTEAD OF CONTROLLING EVERY DECISION?**

What are they GOING TO DO? And then YOU HAVE TO MAKE A DECISION. And then you GET YOUR BUTT OUT ON THE PRACTICE FIELD."**

**Raider Nation, that's the right answer. Decide on Wednesday. Stop out-guessing. Go execute.**

## AND FOR FERNANDO MENDOZA — THIS IS SERIOUS, BUT I LIKE IT

Raider Nation, let's be honest about what the kid is walking into.

**BYRON MURPHY II, JARRAN REED and DEMARCUS LAWRENCE CAN STILL MAKE LIFE DIFFICULT — EVEN WITH LEONARD WILLIAMS OUT.**

**That's a real front. Leonard Williams being out helps enormously, but those three are still going to test a young quarterback's pocket.**

**THE IMPORTANT THING IS NOT ASKING THE YOUNG QUARTERBACK TO WIN EVERYTHING ON ONE THROW.**

**GIVE HIM PLAYS THAT LET HIM GET COMFORTABLE. LET HIM RECOGNIZE THE DEFENSE, TAKE THE EASY COMPLETION, AND BUILD SOME RHYTHM.**

Raider Nation, **and this is where I'll push back gently on the "nothing peeled back" plan.** **I understand the logic — Kubiak said "we're NOT going to ask him to do ANYTHING WE HAVEN'T DONE IN PRACTICE," and a watered-down offense would tell Macdonald exactly what's coming.**

**But "full playbook" doesn't have to mean "hero throws." It can mean the full playbook with a lot of easy completions early. Let him hit Brock Bowers on three straight shallow routes and feel his feet.**

**And here's the real advantage: SEATTLE HASN'T HAD MUCH FILM OF HIM AS AN NFL STARTER. He's played before, but SUNDAY IS HIS FIRST START.**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S A WAY TO COMPETE. NOT A GUARANTEE. But Mike Macdonald can't scout tendencies that don't exist yet.**

**And remember what Rob Leonard — the man who's coached against him every day since April — said about him: "TIGHT WINDOW THROWS. ANTICIPATION." And Kubiak: "ACCURACY and GOOD DECISIONS. Those TWO THINGS REALLY JUMP OUT." And: "I LIKE FERNANDO'S INSTINCTS."**
