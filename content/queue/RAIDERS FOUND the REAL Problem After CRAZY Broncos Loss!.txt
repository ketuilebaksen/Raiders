## AND NOW TO THE TAPE — THE BECH TOUCHDOWN WAS A COACHING WIN

Raider Nation, let's watch this one, because it's a thing of beauty.

**MENDOZA ROLLS OUT TO HIS LEFT WHILE DENVER SENDS A BLITZ FROM THE RIGHT SIDE.**

**AND YOU CAN TELL THIS WAS A DESIGNED PLAY. THE RAIDERS HAD CLEARLY SEEN SOMETHING ON FILM — MAYBE A TENDENCY IN THE WAY DENVER HANDLES GOAL LINE LOOKS. AND KUBAK CALLED THE PERFECT PLAY FOR IT.**

**THE BRONCOS TRIED TO SWITCH ASSIGNMENTS AT THE TOP OF THE ROUTE — AND THEY COULD NOT MAKE THAT EXCHANGE WORK.**

**THE RESULT WAS A WIDE-OPEN BECH AND A PERFECTLY PLACED THROW FROM HIS ROOKIE QUARTERBACK.**

Raider Nation, **look at the football intelligence in that design: the blitz comes from the RIGHT, so Kubiak had his quarterback roll LEFT — AWAY from the pressure.**

**That is the exact thing I begged for after Seattle: MOVE HIM. GET HIM OUTSIDE THE POCKET. That's where the first-half magic came from in his debut — play action and moving pockets, seven-for-seven to start his career.**

**And the "SWITCH ASSIGNMENTS AT THE TOP OF THE ROUTE" detail is the coaching win. Kubiak found a route combination that FORCES two defenders to trade responsibilities while running — and when they can't make the handoff cleanly, somebody is standing wide open in the end zone.**

**THE OFFICIALS INITIALLY DID NOT SIGNAL A TOUCHDOWN, WHICH SENT EVERYBODY SCRAMBLING — BUT AFTER REVIEW, THE SCORE STOOD.**

**AND THAT TOUCHDOWN MADE IT A ONE-POSSESSION GAME.**

**BEAUTIFUL DESIGN. BEAUTIFUL EXECUTION.**

**And Raider Nation — on a day with NINE FLAGS to Denver's TWO, and a head coach going on live television at halftime to say "THEY NEED TO CALL IT BOTH WAYS," at least the replay system got that one right.**
