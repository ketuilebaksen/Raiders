# "HARD TO SLEEP AFTERWARDS." KIRK COUSINS TAKES THE BLAME PLAY BY PLAY — And The Decision Now Sitting On Ownership's Desk 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, before anything else, two corrections so nobody is confused about where this team stands.

**This is three straight losses, not four. And the record is 3-3.** Kansas City 30-27, New England 45-17, Buffalo 33-30. I'm not going to let a worse number than the truth get into your head.

And second: **there has been no announcement from ownership.** No statement from Mark Davis, nothing from Tom Brady, no firing, no declaration. Anybody telling you otherwise is guessing.

**But there is a decision sitting on that desk right now**, and it is the biggest one of this season — because at 3-3 with three straight losses and a trade deadline three weeks away, this organization has to decide whether it's a buyer or a builder. We'll get to exactly what that means.

First though, Kirk Cousins stepped to the podium after this three-point loss and delivered the most self-critical press conference I have heard from a quarterback all year. He named the specific throws. He named the exact possessions. He explained precisely what Buffalo did to erase Brock Bowers. And he said something about losing streaks that every single one of you should hear.

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing 6,000 believers — hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and let's go.

## "ONE OF THESE GAMES WHERE IT'S HARD TO SLEEP AFTERWARDS"

He opened before anybody asked him a question.

**Cousins: "One of these games where it's hard to sleep afterwards. When you kind of think through the plays that you could have or should have made — and in the red zone, plays that you leave points out there when you have to kick field goals in a tight game like that, it comes back to haunt you. So, difficult to lose that one. We'll have to talk through it tomorrow."**

Raider Nation, that's a 38-year-old man with fourteen years of starts telling you he isn't going to sleep.

And notice what he chose to lead with — **the red zone.** Not the penalties, not the defense, not the schedule. The same number his head coach called unacceptable: two for five. Both men walked into separate rooms and independently named the same failure.

**"It comes back to haunt you."** In a game decided by three points, every field goal instead of a touchdown is a ghost.

## THE PENALTIES, AND A FAIR POINT

**Reporter: "The penalties were a killer today. As a leader, is it young guys working through it, older guys making mistakes?"**
**Cousins: "I didn't pay too much attention to any special teams or defense penalties, but we were able to overcome a holding call at one point, which was good by our offense to be able to do that. Certainly the fourth-and-one was a tough one. Not exactly sure what happened, but it's a part of the game. The penalties are going to happen. You've got to find ways to overcome them. At times we did, at times we didn't."**

That's a quarterback refusing to throw anybody under the bus while still admitting the cost. And the detail he highlights is a fair one — **this offense did overcome a holding call on one drive.** That's real. Seven penalties didn't all become disasters.

But "at times we did, at times we didn't" is the honest summary of a team that has now taken fifteen penalties in two weeks after being one of the league's cleanest for a month.

## AND HE COMPLICATED THE FALSE START STORY

Now here's where it gets genuinely interesting, because Cousins gave a different read than his head coach on the fourth-down false start.

Remember Kubiak's answer: **"It's happened three times and it's killing us. Absolutely killing us. And I have not fixed it. So shame on me."**

Here's the quarterback's version:

**Cousins: "I don't know what happened. I didn't talk to the ref. Sometimes I can't even hear what the ref's microphone is saying in the stadium — it's just muffled on the field. But I think it was a very different call from what had happened in prior games, so it's kind of a totally separate thing. I'm sure it was just the crowd noise and just not being able to hear. I'm guessing, but I didn't talk to him, so I'm not sure of the specifics."**

Raider Nation, that's an important nuance and I want to give it to you straight.

**Cousins believes this one was a different call than the previous ones** — a separate issue rather than the same recurring mistake. And his theory is crowd noise, which creates a genuinely strange situation: **this was a home game.** Allegiant was loud, our own crowd was loud, and a lineman couldn't hear the cadence.

So which is it? Kubiak says it's the third time and he hasn't fixed it. Cousins says it was a different call entirely.

**Here's my read: both can be true and it doesn't change the conclusion.** Maybe the mechanism was different this time. But three fourth-down attempts have now died to pre-snap penalties, and whatever the cause — cadence, noise, snap timing — the result is identical. The head coach taking it on himself is still the correct response, and the fix is still operational: silent cadence, a clearer signal, more reps in noise.

## WHAT BUFFALO ACTUALLY DID TO BOWERS

Now the most useful football explanation of the day.

Kubiak said Buffalo "had a good plan" to keep Bowers quiet for two and a half quarters. Cousins described the plan itself:

**"Just playing with certain leverages and doubling him, putting two people on him and having eyeballs on him. It wasn't every play — they had to have variety to their coverages — but at times, just putting a lot of attention on him with two players."**

Raider Nation, let me translate each piece, because this is a masterclass in how you erase one player.

**"Certain leverages"** means a defender aligning deliberately inside or outside of him to deny a specific route before the ball is even snapped.

**"Doubling him, putting two people on him"** is the bracket — one underneath, one over the top, so there's no window.

**"Having eyeballs on him"** is the subtlest one and maybe the most important: a zone defender whose first job is simply to find number 89 and react to him, regardless of his own assignment.

**And "it wasn't every play — they had to have variety"** is the part that makes it work. If you bracket him on every snap, a good quarterback diagnoses it instantly and attacks the resulting weakness. Buffalo mixed it, so Cousins never knew pre-snap which look he was getting.

**That is exactly what Kubiak warned us about on Friday** — that the coverages he sees on Sunday aren't the ones he expects going in, and it's been happening for weeks. Now we have the mechanics from the man taking the snaps.

And here's the unavoidable conclusion: **the only cure is making the double team expensive.** Six receptions from the entire receiver room, five by Tre Tucker, means it wasn't expensive at all.

## COUSINS ON THE RECEIVER PROBLEM — AND HE BLAMED HIMSELF

Speaking of which, he was asked directly about the lack of production outside Tucker.

**Cousins: "I think again it's about taking advantage of the opportunities you do have. So for example, on a hitch to Tre in the red zone today, I just left it a little outside and ended up being incomplete. And that's one where you want to put it right on and make it easy for him. At times it's just you're going where your reads are taking you — and if that's the back, if that's the tight end, so be it. But we definitely want to find explosives in the pass game. And if we're finding those, I don't get too caught up in who it's going to, because you never know on the next play where it might go."**

Raider Nation, look at what he did there. He was handed a question that invited him to criticize his receivers, and his answer was to describe **a throw he left outside in the red zone.**

In the red zone. On a day the team went two for five in the red zone. He volunteered his own incompletion as the example.

And then the philosophy: **"you're going where your reads are taking you — and if that's the back, if that's the tight end, so be it."** That's the correct way for a quarterback to think. You don't force the ball to a position group to satisfy a stat line; you throw it to whoever the defense leaves open.

**"I don't get too caught up in who it's going to."** That's a professional. It's also, frankly, the luxury of a quarterback who refuses to complain about his tools — and it makes the front office's job clearer, not murkier, because he's not going to ask for help publicly.

## "YOU'VE GOT TO FIND THE INCHES IN THE FOURTH QUARTER"

Now the section that explains this entire season, and it's the best answer anybody has given.

He was asked what it will take to close games out — the exact phrase Kubiak used.

**Cousins: "No doubt that's what it is. We've got to find ways to close. You've got to find the inches in the fourth quarter. You can't go out on the second-to-last possession and throw three incompletions. You can't, in the third quarter, throw an interception that gives them a short field and they get a touchdown off of it. You can't go two for five in the red zone. Third-and-goal, we've got to come away with those four-point plays and get the touchdown and not be kicking the field goal. Those are all the plays throughout the game that you look back on and say, it didn't give us a chance to win. It's just about making plays. You've got to make plays and be smart and manage the game well. And if you do that, you'd like to think you give yourself a chance to close it out in the fourth quarter."**

Raider Nation, this is a quarterback performing an autopsy on his own loss, in public, within an hour of the final whistle. Let me pull out the three specifics, because two of them are new information.

**One: three incompletions on the second-to-last possession.** A full drive wasted at the moment the game was still winnable.

**Two — and this is a detail nobody had — the third-quarter interception gave Buffalo a SHORT FIELD and they scored a TOUCHDOWN off it.** So that turnover wasn't just a lost possession; it directly produced seven Buffalo points. In a three-point game, that single play is the margin twice over.

**Three: "those four-point plays."** I love this phrase, and it's how professional offenses actually talk. A third-and-goal isn't a choice between seven points and zero — it's a choice between seven and three. **The four-point play.** Go two for five in the red zone and you've handed away twelve points in a game you lost by three.

That's the whole season in one paragraph.

## THE VETERAN ACCOUNTABILITY, THROW BY THROW

And then a reporter noted the accountability he was showing, and asked whether Buffalo was reading him better or whether he should have read them better.

**Cousins: "It all runs together right now. Again, I go back to — there was a lot of explosiveness in the pass game, a lot of production. I don't come away saying we're not explosive or good enough in the pass game. That's not my takeaway from today. But I just go back to that one possession, the three incompletions. The first was the dagger route — we just weren't able to connect. And then the next one, I felt like I just led Brock too far, and was mad at myself for that. And then the third one just felt like it got kind of hung up, and I wish I had gotten to Tre a little quicker. So sometimes it's a timing thing, sometimes it's a ball location thing, sometimes it's a read thing — but each play has its own reason. We certainly made a lot of plays in the pass game today, especially when our backs were against the wall. But there's always going to be — you've got to be hard on yourself. There were a few today that we're capable of making. I'm capable of making."**

Raider Nation, he just walked through three failed throws, one at a time, and assigned himself a reason for each.

**The dagger route** — a deep in-breaking concept that requires perfect timing. Didn't connect.
**The throw to Bowers** — "I led him too far, and was mad at myself."
**The throw to Tucker** — "I wish I had gotten to him a little quicker."

Timing. Ball location. Read. He didn't blame the protection, the route, the noise, or the officials.

And catch the last five words: **"I'm capable of making."** He shifted from "we" to "I" at the end of his own answer.

That's the man his head coach just called "gritty, gutty" and said is "playing superb football right now." That's also, for the record, the second-leading touchdown passer in the NFL — and he spent his podium time apologizing for three throws.

## HIS ANSWER ON HOW TO SNAP A SKID

This is the part I'd put on the wall of the facility.

**Reporter: "What's important to help a team snap out of a skid like this?"**
**Cousins: "All I know to do is go back to work, to have candid conversations on Monday that make us better as a football team. To have your process, your habits that get you ready to play well, and then to go through that process — to not change anything when you're winning, to not change too much when you're losing. I think the only part you change is when you go back and see where we've got to be better, and you emphasize that during the week. We've just got to go back to work and put the work in, and expect that if you do that, the breaks will fly your way."**

**"Not change anything when you're winning. Not change too much when you're losing."**

Raider Nation, that is the single most valuable sentence in this entire press conference, and it's the antidote to everything happening in comment sections right now.

A team on a three-game slide faces enormous pressure to blow things up — change the quarterback, fire the coordinator, trade the veterans, overhaul the scheme. And the veteran's answer is: **change only what the film says to change, and emphasize it during the week.** Everything else stays.

Note he didn't say "change nothing." He said **the part you change is where you have to be better.** Red zone. Pre-snap discipline. Third-quarter starts. That's a scalpel, not a sledgehammer.

And "candid conversations on Monday" — he used that word twice this week. This is a locker room that talks honestly, which is exactly why fifteen penalties hasn't become fifteen leaks to reporters.

## THE RED ZONE, POSSESSION BY POSSESSION

He was asked specifically what's going wrong inside the twenty, and gave the most detailed answer of the day.

**Cousins: "We can go back — whether it was the end of the half, the clock was up against us, so that made it tough to be able to get through a progression. We kind of had to pick and stick, and then had to settle for a field goal there. Then you had the first-and-goal from the two that we were unable to go forward, and then we had the incompletion. I'll go back and watch the tape, but I felt like whether it's staying with Brock there even though he was doubled and had tough leverage — you stay with him — or you put a better ball on Cody. Those are the things we'll be talking about tomorrow. And honestly, the other several possessions run together right now. But anytime you leave points on the board in the red zone, it's hard."**

Three separate failures, three separate causes.

**The end of the half:** the clock forced them to **"pick and stick"** — choose one receiver pre-snap and commit, because there was no time to work a progression. Field goal.

**First-and-goal from the two:** couldn't advance. Raider Nation, first-and-goal from the two-yard line should be a touchdown drive the overwhelming majority of the time. That's a short-yardage, physicality failure — and it's the same team that averaged 2.1 yards a carry in New England and has struggled all year at the line of scrimmage.

**And then the choice he's still wrestling with:** stay with a double-teamed Bowers anyway, or **"put a better ball on Cody"** — Cody White, the practice squad hero with three touchdowns. He's saying the throw existed and he didn't make it well enough.

**"Anytime you leave points on the board in the red zone, it's hard."** Twelve points, in a three-point loss.

## AND HE DIDN'T SEE THE EJECTION EITHER

Asked what he saw in the interaction **between Brock Bowers and C.J. Gardner-Johnson** that led to the ejection:

**Cousins: "That was another one of those moments where I don't know what's going on. The refs are just suddenly walking the ball further and further down the field, and I'm getting the next play call, and I'm like, okay..."**

So the quarterback's view was the same as his coach's — eyes on the play sheet, no idea what happened behind him. And now we know the interaction involved **Bowers**, which is its own small detail: an opponent went after the best player on this offense and got himself thrown out of a football game for it. JJ Watt's description was blunt: you spit on somebody, you get ejected.

And remember Kubiak's line — the players knew before kickoff that anybody who took the bait would be standing next to him on the sideline. **Nobody took the bait.** On a day with seven penalties, that's the one discipline test this team passed.

## NOW THE BIG ONE: THE DECISION IN FRONT OF OWNERSHIP

So let's get to what this actually means for the people who own this football team.

A reporter put the record in perspective, and Cousins' answer sets the whole frame.

**Reporter: "Heading into this season, I'm not sure anyone outside the locker room would have said the Raiders will be 3-3 after six. And when you consider the teams you lost to, two of them could have been victories. Is the trajectory still promising?"**
**Cousins: "I'm sure that's the messaging tomorrow. But it's a week-to-week league, and you just feel like no matter what has happened — 0-6, 6-0, or somewhere in between — you've got to come back and do it again. And your next opponent doesn't really care what happened the previous six weeks. So they are each their own stages. You get to the end of 17 and you settle them up, but they really are their own stages, and we've got to treat it that way and be absolute pros each time we step between the white lines."**

**"You get to the end of 17 and you settle them up."**

Raider Nation, that's the professional's view and it's correct for players. But it is **not** how a front office can think, because a front office has to make decisions before the end of 17 — and the trade deadline is three weeks away.

So here is the decision actually sitting on ownership's desk, stated plainly.

**Option one: this team is a buyer.** The evidence is real. They're 3-3 with three losses to teams that are 4-0, 5-1 and a reigning conference champion. **Two of those three were three-point games.** Jeremy Fowler reported weeks ago that Las Vegas could be buyers. Cousins is second in the NFL in touchdowns. Thirty points against Buffalo with six receptions from the entire receiver room. This is a roster that cannot close, not a roster that cannot compete — and closing is coachable.

**Option two: this team is a builder.** The schedule ahead is brutal. The receiver room needs an offseason, not a rental. Fernando Mendoza is the number one overall pick and every game Cousins plays is a game the rookie doesn't. Dan Graziano has already laid out the framework: if the Raiders fall out of contention, evaluating the first overall pick becomes the logical organizational priority — and the Week Eight or post-Week Ten windows are the natural spots.

**My position, and I'll be clear:** buy small and stay the course. Spend a day-three pick on a receiver who can run a slant, because six catches from a position group is indefensible with a deadline available. Do not spend premium capital, do not trade the quarterback who is keeping this team in games, and do not rush the rookie behind a line that's still being rebuilt in-season.

**And above all: do not make a decision about this team based on a three-point loss to a 5-1 opponent.** The two words from both the coach and the quarterback were the same — close games out. **That is a correction, not a teardown.**

## WHERE THIS LEAVES US

Let me put it together.

Three straight losses, 3-3, with the Rams next. Two for five in the red zone, including first-and-goal from the two. A third-quarter interception that handed Buffalo a short field and a touchdown. Three incompletions on the second-to-last possession, each of which the quarterback dissected and blamed on himself. Seven penalties and a fourth-down false start that Kubiak owns and Cousins thinks was a different call entirely. Buffalo erasing Bowers with leverages, brackets and eyeballs for two and a half quarters. And a receiver room that produced six catches, five by one man.

Against all of that: 30 points on a 5-1 team, a run game the coach is finally encouraged about, a spark from Washington Jr., a quarterback playing superb football, a defense that held Josh Allen to one rushing yard, no new injuries, and a locker room that refused to be goaded into losing its composure.

**This team is four correctable mistakes away from being 5-1.** That's not a fan's cope — that's the arithmetic of two three-point losses.
