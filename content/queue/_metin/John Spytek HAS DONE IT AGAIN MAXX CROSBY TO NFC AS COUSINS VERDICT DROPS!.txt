## THE ARGUMENT NOBODY IN RAIDER NATION WANTS TO HEAR

**Pete Prisco of CBS Sports believes the Las Vegas Raiders should seriously consider moving Maxx Crosby before the NFL trade deadline.**

And Raider Nation, I'm going to present his case properly and fairly, **because you can't argue with something you refuse to understand.**

Here's the setup. **Crosby entered this league as a FOURTH-ROUND selection out of Eastern Michigan** and turned himself into **the emotional and competitive foundation of this entire defense.** When the Raiders win, his relentless pressure, his effort, his ability to disrupt an entire possession by himself — **all of it gets the praise it deserves.**

**Prisco's argument is about consistency of scrutiny.** If Crosby gets the credit when this defense is great, **the same level of examination has to apply when the defense collapses and the team starts drifting away from contention.**

**And his core point is the one that's hard to dismiss:** Crosby **remains a highly desirable commodity**, and Las Vegas could acquire **valuable assets** for a player who **may no longer be in his prime by the time this rebuild finally produces a complete football team.**

Now, Prisco saying it does not mean the Raiders are shopping him. **It doesn't.** But Raider Nation, **it raises a legitimate organizational question, and I'd be a coward to skip it.**

## AND HERE IS THE TIMELINE PROBLEM, STATED HONESTLY

Look at this roster's clock.

**Fernando Mendoza is the future at quarterback — the first overall pick, learning from the sideline.** **Ashton Jeanty, Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer are part of a young offensive foundation** that's barely begun.

**And Maxx Crosby is already FIRMLY in his prime.**

**Which means his trade value may never be higher than it is right now.**

That's the cold math. **The young guys' arrows point up for the next four or five years. Crosby's arrow points up for the next two or three.** If the Raiders are genuinely several seasons from contending, **you are asking your best player to spend his very best football on a team that isn't ready for it.**

**And the diagnosis of this roster supports the concern.** Las Vegas **can produce points despite lacking a dependable number one wide receiver** — Cousins is tied for the league lead in touchdown passes, after all. **But the offensive line has struggled to sustain blocks. Ashton Jeanty has battled injuries. And the defense has repeatedly failed to control the running game** — four straight games allowing 30 or more points.

**The honest version of Prisco's case: if the holes are everywhere, one great player can't cover them, and his value expires before the fixes arrive.**

## NOW THE PROPOSAL — AND THE NUMBER IS ENORMOUS

The speculation has advanced from a general argument into a specific concept, and this is where it gets real.

**In an October 27th article, analyst Zachary Rottman presented three realistic trade concepts for Maxx Crosby — including one that would send him to the CHICAGO BEARS.**

**Here's the structure:**

**Chicago gives Las Vegas FIRST-ROUND SELECTIONS IN 2027 AND 2028.**
**The Bears receive Crosby and a 2027 FIFTH-ROUND pick.**

**Two first-round picks, Raider Nation.**

And the reasoning behind the details is sharp. **Rottman compared the structure to the package connected to Baltimore during the offseason**, with two important differences:

**One: the Raiders would probably receive LATER first-round selections, because Chicago is expected to compete.** A contender's first-rounder lands in the twenties, not the top five. **That's the price of trading with a good team.**

**Two: Las Vegas would surrender an additional fifth-rounder, because the Bears would only be acquiring the SECOND HALF of Crosby's current season.** Half a year of a superstar costs less than a full one, so the sweetener goes the other way.

**And his conclusion was that it could benefit both organizations.** Chicago adds **an immediate pass-rushing force during what it views as a legitimate contention window.** Las Vegas obtains **two premium assets capable of accelerating a rebuild centered on its young quarterback and offensive playmakers.**

**And then the disclaimer that matters, which I'm repeating because it's the truth: this remains an ANALYST'S IDEA. It is not evidence that Chicago has contacted the Raiders, and it is not evidence that John Spytek is negotiating a deal.**

## WHAT WOULD TWO FIRSTS ACTUALLY BUY US?

Let's be honest about the upside, because this is why the proposal is seductive.

**The return could provide impactful draft capital for a top wide receiver, offensive line help, and defensive reinforcements.**

Raider Nation, **that's the entire shopping list of this football team in one sentence.** We've spent six weeks on this channel screaming about exactly three things: **a receiver who can separate against man coverage, an offensive line that can open a lane for Ashton Jeanty, and an interior defender who stops opponents from running through the middle of us.**

**Two first-round picks plus what this team already holds — every round in 2027 but the seventh — is enough ammunition to fix all three.**

**So the question, Raider Nation, is no longer theoretical: would you accept a painful deal if it gave Las Vegas the resources to build properly around Fernando Mendoza?**

## AND NOW HERE IS WHY I SAY NO

**I would not trade Maxx Crosby. Let me tell you exactly why, and I'm not going to be sentimental about it.**

**Reason one: trading him creates another massive hole, and this roster is already made of holes.** You'd need to replace a man **capable of affecting quarterbacks AND defending the run for an entire football game.** Those players do not grow on trees, and **you don't find them in the twenties of a contender's first round.** You'd be solving three problems by creating a fourth — and the fourth might be the hardest of all to fill.

**Reason two: look at WHY he hasn't been dominant lately.** We covered this stat two weeks ago and it's the single most important number on this defense: **Maxx Crosby is seeing the highest rate of chips and double teams of his entire career — SIXTEEN of them in the Buffalo game alone.**

**Sixteen.** In one game.

**That's not decline. That's a man being triple-teamed because nobody beside him scares anybody.** Rob Leonard stood at that podium asking out loud, **"Can we rush with four and get home?"** Kubiak admitted the **time to pressure has gotten longer because the league has their tape.**

**You don't trade the man who's being doubled. You buy him help.** Trade Crosby and the Raiders don't have a pass rush at all — **they have a rumor of one.**

**Reason three: leadership you cannot draft.** And that brings me to what he said after the Rams game.

## CROSBY'S OWN RESPONSE — AND IT'S EXACTLY WHO HE IS

After the 35-21 loss, Crosby didn't make excuses and didn't ask for help.

**He said the Raiders must keep finding ways to improve, because their current performance is not good enough.** He pointed specifically to **the repeated ups and downs, the slow starts, and the need to find solutions quickly before facing New York.**

**And his message was direct: every player must look in the mirror and become better.**

Raider Nation, **that's the fifth voice in that building this month to take accountability instead of handing it out.** Kubiak said the red zone **"starts with me"** and that he did **"a really poor job offensively"** of getting the offense going. Rob Leonard named his own 76-yard failure. Tyler Linderbaum owned his false start. Cousins apologized for three throws after a 313-yard day.

**And now the best player on the team is telling everybody to look in the mirror, starting with himself.**

**That accountable response is precisely why trading a man this competitive and this connected to the franchise would be emotionally brutal.** But I'm not going to pretend emotion decides these things. **The proposal forces this front office to weigh that leadership against the rebuilding acceleration two first-round picks would provide.**

**I've weighed it. I keep him. But I understand the people who don't.**

## THE HONEST PART ABOUT THE RAMS GAME

I'm not going to protect anybody here, including Crosby.

**Against the Rams, too many Raiders were displaced at the point of attack.** And **Crosby was unable to deliver the disruptive performance Las Vegas needed from its defensive centerpiece.** That's the truth. **A 2-4 team missing its best players walked into Allegiant Stadium and ran the ball on us.**

And one of his teammates said the fix out loud. **Linebacker Quay Walker, after the loss, said the solution begins with GROWN MEN DEFEATING BLOCKS, winning individual matchups, and making tackles.**

**Walker did not single out Crosby** — and I want to be clear about that, because the internet will say he did. **His comments applied to every defender responsible for gap integrity.** Every single one.

Raider Nation, **do you hear the echo?** On the broadcast after that game, a film analyst said the defensive problem was players not trusting each other — **"If your brother's got the A gap, let him play the A gap."** And now a linebacker in that locker room is saying **grown men have to defeat blocks and make tackles.**

**Same diagnosis, from inside the building and outside it. This defense is losing at the point of attack, one man at a time.** And that is not a scheme problem you fix with a phone call. **That's a Wednesday-practice problem.**

## NOW THE KIRK COUSINS VERDICT — AND HERE IT IS

Raider Nation, everybody keeps asking when the Cousins verdict drops.

**I'm going to tell you something that might annoy you: the verdict already dropped. Months ago. And it wasn't delivered by a reporter or a coach. It was delivered by a spreadsheet.**

Here's the contract, and once you see it you can't unsee it.

**Cousins signed in April on a deal that LOOKED like five years and $172 million — but is really a one-year, fully guaranteed $20 million bridge.** The Atlanta Falcons are covering roughly **$8.7 million** of it, and the Raiders' own cash outlay this season is around **$1.3 million.**

**And then the fork in the road.** **A fully guaranteed $10 MILLION roster bonus comes due on the third day of the new league year. And if Kirk Cousins is still on this roster on the FIFTH day — around mid-March — a two-year, $80 MILLION option becomes FULLY GUARANTEED.**

**So the verdict is this: unless this franchise intends to make a surprising long-term financial commitment to a 39-year-old quarterback, Kirk Cousins is not a Las Vegas Raider next season.**

**That's not a hot take. That's arithmetic.**

**The only live question is WHEN the bridge ends — in November, or in March.**

## AND HERE'S WHAT I'D ACTUALLY DO ABOUT IT

**Two paths, and they both make sense depending on one football game.**

**Path one: you keep him.** He's **tied for the NFL lead with 14 touchdown passes.** He's started every game. He is a **steadying presence for a first-year head coach who knows him from their Minnesota years, 2019 through 2021.** Andrew Janocko told us he **"thrives on accountability, wants to be pushed, wants to be coached"** — and that when young players watch a veteran say *"I've got to step up,"* **"that's the type of stuff young guys should mimic."** Even **Aaron Donald said: "He looks good. He's playing well."**

**And the thing nobody mentions: Fernando Mendoza is out on an empty practice field after the building clears out, asking Kirk WHY.** You trade Cousins and that education stops.

**Path two: you trade him and get something.** Because the alternative — **benching him and letting him gather dust** — gets you **nothing.** **Jon Gruden said on his podcast that if he were Philadelphia or Minnesota, he'd offer the Raiders a second-round pick.** That's his opinion, not a report. **But a second-round pick for a quarterback you already know won't be back is the kind of gift most front offices dream about.**

**My verdict: if they beat the Jets, you don't touch him. If they lose to the Jets, you answer the phone.**

## WHICH BRINGS ME TO THE LOCKER ROOM, AND TYLER LINDERBAUM

Here's the part of this story I found most telling.

**Tyler Linderbaum addressed the team after its fourth consecutive defeat** — the 35-21 loss that dropped Las Vegas to 3-4 after a 3-0 start. **The Raiders again struggled to establish the run and to protect the quarterback against pressure**, which left this offense unable to control the game or consistently finish drives.

**And Linderbaum's assessment was that these issues RESEMBLE PROBLEMS THE UNIT HAS FACED BEFORE.**

Raider Nation, that's an honest and slightly chilling thing for a leader to say in Week 7. **Not "this was new." Not "they surprised us." The same problems. Again.**

**His view of the fix is beautifully simple and completely unglamorous: a successful running game requires EVERY blocker to sustain his assignment on EVERY down.**

Not a scheme change. Not a new play. **Five men holding their blocks for one extra second, sixty times a game.**

**And he said he remains confident this offensive line has enough talent to become a strong rushing unit — but confidence alone isn't going to get it done.**

Raider Nation, **I believe him about the talent.** Look at the evidence: **Cousins went 41 attempts without being sacked against Buffalo.** **Spencer Burford reportedly allowed zero pressures on 47 pass plays with the best pass-blocking grade on the team.** **DJ Glaze is making a year-three jump.**

**The pass protection is real. The run blocking is the lie.** And Linderbaum just told us why: **sustaining blocks on every single down.** It's the same thing Quay Walker said about the defense, from the other side of the ball. **Defeat your block. Sustain your block. One man at a time.**

**This whole football team is losing the one-on-one fight, on both sides of the line, and every leader in that building has now said it out loud.**
