## SO HOW DO YOU ACTUALLY FIX IT? THE PREGAME IDEAS WERE RIGHT

Raider Nation, this is the most useful part of the whole breakdown, so stay with me — because these are the fixes, and they were sitting on the table before the game.

### FIX ONE: STOP PLAYING THE WIDE BOX

**The Raiders have been playing a WIDE BOX a lot — meaning SIX OR FEWER DEFENDERS near the line of scrimmage.**

**And against a team that wants to stretch you HORIZONTALLY, that is an INVITATION TO BE RUN OVER.**

Raider Nation, **this is the single most concrete, fixable, tactical thing on this entire list.** **Six in the box against outside zone is a math problem you cannot solve with effort.** **There are not enough bodies to cover the width of the field. Period.**

**MORE PLAYERS HAVE TO SET THE EDGE. MORE LINEBACKERS HAVE TO PLAY DOWNHILL. AND NOBODY SHOULD BE SHY ABOUT IT.**

**Nobody should be shy about it. Raider Nation, that's the line. That's the whole thing. This defense has been playing scared of getting beaten over the top, and as a result it's been getting beaten on the ground every single week for six weeks.**

**Pick your poison. At some point you have to actually pick.**

### FIX TWO: USE THE LINEBACKERS AS WEAPONS, NOT JUST READERS

**Tommy Eichenberg has quietly been doing a solid job against the run — and he deserves that credit.**

**And the idea going around the film room is a good one: use QUAY WALKER CLOSER TO THE LINE OF SCRIMMAGE and let KOBE DEAN BLITZ MORE.**

**That's a creative way to get pressure from places the Niners don't expect.**

**Because let's face it, Raider Nation — YOU CAN'T BEAT BROCK PURDY BY JUST HOPING.**

**You can't beat a 69-percent passer with a four-man rush and a prayer. You have to make him uncomfortable from an angle he hasn't seen on tape.**

### FIX THREE: MORE ZONE COVERAGE

**This is the piece Rob Leonard has to consider: PLAY MORE ZONE.**

**The Raiders already play plenty of zone — but the argument is for more of it, and here's the logic behind it.**

Raider Nation, **when you play man coverage, your defensive backs turn their backs to the football and run with receivers. Nobody's eyes are on the backfield.** **Against a scheme built on play-action and misdirection off wide zone, that is how you get carved up by a tight end running free into a vacated zone.**

**Zone coverage keeps eyes in the backfield. Eyes in the backfield means fewer sixty-yard completions off a fake handoff. It also means more bodies available to fill run lanes.**

**And with their receiver room gutted — Evans out, Kirk out, Deebo out — zone was the obvious call. You don't need man coverage to handle a depleted receiver group. You need bodies near the ball.**
