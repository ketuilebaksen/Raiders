**NOT A WIDE RECEIVER. A DEFENSIVE TACKLE.**

And Raider Nation, I know what some of you are already typing. **WIDE RECEIVER REMAINS AN OBVIOUS NEED. THAT ROOM COULD BE AMONG THE WEAKEST IN THE LEAGUE.**

**Five catches for forty yards from the entire receiver group against Seattle. Tre Tucker one for nine. Jaylen Nailor one for minus one. We've all seen it.**

**BUT THE DEFENSE GIVES LAS VEGAS ANOTHER URGENT REASON TO THINK CAREFULLY ABOUT ITS FIRST SELECTION.**

**And here are the two numbers that make the case:**

**THE RAIDERS ARE ALLOWING MORE POINTS PER GAME THAN ANY TEAM IN THE LEAGUE.**

**AND THEY'RE GIVING UP 151.1 RUSHING YARDS PER GAME — RANKING 31ST.**

**Raider Nation, most points allowed in football. 31st against the run. Fifth team in NFL history to give up thirty-plus in six straight games.**

**That's not a need. That's an emergency.**

Raider Nation, the scouting report is genuinely exciting, so let's go through it.

**He has CLIMBED THE RANKINGS because of his EXPLOSIVE FIRST STEP, CLOSING BURST, and ABILITY TO PRESSURE THE QUARTERBACK.**

**SIX SACKS AND TWENTY-FIVE PRESSURES THIS SEASON — with that pressure total ranking SIXTH AMONG ALL FBS DEFENSIVE TACKLES.**

**ESPN RANKS HIM 14TH OVERALL IN THE DRAFT CLASS AND SECOND AT HIS POSITION.**

**Raider Nation, that matters a lot — because the projection has Las Vegas selecting him right around his actual overall ranking. That's not a reach. That's value meeting need.**

**And the scouting assessment goes beyond the sack numbers, which is the part that excites me most:**

**He can PENETRATE THE FIRST LEVEL QUICKLY and use ACTIVE HANDS and DIFFERENT APPROACHES to beat pass protection.**

**AND AGAINST THE RUN — he can ANCHOR AT THE POINT OF ATTACK, ENGAGE AND SHED BLOCKS, and DISRUPT THE TIMING OF A RUSHING PLAY.**

Raider Nation, **stop right there. "ANCHOR AT THE POINT OF ATTACK. ENGAGE AND SHED BLOCKS."**

**That is LITERALLY the exact thing this defense cannot do. Word for word.**

**Remember what we've said since September: KEEP THE INTERIOR LINEMEN OFF OUR LINEBACKERS. Remember the question: "WHERE'S THE GUY YOU TRUST ON EVERY DOWN?" Adam Butler can rush but struggles against the run. Tonka Hemingway is explosive but needs pass-rush moves. Fonz Faticasi and JJ Pegues are run defenders.**

**NOBODY ON THIS ROSTER IS A THREE-DOWN INTERIOR DEFENDER. And that's why teams can run on second down, hurry to the line on third, and trap our run-stuffing tackles on a passing down.**

**FINDING AN INTERIOR DEFENDER WHO CAN CONTRIBUTE IN BOTH PHASES WOULD ADDRESS MORE THAN ONE WEAKNESS.**

**And the report is honest about the other need: THE RAIDERS COULD USE ANOTHER EDGE RUSHER TOO — BUT OPPONENTS HAVE BEEN FINDING TOO MUCH ROOM THROUGH THE MIDDLE.**

## NOW THE BENCHING — AND HERE'S EXACTLY WHAT HAPPENED

Raider Nation, this is a play worth understanding in full, because it explains a loss.

**AGAINST SEATTLE, ERIC STOKES SLOWED DOWN WHILE COVERING COOPER KUPP — AND DREW LOCK FOUND KUPP FOR A 34-YARD TOUCHDOWN.**

**And here's the context that makes it hurt: COOPER KUPP IS NO LONGER THE RECEIVER HE WAS AT HIS PEAK AND DOESN'T CONSISTENTLY CREATE THE SAME SEPARATION.**

**So why did he get open? BECAUSE STOKES EXPLAINED AFTER WEDNESDAY'S PRACTICE THAT HE THOUGHT LOCK WAS GOING DOWN.**

**THE QUARTERBACK STAYED ALIVE LONG ENOUGH TO RELEASE THE PASS — AND A PLAY STOKES THOUGHT WAS NEARLY OVER BECAME AN EASY TOUCHDOWN.**

**AND KUBIAK BENCHED HIM FOR A COUPLE OF PLAYS AND ADDRESSED THE LAPSE PUBLICLY.**

Raider Nation, **this is the single play Klint Kubiak was describing when he gave us the phrase of the week:**

**"YOU'VE GOT TO PLAY THROUGH THE ECHO OF THE WHISTLE. AND WE DIDN'T DO THAT ON THOSE TWO PLAYS — AND GAVE UP FOURTEEN POINTS."**

**"THE EXPECTATION IS FOR US TO FINISH EVERY PLAY. AND WHEN YOU DON'T, YOU SEE WHAT HAPPENS — AND IT CHANGES THE ENTIRE GAME."**

**Two plays. 105 yards. Fourteen points. In a game we lost by eight.**

**And now we know what one of them was: a cornerback who stopped running because he thought his quarterback had the sack.**

## AND STOKES' RESPONSE WAS EXACTLY WHAT A VETERAN SHOULD SAY

Raider Nation, this is why I'm not piling on him today.

**STOKES ACCEPTED THE CRITICISM.**

**"I HAVE TO FINISH," he said. "I CAN'T STOP. I JUST HAVE TO BE BETTER."**

**HE ALSO ACKNOWLEDGED THAT MISREADING LOCK'S SITUATION WAS HIS RESPONSIBILITY — AND SAID HE NEEDED TO STRAIN MORE.**

Raider Nation, **"I NEEDED TO STRAIN MORE" is football language and it's the perfect word.**

**Straining is the ugly part of coverage. It's the extra three steps when you're exhausted and you're pretty sure the play is dead. It's effort with no reward ninety-nine times out of a hundred.**

**And on the hundredth time, it's fourteen points.**

**And this was PARTICULARLY FRUSTRATING because THE DEFENSE HAD AN OPPORTUNITY TO HELP AN OFFENSE THAT STRUGGLED AGAINST SEATTLE.**

**Raider Nation, THE OFFENSE HAS CARRIED THE DEFENSE FOR MUCH OF THIS SEASON — 27.6 points a game, sixth in scoring, fifth in passing yards, third in touchdown passes.**

**And on the ONE Sunday the roles reversed — when the offense managed twenty-seven yards in a half and five straight possessions of negative yardage — THE RAIDERS MOSTLY KEPT THE SEAHAWKS OUT OF THE END ZONE.**

**Four first downs allowed in the first half. Plus two in the turnover battle. Twenty-one points.**

**THE BREAKDOWNS AT KEY MOMENTS MADE THE DIFFERENCE.**

**Raider Nation, that's the cruelest version of this season. The defense finally showed up, and two plays undid it.**

## AND KUBIAK'S HANDLING OF IT WAS A MASTERCLASS

Raider Nation, this is the part I want you to notice, because it's why that locker room works.

**KUBIAK CONNECTED THE BIG PLAY TO THE FAILURE TO FINISH THE DOWN. But his assessment of Stokes ALSO INCLUDED WHY THE TEAM STILL NEEDS HIM.**

**HE CALLED THE CORNERBACK ONE OF THEIR KEY PLAYERS.**

**HE NOTED THAT HE COVERS THE BEST RECEIVERS EVERY WEEK.**

**AND HE PRAISED HIS USUAL EFFORT.**

**THERE WERE A COUPLE OF PLAYS WHEN THAT STANDARD SLIPPED. AND KUBIAK STILL EXPECTS TO COUNT ON HIM GOING FORWARD.**

Raider Nation, **that is how you do it. Bench the man for two plays so the message lands. Tell the truth publicly so the standard is clear. And then say — in the same breath — that he covers the best receiver on the other team every single week and you're counting on him.**

**And then he did the thing he ALWAYS does: HE SPREAD THE BLAME.**

**HE ALSO EXTENDED THE RESPONSIBILITY TO THE PASS RUSH. THE DEFENSE HAS TO CONTINUE ATTACKING THE QUARTERBACK AND PREVENT HIM FROM GETTING THE THROWAWAY.**

**ONE PLAYER GETS HIGHLIGHTED WHEN THE BALL REACHES AN OPEN RECEIVER — BUT STOPPING AN EXPLOSIVE PLAY REQUIRES THE ENTIRE UNIT TO FINISH ITS ASSIGNMENT.**

**Raider Nation, that's completely fair. Drew Lock was ALMOST DOWN. If the rush FINISHES him, Stokes' lapse never matters. The cornerback stopped because the sack looked certain — and the sack didn't happen.**

**"WE WIN TOGETHER AND WE LOSE TOGETHER." He's said it every single week.**

## AND THEN STOKES MADE IT ABOUT LEADERSHIP — AND THIS IS THE BEST PART

Raider Nation, this is the quote of the week and almost nobody will cover it.

**STOKES REFERRED TO THE TEAM'S OWN MESSAGE — AN ACRONYM THEY USE IN THAT BUILDING — AND EXPLAINED THAT THE FIRST LETTER STANDS FOR EFFORT.**

**HE SAID HE FAILED TO SHOW IT ON THAT PLAY. HE CALLED IT HIS LAPSE.**

**AND HE ACKNOWLEDGED THAT BEING A LEADER REQUIRES HOLDING HIMSELF TO A HIGHER STANDARD AND DOING THE LITTLE THINGS BETTER.**

Raider Nation, **a veteran cornerback took his team's own internal motto, pointed at the first letter of it, and said: I DIDN'T DO THAT.**

**That's not damage control. That's accountability with specifics. And it is EXACTLY the culture Klint Kubiak described from day one: "You can't sit in those chairs if you're not an ACCOUNTABLE HUMAN." And: "There's NO FINGER POINTING. IT'S THUMB POINTING."**

**HE RECEIVED A MAJOR PAYDAY THIS OFFSEASON — AND A YOUNG SECONDARY NEEDS CONSISTENCY FROM ITS VETERAN CORNER.**

**Raider Nation, that's the real stake. Hezekiah Masses is a rookie. Germad McCoy is still finding himself. Traden Stokes is a rookie safety. Darien Porter is young.**

**That room needs its paid veteran to be the one who NEVER stops running. Because the rookies are watching him do it.**

**AND AGAINST DENVER, THE NEXT STEP IS FINISHING THE COVERAGE EVEN WHEN THE QUARTERBACK APPEARS TO BE IN TROUBLE.**

**HE TALKED ABOUT THE HISTORY. THE HATRED. AND THE BACK AND FORTH BETWEEN THESE FRANCHISES.**

Raider Nation, **GEORGE ATKINSON. The Raiders safety from the 1970s. The man at the center of some of the most violent, most legendary, most CONTESTED moments in the history of this rivalry.**

## AND HE WAS HONEST ABOUT THE DISADVANTAGE

Raider Nation, he didn't pretend this is a fair fight.

**THERE'S A PREPARATION DISADVANTAGE TO ACCOUNT FOR. THE BRONCOS ARE COMING OFF THEIR BYE — GIVING THEM REST AND EXTRA TIME TO STUDY THE RAIDERS.**

**CROSBY ACKNOWLEDGED THAT DENVER IS A GOOD TEAM THAT HAS WON PLENTY OF GAMES — AND THAT SEAN PAYTON HAS HIS PLAYERS READY.**

**AND HIS ANSWER WAS TO KEEP THE FOCUS ON LAS VEGAS IMPROVING AS A UNIT.**

**"AS THE SEASON DEVELOPS," he said, "PLAYERS HAVE TO RECOGNIZE THEIR STRENGTHS, ADDRESS WHAT THEY'VE STRUGGLED WITH, AND KEEP REFINING THEIR WORK."**

**THE RAIDERS HAVE EXPERIENCED HIGHS AND LOWS — AND CROSBY SEES ANOTHER QUALITY OPPONENT AS A TEST THAT REVEALS WHERE THEY STAND.**

Raider Nation, **7-2, rested, at altitude, with Sean Payton — a man Kubiak himself called a future Hall of Famer whose teams are famous for how well they play IN THE FOURTH QUARTER.**

**And we're the team that scored twenty-seven yards in a second half.**

**That's the test. That's exactly what Crosby means.**

## AND SO — DID SEATTLE GIVE DENVER THE BLUEPRINT?

Raider Nation, here's the answer, and it's yes.

**Mendoza completed 17 OF 29 FOR 143 YARDS against Seattle, ran for a touchdown, posted a 71.5 PASSER RATING — AND TOOK SIX SACKS.**

**And LINCOLN KENNEDY — the former Raiders offensive lineman — warned that THOSE HITS CANNOT CONTINUE.**

**In his assessment, A QUARTERBACK'S YOUTH AND DURABILITY WON'T PROTECT HIM INDEFINITELY FROM REPEATED PUNISHMENT.**

**KENNEDY SAID THE BREAKDOWN HAS TO BE FIXED, AND STRESSED KNOWING WHERE A BLITZ IS COMING FROM. He specifically mentioned RECOGNIZING THE POSSIBILITY OF NICKEL PRESSURE AROUND A STACKED RECEIVER ALIGNMENT.**

**AND DENVER NOW HAS A RECIPE TO STUDY FROM WHAT OTHER DEFENSES HAVE DONE. AND KENNEDY EXPECTS COACHES FACING A YOUNG QUARTERBACK TO BRING PRESSURE.**

Raider Nation, **and here's the stat that makes it terrifying:**

**DENVER'S DEFENSE HAS TWENTY-SIX TEAM SACKS THIS SEASON.**

**Twenty-six. With Nik Bonitto and Zach Allen. Off a bye week. With two weeks to install whatever Seattle did to our rookie.**

**And Kubiak himself scouted them: "THEIR FRONT, THEIR BACKERS — DEFENSIVELY, VERY STOUT, VERY PHYSICAL TEAM."**

**And Kennedy's deeper point: a defense WILL KEEP RETURNING TO A PRESSURE UNTIL THE OFFENSE PROVES IT HAS AN ANSWER.**

**So yes, Raider Nation. The blueprint exists. It's on tape. And it works.**

## AND HERE'S HOW THE TWO STORIES CONNECT

Raider Nation, this is the smartest observation of the week and I want to make sure you catch it.

**CROSBY'S RIVALRY MESSAGE AND THE OFFENSE'S PROBLEM ARE THE SAME STORY.**

**DEFENSIVE INTENSITY CAN HELP KEEP THE SCORE MANAGEABLE. THE OFFENSE THEN HAS TO RECOGNIZE PRESSURE AND PROTECT ITS QUARTERBACK WELL ENOUGH TO TAKE ADVANTAGE OF THOSE CHANCES.**

Raider Nation, **that's complementary football described in two sentences. The defense buys time. The offense has to spend it.**

**And against Seattle, the defense bought four first downs' worth of time in a half — and the offense spent it on five possessions of negative yardage.**

**That cannot happen again in Denver.**

## AND NOW MENDOZA'S RESPONSE — AND I LOVE HIS ATTITUDE

Raider Nation, he was asked about facing another elite defense, and his answer was perfect.

**HE SAID HE BELIEVES BEING THROWN INTO THE FIRE IS THE BEST WAY TO LEARN.**

**HE DESCRIBED BOTH SEATTLE AND DENVER AS HAVING ELITE DEFENSES — AND THEN EXTENDED THAT RESPECT ACROSS THE ENTIRE LEAGUE.**

**"AT THIS LEVEL," he said, "EVERY DEFENSE IS ELITE — BECAUSE THE MARGIN FOR ERROR IS SMALL AND THE COORDINATORS ARE SO CAPABLE."**

Raider Nation, **"EVERY DEFENSE IS ELITE BECAUSE THE MARGIN FOR ERROR IS SMALL AND THE COORDINATORS ARE SO CAPABLE."**

**That's a 22-year-old refusing to give himself an excuse. He could have said "well, I drew the two best defenses in football in my first two starts" — AND HE WOULD HAVE BEEN RIGHT.**

**And instead he said: they're all elite, so there's nothing to complain about.**

**And remember Kirk Cousins' famous joke this season: "ANOTHER TOP-FIVE DEFENSE. I don't know who the bottom five defenses are — but WE'RE NOT PLAYING THEM."**

**Same observation. The kid just chose not to make it a punchline.**

**And "THROWN INTO THE FIRE IS THE BEST WAY TO LEARN" — Raider Nation, that's the same man who said after the loss: "IT'S GOOD TO GET PUNCHED IN THE FACE, because now it's A GREAT OPPORTUNITY TO GROW AND LEARN and to REALLY UNVEIL ALL THE DEFICIENCIES THAT I HAVE IN MY GAME."**

**He's consistent. That's who he is.**

## AND HE CREDITED THE MEN WHO MADE HIM READY

Raider Nation, this detail is lovely.

**MENDOZA CREDITED ROB LEONARD AND THE RAIDERS DEFENSE FOR HELPING PREPARE HIM.**

**FACING THAT GROUP DURING ORGANIZED TEAM ACTIVITIES, FALL CAMP AND TRAINING CAMP GAVE HIM WORK HE BELIEVES HE CAN DRAW ON NOW.**

**HE SAID HE WAS HONORED TO HAVE THAT PREPARATION AND EXCITED TO TAKE THE NEXT STEPS WITH HIS TEAMMATES.**

Raider Nation, **remember that a lot of national media LAUGHED at the idea that practicing against this defense proved anything. "If you're practicing against the Raiders defense, that's probably not the best measuring stick."**

**And the kid went out of his way to thank them anyway.**

**And it works both directions — Rob Leonard said about HIM: "TIGHT WINDOW THROWS. ANTICIPATION. It's been FUN TO SEE HIM MATURE — a guy playing with A LOT OF CONFIDENCE."**

**A quarterback and a defensive coordinator publicly crediting each other for each other's development. That's a healthy building, whatever the record says.**

## AND HERE'S WHAT HE SAYS HE NEEDS — AND IT'S NOT A RECEIVER

Raider Nation, this is the most revealing thing he said, and it's not what you'd expect.

**THAT CONFIDENCE COMES WITH AN ACKNOWLEDGEMENT THAT HE STILL NEEDS BETTER CHEMISTRY THROUGHOUT THE OFFENSE.**

**On Bowers — whose Week 11 availability remains unresolved — he called him A FANTASTIC ALL-WORLD PLAYER AND AN INTEGRAL PART OF THE ATTACK.**

**BUT HE ALSO MADE CLEAR THAT HIS WORK HAS TO EXTEND ACROSS THE LINEUP.**

**HE MENTIONED THE OPERATIONAL CONNECTION WITH THE OFFENSIVE LINE. GETTING MORE SNAPS WITH CENTER TYLER LINDERBAUM. COMMUNICATING WITH CONNOR HEYWARD. AND WORKING WITH THE TIGHT ENDS.**

Raider Nation, **look at that list. It's not "I need a star receiver." It's SNAPS WITH MY CENTER.**

**And that detail matters enormously. The quarterback-center exchange is where this all started going wrong — remember San Francisco, when Kirk Cousins fumbled the exchange on the first play of the second half and it became a pick-six? Kubiak: "IT STARTED WITH THE EXCHANGE."**

**And remember what Mendoza said about his own touchdown: "GREAT COMMUNICATION BY TYLER LINDERBAUM there, POINTING OUT WHERE THE PRESSURE WOULD COME FROM."**

**That's the partnership that beats a blitz. The center identifies it, the quarterback confirms it, and the protection slides correctly. Mike Macdonald beat them on exactly that — Mendoza slid the protection one way and Seattle came the other.**

**THOSE DETAILS SHOW HOW MUCH A QUARTERBACK HAS TO COORDINATE BEYOND CHOOSING A RECEIVER AND THROWING THE BALL.**

**MORE TIME TOGETHER CAN HELP PLAYERS UNDERSTAND WHAT EACH OTHER EXPECTS BEFORE AND DURING A PLAY.**

**AND MENDOZA ALSO EXPRESSED CONFIDENCE IN THE OTHER TARGETS AND PIECES AROUND HIM.**

**Raider Nation, he had every opportunity to hint that he needs better weapons. Five catches for forty yards from the receiver room gave him an open door.**

**He said he's confident in them, and the thing HE needs to fix is his communication. That's a leader.**
