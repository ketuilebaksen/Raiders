THE JEANTY SIGNAL EVERYBODY SHOULD READ CORRECTLY

First, the big one: the Raiders did NOT place Ashton Jeanty on injured reserve. And Raider Nation, that is a genuinely important development — but to understand WHY, you need to understand how the IR rules actually work, because this is where casual fans get lost.

Here's the quick classroom session. When a team places a player on injured reserve at this point of the calendar, that player is locked out for a minimum of four games — no exceptions, no take-backs. It clears a roster spot, sure, but it also removes any chance of seeing that player in September. So when a team keeps an injured player ON the 53 through cut-down day, it's making a public statement with real cost attached: we believe this man returns soon enough that a roster spot is worth burning while he heals. That's exactly what Las Vegas just did with Jeanty. By keeping him off IR, the Raiders are operating under the clear expectation that he's back within the first four weeks — with the door to Week 1 itself still technically open.

Now, let's stay measured. This does not mean Jeanty is a lock for the opener. The Raiders can and probably should take a cautious approach depending on how the ankle progresses. But it powerfully reinforces what Schefter reported from day one: this is not a long-term injury. And for an offense built around this man — remember, 1,321 scrimmage yards and ten touchdowns as a rookie behind the WORST line situation in football — that's everything. The entire Kubiak blueprint runs through Jeanty's legs: the downhill run game, the play-action off it, the checkdowns that turn into chunk plays. They paid Tyler Linderbaum $81 million to fix the interior specifically so this player could eat. Losing him long-term would have knocked the foundation out from under the whole project before a single meaningful snap.

Think about what this means for Miami specifically. If Jeanty suits up Week 1, even in a managed workload, defenses have to honor the threat that makes Kubiak's entire play-action structure work. If he doesn't, the game plan tilts toward the two backs who spent August EARNING trust. Mike Washington Jr. becomes a massive factor — and let's give the résumé its due, because it's now three games deep: six carries for 63 yards with the 53-yard house call against Arizona, nine carries at over six yards a pop against Houston, then 49 more efficient yards with the first team in the finale. Three games, three statements, zero flukes. And Dylan Laube — who ran his way onto this roster with that special-teams-plus-touches profile and a touchdown against Houston — rounds out the room. Either way, this is why we kept saying RB depth is insurance: the policy is paid up, and the room can survive a cautious timeline.

Quick note for my fantasy football people, because I know you're drafting and setting lineups right now: Jeanty's Week 1 status is genuinely uncertain, so have a plan. If Washington Jr. is still sitting on your waiver wire, that's potentially free gold — grab him before your league mates watch this video. But for the actual football team, the priority is simple: get Jeanty HEALTHY, and do not rush him back one day before he's ready. This is a franchise cornerstone on a rookie contract with a decade of football ahead of him. We need him for eighteen weeks — and for the next eight Januarys — not for one emotional opener.

THE MEREDITH TRADE, FULLY EXPLAINED

Now the offensive line — because we finally have the full details on the Jordan Meredith trade. Per Ian Rapoport, the Jets are sending the Raiders a 2027 SIXTH-round pick in exchange for a 2029 seventh-rounder. Strip it down: Las Vegas moved a veteran who was no longer part of the immediate plan and upgraded a far-future seventh into a much nearer sixth. That's found money.

And let's give Meredith his due, because this wasn't some anonymous camp body — and honestly, this channel has lived his entire arc with you. He's 28. Last season he played 13 games with 11 starts, brought real versatility with starting experience at right guard, and originally joined this organization back in 2022 on a futures contract — 45 games and 20 starts across four seasons in silver and black. The center transition had its struggles, and we documented them honestly: remember the Houston game, the A-gap communication bust, the criticism we didn't sugarcoat. But we also documented the response — the preseason finale where he allowed nothing in pass protection and graded out as the best offensive player on the field. That finale tape is exactly what made him SELLABLE. The Jets needed interior depth, they saw an experienced multi-position player closing camp on his best film, and Spytek turned that into draft capital instead of a farewell handshake.

There's a financial layer too, and it matters: Meredith was signed for $3.5 million in 2026 before hitting free agency in 2027. So the Raiders cleared that money AND added the pick — while the Jets get a plug-and-play veteran on a reasonable one-year cost. Everybody walks away satisfied, which is what good trades look like. Fit, timing, opportunity. That's the NFL.

And zoom out, because this is the Spytek signature we've tracked all summer: he does not let value walk out the door for free. The pattern is unmistakable — extract a pick for Meredith instead of cutting him, strike fast on Fatukasi when a need appeared, claim Luter with surgical selectivity, stash reclamation projects on the practice squad. Front offices that treat the bottom of the roster like a portfolio are the ones that survive the injury waves of November. For a franchise that spent two decades bleeding assets, this is what adult supervision looks like.

THE FINAL CUTS: TOKYO TOE, A FAMOUS NAME, AND ONE PIECE OF GOOD NEWS

The roster is down from 90 to 53, and while there were no earthquakes, several names deserve a proper goodbye — and one deserves a celebration.

Kansei Matsuzawa is out. No surprise — the Raiders had been open about where this was heading, and Matt Gay simply won the job on the field, exactly as we called it when he drilled that 54-yarder in the mock game weeks ago and then went four-of-five in the finale while Tokyo Toe watched in street clothes. But don't close the book on the dream yet, and let's remember what the dream IS: no player born in Japan has ever appeared in a regular season NFL game. That's what Matsuzawa is chasing, and it's why this fan base adopted him from day one. If he clears waivers, the practice squad path is real — and the international player pathway exemption we've talked about since the FIRST week of this channel could let the Raiders develop him without burning a standard spot. The pursuit isn't dead. It's delayed. And one more wrinkle worth remembering: Gay's leash isn't infinite either — his 73.9 percent from last season was worse than Daniel Carlson's 81.5, and Carlson just landed with the Saints. If the veteran wobbles in October, this kicking story gets a sequel. For now, though: Matt Gay is the Raiders' kicker, full stop.

Shedrick Jackson is also out — and here's the detail that makes this one sting extra: Jackson is the nephew of Raiders LEGEND Bo Jackson. The bloodline is real, and honestly, so were the flashes. Rewind our whole series and you'll find him in literally the first depth chart video, locked in that battle with Dererick Young for the last receiver spot — a fight we said back then would be decided "in the span of sixty preseason snaps." That's precisely how it went: Young's special teams value won the spot, and Jackson's flashes never quite became weekly consistency. The deep ball he couldn't quite haul in against Arizona, the moments that stayed moments. If he clears waivers, a practice squad return makes all the sense in the world — the talent is real, and some players simply need a longer runway. Bo knows patience. Hopefully his nephew gets to use some.

And now the GOOD news: Dont'e Thornton Jr. MADE the roster — and as of today's 53, he is NOT on injured reserve. That's a meaningful signal that the Raiders believe he has a real chance to be ready as the season begins, and with this receiver room's depth question, having Thornton's rare size-speed combination available instead of shelved genuinely matters. One caveat from your resident roster-rules nerd, though: keep an eye on this one over the next few days. Teams sometimes carry a player through cut-down day precisely so that a LATER move to injured reserve preserves his right to return during the season — remember, the timing of an IR move changes everything. So if you wake up to a "Thornton to IR" headline this week, don't panic and don't call anyone a liar: it would be the chess version of the move, not the checkers version. Either way, today's roster says the Raiders are protecting his season. That's the takeaway.
