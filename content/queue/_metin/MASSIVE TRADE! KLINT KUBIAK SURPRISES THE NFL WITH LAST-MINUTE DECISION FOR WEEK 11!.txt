## FIRST: HOW HE EXPLAINED MENDOZA'S START

Raider Nation, the whole country is talking about this kid, and here's the line: **ELEVEN OF THIRTEEN FOR NINETY YARDS AND A TOUCHDOWN in that first half.**

Asked how he had him ready to go:

**"I think you start with just THE WEEK BEFORE — him TAKING THE REINS and having a REALLY GOOD WEEK OF PRACTICE."**

**"He SHOWED HIS TEAMMATES THAT HE WAS READY TO GO. Very EXCITED ABOUT WHERE HIS CAREER CAN BE — BUT IT'S ONE GAME AT A TIME."**

**"He was PREPARED. And as a coaching staff, as a team, YOU'VE GOT TO FINISH THE GAME. THAT'S THE MOST IMPORTANT THING — WINNING THESE GAMES."**

**"But I WAS ENCOURAGED WITH WHAT WE SAW."**

Raider Nation, **notice the order again. Praise the kid, then immediately take the failure onto the coaching staff: "AS A COACHING STAFF, AS A TEAM, YOU'VE GOT TO FINISH THE GAME."**

**Eleven of thirteen in a half. Seven-for-seven to start his career. And then zero for seven on third down after halftime and twenty-seven total yards in the second half.**

**"He showed his TEAMMATES that he was ready." Raider Nation, that's the second time he's framed it that way — not that he showed the COACHES, but that he showed the LOCKER ROOM. That's how a quarterback earns a huddle.**

**And "THE EXTRA DIMENSION OF USING HIS FEET." That's the third time this week Kubiak has used that word. "It GIVES US A DIFFERENT DIMENSION." That's the thing Kirk Cousins could never give this team.**

## AND NOW BROCK BOWERS — AND THE "ONE WHEEL" STORY

Raider Nation, this is the headline and I want to give you every word.

Asked what he can tell us about Bowers and how he's recovered from injuries in the past:

**"I can just tell you — the fans know this — but JUST HOW TOUGH HE IS."**

**"Because HE CAME BACK IN THAT GAME AND TRIED TO GUT IT OUT. And MADE A BIG CATCH FOR US. I think it was A THIRD DOWN PLAY THAT HE CAUGHT — ON ONE WHEEL."**

**"HE'S JUST ALL RAIDERS, ALL TEAM, AND A TOUGH GUY."**

**"AND HE'LL BE BACK. AND HOPEFULLY IT'S THIS WEEK."**

Raider Nation. **ON ONE WHEEL.**

**Here's the full sequence of what happened on Sunday, and I want you to appreciate it: on the OPENING DRIVE, Kolton Miller and a Seahawks defender rolled onto his RIGHT ankle during an Ashton Jeanty run. He walked off. He got treatment. He went back out there. And he caught a twelve-yard pass ON THIRD DOWN while barely able to push off that leg.**

**And then his body said no, and the team ruled him out.**

**Raider Nation, two catches for eighteen yards is a stat line that looks like nothing. It was actually a man dragging a hurt ankle back onto the field to convert a third down for a rookie quarterback in his first start.**

**"ALL RAIDERS, ALL TEAM, AND A TOUGH GUY."**

**And "HOPEFULLY IT'S THIS WEEK" — Raider Nation, I'm not going to promise you anything, and neither did he. Wednesday and Friday's practice reports are the only real answers. But that is a warmer forecast than we had on Monday, and I'll take it.**

## AND THE TURNOVER NUMBER IS THE BEST DEFENSIVE NEWS OF THE SEASON

Raider Nation, this is genuinely important.

Asked about causing two turnovers:

**"That SECOND PLAY OF THE GAME — our defense came up with a BIG ONE. And PLAYED COMPLIMENTARY FOOTBALL by HAVING A SCORING DRIVE AFTER THAT. And then LATE IN THE GAME, obviously, HAVING ANOTHER ONE."**

**"That was A STEP IN THE RIGHT DIRECTION DEFENSIVELY. And just REALLY, REALLY PLEASED with our guys TAKING THE BALL AWAY — BEING PLUS TWO IN THE TURNOVER BATTLE. YOU'VE GOT TO WIN THOSE GAMES."**

**PLUS TWO IN THE TURNOVER BATTLE.**

Raider Nation, **think about where this team was two weeks ago. FIVE TURNOVERS in a single half in San Francisco. Four consecutive giveaway possessions. Kubiak: "When you turn the ball over five times, YOU'RE NOT GOING TO WIN. EVER."**

**And on Sunday: PLUS TWO. Zero giveaways. Two takeaways. The second play of the game — Quay Walker's fumble recovery at Seattle's 46 — and another one late.**

**"YOU'VE GOT TO WIN THOSE GAMES."**

**Raider Nation, he's right, and that's what makes this loss so maddening. We won the turnover battle by two, committed zero penalties, held the defending champions to FOUR FIRST DOWNS in the first half, and led 10-7 at halftime.**

**And we lost 21-13, because the offense managed twenty-seven yards in the second half.**

**And look at the phrase he used: "PLAYED COMPLIMENTARY FOOTBALL by having a SCORING DRIVE AFTER THAT." Raider Nation, that's the thing nobody on this team has managed all season. For one drive in the first quarter, it finally happened.**

**Raider Nation, that's not a coach losing his locker room. That's a coach who has one.**

## AND NOW THE FOUR WORDS THAT EXPLAIN THE WHOLE LOSS

Raider Nation, this is the most important thing he said all week, and it's going to be written on a wall in that building.

Asked about the two Seattle touchdowns that everybody described as BROKEN PLAYS — the Cooper Kupp one and the Jaxon Smith-Njigba catch across the middle where he just took off:

**"Number one is — YOU'VE GOT TO PLAY THROUGH THE ECHO OF THE WHISTLE. AND WE DIDN'T DO THAT ON THOSE TWO PLAYS. AND GAVE UP FOURTEEN POINTS."**

**"So you SHOW THE TEAM. SHOW THEM HOW YOU HAVE TO FINISH PLAYS."**

**"THE EXPECTATION IS FOR US TO FINISH EVERY PLAY. AND WHEN YOU DON'T, YOU SEE WHAT HAPPENS — AND IT CHANGES THE ENTIRE GAME."**

**"SO WE'VE GOT TO LEARN FROM IT REAL FAST."**

**PLAY THROUGH THE ECHO OF THE WHISTLE.**

Raider Nation, **that is a beautiful phrase and it is the most precise diagnosis of this loss anybody has offered.**

**Not until the whistle. Through the ECHO of the whistle. Keep running until the sound has stopped bouncing off the back wall of the stadium.**

**And here's why it's exactly right: BOTH of Seattle's touchdowns were off-script. A play broke down, Drew Lock extended it outside the pocket, and somewhere on that field a Raider slowed down because the play looked over.**

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba had ONE CATCH FOR ELEVEN YARDS in the first half. Then 144 YARDS IN THE THIRD QUARTER ALONE.**

**Fourteen points on two plays. In a game we lost by eight.**

**Raider Nation, take those two snaps back and we're flying home from Las Vegas with a win over the defending world champions.**

**And remember what Rob Leonard told us Thursday: "FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL. When you're on the field a long time, MISTAKES TEND TO HAPPEN MORE OFTEN."** **Five consecutive three-and-outs by the offense put those eleven men right back on the grass. And on snap number forty-something, somebody's legs stopped running through the echo.**

**That's the whole game. The offense caused the fatigue. The fatigue caused the fourteen points.**

## AND NOW THE SIX SACKS — AND HE FLIPPED IT COMPLETELY

Raider Nation, this is the answer that genuinely changed my outlook, and I did not expect it.

Asked how you clean up six sacks knowing other defenses will try the same thing — especially after Fernando took so much ownership at the podium:

**"The SECOND HALF of that game was VERY BLITZ HEAVY. And CREDIT TO SEATTLE for having some GOOD PRESSURES."**

**"And I think that's just something YOU'RE GOING TO SEE AS A ROOKIE QUARTERBACK."**

**And then — Raider Nation, here it is:**

**"And NOW IT'S THE ABILITY TO GO — WHEN THEY'RE BLITZING, THEY'RE GOING TO BE SHORT IN COVERAGE. SO THAT'S A CHANCE FOR US TO MAKE BIG PLAYS."**

**"SO YOU WANT TEAMS TO BLITZ YOU."**

**YOU WANT TEAMS TO BLITZ YOU.**

Raider Nation, **that is the single most encouraging sentence of this entire week, and here's the football behind it.**

**A blitz is a TRADE. Every extra man you send at the quarterback is one fewer man in coverage. Send six and you've got five defenders covering five receivers with nobody deep to help.**

**That means somebody is open. Always. Every single time.**

**The problem on Sunday wasn't that Seattle blitzed. The problem was that Mendoza didn't find the man the blitz left uncovered — he said so himself: "I NEED TO UNDERSTAND MY HOT ROUTES — WHICH I NEED TO DO BETTER."**

**And Raider Nation, the hot route is THE ANSWER TO A BLITZ. It's a pre-arranged quick route that beats the pressure. Six sacks means six times the answer existed and nobody got to it in time.**

**And here's the wrinkle Mike Macdonald used to make it so hard — Mendoza described it himself: "they were showing a FOUR-WEAK BLITZ TO THE BOUNDARY. So I made a check, SLID THE PROTECTION THAT WAY — AND THEY DROPPED THAT SIDE AND CAME THE OTHER SIDE. HE WAS PLAYING CHESS."**

**Raider Nation, so the fix isn't "block better." It's: diagnose the disguise, find the uncovered man, and let it go. And when he does? "THAT'S A CHANCE FOR US TO MAKE BIG PLAYS."**

**A head coach who says "you WANT teams to blitz you" is telling you he thinks this is a solvable problem and the solution is EXPLOSIVE. That's not spin. That's math.**

## AND MAXX CROSBY HAD HIMSELF AN AFTERNOON

Raider Nation, this got buried under the loss and it shouldn't have.

**MAXX CROSBY HAD FOUR TACKLES FOR LOSS AND A SACK.**

**Four tackles for loss. From a man who gets chipped and doubled on essentially every snap — sixteen chips and doubles in a single game earlier this season.**

And Kubiak's answer about him was a philosophy statement:

**"Maxx EVERY WEEK has that kind of EFFORT. And it's GOOD TO SEE THE PRODUCTION COME WITH THAT."**

**"When 98'S IN THE GAME — when he's playing with that kind of energy — HE AFFECTS THE ENERGY OF THE WHOLE BUILDING. OFFENSIVELY AND DEFENSIVELY."**

**"And that AGGRESSION GOT HIM IN TROUBLE IN THE FOUR MINUTE. SO WE'VE GOT TO CONTAIN THE QUARTERBACK THERE."**

**"BUT THAT'S WHAT WE WANT OUR PROGRAM TO BE ABOUT — THE EFFORT THAT HE SHOWS ON A DOWN-TO-DOWN BASIS."**

Raider Nation, **there was a CONTAIN ISSUE on a BOOTLEG in the four-minute — and that's a real coaching point, because that's exactly how Drew Lock extended plays. An edge rusher who crashes upfield opens a highway for a quarterback rolling the other way.**

**But look at how he handled it: named the mistake, explained the fix, and then said THAT EFFORT IS WHAT WE WANT OUR PROGRAM TO BE ABOUT.**

**And "when 98's in the game he affects the energy of the whole building" — Raider Nation, that's why John Spytek said no to every team that called about him at the deadline. "WE SAY NO A LOT MORE THAN WE SAY YES."**

**He's still a Raider. Four tackles for loss. Good.**

## NOW DENVER — AND THE SCOUTING REPORT IS SOBERING

Raider Nation, here's what he sees. Let's go through it.

**ON THE RUNNING BACK, JONAH COLEMAN — a rookie who's been put into a big spot:**

**"I see A VERY GOOD PLAYER. I see OBVIOUSLY AN OFFENSIVE LINE THAT IS PHYSICAL. THEY'VE PLAYED TOGETHER FOR A LONG TIME. And THEY HAVE AN IDENTITY OF RUNNING THE FOOTBALL."**

Raider Nation, **stop right there, because that is the worst possible sentence for our defense.**

**"THEY'VE PLAYED TOGETHER FOR A LONG TIME." That's CONTINUITY — the exact thing Kirk Cousins warned us about with San Francisco: "they've got a lot of CONTINUITY AND IT SHOWS IN THE TAPE."**

**"AN IDENTITY OF RUNNING THE FOOTBALL." Raider Nation, we allow 151 RUSHING YARDS A GAME — THE MOST IN THE LEAGUE. And now a physical, veteran offensive line that WANTS to run it is coming.**

**That's the whole game right there. If Denver runs it successfully, Bo Nix — 1,730 yards and eleven touchdowns — never has to make a difficult throw.**

**ON THE RECEIVERS: "COURTLAND SUTTON and JAYLEN WADDLE. He's QUESTIONABLE — we'll wait and see at the end of the week. But THEY HAVE RECEIVERS WHO ARE STRONG ACROSS THE MIDDLE, AND THEY'VE GOT A BURNER ON THE OUTSIDE."**

**"Courtland's been MAKING PLAYS FOR A LONG TIME. They have A LOT OF GUYS WITH DIFFERENT SKILL SETS. They have A VERY TALENTED OFFENSE."**

Raider Nation, **"STRONG ACROSS THE MIDDLE and a BURNER ON THE OUTSIDE" — that is EXACTLY the combination that beat us on Sunday.**

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba went for 144 yards in a quarter by attacking the middle of the field. And remember the analysis: Seattle "MOVED SMITH-NJIGBA AROUND AND ATTACKED UNDERNEATH AREAS WHERE OUR SECOND-LEVEL DEFENDERS HAVE STRUGGLED."**

**Our second level has struggled against exactly that. And Denver has it. Watch Jaylen Waddle's status all week.**
