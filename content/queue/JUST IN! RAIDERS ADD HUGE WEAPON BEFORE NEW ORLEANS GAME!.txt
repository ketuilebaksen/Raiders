Klint Kubiak walks into the Caesars Superdome on Sunday afternoon, and that building used to be his. He called plays for the Saints. Now he's on the opposite sideline, trying to beat them in their own house, in their home opener, in front of a crowd that is going to be absolutely unhinged.

If you put that in a movie, somebody would say it's too on the nose. This is real, and 3-0 is on the table.

FIRST, SOMETHING BIGGER THAN FOOTBALL

Before any of the football, I want to start with this, because it matters more.

Aidan O'Connell has been away from the team dealing with a personal family matter, and on Thursday night he shared why. His father, Jim, passed away. He posted a tribute calling his father a gift to him and to so many others, and said his family is leaning on their faith to get through it.

This is the second time this family has been through a loss like this, having lost Aidan's older brother Sean in 2022.

Maxx Crosby and Brock Bowers were among the teammates who publicly sent their support, and I want this channel to do the same. Aidan, from all of Raider Nation, we're thinking about you and your family.

I'll also say this plainly: this week, this channel has nothing to say about trade speculation involving him. There was talk about his value around the league because of the quarterback injuries elsewhere. That conversation can wait. A man just lost his father.

MASSES SWEPT THE AWARDS, AND MADE HISTORY

Now to the football, and to the most surprising story in the entire NFL through two weeks.

Hezekiah Masses, a fifth-round pick out of Cal, was named the AFC Defensive Player of the Week. He was also named the league's Rookie of the Week. Both awards, same week, off one football game.

In his first career start, thrown into the fire against Justin Herbert, he finished with two interceptions, five tackles, a tackle for loss, and two more passes defended.

And the history is remarkable. He's the seventh rookie in Raiders franchise history to grab two interceptions in a single game, and the first to do it since 1979. Nineteen seventy-nine. Nearly half a century of Raiders football, and a fifth-round pick nobody was talking about did it in his first start.

He's also the first Raiders rookie to win a league weekly award since a guy named Maxx Crosby in 2019. If that comparison doesn't tell you what the ceiling might be here, nothing will.

But here's the number that should worry every quarterback left on our schedule. Through two games, Masses has been targeted seven times. He has allowed two completions. Two, for a total of 16 yards. Add the two interceptions and three passes defended, and the passer rating allowed when the ball comes his way sits at 0.0.

There are only two cornerbacks in the entire NFL who can say that through two weeks, and the other one plays in Kansas City. A fifth-rounder out of Cal, standing next to a first-rounder on that list.

THE DEFENSE IS THE IDENTITY NOW

And this isn't a one-man show. Look at where this unit ranks through two weeks.

First in the NFL in pressure rate against opposing quarterbacks. Tied for first in sacks with eight. Tied for first in forced fumbles with six. Second in passer rating allowed. Second in completion percentage allowed. Second in takeaways.

That's not a fluke. That's a defense that has flipped the identity of this franchise in fourteen days.

And the wildest part, the part I keep coming back to, is how they're doing it. They're getting home with four and five rushers, dropping seven and eight into coverage, and still leading the league in pressure. That's the luxury every defensive coordinator in football wants and almost nobody has. It lets Rob Leonard disguise coverages, put extra bodies in throwing lanes, and still make the quarterback uncomfortable. Most teams have to choose. We don't.

THE MATCHUP THAT MAKES ME SMILE

Now the reason I feel good about Sunday.

New Orleans lost starting left tackle Kelvin Banks Jr. last week to a severe high ankle sprain that requires surgery and could keep him out for a couple of months. Banks was a first-round pick a year ago and had been playing at a high level. In his place steps Asim Richards, a backup who has started five games in his entire career, only one of them at left tackle.

And who's lining up across from that? Maxx Crosby. The same Crosby who drew three false starts out of the Chargers' left tackle and closed that game with a safety, leading a pass rush that ranks first in the league in pressure rate.

That's a matchup that makes defensive coordinators drool: an inexperienced tackle making one of his first starts at the position, in a loud building, with a unit behind him that has already forced six fumbles.

But I'll be fair, because the Saints have their own front. Chase Young is a real problem off the edge, and Kaden Elliss is a linebacker who blitzes effectively, which has given New Orleans one of the more disruptive fronts in the league so far. This is genuinely an even chess match up front.

The good news is that Kirk Cousins has had more time than anybody expected this season with Kolton Miller anchoring that line. Now, I'll add my own note here, because I watched the Chargers film: the interior of our offensive line had a rough afternoon at SoFi, and the run game suffered for it. So the national view that this line has been outstanding and my own view that the middle needs work can both be true. Give Cousins time and this passing game hurts you. That's the part everybody agrees on.

THE INJURY REPORT

Here's where we stand going in.

Trayden Stokes has officially been ruled out. He did not practice all week and remains in the concussion protocol. Kubiak said only that Stokes is doing everything the doctors ask and they'll have him back when they have him back, which is coach language for nobody knows yet. That's the right way to handle a head injury, and I won't complain about it once. But losing a starting safety against a team that wants to throw is bad timing.

The silver lining is significant: Darien Porter is back. He missed the Chargers game with a foot injury, was a full participant all week, and looks ready to return to the lineup.

That gives Rob Leonard the best kind of problem. Does Porter slide straight back into his old job? Does Masses keep the job he just won Defensive Player of the Week for? And where does Jermod McCoy fit, the Tennessee product who fell to the fourth round purely because of a knee injury and was considered one of the best cover corners in the entire draft class? A month ago this room was called a liability. Now it's a logjam.

Brock Bowers is listed as questionable. He was limited most of the week and reportedly held out entirely on the final day. Remember how they handled him last week: warmups on the field, decision right up against kickoff. Expect the same.

And watch Kwity Paye, who was added to the report Thursday with an illness. Paye is the threat opposite Crosby that forces offensive coordinators to deal with two problems on passing downs. If he's limited, one nightmare comes off the board for that backup tackle.

Here's the peace of mind, though. This offense has won two straight games without its best weapon playing a meaningful snap. Cousins has had to spread the ball around instead of leaning on number 89 every time he's in trouble, and that's made this offense harder to defend, not easier. If Bowers plays, it's a bonus. If he doesn't, we've already proven it twice.

KNOW THE OPPONENT: DREADFUL FIRST HALVES, DEADLY FINISHES

The Saints are 1-1, and they've been anything but boring.

In Week One they nearly stunned Detroit in overtime, coming up a two-point conversion short. Last week they trailed Baltimore most of the afternoon and then roared back for a 24-17 comeback win as significant underdogs.

That's the pattern: genuinely poor first halves, and then they come alive after the break. And Tyler Shough leads the entire NFL in passing yards right now, partly because they've had to throw it around with an unreliable run game and a defense that has its own issues.

So the message for Sunday is simple. Do not let this team hang around. If the Saints are within a score in the fourth quarter, history says they'll find a way to make it painful.

There's also a specific tendency worth exploiting: when Shough is under pressure and forced to hold the ball, he'll take the sack rather than throw it away. That plays directly into the hands of a defense ranked first in pressure rate without blitzing. Get home clean, force one bad decision, and the takeaway numbers keep climbing.

THE KEY TO THE WHOLE GAME: THEIR RUN DEFENSE

And now the number that decides this game for me.

The Saints' run defense ranks 27th in the league, allowing well over 130 yards a game on the ground.

Twenty-seventh. Against a team whose head coach wants nothing more than to run the football.

That's the answer to last week's problem. Ashton Jeanty was bottled up at SoFi for 2.3 yards a carry, and the play calling stayed committed anyway. This is the week that patience gets paid. Hammer that weakness early, and everything else opens up: play action works, third downs get short, Shough's offense stays on the sideline, and a secondary missing a starting safety spends less time on the field.

If Bowers can go even in a limited role, it gets better, because now a bad run defense also has to respect a tight end who wins in coverage.

THE OTHER NON-NEGOTIABLE: NO EXPLOSIVE PLAYS

There's one more defensive rule for Sunday, and with a starting safety out, it becomes the whole ballgame.

This Saints offense wants it both ways: a quick strike over the top, and then a long methodical drive when you take the deep ball away. The job is to remove the first option entirely. Make them earn every single yard. Twelve-play drives are survivable; two 50-yard touchdowns are not.

And remember the specific warning from the Baltimore tape. When safeties were the primary coverage defenders, Shough completed all eight of his passes for 118 yards and a touchdown. With Stokes ruled out, whoever plays that spot is going to get tested early and often, and Isaiah Pola-Mao has already had a moment against the Chargers where a receiver got in behind him in zone.

The answer isn't to play scared. It's the same answer it always is with this defense: get home with four rushers so that deep routes never have time to develop. A quarterback on the ground can't take the top off anything.

THE LITTLE THINGS

Two more things that could decide a close road game.

Clean football. No untimely penalties, no unforced turnovers, no twelve men on the field coming out of a timeout. This team has been fundamentally sound for two weeks. That discipline now has to travel into a building designed to create exactly those mistakes.

And special teams, which has quietly been among the best in football. A.J. Cole has been putting on a clinic flipping the field, and Matt Gay, who had question marks entering the season, just drilled a career-long 59-yarder. If this turns into a field-position game, having a punter like Cole is a real weapon.

THE STAKES

The oddsmakers have the Saints as three-point home favorites with the total around 44 and a half, and analysts around the league have started quietly calling this the sneaky best game of the Week Three slate.

Think about what a win does. It makes this team 3-0 heading into a home date with Kansas City at Allegiant. It turns a feel-good story into a statement. And every lazy "yeah, but" excuse used to explain away this start stops working, because you cannot call the Saints an NFC South favorite and then dismiss the team that beat them in their own building.

A defense that leads the league in pressure. A rookie corner making history. A head coach walking back into the building where he used to work. And an opponent that's never dead until the clock hits zero.

I'm staying with my pick: Raiders 27, Saints 23. Stop the run, feed Jeanty against the 27th-ranked run defense, let Crosby hunt a backup tackle, and survive the fourth quarter.
