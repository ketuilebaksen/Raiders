## NOW THE FILM — HERE IS EXACTLY HOW McVAY DID IT

This is the part of the video I need you to really watch, because the breakdown on the broadcast was outstanding and it explains everything.

**Sean McVay came in with a very good game plan built on MISDIRECTION. And he aimed it directly at the youngest players on the Raiders' defense.**

Here's the mechanism, step by step, because once you see it you can't unsee it.

**Step one: McVay knew his own team wasn't healthy.** So instead of trying to win with firepower he didn't have, **he spent the week circling young Raiders defenders on the film.** Not schemes. **Kids.**

**Step two: he used MOTION to make the defense declare itself.** When you motion a receiver across the formation and a defender travels with him, **you've just told the quarterback: that's man-to-man.** Free information, before the snap, every single time.

**Step three — and this is the kill shot — he paired that man coverage with PLAY ACTION.** So now a young corner who knows he's alone on an island has a run fake happening right in front of him. **His eyes go to the backfield. Are they handing it off?**

**And as the analysts put it: it only takes ONE STEP in that kind of red zone situation to put you at risk.**

**One step. That's it.** The young man peeks, the receiver runs past him, **and the quarterback — facing a Raiders defense with a reigning MVP under center — puts it right on the money.** Touchdown.

**They showed it again and again.** They motioned **Puka Nacua — who the broadcast called maybe the best receiver in the National Football League** — just to make the defense reveal itself and adjust. **Bump, bump, bump across the formation.** And then one of our young corners, matched up against a 30-year-old veteran who has seen literally everything in this sport, **had his eyes in the wrong place for a half-second, and a man was standing wide open in the end zone.**

**And the brutal detail: "He's just running through. Still got his flags on you."** Untouched. **Nobody even got a hand on him.**

Raider Nation, **that's not a talent gap. That's an EXPERIENCE gap.** As the broadcast said: *"These are young guys. First time in a situation they've never been in before."* **McVay found the inexperience on our roster and attacked it with surgical precision for four quarters.**

**He will learn from these mistakes.** That's the honest, hopeful version. **But it cost us a football game.**

## "TRUST YOUR BROTHER" — THE BEST COACHING POINT OF THE DAY

Now here's my favorite moment of the whole broadcast, because it explained the missed tackles AND the blown coverages with one idea.

One of the analysts — a man who said he coaches high school and youth football — got genuinely passionate about it:

**"I tell my kids defensively: TRUST YOUR BROTHER. You can't make 19 tackles every single play. Trust your brother. If your brother's got the A gap, let him play the A gap. You don't play the A gap."**

And here's the diagnosis: **when the Rams were running the air out of the football, Raiders defenders stopped trusting the man beside them.** They started peeking into the backfield, trying to make a play that wasn't theirs to make — **and that's exactly when Sean McVay hit them over the top.**

Raider Nation, **that's what a coach means when he says a loss is "mental."** Not stupid. **Not lazy. Over-eager.** A young defense trying so hard to fix a run game problem that they abandoned their own assignments and gave up touchdowns.

**And remember what the special teams coordinator said on Friday: "We have a group of pleasers here. People whose job is important to them."** Same disease, different unit. **These guys care so much that they're playing out of position trying to be the hero.**

**And the tackling: "Guys getting out of there for missed tackles. We can't have this if we're going to be a quality defense."** Rob Leonard has been drilling space tackling with rugby bags in Week 7 precisely because he saw this coming. **It hasn't taken hold yet.**

## NOW THE OFFENSIVE CRISIS — AND IT'S THE BIGGEST STORY ON THIS TEAM

Raider Nation, buckle up, because this section is the real diagnosis of the 2026 Las Vegas Raiders.

The analysts went to the offensive tape and found one thing over and over: **man-to-man coverage, and nobody getting open.**

**Here's the first example.** Rams brought empty pressure — **more rushers than the Raiders could block, with a free runner coming clean.** Cousins tried to get the ball out fast. **And the receiver coaching point was gold:** when you know empty pressure is coming, **your route gets sped up and your eyes snap back to the quarterback faster, so you can find the football.** **"We've got to get better in these situations."**

**Here's the second, and it's smart.** The Raiders do a great job getting into **bunch formations**, because — in the analyst's words — **"Coach Kubiak knows teams want to play a lot of man-to-man, and the hardest thing about playing man is defending bunch formations."** You have to wade through all that garbage. **So the scheme is right.** **The scheme is not the problem.**

**And then came the hardest thing said about this team all season.**

They pulled up a clip of **Brock Bowers getting zero separation** in man coverage, and the analyst did not flinch:

**"I don't care if this is Tom Brady, Aaron Rodgers, Kirk Cousins or Dan Marino. I don't care who it is. This is NO SEPARATION by Brock Bowers. Cousins does a good job stepping up in the pocket, but that's covered. Sticky. There's nowhere to put that football."**

Raider Nation, **I love Brock Bowers. He is the best tight end in football and one of the best players this franchise has.** But he is being asked to function as a number one WIDE RECEIVER because this team doesn't have one — **and against a disciplined man-coverage team, a tight end running a receiver's route sheet gets blanketed.**

**And then: "We have to give credit to the Rams for playing really good man-to-man coverage. That's an inch of separation. Kirk really has nowhere to put that football."**
