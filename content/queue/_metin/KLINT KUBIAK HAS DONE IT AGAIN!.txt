## FIRST: THE DIAGNOSIS IS NOW OFFICIALLY "FOUR QUARTERS"

Raider Nation, asked whether what he sees on film is just the angles or something deeper, Kubiak kept it simple:

**"It's just playing BETTER FOUR QUARTERS. We've had SOLID halves. We've had MORE AGGRESSIVE halves. And we've had some REALLY BAD FIRST HALVES."**

**"So we've got to find a way to play a FOUR QUARTER GAME."**

Raider Nation, **that's the whole season in one sentence, and it's a different diagnosis from "the defense is bad."**

**It's not that this team can't play. It's that this team plays in HALVES.**

**And the proof is in the ugliest number from Sunday.**

## AND HERE IS THAT NUMBER — IT'S THE WORST STAT OF THE SEASON

Asked what it takes to get off to a faster start, after multiple players brought up the slow starts in the locker room, Kubiak laid out the first half snap by snap.

**And then he dropped this: "Overall you look at just the PLAY COUNT, and it was like 38 TO 17. That's the EXACT OPPOSITE of how you want time of possession to go in a first half."**

**Thirty-eight plays to seventeen.**

Raider Nation, **San Francisco ran more than DOUBLE the offensive plays we did in the first half.**

**That's not a bad defense. That's a defense that was never allowed off the field.** **And it's not a bad offense either — it's an offense that never got to touch the ball.**

**You cannot fix a run defense that plays 38 snaps in a half. You can't fix ANYTHING at 38 to 17. Players get tired. Linemen stop holding ground. Linebackers stop playing downhill. By the third drive, the wide box isn't a scheme choice — it's a surrender.**

**Raider Nation, THAT is the real story of the 38-17 loss. Time of possession.**

## AND HE WENT THROUGH THE FIRST DRIVE LIKE A MAN WHO'S WATCHED IT TWENTY TIMES

Raider Nation, listen to the detail here, because this is a coach who's done his homework.

**Defensively: "Being able to have the MENTAL TOUGHNESS on long drives to FINISH DRIVES OUT — okay, even if it's with FIELD GOALS. Defensively, start there."**

**Raider Nation, that's a brutal admission dressed up politely. He's saying: I'll take giving up THREE. Just get off the field.** Three long drives turning into touchdowns instead of field goals is the entire difference between 38-17 and a one-score game.

**Offensively: "We had opportunities on that first drive. We had TWO OF THOSE THREE opportunities that we MISSED. Especially — we get to THIRD AND SHORT on the very FIRST THIRD DOWN of the game. We don't convert."**

**"When you get to THIRD AND THREE on the first drive, you EXPECT TO CONVERT and maintain possession right there."**

**"The second drive was MUCH, MUCH better."**

Raider Nation, **third and three on the opening drive of the football game. That's the snap.** **Convert it and you keep the ball, you keep their offense on the sideline, and you don't start a 38-to-17 play-count spiral.**

**One third and three. That's how close the margin is.**

## AND THEN HE TOLD US HOW CLOSE THIS GAME ACTUALLY WAS

Raider Nation, this is the part that's going to make you sick.

**"But then you get the game to 21 TO 10, with the ball coming RIGHT BACK TO YOU, and you put yourself in position."**

**"And we turned the ball over FIVE TIMES in the second half as an offense. So that's not going to give you opportunities to win any games."**

**And then the one that really stings: "Even LATE in the game — defense gets an INTERCEPTION. We get a STOP ON FOURTH DOWN. And there's TWO CHANCES to make that a 28-24 BALL GAME. And we TURN THE FOOTBALL OVER."**

**"So — SHOT OURSELVES IN THE FOOT."**

**Raider Nation, 28-24. He said it himself. TWENTY-EIGHT, TWENTY-FOUR.**

**That's the game that was sitting there. The defense handed the offense an interception. The defense handed the offense a fourth-down stop. Two possessions, two chances to make it a one-score game in the fourth quarter — and both ended in giveaways.**

**A 38-17 final score that was a four-point game waiting to happen. That's the most Raiders sentence of this entire season.**

## AND THE FOURTEEN POINTS HE NAMED

Raider Nation, he came back to this later and put an actual number on what was left on the field.

Asked about the play where **Brock Bowers appeared to reach the ball over the goal line on a catch-and-run in the fourth quarter** — and why it wasn't challenged:

**"As it was going, upstairs we didn't feel like we had the CONFIDENT LOOK. So — in HINDSIGHT, it looks like HE MIGHT HAVE BROKE THE PLANE right there. In real time, that was the information we got."**

**"We stayed on the ball, and we got STUFFED. And then we got an OFFENSIVE PASS INTERFERENCE. And then we THROW A PICK."**

**"So we've got to find a way to get that ball in REGARDLESS."**

**And then: "We missed out on — there's that one, there's THERE'S FOURTEEN POINTS that we missed out on that makes that a MUCH CLOSER GAME."**

**Fourteen points.**

Raider Nation, **I want to be fair to the coach on the challenge itself, because this is how replay actually works.** **The booth told him they didn't have a confident look. If you challenge without a confident look and lose, you've burned a timeout AND a challenge in a game you're trying to climb back into.** **That's a defensible real-time decision with bad information.**

**But his own point is the better one: GET THE BALL IN REGARDLESS. Don't leave it to a replay official. Score from the one.**

**And the sequence after is the whole season in four plays: stuffed, offensive pass interference, interception. Fourteen points vanish and the scoreboard says twenty-one.**

Raider Nation, this is my favorite answer of the press conference, and almost nobody will write about it.

A reporter asked about the play where **Tonka Hemingway and Adam Butler dropped back in coverage, and Tonka ended up covering Christian McCaffrey.** The reporter was careful: *I'm not blaming either one of them.*

**And Kubiak's answer was four short sentences: "It was. We did NOT PEEL ON THE HALFBACK. That was NOT THOSE GUYS' JOB."**

**That was not those guys' job.**

Raider Nation, **think about what just happened there.** **He was handed a free opportunity to let two defensive linemen take the blame for a coverage bust. The question was even TEED UP for it.**

**And he said: somebody else didn't do their assignment, and it wasn't them.**

**He didn't name the player who missed it. He just took the blame off the two guys who were on camera looking lost.**

**Raider Nation, THAT is the thumb-pointing culture in action.** **You can roll your eyes at the quotes, but that right there is how a coach earns a locker room. He protected two guys in public and he'll handle the actual mistake in private.**

**And "peeling on the halfback" — that's a specific, teachable, fixable assignment on a zone-blitz. That's the kind of mistake that disappears with reps.**

Raider Nation, **a player nobody was sure would play a single down this season is now forcing this staff to play more defensive backs because he's covering too well to keep off the field.** **That's a development win in a season that needs development wins.**

But we've got YOUNG GUYS PLAYING GOOD FOOTBALL that we're going to KEEP PLAYING — all of them. Zeke and DP and Germad. We'll LET THE CREAM RISE TO THE TOP and those guys will play more."**

## AND HE DEFENDED A YOUNG CORNER ON THE TOUCHDOWN HE GAVE UP

Raider Nation, one more, because this one shows you the man.

A reporter asked about the Hezekiah touchdown and was honest about it: *watching the film, that's pretty textbook coverage. The guy just made a great play on a great throw. Am I simplifying it and covering for Zeke, or is that how you saw it?*

**Kubiak: "Yeah, it was a REALLY GOOD THROW AND CATCH. And Zeke's a guy that is ONE OF OUR BEST MAN GUYS out there. So he CAN make that play, and he WILL — he'll be given OPPORTUNITIES to do so moving forward."**

**He could have said "yeah, we've got to cover better." He said: that was a great throw and a great catch, my guy is one of our best man-coverage defenders, and he's going to keep getting those matchups.**

**Raider Nation, that's how you build a young corner instead of breaking him.**

Asked whether he has to say anything to keep spirits up after the fumbles:

Raider Nation, **"we've got to coach ball security better" is him taking a fumble and putting it on the coaching staff.** **And "those guys are harder on themselves than anybody" is him telling the media to stop asking him to publicly scold two of his best offensive players.**

## AND THE RUNNING BACK NUMBER IS GENUINELY ALARMING

Raider Nation, this is the stat that hit me hardest.

**A reporter laid it out: between Ashton Jeanty and Mike Washington Jr., rushing AND receiving combined, they had SEVENTEEN TOUCHES FOR SEVENTEEN YARDS.**

**Seventeen touches. Seventeen yards. One yard per touch.**

**"Nobody in this room thinks either one of those guys is not talented — but what has to happen to get more production from the running back position, which is so key to your offense?"**

**And Kubiak took it ALL: "Well, it STARTS WITH ME — just making sure that we're OPENING RUN LANES for them, and that we're getting them in POSITIONS TO TOUCH THE BALL MORE. Touch the ball more ON THE GROUND especially."**

Raider Nation, **a one-yard-per-touch afternoon from a backfield this talented is not a running back problem.** **That's blocking, that's scheme, that's being down two scores in the second quarter, and that's running outside zone at a defense that practices against outside zone every single day — which we warned about before kickoff.**

**And the head coach said "it starts with me." Again.**

Raider Nation, this was an honest self-assessment and I respect it.

**And pressed on it with the 30-points-in-six-weeks number: "ANY AREA OF THE TEAM THAT'S NOT MEETING THE STANDARD WE WANT TO MEET — you're going to spend EXTRA TIME THERE. Yes."**

**"Kirk has been an EXTREMELY SOLID DECISION MAKER ALL SEASON. There's ones he WANTS BACK. He wants back the FIRST PLAY of the second half. He wants back the one in the red zone — WHERE WE DID NOT GIVE HIM PROTECTION to make a good decision."**
