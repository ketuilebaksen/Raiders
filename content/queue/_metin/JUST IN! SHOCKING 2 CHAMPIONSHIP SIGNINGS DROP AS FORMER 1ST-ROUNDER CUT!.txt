# JUST IN! TWO CHAMPIONSHIP LINEMEN SIGNED — AND A FORMER TOP-10 PICK IS GONE 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, after three straight losses, Las Vegas just responded — and the response came in the trenches.

**Two offensive linemen with championship experience are in the building.** One of them has **three Super Bowl rings.** And to make room, the Raiders cut ties with a **former top-10 draft pick** who never played a single snap for this team.

That's not all. A national analyst is now openly calling for a $35 million starter to be removed from the lineup. The NFL handed down an official punishment over what happened to Brock Bowers on Sunday. And we have the full box score from a game where three different Raiders went over 70 receiving yards.

Let me be straight with you about the scale up front, because I always am: **both signings are to the practice squad.** Nobody is starting Sunday. But the names matter, the reasons matter, and the player they cut tells you more about this offensive line than anything the coaching staff has said out loud.

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing 6,000 believers — hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and let's go.

## THE MOVES: TWO TACKLES IN, TWO PLAYERS OUT

Here's the transaction, clean.

**Las Vegas signed offensive tackles Amari Kight and Darian Kinnard to the practice squad** after falling to 3-3 with the 33-30 loss to Buffalo. **To make room, the Raiders released tight end Chris Mieric and offensive lineman Evan Neal from the practice squad.**

And the purpose is obvious: **increase competition along an offensive line that has failed to establish the running game.**

Raider Nation, that sentence is the story of this entire season. Pass protection has been adequate in stretches — the head coach said Monday he genuinely likes what they're doing protecting the quarterback. But this team has consistently struggled to create running lanes, which leaves the whole offense **dangerously dependent on Kirk Cousins and the passing game.**

Every single problem we've documented flows from that dependence.

## AMARI KIGHT: ANOTHER KUBIAK GUY

The first name follows a pattern you already know by heart.

**Kight brings familiarity with Klint Kubiak after working with the Raiders head coach in Seattle last season.** He entered the league as an undrafted free agent and **belonged to the Seahawks team that won the Super Bowl.**

Raider Nation, that's now the sixth or seventh time this season we've watched this exact move. Cody White from Seattle. Dariq Young from Seattle. Jamari Thrash. Roman Hemby. Jerry Jeudy connected to Denver in 2022, Jauan Jennings to San Francisco in 2023, Raheem Mostert to the Shanahan system. **This head coach keeps betting on men he has personally coached, because he doesn't have to guess about them.**

Now the honest part: **he played only limited snaps across four games**, so his lack of extensive professional experience makes him **a developmental addition rather than an immediate solution.**

But Kubiak's decision to bring him here suggests the coach saw enough during their season together to believe another opportunity was justified. And remember what happened the last time this staff elevated a Seattle guy off the practice squad: Cody White caught two touchdowns at SoFi.

## DARIAN KINNARD: THREE RINGS

The second name is the one that makes the title of this video true.

**Kinnard arrives with a much larger championship résumé. He has collected THREE Super Bowl rings through his time with the Kansas City Chiefs and the Philadelphia Eagles — and he appeared in 17 games for the Green Bay Packers last season.**

Three rings. And seventeen games of real NFL work last year.

Raider Nation, that experience gives him **a genuinely realistic chance of eventually reaching the active roster** if the current group keeps struggling or another injury creates an opening — and injuries have created openings on this line roughly every other week.

And I want to point out what three rings actually means in a practical sense, beyond jewelry. A man who has been in Kansas City's and Philadelphia's offensive line rooms has been coached in two of the best run-blocking cultures in modern football. He knows what a functioning run game feels like, what the standard of communication looks like, and how a championship line behaves in a bad week. **In a room that has given up a league-worst pressure total at one guard spot and ranks last in yards per carry, that presence has value even on a practice field.**

Is he the solution? No. Is he the most qualified body this team has added to that room all season? Yes.

## THE CUT THAT TELLS YOU EVERYTHING: EVAN NEAL

Now the move that actually reveals something, and it's the most instructive detail of the week.

**Evan Neal is the more surprising departure, because the New York Giants originally selected him inside the top 10 of the draft.** The Raiders hoped that **moving him from tackle to guard** might unlock the ability that once made him one of the most highly regarded linemen in his class.

**Instead, he never appeared in a game — despite injuries creating uncertainty at guard earlier in the season.**

Raider Nation, stop and absorb that.

This team had **Jackson Powers-Johnson out with a groin injury.** It had **Spencer Burford allowing more pressures than any left guard in football.** It had **Trey Zuhn out and then questionable with a knee.** It ran an open competition at guard that the head coach refused to resolve publicly for an entire week.

**And through all of it, a former top-10 pick sat on the practice squad and never dressed.**

That tells you everything about what this coaching staff was seeing Monday through Friday. When a team is desperate at a position and still won't elevate the most pedigreed player available, the practice tape has already answered the question. And now Las Vegas has decided **it was no longer willing to wait for that experiment to work.**

His draft pedigree may well earn him another opportunity elsewhere, and I hope it does — a top-10 pick is a top-10 pick and somebody will try again. But this is a front office being honest with itself, and I respect that more than another month of hope.

**And the tight end release follows simple roster math:** the Raiders already have considerable depth at that position, and **Carter Renan is approaching his return from injury**, which makes tight end far less urgent than the offensive line right now.

## SO ARE THESE REAL MOVES OR PAPERWORK?

Here's the honest assessment, because I'm not going to oversell two practice squad signings.

**These moves do not guarantee an immediate transformation.** Both additions are joining the practice squad and cannot repair the ground attack by themselves. Nobody signed yesterday is fixing first-and-goal from the two on Sunday.

But remember what Kubiak said Monday — that his backs **ran harder against Buffalo, broke tackles and moved the line of scrimmage when clean openings weren't available** — and that he credited **running backs coach Omar Young** for challenging the group and expects continued improvement.

So the real question is this: **are these signings merely depth adjustments, or the beginning of broader changes to a unit that can no longer avoid responsibility for one of the league's least productive rushing attacks?**

My read: **they're insurance with a message attached.** Nobody's job is safe, the staff is actively shopping for competition, and two men with championship pedigree are now practicing against this defense every day while the starters try to fix the run game. That's pressure applied the right way.

## THE RUN GAME NUMBERS ARE BRUTAL

And here's why the pressure is justified. The numbers are worse than I realized.

**The Raiders managed only 65 rushing yards against Buffalo. And they are averaging 79.2 yards per game — the third lowest figure in the NFL.**

Third lowest. In the entire league.

Raider Nation, remember the promise. This organization hired Klint Kubiak specifically because his previous offenses ranked among the league's most productive rushing attacks. It signed elite center Tyler Linderbaum. It drafted a running back sixth overall. **And it currently has the third-worst rushing offense in professional football.**

That is the single largest gap between plan and reality on this roster, and it's why two tackles got signed the day after a loss.

## THE ARGUMENT TO BENCH ASHTON JEANTY

Which brings us to the most uncomfortable conversation of the week.

**Ashton Jeanty has become the central figure in that failure.** He produced **only 22 yards on 10 carries in Week Six**. He is **averaging 3.2 yards per attempt this season**. And he has **collected only 49 yards on 20 carries across the past two games.**

Las Vegas selected him with the sixth overall pick a year ago, creating the expectation that he would be the foundation of this ground attack.

Instead, **foot and ankle injuries have limited his effectiveness**, while poor blocking has repeatedly forced him to meet defenders before he can build any momentum.

And **Fox Sports analyst Ralph Vacchiano** argues that those circumstances are precisely the reason to take him out of the lineup temporarily.

## VACCHIANO'S CASE, STATED FAIRLY

His argument has two halves and I want to represent both accurately.

**On the quarterback: Cousins will not be benched after throwing for 313 yards against Buffalo.** That part isn't in dispute.

**On the running back: Jeanty should be allowed to recover on the sideline.** His proposed replacement is **Washington Jr., the fourth-round rookie who gained 43 yards on nine carries against the Bills** and is averaging roughly **4.9 yards per attempt** on the season while looking considerably more explosive whenever he's given opportunities. Vacchiano believes increasing his workload for several weeks could give this struggling rushing offense the energy it needs to keep the Raiders' playoff hopes alive.

And crucially, **the argument is not that Jeanty has permanently lost his role or lacks the talent expected from an early draft pick.** The concern is that **playing while physically compromised may hurt both him and the offense** — especially if the injuries are preventing him from cutting, accelerating, or running through contact normally.

## AND HERE'S THE STRONGEST POINT IN THE WHOLE DEBATE

Now the piece of logic that I think settles it, and it's elegant.

**The offensive line deserves substantial blame. But that strengthens the case for using the healthier runner — who is currently producing more behind the SAME blockers.**

Raider Nation, that's the argument I hadn't put together properly, so let me make sure you have it.

For weeks the defense of Jeanty's numbers has been "look at the blocking," and that defense is legitimate — 65 team rushing yards is not one man's fault. But **Washington Jr. runs behind that same offensive line.** Same guards, same center, same stacked boxes, same scheme. And he's averaging nearly five yards a carry while the starter averages 3.2.

The blocking can't explain a gap that exists **between two players sharing it.**

## MY POSITION, UPDATED HONESTLY

So let me tell you where I land, and I'll own my own zigzag on this.

Two weeks ago I said rest him. Then he practiced in full, Kubiak said "he's playing," and I updated — because a full practice is real evidence and I wasn't going to keep demanding a scratch for a man who completed a week of work.

**Now the data has gotten worse. 22 yards on 10 carries. 49 yards on 20 carries in two games. 3.2 per attempt.**

**So here's my verdict: give Washington Jr. the start and the lead share against the Rams, and keep Jeanty active with a limited role.** Not a benching — a workload inversion. Twelve to fifteen touches for the rookie who's producing, six or eight for the sixth overall pick while his foot and ankle get two more weeks of relief inside a game instead of outside one.

And understand what that decision would signal, because this is bigger than one Sunday: **it would show that production and health matter more than contract value or draft position.** That's the same principle Kubiak stated himself on Monday — the accountable players who earn trust get more playing time. Apply it to the backfield and the answer writes itself.

## BUT LET'S BE FAIR ABOUT THE SCHEDULE

And now the context that every honest analysis has to include.

**Las Vegas has faced three legitimate Super Bowl contenders during this skid: the Kansas City Chiefs, the New England Patriots and the Buffalo Bills.** Those teams are 4-0, a reigning conference champion, and 5-1.

That matters. A three-game losing streak against that gauntlet is not the same as losing three to mediocre teams, and anybody skipping that detail is selling panic.

**However** — and this is the part that can't be waved away — **the Raiders cannot ignore a ground game that opponents no longer have to respect.** Cousins and the passing attack have generally stayed productive. But Kubiak's offense functions best when **play action, personnel variation and rushing credibility force defenses to hesitate.**

Raider Nation, that word. **Hesitate.** It's the same word from the offensive coordinator's answer about unfamiliar looks. The entire design of this offense is built on making a defender freeze for a quarter-second — and a defender only freezes if he's afraid of the run.

**Without rushing credibility, play action is just a slower pass.** That's why 79.2 yards a game is an offensive problem even in a week where the quarterback threw for 313.

**The Los Angeles Rams are next**, and they're the test of whether any of this got fixed.

## THE NFL HANDED DOWN A SUSPENSION

Now the official league decision on Sunday's ugliest moment.

**C.J. Gardner-Johnson received a one-game suspension for spitting in the direction of Brock Bowers and other Raiders players during Buffalo's victory.**

Officials immediately penalized him for unsportsmanlike conduct and ejected him. **But the NFL decided that removal from the game was not sufficient.** NFL Network's **Mike Garafolo** confirmed the suspension, ending the speculation about whether additional discipline was coming.

And here's the detail that makes it bite: **because Buffalo is entering its bye week, Gardner-Johnson will spend two full weeks away from game action** before becoming eligible to return.

There had been some belief that the ejection alone might satisfy the league, since the spit did not appear to strike another player. But recent precedent made further punishment likely — **Jalen Carter and Ja'Marr Chase were both suspended for separate spitting incidents last season.** The league has drawn a line on this, and it applied it consistently.

## AND HE DIDN'T HIDE FROM IT — INCLUDING ABOUT BOWERS

Now, I said after the game that I wasn't going to pile on this man beyond what the officials decided. I'm going to stick to that, and the reason is that his own response was genuinely accountable.

He acknowledged he needed to behave like **a better player and a better veteran.** He admitted his **emotions took control.** He recognized that his actions **harm the Bills** — explaining that Buffalo brought him in to perform in important games, not to remove himself from them through preventable misconduct. And he said he has to accept the consequences **like an adult**, regardless of whether the league chose a fine or another form of discipline. Those comments suggest an appeal is unlikely.

**And this is the part I think matters most for us: he made it clear that Bowers did not do anything extreme to provoke the reaction.** By his own account the two players were simply arguing and exchanging words in the intensity of a football game, which made the gesture unjustified.

Raider Nation, that's an opponent publicly clearing our player. Bowers himself was less certain how the confrontation escalated, saying only that the players had been talking beforehand.

So the record is clean: **nothing Brock Bowers did caused this**, and the man responsible said so himself.

## THE LINDERBAUM DETAIL NOBODY NOTICED

And here's a small thing I love.

**Tyler Linderbaum played an important role by noticing the act and directing the officials' attention toward it.**

Our center saw it, and made sure the officials saw it. That's situational awareness in the middle of a play, and it's the reason the ejection happened at all.

Afterward he delivered a blunt assessment: **behavior like that has no place in football and should result in an ejection.**

Raider Nation, remember who we're talking about. This is the same man who ripped his own performance after the game, whom Kubiak called a major bright spot and the reason this offense scored on six of ten possessions, and who his coach defended by saying "a guy that is hard on himself usually tells you that he cares."

**He protected his teammate, he alerted the officials, and then he criticized himself.** That's a captain.

The suspension won't change the result of the loss. But it confirms the league considered the act serious enough to extend the consequences past Sunday, and Gardner-Johnson now has to prove his emotion can strengthen his play instead of becoming a liability.

## THE BOX SCORE — AND AN IMPORTANT RECONCILIATION

Now let's get the full passing numbers on the record, because they change the picture.

**Cousins completed 23 of 41 passes for 313 yards, two touchdowns and one interception, finishing with an 86.7 passer rating.**

And here's the detail I didn't have yesterday: **Tre Tucker, Michael Mayer and Brock Bowers EACH finished with at least 70 receiving yards — and Tucker caught TWO touchdown passes.**

Raider Nation, I need to reconcile this with the criticism I made, because it's important to be precise.

**Both things are true.** The wide receiver room produced only six receptions, five of them by Tucker — that's still an indictment of the depth at that position. **But Tucker's five catches included two touchdowns and over 70 yards**, and three different players cleared 70 receiving yards in the same game.

So this wasn't a passing game that failed. **It was a passing game carried by three players while the rest of the receiver room contributed one catch.** The offense scored on six of ten possessions. The problem was never moving the ball — it was the last twenty yards, the third quarter, and the ground game.

That distinction matters, and anybody telling you the pass offense is broken isn't reading the box score.

## COUSINS' MESSAGE, AND WHY I AGREE WITH IT

His response to the skid remains the steadiest voice in this building: go back to work, hold honest conversations on Monday, and maintain the habits that prepare you to play well. **He doesn't believe players should dramatically change their process when they win, or panic and abandon it when they lose** — the adjustments come from identifying specific weaknesses and emphasizing those corrections through the week.

And he refused to let himself off the hook: the red zone throw he left outside, the three incompletions on the second-to-last possession, and the third-quarter interception that gave Buffalo a short field which became a touchdown. **He named his own failures as reasons this team lost**, and said the offense has to turn third-and-goal into those four-point plays instead of settling for field goals.

But he also rejected the idea that three losses should force this organization to abandon everything it has built.

Raider Nation, I'm with him, and here's why the math supports it: **this team has fought until the end in two of the three games during this streak.** Both became losses anyway. That's the difference between a team that isn't good enough and a team that can't finish — and the second one is fixable.

Let me put the whole day together.
