THE WEAPON CAME BACK, AND HE WAS UNSTOPPABLE

Start with number 89, because everything else flowed from him.

Bowers had a procedure on his knee five days before the season, missed two games, and was listed as questionable right up to kickoff. Then he walked onto the field in New Orleans and caught ten passes for 116 yards and a touchdown.

Ten targets' worth of trust in his first game back. The Saints could not stop him. One analyst on the national broadcast put it simply: when you have Brock Bowers to throw the ball to in a tough situation, you throw him the ball, and he was unstoppable.

He also gave us the scariest moment of the afternoon, taking a hit near the goal line hard enough that he broke the frame of the goal-line marker. Thankfully, that's all that broke. He got up.

Now think about what this means going forward. This offense scored at least 26 points in two games without him. Now it just scored 35, the most points this franchise has scored in its last 40 games. That's not a coincidence. That's what happens when a defense finally has to account for a tight end who wins every matchup, and suddenly Tre Tucker, Jack Bech, Michael Mayer and Ashton Jeanty are all working against defenders who have one less pair of eyes to spare.

THE FOURTH QUARTER THAT DEFINED THIS TEAM

Here's the part that tells you who these Raiders are.

Down 27-22 entering the fourth, on the road, in one of the loudest buildings in football, against an opponent whose entire identity is finishing games. We spent all week on this channel warning you that New Orleans plays its best football late and that their quarterback has been closing games since last November.

The Raiders didn't fold. They outscored the Saints 13-0 in the final quarter.

And the turning point was exactly what we asked for. New Orleans was driving, going for it, trying to put the game away. Tyler Shough got strip-sacked, the Raiders recovered, and this team finished the game with defense. Then Hezekiah Masses added a fourth-quarter interception to slam the door.

Kirk Cousins finished with three touchdown passes and no interceptions. He's not throwing for 400 yards and nobody's calling him an MVP candidate. He's doing exactly what this team needs: distributing the football, protecting it, and delivering when the game is on the line. That's nine touchdown passes in three games.

And the rookie deserves a mention: Mike Washington Jr. ripped off a 35-yard touchdown run that gave this offense the kind of explosive play it hasn't had on the ground all year. Remember, we said after the Chargers game that when Jeanty is being bottled up, a fresh set of legs might find a crease. That's exactly what happened.

FOUR TURNOVERS, AND A DEFENSE WITH NO NAMES

The defense created four turnovers. Four. In a road game, against the second-ranked passing offense in the NFL.

One analyst described this unit as a no-name defense, and I love that description. Everybody knows Maxx Crosby. After that, you have to be a real football person to know these guys. Hezekiah Masses, a fifth-round rookie with a passer rating allowed of 0.0 coming into the day. Malcolm Koonce, who has turned into a genuine pass-rush weapon since the staff moved him into the right situations. Foley Fatukasi, signed for the veteran minimum.

And the man setting the tone: Nakobe Dean. He forced another fumble, he's led this team in tackles just about every week, and he's always around the football. Remember where he came from. A third-round pick in Philadelphia with high expectations to anchor that defense, in and out of the lineup with injuries, never quite getting the runway. Now he's entrenched here, wearing the green dot, and he's the heartbeat of a unit ranked at or near the top of the league in pressure rate, sacks, forced fumbles, and takeaways.

That's the roster-building formula working exactly as designed: let the young talent play, then infuse it with the right veterans.

And look at what those four takeaways are worth in practical terms. This offense produced 35 points, and some of those points came on short fields the defense handed over. That's the thing we said after the Chargers game and it keeps being true: this team wins the margins. The Saints outgained us in yards through most of this season, and it hasn't mattered once, because turnovers are worth more than yards and nobody on our schedule has figured out how to stop this front from taking the ball away.

THE HISTORY THIS START JUST MADE

Now let me give you the numbers that put this into perspective, because some of them are remarkable.

The Raiders are only the second team in NFL history to start a season 3-0 after two consecutive seasons of 13 or more losses. The last team to do it was the 1980 San Francisco 49ers. You know what happened to that organization shortly afterward.

They're also the fourth team in the common draft era to start 3-0 after picking first overall.

Those 35 points are the most this team has scored in its last 40 games.

And here's the one that stings and satisfies at the same time: the Raiders have already matched their entire win total from last season. Three games. Three wins. That's the whole 2025 season, done by the end of September.

Oh, and one wild note from the game itself. There were six combined touchdowns by tight ends on Sunday, which matches the NFL single-game record. Shough threw four touchdowns, all of them to tight ends, two to Juwan Johnson. And the other time that record was set? It involved the Raiders, against the Dolphins, back in 1979.

THE HONEST PROBLEM

Now the part this channel always does, because I'm not going to sell you a perfect team.

The Raiders allowed 27 points before shutting the Saints down in the fourth quarter. For three quarters, New Orleans found answers, and the tight end coverage was a genuine problem. Four touchdown passes to tight ends is not a fluke or bad luck. That's a scheme issue, and with Trayden Stokes out of the lineup in the concussion protocol, the middle of the field was exactly the area we warned about all week.

Here's why that matters more than usual: Kansas City is next. If a Saints offense can throw four touchdowns to tight ends, you can imagine what a Chiefs offense does with the middle of the field if that doesn't get cleaned up in seven days.

The win is real. So is the film.

I'd also keep an eye on how long this team can keep leaning on the same formula. Three games in, the identity is clear: score enough, take the ball away, and win the fourth quarter. That works. But a defense that gives up 27 points and then relies on takeaways to survive is playing with a smaller margin than the final score suggests. Takeaways come in bunches, and they can disappear for a month. The units that sustain a season are the ones that also get off the field on third down without needing a fumble to do it.

WHAT THIS DOES TO THE MENDOZA CONVERSATION

There's one more consequence of this win that deserves its own moment, and it's about the most important player in this franchise's future.

Coming into the season, the expectation across the league was that Fernando Mendoza would be starting by now. Draft a quarterback first overall and the clock usually runs out on the veteran by the end of September. That's been the pattern for years.

Three weeks in, Cousins has nine touchdown passes and this team is undefeated. As one national analyst admitted on Sunday, he had argued all along that the Raiders should just play the rookie and get on with it, and now you simply can't.

And remember the detail we covered earlier this week: Mendoza wears his helmet on the sideline so he can hear the play call going into Cousins, then recites it back to the quarterbacks coach and talks through what he's seeing from the defense. Every drive of this 3-0 start is a lesson he's taking notes on, and he's doing it in a winning locker room instead of a losing one.

That is how you develop a franchise quarterback. Not by throwing him into a rebuild and hoping he survives it.
