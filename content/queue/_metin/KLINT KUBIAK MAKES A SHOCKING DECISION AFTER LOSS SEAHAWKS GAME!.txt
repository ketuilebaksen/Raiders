# KLINT KUBIAK AFTER THE SEAHAWKS LOSS — "THERE'S NO ONE ELSE TO BLAME BUT ME" 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, I need to tell you up front: Klint Kubiak didn't announce a firing, a benching or a trade after that loss.

**What he did was something I've almost never seen a head coach do. He took the ENTIRE football game onto his own shoulders, in public, in one sentence:**

**"I PUT IT ON MYSELF. THERE'S NO ONE ELSE TO BLAME BUT ME."**

**And then his 22-year-old rookie quarterback walked to the same podium and did the exact same thing about the six sacks:**

**"A LOT OF THE SACKS TODAY — IF NOT ALL OF THEM — WERE ON ME. NOT ANY ON THE OFFENSIVE LINE. I thought they PLAYED GREAT and had to display GREAT LEADERSHIP today."**

**Raider Nation, a head coach and a rookie quarterback fighting each other over who gets to take the blame. That's the shocking part of this press conference.**

But Raider Nation, there's something else from that stadium yesterday that I need you to hear, **and it's the most beautiful thing that has ever happened in that building.**

**When Fernando Mendoza scored his first NFL touchdown — THOUSANDS OF RAIDERS FANS CHEERED WHILE REMAINING SEATED. On purpose. To honor his mother. Because that's what his father does.**

**And nobody told him. He found out at the podium, from a reporter.**

**Raider Nation, I'll get to all of it. The 0-for-7. The six sacks. Brock Bowers' ankle. And the single most Raider thing a crowd has ever done.**

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're pushing for **6,000 believers** — hit that like button, drop **"I AM A RAIDER"** in the comments, and let's go.

## FIRST: KUBIAK'S HONEST VERDICT ON THE DEBUT

Raider Nation, asked what he made of Fernando's first start:

**"He was WELL PREPARED. He STARTED THE GAME REALLY WELL. And overall, for his first start, I thought IT WAS A GOOD START."**

**And then he immediately moved the blame to himself: "There's a lot we'll watch on film together. But OVERALL, WE HAVE TO PLAY BETTER. WE'VE GOT TO SUPPORT HIM BETTER. I'VE GOT TO HELP HIM BETTER IN THE SECOND HALF."**

**And then the number that defines this entire football game:**

**"WHAT HAPPENED IN THE SECOND HALF TO GO ZERO FOR SEVEN ON THIRD DOWN IS UNACCEPTABLE. THERE'S NO REASON THAT WE SHOULD DO THAT. CREDIT TO SEATTLE."**

**ZERO FOR SEVEN ON THIRD DOWN IN THE SECOND HALF.**

Raider Nation, **that's the whole loss in one statistic.**

**Seven third downs. Seven failures. Seven times the offense walked off and sent a defense that was actually playing well right back out onto the field.**

**And remember what Rob Leonard told us on Thursday: "FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL. When you're on the field a long time, mistakes tend to happen more often."**

**Raider Nation, that's exactly what this offense did to that defense in the second half. Zero for seven.**

## AND THE FIRST HALF WAS GENUINELY GOOD — THAT'S WHAT HURTS

Raider Nation, this is the frustrating part.

**On the run game: "It was a POSITIVE FIRST HALF. YOU HAVE TO SUSTAIN IT FOR FOUR QUARTERS. WEREN'T ABLE TO DO THAT."**

**"GOT TO COME OUT OF THE TUNNEL AND RUN THE BALL. The FIRST CALL OF THE SECOND HALF IS A RUN — AND WE TAKE A LOSS."**

**"But I PUT IT ON MYSELF. THERE'S NO ONE ELSE TO BLAME BUT ME."**

**Raider Nation, the first play of the second half was a run that lost yardage. And the head coach just said that one is his.**

**And remember his diagnosis all season: "I SEE IT WORK. JUST NOT FOR FOUR QUARTERS." And: "There's got to be a HAPPY MEDIUM somewhere between that first half and second half."**

**Ten games in, and it's STILL the same sentence. Good half, bad half, lose.**

## AND HE CONFIRMED THERE WAS A FIERY MOMENT ON THE SIDELINE

Raider Nation, the cameras caught it and a reporter asked about it directly — **it looked like he had a FIERY MESSAGE FOR THE OFFENSIVE LINE after that first drive out of the second half.**

**"We just — WE EXPECT MORE FROM OURSELVES COMING OUT OF THAT TUNNEL IN THE THIRD QUARTER. And I EXPECT MORE OUT OF MYSELF AS WELL."**

**"Obviously the second half was NOT GOOD ENOUGH. YOU DON'T GIVE YOURSELF A CHANCE TO WIN."**

Raider Nation, **"I expect more out of myself as well" — in the same breath as the criticism.** **Even when he gets animated at his own offensive line, he puts himself in the sentence.**

**That's the thumb-pointing. Ten games and it hasn't wavered once.**

## AND HERE'S THE GOOD NEWS — AND IT'S REAL

Raider Nation, write this one down, because it's the thing I've been begging for since September.

**"I THOUGHT DEFENSIVELY WE TOOK A STEP FORWARD TODAY. THEY KEPT US IN THE BALL GAME. AND WE HAVE TO PLAY COMPLIMENTARY FOOTBALL."**

**THE DEFENSE TOOK A STEP FORWARD. THEY KEPT US IN THE BALL GAME.**

**And Mendoza confirmed it with specifics: "DEFENSE PLAYED FANTASTIC tonight. I believe had TWO TURNOVERS — TWO CRUCIAL TURNOVERS."**

Raider Nation, **against the defending world champions. The unit that was the fifth team in NFL history to allow thirty-plus in six straight games. TWO TAKEAWAYS and they kept us in it.**

**Rob Leonard's answer to the whole controversy was exactly this: "he has a chance to make that entire controversy FEEL LESS IMPORTANT by putting a BETTER DEFENSE ON THE FIELD."**

**He did it. On the week everybody wanted his head on a platter, his unit showed up.**

**And then the offense went 0-for-7 on third down and wasted it.**

**Raider Nation, this is the cruelest version of this season yet. For nine weeks the offense carried a broken defense. The one week the defense held up, the offense couldn't convert a single third down after halftime.**

**"We have to play COMPLIMENTARY FOOTBALL." Kirk Cousins said the same words on his way out the door two weeks ago. Nobody in this building has managed it yet.**

## NOW THE BROCK BOWERS INJURY — AND IT CHANGED THE GAME

Raider Nation, here's the hinge of the whole afternoon.

Asked whether the game plan felt stagnant at times:

**"I thought overall WHEN WE LOST BROCK, you definitely SAW A BIG CHANGE IN PRODUCTION."**

**"So I look at MYSELF. I have to do a MUCH BETTER JOB of MOVING THE BALL and SPREADING THE BALL AROUND."**

**And on the injury itself: "THERE'S NOT [any sense of severity]. No. ALL I KNOW IS IT'S AN ANKLE."**

Raider Nation, **an ankle. That's all we know, and that's all I'm going to say about it. No speculation, no timelines, no guessing from me. We wait for the team.**

**But the football impact is undeniable and the head coach said it himself: when Brock Bowers left, the production fell off a cliff.**

**And that makes complete sense. Think about what Bowers has been for this offense: 41 catches, 547 yards coming into last week, then seven catches for 111 yards against San Francisco. Kubiak said it two weeks ago: "BROCK DEMANDS EXCELLENT COVERAGE FROM THE BEST PLAYERS EVERY WEEK."**

**Raider Nation, he's not just the best receiver. He's the SAFETY VALVE. He's the man a rookie quarterback finds on third and six when everything else is covered. And against the best defense since 1999, that outlet disappeared at halftime.**

## AND MENDOZA'S ANSWER ABOUT LOSING HIM WAS EXACTLY RIGHT

Raider Nation, asked how losing his security blanket changed his thought process:

**"Brock's a FANTASTIC PLAYER. He's a SPECIAL HUMAN BEING ON AND OFF THE FIELD."**

**"HOWEVER — Coach Kubiak always preaches EVERYBODY DOING THEIR JOB. So THE NEXT GUY UP, I HAVE AS MUCH CONFIDENCE IN."**

**"And so I just need to GET THE BALL TO THE RIGHT PLAYERS ON TIME AND EFFICIENTLY AND ACCURATELY."**

Raider Nation, **"the next guy up, I have as much confidence in" — that's a 22-year-old refusing to use the loss of a star as an excuse.**

**And "get the ball to the right players ON TIME and ACCURATELY" — Raider Nation, that's almost word for word what the national analysts said was the test: "get it out quick BUT TO THE RIGHT GUY. That will be the TRUE MARKER."**

**He knows. He identified his own test.**

## NOW THE SIX SACKS — AND THE DB BLITZES THAT KILLED US

Raider Nation, this is the technical heart of the loss.

Kubiak was asked why Seattle was so effective on those DB blitzes — **Whitman got home like THREE TIMES on Mendoza.**

**"Yeah, they had GOOD CALLS. And CREDIT TO THEM."**

**And on the six sacks overall: "There's a LOT that goes into that. NUMBER ONE: CREDIT SEATTLE. They did a good job. NUMBER TWO: WE HAVE TO BE BETTER HELPING OUR QUARTERBACK. And NUMBER THREE: with a rookie quarterback, there's CHANCES TO THROW THE BALL AWAY at times."**

**"There's a lot more than just one thing. BUT YOU'RE NOT GOING TO WIN A GAME TAKING SIX SACKS."**

Raider Nation, **six sacks. From an offensive line that gave up ZERO to Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner the week before.**

**And THAT is the answer to the question we asked on Saturday: how much of that zero-sack game was the line, and how much was Kirk Cousins' internal clock?**

**We just found out. A lot of it was the clock.**

## AND MENDOZA'S BREAKDOWN OF THE SACK WAS THE BEST THING IN THE WHOLE PRESSER

Raider Nation, listen to this, because a 22-year-old just diagnosed his own failure with the precision of a coordinator.

Asked to walk through the DB blitzes:

**"I need to WATCH BACK THE FILM to tell you exactly what it was. But there's ONE I DO REMEMBER on THIRD DOWN — they were showing a FOUR-WEAK BLITZ TO THE BOUNDARY. So I MADE A CHECK, SLID THE PROTECTION THAT WAY — AND THEN THEY DROPPED THAT SIDE AND CAME THE OTHER SIDE."**

**"And I NEED TO BE AWARE OF THAT and HIT MY HOT ANSWER ON THE LEFT."**

**"So I GOT US IN THE WRONG CHECK, and then they SACKED ME."**

**"And that's why A LOT OF THE SACKS TODAY — IF NOT ALL OF THEM — WERE ON ME. NOT ANY ON THE OFFENSIVE LINE. I thought they PLAYED GREAT and displayed GREAT LEADERSHIP today. And I NEED TO BE BETTER, especially with those DB blitzes."**

Raider Nation, **I need you to understand what that explanation actually describes, because it's beautiful and it's brutal.**

**Seattle showed a blitz from one side. Mendoza read it correctly, called out a protection slide to meet it — which is EXACTLY what you're coached to do. And then Mike Macdonald DROPPED those blitzers and sent the pressure from the OTHER side, into the protection that had just vacated.**

**That's not a rookie failing to read a blitz. That's an elite defensive coordinator SETTING A TRAP for a rookie using his own correct read against him.**

**And Mendoza described it himself: "COACH MACDONALD, HE DIALED IT UP AND HE DID GREAT. There were a couple times I tried to CHECK to something else in protection — and HE WAS PLAYING CHESS. HE FOOLED ME. HE GOT ME A COUPLE OF TIMES."**

**He was playing chess. And he fooled me.**

**Raider Nation, remember what the national panel said on Saturday: "IF ANYBODY KNOWS KUBIAK'S OFFENSE, WHO IS IT? THE GUY WHO HIRED HIM TO BE HIS OC LAST YEAR."**

**They were right. And it showed up in the one place that hurts a rookie most: protection checks.**

## AND HE IDENTIFIED HIS OWN FIX IN ONE WORD: MATURITY

Raider Nation, asked where this performance fell against his own standards:

**"It's ALWAYS ABOUT DOING YOUR JOB — that's what Coach Kubiak always preaches."**

**"In the first half, I thought WE GOT OFF TO A GOOD START. Having the second half be ZERO FOR SEVEN on third downs — and those being PASSING DOWNS — and NOT THROWING THE BALL AWAY ENOUGH."**

**"There were a lot of sacks there. BUT WE SHOULD HAVE HAD HALF AS MANY, IF NOT LESS."**

**"So I NEED TO MATURE AS A QUARTERBACK: THROW THE BALL AWAY. Throw MORE ACCURATE BALLS ON THIRD DOWN. And making sure WE CAN MOVE THE CHAINS TO GIVE OUR DEFENSE TIME TO RECUPERATE ON THE SIDELINE."**

Raider Nation, **"give our defense time to recuperate on the sideline."**

**A rookie quarterback, in his first career press conference after his first career start, independently named the exact thing his defensive coordinator said three days earlier. FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL.**

**He gets it. He understands what 0-for-7 did to those eleven men.**

**And "throw the ball away" — three different people said it. Kubiak said there are "chances to throw the ball away at times." Mendoza said it twice. And remember Kirk Cousins' own self-criticism about his first interception in San Francisco: "THE BALL JUST NEEDS TO BE THROWN INTO THE FIRST ROW OF THE STANDS."**

**Raider Nation, that lesson is apparently the hardest one in football to learn, and every quarterback has to learn it the same painful way.**

## AND HE WAS HONEST ABOUT WHEN TO RUN AND WHEN TO STAND IN THERE

Raider Nation, this answer is the most important one for his development.

**"I NEED TO STAND IN THERE MORE. I WAS A LITTLE SPED UP TODAY."**

**"There were some times where if you can EXTEND THE PLAY and it gives a good chance to GET OUT CLEAN, not take a sack and extend the play — because let's say the defense did a really good job covering — then THAT'S ACCEPTABLE."**

**"BUT THERE ARE SOME TIMES I SHOULD HAVE STAYED IN THERE, STEPPED UP WITH ONE HITCH, AND THEN DELIVERED A STRIKE — RATHER THAN ALREADY LOOKING TO SCRAMBLE."**

**"It's credit to Seattle's defense. AND I NEED TO BE BETTER, BE MORE COMPOSED IN THE POCKET."**

Raider Nation, **"I was a little sped up today" is the most honest self-assessment a rookie can give.**

**And it's the OPPOSITE of what everybody predicted. The national analysts all warned he HOLDS THE BALL TOO LONG and takes too many sacks — that was the knock from the preseason and from college.**

**And what actually happened was the reverse: HE GOT SPED UP. He was looking to scramble before the play had developed.**

**That's a 22-year-old's heart rate in his first NFL start against the best defense in football. And "step up with one hitch and deliver a strike" is exactly the right coaching point. One hitch. That's all. Then throw.**

**And Kubiak told us this was coming: "It's a PROCESS from now UNTIL THE END OF HIS CAREER. You learn way more from FULL SPEED PRACTICE REPS."**

## AND THE PRE-SNAP GAP BETWEEN COLLEGE AND THE NFL IS REAL

Raider Nation, asked how different learning pre-snap in the NFL is from college:

**"It's A LOT DIFFERENT. The NFL is A WHOLE DIFFERENT LEVEL."**

**"You need to UNDERSTAND YOUR HOT ROUTES — WHICH I NEED TO DO BETTER. And we need to ESSENTIALLY BE A MASTER AT THE PROTECTION SCHEME."**

**"Because it's ONE THING when we're in the MEETING ROOM saying 'okay, I would do this' — RATHER THAN IN THE HEAT OF THE BATTLE."**

Raider Nation, **"it's one thing in the meeting room versus the heat of battle" is the single truest sentence about rookie quarterbacks ever spoken.**

**And remember Kubiak's diagnosis on Friday: "usually it's those EMOTIONALLY STABLE INDIVIDUALS who are able to TREAT PRACTICE AND THE GAMES THE SAME WAY."**

**Well — the kid admitted he didn't quite manage that yesterday. And then he named exactly what he'll fix.**

## AND KUBIAK'S ANSWER ABOUT HIS POISE WAS THE BEST COMPLIMENT OF THE DAY

Raider Nation, a reporter asked a genuinely great question: how much do you zero in on his response to the BIG HITS, the plays that FAILED — the poise and composure, more than the successful drives?

**And Kubiak said: "THAT'S NOT A SURPRISE. THAT'S WHO HE IS."**

**"That's why HE IS WHO HE IS. Obviously he's an EXCELLENT PLAYER — but he's got POISE, he's got MATURITY, he's got LEADERSHIP. And THAT SHOWED, ALL THROUGHOUT THE WEEK."**

Raider Nation, **"that's not a surprise, that's who he is" — the head coach wasn't impressed by his rookie's composure because he already knew it was there.**

**Six sacks. A 0-for-7 second half. His best weapon leaving with an ankle. The best defense since 1999 playing chess with his protection checks. And the kid kept his head up all afternoon.**

**That's the thing you cannot teach and cannot buy. And Kubiak said he saw it all week before kickoff.**

## AND HE SCORED A TOUCHDOWN — AND LISTEN TO HOW HE DESCRIBED IT

Raider Nation, this is the loveliest football answer of the day. Asked about the emotion of scoring that first touchdown:

**"It was GREAT. DEFENSE PLAYED FANTASTIC tonight — two crucial turnovers. We were able to get a turnover and get the SHORT SIDE OF THE FIELD."**

**"RUN GAME WAS FANTASTIC and it set it up to a THIRD DOWN IN THE RED ZONE. And the OFFENSIVE LINEMEN BLOCKED FANTASTICALLY."**

**"GREAT COMMUNICATION BY TYLER LINDERBAUM there, pointing out where the pressure would come from — and we were able to SCORE A TOUCHDOWN."**

**"It was a GREAT MOMENT to celebrate with all the guys."**

Raider Nation, **count the people he thanked before he mentioned himself: the defense, the turnover, the field position, the run game, the offensive line, and Tyler Linderbaum's communication.**

**HE SCORED THE TOUCHDOWN HIMSELF. With his own legs. And he credited six other groups before getting to the part where he crossed the goal line.**

**And Kubiak confirmed the value of that mobility: asked how much Fernando's WILLINGNESS and ABILITY to run helps an offense that's struggled on the ground all season —**

**"YES, THAT HELPS. HIS MOBILITY HELPS. IT GIVES US A DIFFERENT DIMENSION."**

**Raider Nation, a different dimension. That's the thing Kirk Cousins could never give this team, and it showed up in the red zone in game one.**

## AND NOW — RAIDER NATION, THE MOMENT I'LL REMEMBER FROM THIS SEASON

Raider Nation, I need you to sit down for this one, because I did not know about it until the press conference either.

A reporter told Fernando Mendoza that **the fans wanted to do a SALUTE TO HIS MOM. And they asked the crowd that when he scored his first touchdown, THEY REMAIN SEATED — LIKE HIS FATHER DOES WITH HIS MOM.**

**And the reporter told him: "I don't know if you noticed, but THOUSANDS — not all, obviously, but THOUSANDS OF FANS — when you scored, were CHEERING BUT SITTING, TO HONOR YOUR MOM."**

**And Mendoza said: "I WAS NOT AWARE OF THAT."**

**"However, I'M SUPER GRATEFUL. And TO THE SILVER AND BLACK FOR DOING THAT — IT MEANS A LOT TO MY FAMILY AND I. And I JUST CAN'T THANK YOU GUYS ENOUGH."**

**"AND A DARK DAY THAT BRINGS A SMILE TO MY FACE. So I really thank everybody for RESPECTING MY FAMILY and I appreciate all their support."**

Raider Nation. **Thousands of people in that building cheered a touchdown FROM THEIR SEATS.**

**Because his father sits with his mother. So they sat too.**

**Raider Nation, I'm not going to say one word about anybody's health — that's the Mendoza family's to share and nobody else's. But you all know this young man sold a burrito with his own name on it at Cal to raise money for an MS charity because of his mom. And you know what he said at his first press conference as a starter: "FAITH, FAMILY AND FOOTBALL."**

**And a stadium full of strangers in silver and black found a way to tell him they'd heard him.**

**"A DARK DAY THAT BRINGS A SMILE TO MY FACE."**

**Raider Nation, that's the best thing this fan base has ever done. I don't care about the final score. That happened in OUR building, and I will be proud of it for the rest of my life.**

## AND HE THANKED YOU IN TWO LANGUAGES

Raider Nation, this is worth noting too.

**"I can't thank everybody enough for all the APOYO — WHICH IS SUPPORT IN SPANISH — especially all the FANS, and just my TEAMMATES, MY COACHES, who had A LOT OF SUPPORT IN ME, GIVING ME THIS OPPORTUNITY AND CHANCE."**

**"And I NEED TO BE BETTER FOR THEM."**

**And he added: "It's ALWAYS GREAT TO GET THE EXPERIENCE of your FIRST NFL START, and now have MORE TANGIBLE ITEMS TO IMPROVE OFF OF and SEE DEFICIENCIES AND THEN ATTACK THEM this week. The GAME REPS ARE ALWAYS MORE VALUABLE THAN PRACTICE REPS — although they're all valuable."**

**Raider Nation, "apoyo." He stopped and translated it for us. That's a young man who knows exactly who's watching him and where they're watching from.**

## AND HE NAMED THE VETERANS WHO PICKED HIM UP

Raider Nation, asked whether any veteran pulled him aside during the game:

**"There's a TON OF LEADERS in our offense. I could go on and on about all the names. TWO that come to mind right now are KOLTON MILLER and AIDAN O'CONNELL."**

**"AIDAN'S BEEN EXTREMELY HELPFUL, especially as HE'S GONE THROUGH A SIMILAR EXPERIENCE — teaching me and helping me with the ropes."**

**"And KOLTON IS A GREAT LEADER off the offensive line. Essentially EVERYBODY ON THE OFFENSE HAS RALLIED AROUND EACH OTHER and ALWAYS GIVEN SUPPORT NO MATTER WHAT HAPPENS."**

Raider Nation, **AIDAN O'CONNELL AGAIN.**

**The man who got jumped on the depth chart. The man who's now one snap from the field with no Plan B behind him. And he spent an entire afternoon on that sideline coaching up the kid who passed him — because he's been that rookie himself.**

**Kubiak called him "a PROFESSIONAL" and "an EXCELLENT PLAYER" who "does things THE RIGHT WAY — VERY SIMILAR TO KIRK." Mendoza has now named him in every single press conference he's given.**

**Raider Nation, give Aidan O'Connell his flowers. That's a pro.**

## AND THE LESSON FROM KIRK COUSINS THAT SHOWED UP

Raider Nation, asked what Kirk taught him that manifested yesterday:

**"One of the biggest lessons he showed the entire team is that — ESPECIALLY IN NFL GAMES — YOU'RE NEVER OUT OF IT."**

**"Even in the fourth quarter, after a PRETTY AWFUL THIRD QUARTER performance, WE WERE ABLE TO ALMOST GET BACK INTO IT. AND IT WAS A ONE-SCORE GAME."**

**"Kirk ALWAYS FIGHTING, having GREAT LEADERSHIP ON THE SIDELINES, and KEEPING ON FIGHTING THROUGH THICK OR THIN — no matter the momentum or the situation."**

**"That's something that POPPED UP IN OUR LITTLE QUARTERBACK SIDELINE MEETING HUDDLE — when we recoup and watch the film in the huddle."**

Raider Nation, **IT WAS A ONE-SCORE GAME.**

**Six sacks. 0-for-7 on third down in the second half. Brock Bowers out with an ankle. Under three yards a carry. Against the best defense since 1999. And they got it to a one-score game.**

**And the reason, by the rookie's own account, is a lesson from a man who's been in Minnesota for a week.**

**And asked whether sitting those first nine weeks helped: "I THINK IT HELPED ME A LOT. Evidently it didn't PLAY UP TO THE STANDARD. But I felt that IF I STARTED LIKE WEEK ONE, IT WOULD HAVE LOOKED A LOT DIFFERENT WITHOUT KIRK'S MENTORSHIP. His mentorship has HELPED ME A LOT. And although it was a tough performance and not up to the standard, I REALLY DO APPRECIATE HIM."**

## AND ON THE DEEP BALL — KUBIAK EXPLAINED WHY IT NEVER CAME

Raider Nation, asked about the lack of deep shots:

**"That's what makes this defense SO GREAT. They TAKE AWAY — they PLAY SHELL COVERAGE and TAKE AWAY THE DEEP BALL. AND THEY DID THAT TODAY."**

**"The Seahawks have a FANTASTIC DEFENSE. We've seen it all season. A LOT OF CREDIT TO THEM AND COACH MACDONALD."**

**And on Jaxon Smith-Njigba from the first half to the second: "I thought they made some GREAT OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS. That was a big part of it."**

Raider Nation, **shell coverage means two deep safeties sitting on everything over the top. There was no deep ball available. It wasn't a scheme failure — it was a correct read of an unfavorable picture, over and over.**

**And remember Rob Leonard's entire week was about Jaxon Smith-Njigba — "YOU START WITH HIM DAY ONE. Early down, third down, red zone." He got taken out of script and still made plays. That's the league's leading receiver in receptions being the league's leading receiver.**

## AND THE BEST LINE OF THE WHOLE DAY CAME FROM THE ROOKIE

Raider Nation, this is how he framed the entire experience, and it's the reason I'm not worried about him.

**"It's GOOD TO GET PUNCHED IN THE FACE — because now it's A GREAT OPPORTUNITY TO GROW AND LEARN, and to REALLY UNVEIL ALL THE DEFICIENCIES THAT I HAVE IN MY GAME, and as a FUTURE HOPEFUL LEADER OF THIS OFFENSE."**

**And when a reporter asked if it felt like a RELIEF to get the first start out of the way:**

**"NOT SURE IF RELIEF IS THE RIGHT WORD. It was the experience that happened, and IT WASN'T THE EXPERIENCE THAT THE TEAM WANTED OR THAT I WANTED."**

**"But it's a GREAT OPPORTUNITY TO LEARN AND GET BETTER AND BE BETTER FOR MY TEAMMATES, so I can BETTER BE EQUIPPED to give my teammates the best chances to succeed."**

Raider Nation, **he refused the word "relief." He wouldn't take the easy out that a reporter handed him.**

**"Good to get punched in the face." "Unveil all the deficiencies." "Future HOPEFUL leader of this offense" — he wouldn't even claim the job he already has.**

**That's humility with a spine in it. I'll take that every single Sunday.**

**THE DEFENSE TOOK A STEP FORWARD AND GOT TWO TAKEAWAYS. A rookie quarterback made his first start against the best defense since 1999, scored a touchdown with his legs, kept his poise through six sacks, got it to a one-score game, and then stood at a podium and took every single sack on himself while praising the offensive line.**
