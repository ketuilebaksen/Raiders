Raider Nation, let me set the table.

So today we're asking the question nobody in that building wants to answer out loud: **what on earth is going on with this offensive line?**

Quick honesty note before we start, because that's the deal here: **there has been no trade.** The "move" this week was a quiet set of practice squad transactions, and both additions went to the practice squad, not the starting lineup. But there is genuine good news buried in this film, and there is one statistic in here that is the single worst number on this entire football team — worse than the run game, worse than the receiver room.

## THE MOVE: SCHEME FITS, NOT FAMOUS NAMES

On Monday the Raiders announced a handful of practice squad changes. **They signed offensive tackles Amari Kight and Darian Kinnard, and released tight end Chris Mieric and offensive lineman Evan Neal.**

And that last name deserves the full timeline, because it's more damning than people realize.

**Evan Neal was the SEVENTH overall pick in the 2022 draft, taken by the New York Giants.** The Giants gave up on him **at final cuts.** The Raiders signed him to the practice squad in **early September**, hoping a change of scenery would unlock something.

**It did not. He never got elevated to the active roster — not once — and now he's looking for his next chance.**

Raider Nation, I said this yesterday and the full timeline makes it even louder: this team had a guard crisis for six straight weeks and never once dressed a top-ten pick. **The practice field had already answered the question.**

And the players coming in are far more interesting than the player going out.

**Amari Kight is 25, and the reason he's here is no mystery: he played in Seattle for Klint Kubiak when Kubiak was the Seahawks' offensive coordinator last season.** That's a coach calling a player he already knows — a player who already speaks the language of this offense.

**Darian Kinnard is 26 and has been part of three Super Bowl winning teams. Three rings.** He's a developmental tackle with a trophy case, and when a locker room has been searching for answers, there are worse things than adding a man who has seen up close what winning looks like.

**So what does it tell us? It tells us Kubiak is looking for SCHEME FITS, not FAMOUS NAMES.** He does not care that somebody was a top-ten pick four years ago. He cares whether that player can do what this offense needs.

And with **Kolton Miller coming off some rough weeks** and **DJ Glaze needing to keep his hot streak going**, the Raiders want more bodies they can develop — maybe even kick inside to guard if it works out.

It's a small move on paper. **It is a loud signal.** This staff is not satisfied with what it has up front, and it is quietly stockpiling options.

## THE BENCHING NOBODY IS SAYING OUT LOUD

Which brings us to the real story — the one that played out on the field.

Before kickoff, Kubiak promised competition at guard. He followed through. **Second-year lineman Caleb Rogers started at right guard in place of Jackson Powers-Johnson — and Rogers played EVERY SINGLE SNAP.**

**Powers-Johnson, the second-round pick from 2024, did not take a single snap on offense.**

Raider Nation, stop right there. **Not one snap.**

That is not a rotation. That is not managing a workload after a groin injury. For a player who had been in the starting lineup, **that is a full benching** — and it's a statement louder than anything said at a microphone.

Kubiak took total accountability afterward — "that was my decision" — and he's also said he believes in both players. But let's be honest with each other: **you don't sit a starter for an entire football game to make a point about how much you believe in him.**

## HOW ROGERS ACTUALLY PLAYED

The results were mixed, and I'll give you the fair version.

Rogers had some nice moments and by most accounts **held his own in pass protection.** Kubiak called it **a mix of good and bad** — which is honestly as accurate a scouting report as you'll ever get from a head coach — and described him as a young player who keeps getting better.

**And his pass protection against Buffalo was a clear step up from where this unit looked against New England.** That's the kind of incremental progress a young group needs.

**But the run game was still stuck in the mud.**

## SIXTY-FIVE YARDS — AND ONE SNAPSHOT OF THE WHOLE SEASON

**The backs ran for 65 yards on 20 carries. And the Bills outgained the Raiders on the ground.**

Then came the sequence that tells you everything.

**On a critical stretch in the fourth quarter, the Raiders reached the TWO-YARD LINE and went BACKWARD on back-to-back runs — losing three yards combined. One of those was a two-yard loss when Buffalo defensive tackle Greg Gaines burst straight through and dropped Ashton Jeanty.**

Raider Nation, **that is the entire season in one snapshot.**

Two-yard line. Fourth quarter. Three-point game. And a defensive tackle comes clean through the interior and drops your running back two yards deep before he can even make a decision.

This is what I mean when I say the red zone problem and the run game problem are the same problem. You don't need a clever play at the two-yard line. **You need one man to not get beaten straight up the middle.**

## JEANTY, WASHINGTON, AND THE HONEST CAVEAT

Let's do the backfield numbers, because the conversation is getting louder and it's fair to have it.

**Jeanty had 10 carries for 22 yards — and his FIRST RUSHING TOUCHDOWN OF THE YEAR, a one-yarder on the opening drive.** That's worth saying out loud: the sixth overall pick finally got into the end zone on the ground.

But **he has now been held under three yards per carry in FOUR of the Raiders' six games.**

**Washington Jr., meanwhile, got a career-high 23 snaps and ran for 43 yards on nine carries, including a pair of 10-plus-yard gains.** He has been the more explosive runner every single time he's gotten a chance.

**Now the caveat that honest analysis requires: Buffalo came into this game allowing the second-fewest yards per contact in the league — and a lot of the Raiders' rushing yards were earned AFTER contact.**

And Raider Nation, **that is exactly the point.**

**When your back is making his own yards because the hole isn't there, you have a blocking problem before you have a running back problem.**

## AND HERE I HAVE TO CORRECT MYSELF

Now the part where I push back on a popular narrative — including one I helped spread.

Some people say Kubiak's offense doesn't suit Jeanty. That a **wide zone** system built for a different style of back is a poor fit for a player drafted to thrive on **inside zone** and gap runs. I made a version of this argument myself when I said Washington Jr. should get gap-scheme carries because the staff identified that as his strength.

**Here's what the numbers actually say: the Raiders have run a nearly even split between zone and gap runs this year. And every starting lineman has been BETTER in zone than in gap blocking.**

Read that again. The scheme is already balanced, and this line is **worse** at the gap runs I was asking for.

**So the scheme isn't the culprit. The people executing it up front have to win their blocks.** Until they do, nobody gets to blame the back, the coordinator, or the weather — and I'm including my own gap-scheme suggestion in that. When the data contradicts me, I'd rather tell you than quietly move on.

## TYLER LINDERBAUM — AND I'M GOING TO BE FAIR

Now the man everyone is talking about, and I want to handle this properly because this is a player I like and a player who has been genuinely good in this league.

**Linderbaum signed a massive deal in the offseason, making him the highest-paid center in football at reportedly about $27 million a year.** When you're paid like that, the expectation is that you anchor the line.

**Entering Sunday, his season grade sat around the middle of the pack among centers.** Not bad. Not elite. Middle.

**And on Sunday against Buffalo's interior, he did not look like a man being paid like the best in the business.** Their defensive tackles won more than a few reps in the middle, and there were snaps where he simply **could not reach his man on outside zone** — which is the foundational block of this entire offense.

## AND THEN THE MOMENT THAT DECIDED THE GAME

**Fourth-and-one at the Buffalo 13-yard line. Second quarter. The Raiders went for it with their version of the tush push — and Linderbaum was flagged for a FALSE START.**

**The drive stalled. The Raiders took a field goal. And when the day was over, three points was exactly the margin.**

Raider Nation, that is the cleanest cause-and-effect you will ever see in a football game. Fourth-and-one inside the 15 is a touchdown opportunity. A false start turned it into three points. The final margin was three points.

**And it was the THIRD TIME this season he has been called for a penalty in that exact situation.**

To his credit — and this matters — **he owned it.** He didn't hide, he didn't dodge, he said it was on him. That's the accountability you want from a veteran, and it's the reason I believe he bounces back. Kubiak defended him as a leader and a major bright spot, and said a man who's hard on himself usually cares.

**But belief is not the same as results. At $27 million a year, the results have to come.**

## THE ZERO-SACK PARADOX — THE BEST ARGUMENT OF THE WEEK

Now the dissenting voice in all of this is Kubiak himself. After the game he said he **likes what this team is doing protecting the quarterback**, and he praised Spencer Burford as a really great addition who keeps improving.

And here's why I understand the pushback.

**Kirk Cousins was not sacked all day. Not once. Forty-one pass attempts, 313 yards, two touchdowns — and nobody put him on his back.**

On paper, Raider Nation, **that is elite protection.** Zero sacks on 41 attempts is a genuinely excellent number and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

**But paper doesn't tell the whole story. And if you want proof, go back to the third quarter.**

The Raiders opened the second half with a three-and-out. And on the key play — **third-and-10** — they called a quick-game concept with a **ONE-STEP DROP.**

One step. The ball is out almost before the quarterback finishes the snap.

Now, a quick throw on third-and-10 can be smart if you expect the defense to play off and concede a short completion. **But it can also be a confession that you do not trust your blockers to give your quarterback time to throw a real route.**

**Cousins wasn't sacked — but that's partly because this offense got rid of the ball quickly, used a lot of play action, and leaned on a stable of tight ends to chip and help.**

**Take away those safety nets and the picture gets uglier. That, in my opinion, is the line between a pass protection unit that is WORKING and a pass protection unit that is BEING PROTECTED.**

Raider Nation, that sentence is the whole investigation. And it reconciles everything we've been confused about for a month: how can this line be first in the NFL in pass blocking success rate, allow zero sacks against Buffalo, **and** have a left guard who led all guards in pressures allowed? Because the scheme is hiding it. Quick throws, play action, and tight ends staying in to block.

**And remember what that costs.** Michael Mayer disappeared in New England precisely because he was blocking instead of running routes — Kubiak told us so himself. **The protection isn't free. It's being paid for with your receiving weapons.**

## NOW THE GOOD NEWS, AND IT'S REAL

Let's not skip the positives, because it has not been all bad.

**DJ Glaze has been the surprise of this group all season — he gave up just SEVEN pressures coming into the Buffalo game, and he's shown real improvement in both phases.** Glaze has taken a genuine leap in year three, and I'm happy for him.

Raider Nation, seven pressures in five-plus games is excellent for a young tackle. And remember what his head coach said about him two weeks in a row: most consistent guy, never misses practice, becoming a quiet leader. **Availability plus production equals a cornerstone.** He's it.

**Spencer Burford has had more than his share of rough outings — including a game earlier this month where he gave up FIVE pressures and earned a pass blocking grade of ZERO.** A zero. **Yet he also had a terrific day against Kansas City.** So the story with him is **inconsistency, not hopelessness** — and after I called for his benching nine days ago, I'll note again that the coach says he keeps improving and a reporter who watched the film said he was clearly better.

**And Rogers' pass protection was a step up from New England.** That's incremental progress from a young group.

Add Jeanty's first rushing touchdown and zero sacks allowed, and that's a genuine list of good news. It's just not big enough yet.

## BECAUSE THE BIG NUMBERS ARE BRUTAL

**As a team, the Raiders are averaging about three yards a carry.**

**Kolton Miller gave up a dozen pressures across the previous two games.** That's our highest-paid tackle and a man who has been a quality starter for years — now in a rough stretch.

**Burford entered Sunday with the most pressures allowed among all guards in the league.**

**And then the third quarter. My goodness, the third quarter.**

## THE WORST NUMBER ON THIS FOOTBALL TEAM

Raider Nation, I want you to sit down for this one, because it's the statistic that reframes the entire season.

**The Raiders have been outscored by 26 POINTS in the third quarter this season — the WORST margin in the NFL.**

Worst in the league. Not third worst like the run game. **Worst.**

Kubiak has called the lack of production in that quarter a real problem, said he's upset about how this team starts the second half, and confirmed that the staff is now reviewing the entire halftime process.

And here's the mechanism, because the pieces all fit: **when your offense can't run, your line can't create space, and the other team makes halftime adjustments — you fall behind quickly.** The opponent comes out of the locker room with a new plan. You come out with the same plan and no way to establish the run. Three-and-out, punt, and suddenly it's a two-score game.

**Twenty-six points. One quarter. Worst in football.** If this team fixes nothing else, fixing that alone probably makes them 5-1 instead of 3-3.

## "BUT THEY SCORED 30!" — LET ME ANSWER THAT HONESTLY

Some of you have asked me why I'm so hard on this offense when it put up 30 points on Sunday. That's a completely fair question, so let me answer it directly.

**The Raiders scored 30, yes — but a big chunk of that came late**, including a **44-yard touchdown from Cousins to Tre Tucker when the Raiders were already trailing by double digits.**

That is a gorgeous throw and a beautiful play. I'm not taking it away from either man. **But late scores against a defense playing prevent do not carry the same weight as scores that win football games.**

And remember the other half of it: **this defense has been creating plenty of short fields and extra possessions all season.** Strip that away and ask a different question.

**How often does this offense drive the length of the field for a touchdown when the game is actually on the line?**

**Too rarely.**

The Raiders went **two for five in the red zone**, and Kubiak said that starts with him. **And they kicked field goals in at least two situations where a touchdown would have won the game.**

**That is why the red zone and the run game are connected.** If you can't get a yard on the goal line, you can't punch it in. If you can't punch it in, you settle for three. And when the other team is scoring touchdowns, **three-point trades do not win football games.**

The Raiders lost by three. I hate being the guy who says it, but **that is no coincidence.**

## SO WHAT'S THE VERDICT ON THIS LINE?

Let me give you my honest final grade, position by position, because this is what the evidence supports.

**DJ Glaze: genuinely good and improving.** Seven pressures, never misses practice, year-three leap. Keep him and build around him.

**Caleb Rogers: earned another start.** Good and bad, but a step up in protection and he played all 60 snaps without collapsing. The competition worked.

**Spencer Burford: inconsistent, not hopeless.** A zero-graded game and a terrific game in the same month. That's a player you coach, not a player you cut — but you don't guarantee him anything either.

**Kolton Miller: in a rough patch.** A dozen pressures in two games from your veteran anchor is a real concern, and the staff just signed two tackles.

**Tyler Linderbaum: the one that has to change.** Middle-of-the-pack grade at the highest center salary in football, beaten on outside zone, and a third pre-snap penalty in the same situation that cost this team a touchdown in a three-point loss. He owns it, he cares, and I believe he'll respond — but the response is overdue.

**And the unit as a whole: being protected rather than protecting.** Zero sacks with one-step drops, play action and tight end chips is survival, not strength.

**My watch list for the Rams:** the first play of the third quarter. The carry count split between Washington and Jeanty. Whether a true seven-step concept gets called without a tight end chipping. And whether anybody jumps on fourth down.

Raider Nation, this group is not a disaster — it's the best pass-protecting unit in football by one measure and the third-worst run-blocking unit by another, which means it is exactly half of an offensive line. **Half isn't enough to close games out.**
