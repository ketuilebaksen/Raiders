## FIRST, THE INJURY REPORT

Kubiak opened with it, short and direct:

**"JPJ is questionable, and Patrick Johnson is OUT."**

**So Jackson Powers-Johnson is questionable.** Raider Nation, that's an interesting one — this is a man who took **zero offensive snaps against Buffalo** after being benched at right guard, whose head coach then went out of his way to publicly praise his attitude and say **"his best football can be in front of him."** **And now he's questionable in a week where the Raiders are actively shaking up the interior.**

**And Patrick Johnson is out.**

Asked whether that opens the door to an elevation, Kubiak gave the most Kubiak answer possible: **"We'll see you tomorrow."**

**He also wished everybody a happy Halloween, which I appreciated.**

## AND THE FOUR DECISIONS THAT LAND TOMORROW

Here's the full list of what this coaching staff has NOT decided yet.

**One: Jack Bech's activation.** Asked if he's anticipating activating Bech this week, Kubiak said: **"Yeah, we've got till tomorrow. So we'll see who it's going to be."**

Raider Nation, **listen very carefully to the phrasing: "we'll see WHO IT'S GOING TO BE."** That's not a man deciding whether to activate somebody. **That's a man deciding WHICH somebody.** Bech is coming back from a **broken forearm** and was designated to return from IR on Monday. **And activating him means somebody else comes off that 53.**

**Two: Dont'e Thornton Jr.'s playing time.** Asked if there's a chance he gets any snaps Sunday, Kubiak said one word: **"TBD."**

**TBD.** After three weeks of this saga, after the activation we celebrated on Tuesday — **his snap count is still to be determined.** I've been pounding the table for this man and I'll keep doing it: **a 4.30 receiver who averaged 25.4 yards a catch to lead the FBS is the cheapest answer to a stacked box that exists on this roster.** But I'm not going to promise you snaps the coach won't promise.

**Three: an elevation for Patrick Johnson's spot.** "We'll see you tomorrow."

**Four — and this is the one I care about most: Trey Zuhn at right guard.**

## THE ZUHN UPDATE, AND IT EXPLAINS THE DELAY

Raider Nation, we finally got the piece of information that makes this whole right guard saga make sense.

Asked what he's seen from Zuhn this week after promising to shake up the line, Kubiak said:

**"Trey had an INJURY a few weeks ago. He's battled back through it, getting healthy. And I just see a guy that's working hard, a guy that is ACCOUNTABLE, and a young man that has a BRIGHT FUTURE ahead of him. He's just got to keep getting better."**

**An injury a few weeks ago.** That's new. **That's why the "Swiss Army knife" who can play tackle, guard and center hasn't been on the field while this offense ran for 48 yards.** He wasn't being held back by the depth chart. **He was hurt.**

**And then the question we all wanted: do you anticipate him getting any playing time on Sunday?**

**Kubiak: "COULD BE."**

**Could be.** Raider Nation, from a coach this guarded, **"could be" is practically a depth chart.** He took first-team reps earlier this week. His head coach called him accountable with a bright future. **I think the rookie plays.**

## NOW THE ANNOUNCEMENT THAT ACTUALLY MATTERS: THE CAPTAINS

**Ashton Jeanty. Maxx Crosby. Dylan Laube.**

Asked what he saw from them in particular this week, Kubiak said: **"It's really a body of work. Every week we name new captains, and those are guys who have been great leaders for our football team, doing things the right way. Excited for Max, excited for Ashton, excited for all three of those guys. Now we've got to go play, got to go perform."**

Raider Nation, **look at that list and tell me it isn't a statement.**

**Maxx Crosby — the man every national columnist spent this week telling the Raiders to trade.** A man with trade rumors circling him like vultures, two sacks in seven games, **and his head coach puts a captain's patch on him three days before the deadline window.** You don't make a man a captain and then ship him out. **That is a message to a locker room and to 31 other front offices.**

**Ashton Jeanty — a ROOKIE running back, carrying a captaincy on a team that's lost four straight**, averaging 3.2 a carry behind a line that's been shuffled all month. **He has every excuse available and he's leading instead.**

**And Dylan Laube. A two-time captain. Who isn't a starter.**

## AND KUBIAK EXPLAINED WHY LAUBE IS A CAPTAIN BETTER THAN I COULD

Asked about Laube being one of the first two-time captains of the year, Kubiak said something that I want every young player in football to hear:

**"Versatility is key. Versatility is KEY. If you can play four core special teams AND be a producer on your side of the ball — then you've got VALUE in this league."**

**"He has a lot of value on our team from his work ethic, how he goes about his business, the type of person that he is. He's made plays for us. And then, as you make plays, the expectations GROW."**

Raider Nation, **that is the single clearest statement of how a modern NFL roster actually works.**

**Four core special teams units plus production on offense equals a career.** It's the same thing Kubiak said about Laube weeks ago: *"If you're not the starter at any position, you have to provide value on multiple different parts of the team. You have to wear a lot of hats and do them well — and that means being accountable and having people COUNT ON YOU all the time."*

**And it's the exact opposite of what happened to Jonah Laulu**, a productive player who became a healthy scratch and then got claimed by Tennessee. **In this building, value is something you manufacture, not something you're owed.**

## AND HERE IS HOW MAXX CROSBY ACTUALLY EARNS IT

This was my favorite exchange of the press conference. **Kwity Paye had talked about how Crosby sets the standard on defense and everybody is chasing it.** So a reporter asked Kubiak how that plays out in practice.

**And listen to this list:**

**"First guy in the building. One of the last guys to leave. Taking care of your body. Being extremely intentional about going through walkthroughs. FINISHING EVERY PLAY IN PRACTICE. Having input with all facets of the game plan. PUSHING HIMSELF IN SPECIAL TEAMS. All the above."**

Raider Nation, **read that last one again. Pushing himself in SPECIAL TEAMS.**

**A man with 69.5 career sacks — 16th since 1999 — who is reportedly being shopped by half the league, is grinding on special teams in practice at 3-4.**

**That's why you don't trade him for two late first-round picks.** You can replace sack production. **You cannot replace the man who sets the clock for everybody else in the building.**

## NICO DEAN WANTS TO TAKE IT UP A NOTCH

And here's the other leadership story, and it's a good one.

**Nico Dean said he felt he'd been holding guys accountable — but that he needs to take it up a notch.** Asked whether he's seen more from him, Kubiak's answer was almost stern:

**"That's a MIDDLE LINEBACKER'S JOB. You've got to be the leader of the defense. He's been our leader — and as he produces and does well, then we continue to put more on his plate and expect more from him."**

Raider Nation, **that's not a pat on the head. That's a standard.** This is the man who said after the Rams loss that **"we can't all be individual mercenaries out there"** — and now he's saying he needs to be louder about it, **and his head coach is saying: good, that's literally your job description, and we're going to ask for more.**

**If this defense turns around, it starts in that huddle.**

## AND HE REFUSED TO BLAME THE INTERIOR DEFENSIVE LINE

Asked how he feels about the interior defensive line's play and whether he needs more from them to stop the run better, Kubiak would not take the bait:

**"It's TEAM DEFENSE. It's team defense — rushing, coverage, working TOGETHER."**

Raider Nation, **I'll be honest: part of me wanted him to be harder on that group.** This defense is **30th in the NFL against the run, allowing 143 yards a game at 5.0 a carry — second worst in football.** They've given up **30, 45, 33 and 35 points in four straight losses. Almost 36 a game.** The Rams ran for **199.**

**But he's also right, and he's consistent.** He told us the leaks are **"tackling, alignment, ASSIGNMENT."** A linebacker called the unit mercenaries. A broadcast analyst said nobody trusts his brother on the A gap. **Everybody in and around that building has landed on the same answer: it isn't one position group. It's eleven men playing one defense instead of eleven.**

**And asked what gives him optimism they can turn the corner on stopping the run AND running the ball this Sunday, his answer was simple and unguarded:**

**"Just belief in our guys. Belief in our guys, belief in our coaches."**

**That's all he's got left to offer, and honestly? At 3-4 with the deadline circling, belief is not a small thing.**

## THE JALEN NAILOR ANSWER THAT GAVE ME HOPE

This exchange is the one I'd circle if I were betting on Sunday.

A reporter made an observation I've made myself watching the tape: **Jalen Nailor IS getting separation. He IS getting open down the field.** So what's it going to take for him and Cousins to turn that into big plays?

**Kubiak: "It's TIME ON TASK, as it continues to go. Those guys have really developed a really good relationship. And we're expecting Jalen to make plays, Tre, whoever's in there — they've got to get open, and Kirk's got to go through his progressions."**

Raider Nation, **this matters a great deal**, because for two weeks the national narrative has been that this offense cannot separate against man coverage. Film analysts said it. **And Kubiak is pushing back with something specific: Nailor IS getting open. The connection just hasn't been made yet.**

**"Time on task" is coach-speak for repetition. And Kirk has to go through his progressions** — which is the coach gently noting that the second and third reads matter as much as the first.

**If that deep connection hits once on Sunday, this whole offense looks different.**

## AND THE TRE TUCKER CONDITIONING STANDARD IS A LESSON

Asked when Tucker's conditioning first announced itself, Kubiak used the question to set a standard for the whole room:

**"That's what a starting NFL receiver has to do. You have to be in great shape. You've got to be able to run. YOU'VE GOT TO GO PLAY 70 PLAYS A GAME AT FULL SPEED. That's the expectation for that room — and he really sticks out. He's stood out since April."**

**Seventy plays a game at full speed.** That's the job.

And when the follow-up asked what a weapon it is that Tucker has a finishing kick in the fourth quarter, **Kubiak flipped it into a challenge: "That's the expectation. It's really good that he's in great shape — but WE'VE GOT TO ALL BE IN AS GOOD SHAPE AS HIM."**

Raider Nation, **that's a head coach using a compliment as a measuring stick.** Tucker leads this team with **29 catches for 474 yards and four touchdowns**, and the message is: **he's not the exception, he's the baseline.**

## AND THE SHORTEST, BEST ANSWER OF THE DAY

Asked about a player talking about needing to trust himself more in running the ball, Kubiak didn't philosophize:

**"We just got to go perform. We've got to go PRODUCE. Go produce on Sunday. That's what it's about for the entire offense."**

**And asked about competition this week: "When you're fighting for playing time, there is NO BETTER MOTIVATOR. We've got a young roster, and we've got to see who's going to be a big part of us moving forward."**

Raider Nation, that's the same man who said **"you can't sit in those chairs in those team meeting rooms if you're not an accountable human."** **Produce or somebody else gets your reps. That's the entire organizing principle here.**

## AND ONE MORE THING HE SAID THAT SHOULD WORRY YOU — OR MOTIVATE YOU

**It's an outdoor game, with an early kickoff.** A reporter asked whether there's anything to take from the New England trip to prepare for similar circumstances.

**Kubiak: "Yes. Yeah, absolutely. Just the importance of getting up, getting out of bed, and getting ready to roll — and getting ready to START FAST."**

Raider Nation, **do you remember what happened in New England? 45-17.** An early road kickoff where this team got run off the field. **And the Rams game ended with a 28-6 halftime deficit at HOME.**

**He knows. He named the exact failure mode: an early body clock and a slow start.** **That's the thing I'm watching in the first fifteen minutes on Sunday more than anything else.**

## NOW THE SPECIAL TEAMS SEGMENT — AND IT'S THE BEST PART OF THIS VIDEO

Raider Nation, the one phase where this team has been genuinely good is special teams. **Kubiak called them "exceptional" after the Rams loss.** And the coordinator gave us a full breakdown.

**On Dylan Laube's returns: "It's pretty self-explanatory. The kid's really having a good year. He brings great preparation — and then it's coming to life during a game. He prepares like that during the week, practices hard, studies hard, watches a lot of film. He's doing a great job for us."**

**And here's a tactical detail I love.** Asked about teams trying to kick away from Laube, he admitted it's a real challenge — **"no question"** — but: **"we also have other guys we feel good about putting the ball in their hands. Last week we went with the SINGLE RETURNER. We won't do that all the time, but certain teams you can do that, and certain teams you can't."**

**A single returner.** That's a deliberate chess move to force the ball to your best man, and they used it against Los Angeles.

**And on how close those returns are to becoming touchdowns, his explanation was genuinely educational:**

**"It's difficult to break a touchdown, because the field is a little constricted. When you break it out to the sideline, it's going to be a smaller space. There aren't many returns that break right back at the middle of the field — and THOSE are usually the ones that score. Obviously we want a touchdown, but we'll take those 50-YARDERS ALL DAY LONG."**

**"Anytime you get the ball to the 50-yard line on any kicking play, it's usually a pretty positive deal, and he's done that for us several times this year. And the guys blocking for him are doing a heck of a job too."**

## AND NOW THE FULL STORY OF THAT FAKE PUNT

Raider Nation, this is the detail I've been waiting on all week.

**AJ Cole — the punter — played quarterback on a fake punt against the Rams.**

**And the coordinator's assessment: "That was good. That was a good look for us. It took some REAL GUTS for coach Kubiak to call it IN THAT END OF THE FIELD. It was still a pretty tight game right there, but we felt like it was a play that was there, and it was good to see it executed."**

**"Real guts." In our own end of the field. In a tight game.**

And he made sure to spread the credit: a reporter pointed out that everybody sees the fake and the throw and the run, **but Alex Ward threw a great block from his position.** The coordinator agreed: **"Very well. When that stuff works, it's always a positive. I just wish we'd have gotten a touchdown out of it — that would have been great. But they've done a good job of working on that stuff all year."**

**And the best answer of the entire week came when somebody asked what he feels watching it from the sideline. Is he holding his breath?**

**"You're QUIETLY CONFIDENT. How's that?"**

**Then the honesty: "Unfortunately, I've been on the other side of it too, where it doesn't always work out. So you just try to get through the down as quick as you can and hopefully your guys execute — which they have up to this point."**

**And when asked whether AJ Cole lobbies for a fake every single week, he laughed: "It depends on who you're playing. Sure, they'd love to run a fake all the time — BUT THEY DON'T HAVE TO DEAL WITH THE CONSEQUENCES EITHER."**

**Raider Nation, that's the funniest and truest thing a coach has said all season.**

## BUT NOW THE WARNING — AND IT'S A BIG ONE

Here's where the mood changed, because the coordinator was blunt about Sunday:

**"We're going to need everything we have this week, because this group that we're playing — the Jets — were NUMBER ONE IN THE LEAGUE LAST YEAR as far as special teams rankings. Or they ARE one or two, as you look back at all the metrics."**

**And then the number that genuinely alarmed me:**

**"They've got TWO RETURNERS THAT HAVE EIGHT TOUCHDOWNS BETWEEN THEM. Which — that's big. I mean, it's really seven, but it's eight, because he did score and it got called back. Number 18."**

**Eight return touchdowns between two men.**

Raider Nation, **think about what that means for a 3-4 team on the road with an early kickoff.** We have spent weeks talking about how this defense gives up 36 points a game and how this offense can't run the ball. **Now add a kick-coverage unit that gives up a 70-yard return and you are not losing 35-21 — you are losing by 24.**

**And he gave the opposing coach full respect: "Coach Banjo — I've got a lot of respect for the way he's coaching those guys. They really play hard."**

**So the one phase where the Raiders are winning is the one phase where the Jets are elite. That's the matchup of the game, and almost nobody is talking about it.**

## SO WHAT IS THE ACTUAL PATH TO BEATING THE JETS?

Let me put it together, because I think it's clearer than it's been in a month.

**One: don't start slow.** Kubiak named it himself — an outdoor early kick, same body clock as New England. **45-17 is what happens when you don't get out of bed.**

**Two: win the kicking game or at least survive it.** Laube to the 50 is a gift. **Giving up a return touchdown to a unit with eight of them is a death sentence.**

**Three: stop the run.** Thirtieth in the league. **"Team defense — rushing, coverage, working together."** Eleven men, one defense, no mercenaries.

**Four: hit ONE deep ball.** Nailor is getting open. Tucker runs all day. **Thornton — if he plays — averaged 25.4 yards a catch in college.** Kubiak said "quit throwing it so much," but one 50-yard completion changes every box count after it.

**Five: produce.** His word. **"Go produce on Sunday."**
