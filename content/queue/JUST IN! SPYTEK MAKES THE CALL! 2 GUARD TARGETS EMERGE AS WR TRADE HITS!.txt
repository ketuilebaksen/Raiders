## FIRST: WHAT SPYTEK ACTUALLY DID

Three moves. Here they are.

**One: the Raiders released 25-year-old receiver Jamari Thrash from the practice squad** — only a few weeks after adding him, **before he ever appeared in a game.** He entered this league as a **fifth-round pick by Cleveland** and never established a permanent role there.

Raider Nation, **I have to own something here.** When the Raiders signed Thrash, I made a whole video about it. **Two: the Raiders released defensive tackle Jonah Laulu.** More on this in a few minutes, because the timing is the most interesting thing in this entire video.

**Three — and this is the headline: Dont'e Thornton Jr. was activated to the active roster.**

## THE THREE-WEEK SAGA IS OVER

Let me walk you through how we got here, because the journey tells you everything about how this staff operates.

**Three weeks ago**, Kubiak was asked if there was a chance Thornton could be activated. **"Yeah. Yes."** I told you it was coming. **Then on Friday: "We are not. No."** And when pressed, Kubiak said something that stunned me — **"He's healthy. He will not be activated for this game"** — and then refused to say whether Thornton would even remain on the 53.

**Then Monday: "That's a decision we don't have to make till Wednesday."**

**And then Wednesday, Kubiak finally told us the real obstacle, and it was never medical:**

**"He's competing for a job. He's got to take the spot of another guy. So he's got to go beat out somebody."**

**And now he has.** Two men were released and Dont'e Thornton Jr. took a chair in that meeting room.

Raider Nation, **that is exactly the philosophy Kubiak described this week:** *"You can't sit in those chairs in those team meeting rooms if you're not an accountable human."* Nobody gets handed anything in that building. **Thornton earned it on a practice field while nobody was watching.**

**But activation is not production.** His physical ability has always created intrigue — **he has not yet proven he can be a consistent impact player.** If he gives this offense **vertical production, or even just forces defenses to respect another outside target**, he relieves pressure on Tre Tucker and the tight ends. **If he doesn't, the trade speculation gets louder, not quieter.**

So here's the room: **Tucker, Nailor, Cody White, a just-activated Thornton who hasn't played a snap, and a Bech who's played three games.** And **Brock Bowers — a tight end — is being used as the de facto number one wide receiver** because nobody else can hold the job.

**And Bech getting healthy forces ANOTHER roster decision within the next several weeks.** Somebody else has to lose a chair.

## THE BIG SWING: GARRETT WILSON

Now the most ambitious possibility on the board, and it's a genuinely exciting one.

**CBS Sports writer Tyler Sullivan identified the Raiders as one of the strongest potential destinations for New York Jets wide receiver Garrett Wilson.**

And Raider Nation, **pay attention to the reasoning, because it's smarter than a normal deadline rumor.** Sullivan's logic is **less about rescuing this season and more about creating the right environment for Fernando Mendoza.**

Think about that. **The first overall pick in the 2026 draft is expected to become the franchise quarterback eventually.** And when he takes over, what does he inherit? **Brock Bowers. Ashton Jeanty. And a receiver room averaging under 16 yards a game.**

**Put an established, proven number one receiver next to Bowers and Jeanty, and you accelerate a rookie quarterback's development by a year.**

**And Wilson would immediately move ahead of Tucker, Nailor and Cody White on that depth chart**, giving this offense **the boundary playmaker it flatly does not have.**

## AND THE AGE AND CONTRACT ARE WHAT MAKE IT REALLY INTERESTING

Here's why this isn't a rental.

**Garrett Wilson is only 26 years old and signed through 2030.**

Raider Nation, **that is the entire difference between this idea and everything else we've discussed at this deadline.** Jerry Jeudy has one year left and will want an extension. DeForest Buckner's deal is expiring. Hollywood Brown is a lottery ticket. **Wilson would be a player entering his prime, locked in for four more years, arriving at exactly the moment the young core matures.**

**And the production is real: he has surpassed 1,000 receiving yards in each of his first three seasons.** Three for three. **That's a legitimate number one receiver's résumé.**

## BUT I'M GOING TO GIVE YOU THE OTHER SIDE, BECAUSE IT'S SUBSTANTIAL

**He missed 10 games last season with an injury.** That's most of a year.

**And his current performance has not been enough to change the direction of a struggling Jets team.**

**And then there's the moment everybody saw.** During New York's Week 7 game against Miami, **television cameras showed Wilson yelling near quarterback Geno Smith.**

Now Raider Nation, **I'm going to be fair here, because I refuse to build a narrative out of a camera angle.** **Wilson later described the exchange as COLLABORATION rather than a personal confrontation.** He said **both players were frustrated by the offense's performance and wanted the unit to become more aggressive.**

**And I believe him.** I've watched enough football to know that two competitors shouting on a sideline about a game plan is not a feud. **But even if the moment didn't represent conflict between teammates, it showed the emotional toll another disappointing season is taking on that building.**

**A frustrated 26-year-old star on a losing team is exactly the kind of player who becomes available.**

## NOW THE PRICE — AND THIS IS WHERE I GET CAREFUL

Here's the obstacle.

**New York would likely demand a FIRST-ROUND selection** for a former first-round pick who has already received a major contract.

**And Raider Nation, that price is too high.** Not because Wilson isn't good — **because of the ten games he missed and because the Jets' continued struggles weaken their leverage, not ours.** A team going nowhere doesn't get to dictate premium terms.

**A second-round choice, though? That would make this proposal far more appealing.** And remember the market comparison we established: **Marvin Harrison Jr. — a former fourth overall pick — went from Arizona to Houston for a 2027 second and a sixth.** The bar exists. **It's a second, not a first.**

**And here's the actual decision John Spytek has to make, and it's a genuinely hard one:** pay **premium capital for a known talent already under contract**, or **preserve flexibility and draft a younger, cheaper receiver** in a rebuild that remains incomplete.

**Wilson would answer one of this roster's biggest questions immediately — but only if the Raiders refuse to let urgency turn a promising fit into an irresponsible price.**

Raider Nation, **that sentence should be taped to the wall in Henderson.** **A second-round pick? I'm making that call today. A first? I hang up and I draft a receiver in April.**

## NOW THE PROBLEM THAT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY RECEIVER

And here's where I have to be blunt.

**Seven games have established the offensive line as this team's largest problem.** Not the receivers. **The line.**

**Despite the significant offseason investment that brought Tyler Linderbaum to the middle of that unit — and he remains an important foundation — and despite DJ Glaze showing enough dependability across multiple seasons to warrant continued consideration, almost EVERY OTHER POSITION on that line could be re-evaluated.**

**Which is precisely what Kubiak confirmed on Wednesday: "We're going to give other guys opportunities. We're going to keep SHAKING IT UP until we find the right mix."**

**And the Rams loss reinforced the hardest truth about this situation: it is extremely difficult for this group to protect an IMMOBILE quarterback consistently.**

Raider Nation, that's the crux of it. **Kubiak has continued supporting Kirk Cousins — he declared him the starter before anyone could ask — which leaves this staff with one option: CHANGE THE PROTECTION RATHER THAN CHANGE THE PASSER.**

**You've committed to a 38-year-old who isn't escaping anybody. So the five men in front of him have to be right. There is no other door.**

## AND HERE IS THE STAT THAT SHOULD MAKE YOU ANGRY

**The offensive line has damaged the passing game AND contributed to one of the league's least productive rushing attacks for a FOURTH CONSECUTIVE SEASON.**

**Four years, Raider Nation.** This is not a Klint Kubiak problem. **This is not an Ashton Jeanty problem.** This is a four-season-old structural failure that three different coaching staffs have now inherited.

**Cousins' limited mobility magnifies every missed assignment. And the absence of a dependable ground game allows defenses to attack without hesitation** — which is exactly what Kubiak admitted when he said opponents can play **extensive man coverage because Las Vegas is not currently a credible rushing threat.**

He wants a more balanced offense. **But falling behind by multiple scores has repeatedly forced this team away from the run in the second half.** He's seen runners break tackles and believes the practice improvement has carried into games — **but balance cannot exist if the defense allows the margin to grow before the rushing attack ever develops.**

**That's the loop. And it starts up front.**

## COUSINS' OWN DIAGNOSIS, AND IT WASN'T ABOUT BLOCKING

I want to give the quarterback credit for the precision of his self-assessment after the Rams game.

**He said the Raiders failed to FINISH DRIVES and did not convert the critical moments that separate field goals from touchdowns.** And he pointed specifically to **possessions that reached scoring territory but ended with three points, no points, or an unsuccessful fourth-down attempt.**

**Remember the number: 0-for-3 in the red zone in the first half. The Rams went 4-for-4.**

Raider Nation, **those failures involve more than blocking — he's right about that.** They involve throws, catches, and separation. **But pressure and an ineffective running game make every single red zone decision harder.** When you're throwing it on second-and-nine inside the 20 because you can't run it, **the defense already knows what's coming.**

## THE TWO GUARD TARGETS: TELLER AND DICKERSON

So who could actually fix it? **Two names have emerged as possible targets: Wyatt Teller and Landon Dickerson.**

Raider Nation, **those are real, proven interior offensive linemen** — exactly the kind of player Tyler Linderbaum needs next to him. We talked last week about what a genuine All-Pro guard would do for a center who's taken criticism for the run game. **Either of these men would stabilize that interior immediately.**

**But I have to be honest about the enormous caveats.**

**One: the availability and acquisition cost of either player remain genuinely uncertain.** Nobody has reported that either is available. **These are analyst possibilities, not shopping-list items.**

**Two: Las Vegas would have to surrender draft capital or a player** — and **a rebuilding organization cannot afford a costly move that produces only a temporary upgrade.**

**Three, and this is the one I keep coming back to: the more realistic outcome may be NO MAJOR TRADE AT ALL.**

**Kubiak has repeatedly indicated that the answers must come from inside the building.** He said it almost word for word after the Rams loss: **"No one's coming to help us. We've got to figure it out ourselves, as a coaching staff, with the players."** That places the responsibility on this staff to **maximize the reconstructed roster it already has** — which is exactly what "shaking it up until we find the right mix" means.

**But maintaining the status quo carries its own cost.** Four consecutive seasons of a bottom-tier running game is not bad luck. **It's a bill that keeps getting forwarded.**

**My honest verdict: a proven guard could stabilize one area, but no single transaction repairs this entire offense.** Spytek has to decide whether a reasonable opportunity represents **genuine long-term value** or **merely an expensive response to four frustrating losses.**

## NOW THE MOST INTERESTING COINCIDENCE IN THIS ENTIRE VIDEO

Raider Nation, buckle up, because this sequence is genuinely juicy.

**The Raiders moved on from 26-year-old defensive tackle Jonah Laulu** after he became a **healthy scratch** and fell behind **Folorunso Fatukasi, Tonka Hemingway, and Thomas Booker IV** in the rotation.

**And NFL insider Jordan Schultz, who reported the transaction, noted something important: Laulu had been PRODUCTIVE last season.**

**His numbers: eight tackles for loss, four and a half sacks, and 86 tackles — the most by any Raiders defensive lineman across his first two seasons.**

**You don't normally release that guy in Week 8.**

**And here's the coincidence.** **Jordan Schultz also reported on October 27th that Indianapolis was ACTIVELY SHOPPING defensive tackle DeForest Buckner — and that several teams had expressed interest.**

**The same insider. The same week. One Raiders defensive tackle released, one All-Pro defensive tackle on the market.**

## BUT I AM NOT GOING TO OVERSELL IT

Raider Nation, **those numbers make the timing notable. They do not prove another acquisition is coming.**

**The release could simply reflect this coaching staff's current evaluation and create flexibility elsewhere on the roster.** In fact, there's a detail that argues for the boring explanation: **the open practice squad position could be used to bring Laulu BACK if he clears the necessary process.** You don't plan a blockbuster around a procedural roster shuffle.

**But I'd be lying if I said my eyebrow didn't go up.**

## AND HERE IS WHAT DEFOREST BUCKNER WOULD ACTUALLY MEAN

**Buckner would represent a dramatically different level of addition.**

**This season: 16 solo tackles, SIX SACKS, 11 hurries, and 20 total pressures.**

Raider Nation, **that is exactly, precisely, the medicine this defense needs.** And I'll tell you why in one number we've hammered all month: **Maxx Crosby is seeing the highest rate of chips and double teams of his entire career — SIXTEEN of them in the Buffalo game alone.** Rob Leonard stood at a podium asking out loud, **"Can we rush with four and get home?"** Kubiak admitted the **time to pressure has grown** because the league has their tape.

**You cannot double the edge when the man inside is already in the quarterback's lap.** Six sacks and 20 pressures from an interior player ends the Crosby double-team problem in one afternoon.

**And the money is manageable. Daniel Popper of The Athletic identified Las Vegas as a logical fit and projected a cap charge of approximately $6.5 MILLION in 2026.**

**Six and a half million. That's nothing for this roster.**

## AND BUCKNER HIMSELF SOUNDS LIKE A MAN READY TO GO

Here's the human side, and it's hard not to sympathize.

**After the Week 6 loss to Tennessee, Buckner said publicly that he was TIRED OF EXPERIENCING THE SAME PATTERN EVERY YEAR.**

Raider Nation, look at the history. **The Colts acquired him in March of 2020. They reached the postseason that season — and have missed it every single year since.** And through that entire decline, **he remained one of their best interior defenders.** Then **a neck injury cost him seven games in 2025**, and he's **appeared healthy again this season.**

**And then his general manager got fired and the franchise started selling.** A great player watching a decade disappear. **I understand the frustration completely.**

**The catch: his contract is expiring.** So Spytek would have to evaluate **both the trade compensation AND the possibility of a future extension** — for a player who turns the wrong side of 32 during the deal. **That's the same rental math we discussed two weeks ago, just with a better player attached.**
