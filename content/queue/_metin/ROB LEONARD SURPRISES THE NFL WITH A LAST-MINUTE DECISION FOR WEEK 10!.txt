Raider Nation, Rob Leonard walked into that room for the first time since his head coach publicly refused to fire him — and he said some things I did not expect to hear from a coordinator under this much heat.

**And he revealed exactly what the last-minute decision is every single week. In his own words:**

**"WHAT PERSONNEL PACKAGE DO WE WANT TO START WITH? WHAT ELEVEN DO WE WANT TO PUT OUT THERE TO START THE GAME? And then HAVE A PLAN TO GET TO A DIFFERENT ONE LATER."**

**"There's a MILLION VARIABLES. Is it COVERAGE? Is it PRESSURE? Is it a DIFFERENT FRONT? How did we PRACTICE that week? What was the BEST THING we're executing? Alright — I LIKE THAT AS A LEAD CALL."**

**"It's a WEEK-LONG CONVERSATION of that back-and-forth decision of what's best."**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S the decision. The opening eleven. And given that this defense has been getting carved up in first halves all season, the eleven men who run onto that field first on Sunday is the single most consequential call he'll make all week.**

**And then he gave us the best line any Raiders coach has delivered this season — about why this defense falls apart:**

**"FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL."**

Plus: what he said when asked about the VETERANS who are struggling — **and he admitted he knew exactly who the reporter was talking about.** What Klint Kubiak's public backing actually meant to him. The three words that are now this defense's entire identity. And what he's seen from **Fernando Mendoza on the scout team** that tells him the kid is ready.

## FIRST: THE PRESSURE QUESTION — AND HE DIDN'T DODGE IT

Raider Nation, the first real question was the big one: **how critical is it for you guys — EVEN IF YOU HAVE TO MANUFACTURE IT — to get pressure on the quarterback?**

**And Leonard said: "It's ALWAYS important. I'm thinking about that CONSTANTLY — like ALL AREAS THAT WE NEED TO IMPROVE ON."**

Raider Nation, **"manufacture it" is the operative word in that question and he didn't push back on it.**

**Because here's the problem in a sentence: when your four-man rush doesn't get home, you have to MANUFACTURE pressure — blitzes, simulated pressures, overloads, zone drops with tackles dropping out. And every single one of those costs you a body in coverage.**

**That's the McCaffrey touchdown. That's why our defensive tackles were out in coverage. That's the whole trade.**

**And the reason you have to manufacture it at all is the thing this channel has been saying since September: when an offense spends all afternoon chipping and doubling Maxx Crosby, SOMEBODY ELSE has to win a one-on-one. And they aren't.**

## AND THEN THE HARDEST QUESTION OF THE DAY — ABOUT THE VETERANS

Raider Nation, a reporter asked the question all of us have been thinking, and he asked it well:

**"You have some ROOKIES who are really playing well — considering they're rookies — and some VETERANS that are STRUGGLING. What do you do to help a vet? Because you know they're good. You PAID THEM A LOT OF MONEY. And you know they're not doing it ON PURPOSE. What do you do as a coach to get them back on track?"**

**And Leonard's answer started with the most revealing thing he said all day:**

**"I mean, just MANAGE them and COACH them, and it's HARD TO ANSWER THE QUESTION. I KNOW WHO EXACTLY YOU'RE TALKING ABOUT."**

**I KNOW WHO EXACTLY YOU'RE TALKING ABOUT.**

Raider Nation, **he could have played dumb. He could have said "I don't know who you mean." He didn't.**

**He acknowledged, on the record, that there are specific, well-paid veterans on this defense who aren't playing to their contracts — and that he knows exactly which ones they are.**

**And then he refused to name them: "You MANAGE ALL OF THEM. BUILD THE CONFIDENCE OF THE ROOKIES."**

**Raider Nation, that's the same thing Klint Kubiak did on Monday when he had the chance to throw two defensive tackles under the bus and said instead "that was not those guys' job."**

**Nobody in this building throws a player to the media. Not the head coach, not the coordinator. You can call that soft if you want. I call it the only reason a locker room keeps playing for you in Week 10 of a 4-5 season.**

**But Raider Nation, let's be honest about the subtext: a coordinator saying "I know exactly who you mean" about underperforming veterans is a coordinator telling you — carefully, politely — that the problem is not entirely the play sheet.**

## AND HE ADMITTED HE FEELS THE WEIGHT OF IT

Raider Nation, this answer was genuinely human and I want to read it to you properly.

Asked how he keeps the faith in his plan and process when the results aren't showing up:

**"I talk to the staff about this ALL THE TIME. I KNOW WHAT WE WANT TO DO, HOW WE WANT TO DO IT. THERE'S EVIDENCE OF US DOING IT. We obviously have to do it MORE CONSISTENTLY."**

**And then: "There's a WEIGHT THAT YOU FEEL THAT YOU CARRY — especially WHEN THE OFFENSE IS SCORING, that WE NEED TO DO OUR PART. That DOESN'T FEEL GREAT, as you know, ME PERSONALLY, and I know COLLECTIVELY AS A DEFENSE."**

**"And we're VERY TRANSPARENT about that. WE'VE GOT TO DO OUR PART to show improvement."**

**"And I'd argue there have even been times during the year that we've played REALLY, REALLY GOOD FOOTBALL. So you've got to be MORE CONSISTENT. THAT'S JUST TRUTH."**

Raider Nation, **"there's a weight that you feel that you carry, especially when the offense is scoring" — that's a man telling you he knows.**

**He knows the offense averaged 27.6 points a game. He knows they were SIXTH IN SCORING. He knows his unit is the reason this team is 4-5 instead of 7-2. And he said it out loud without being asked to.**

**And "that's just truth" is how he ended it. No spin. Raider Nation, that's exactly what Kubiak said he values about him: "we share a lot of the same values — TRANSPARENCY, HONESTY, STRAIGHT TO THE POINT."**

## AND NOW THE LINE OF THE SEASON — AND IT EXPLAINS EVERYTHING

Raider Nation, asked what's disrupting the consistency right now, Leonard gave the answer that unlocks this entire defense.

**"You play hard. When you play — I don't want to say BAD football — when you're STACKING INCONSISTENT PLAYS, it's always MULTIPLE THINGS."**

**"But I'd say — FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL."**

**"When you're ON THE FIELD A LONG TIME, MISTAKES TEND TO HAPPEN MORE OFTEN. We'll just put it that way."**

**FATIGUE MAKES COWARDS OF US ALL.**

Raider Nation, **stop everything and put that next to the San Francisco box score.**

**San Francisco held the ball for THIRTY-EIGHT MINUTES AND EIGHTEEN SECONDS.**

**They NEVER PUNTED. Not once.**

**They ran the ball FORTY-THREE TIMES.**

**In the first half alone, the play count was THIRTY-EIGHT TO SEVENTEEN.**

**Raider Nation, that defense played thirty-eight minutes of football. Forty-three times somebody had to take on a block on a running play. Their defensive linemen played more snaps in one afternoon than most units play in a game and a half.**

**And Rob Leonard just told you what happens to a human being in minute thirty-four: he stops setting the edge as hard. He stops shedding the block. He takes the easier angle. Not because he's a coward — because he's EMPTY.**

**And Raider Nation, this ties directly into what he said he needs: the offense has to help. Not with points. With POSSESSIONS. With a drive that takes six minutes. With a third-down conversion that lets eleven exhausted men sit down for ninety seconds.**

**You cannot fix a run defense that's on the field for thirty-eight minutes. You can only get it off the field.**

## AND THEN HE TALKED ABOUT KLINT KUBIAK, AND IT WAS GENUINELY MOVING

Raider Nation, this is the part that got me.

Asked what it meant to hear his head coach publicly back him — **"stand behind Rob, stand behind our defensive staff"** — after a sixth straight thirty-point game:

**"IT MEANS A LOT. NOT TOO MANY PEOPLE IN THIS PROFESSION DO THINGS LIKE THAT."**

**"I have a TON of — I can't tell you HOW MUCH RESPECT I have for Klint Kubiak. And AS A MAN FIRST, AS A COACH SECOND."**

**"We share a lot of the SAME VALUES: TRANSPARENCY, HONESTY, STRAIGHT TO THE POINT."**

**"It was VERY HUMBLING."**

Raider Nation, **"not too many people in this profession do things like that" is the saddest true sentence in football.**

**Because he's right. The industry standard is to let the coordinator take the bullet. It buys the head coach four weeks of quiet and it costs him nothing but another man's career.**

**And Klint Kubiak stood at a podium with a franchise record for futility in his hands and said: "I think the WORLD WANTS SOMEONE'S HEAD ON A PLATTER every time things don't go right. But we BROUGHT PEOPLE HERE FOR A REASON."**

**And later Leonard came back to it: "It feels GREAT TO BE ALIGNED. Just having HONEST CONVERSATIONS of what we're doing well, what we're NOT, what areas we need to improve on — and MAKING GAINS."**

**Raider Nation, you can disagree with the decision to keep him. That's a fair argument and I've made both sides of it on this channel. But don't pretend it costs nothing. A first-time coordinator who knows his boss won't sell him out coaches differently — he coaches BRAVER. And brave is what this defense needs on an early down.**

## AND HIS ANSWER ON HIS OWN CONFIDENCE WAS NOT ABOUT HIM AT ALL

Raider Nation, asked if he's seen his confidence waver:

**"NO. All I think about is THE GUYS. I want them to PLAY FAST."**

**And then the part that is genuinely the "last-minute decision" of the week: "IF ANYTHING MESSES WITH THAT, I'M LIKE — WE'LL TAKE IT OUT. WE WON'T DO IT."**

**"I want their confidence to be EXTREMELY HIGH AT ALL TIMES. So just having conversations in and out with them. And the ULTIMATE VISION of at least what I have — WE'LL GET THERE IN TIME."**

Raider Nation, **"if anything messes with that, we'll take it out. We won't do it" is a coordinator telling you he will cut a call out of the game plan on a Friday if his players don't feel fast running it.**

**That's real humility from a man who could easily be defending his scheme right now. He's saying: my beautiful call that nobody trusts is WORSE than a simple call everybody attacks.**

**And "we'll get there in time" — Raider Nation, there's the whole tension of this season in five words. He believes in a vision that requires time, on a team that's 4-5 and has eight games left.**

## AND THE THREE WORDS THAT ARE NOW THIS DEFENSE'S IDENTITY

Raider Nation, here's the fix in his own language.

**"EXECUTION FUELS EMOTION."**

**"We've got to EXECUTE BETTER — ESPECIALLY TO START THE GAME. Especially to start the game, WE'VE GOT TO START FASTER. And that's REALLY ALL ABOUT US."**

**"What I'm calling SENSE OF URGENCY TO GET LINED UP. PLAYING WITH PHYSICALITY. EVERYBODY RUN TO THE BALL. AND IT'LL HAPPEN. Once good plays happen, YOU CAN STACK THEM."**

**EXECUTION FUELS EMOTION.**

Raider Nation, **I love that phrase and here's why it matters.** **Most coaches have it backwards. They give a speech, hope the emotion creates the execution, and then wonder why a fired-up defense gives up 190 rushing yards.**

**Leonard's version is the right way around: you don't get juiced up and then play well. You make a play, and THEN you feel something. Emotion is the RESULT of execution, not the cause of it.**

**And look at his actual to-do list: GET LINED UP WITH URGENCY. PLAY PHYSICAL. EVERYBODY RUN TO THE BALL.**

**Raider Nation, that's not a scheme. That's three things a high school defense can do. And remember what Kubiak said about the McCaffrey touchdown — "we did NOT PEEL ON THE HALFBACK" — and about the first-half collapse. Lining up with urgency IS the fix for a defense that gets to the ball late and blows assignments.**

## AND HE WAS BEAUTIFULLY HONEST ABOUT NOT WATCHING THE OFFENSE

Raider Nation, this made me laugh, and it also tells you how locked in he is.

Asked how much more important it is to help the offense out now that there's a rookie quarterback:

**"It's DEFINITELY important. I mean — MY FOCUS? I DON'T SEE A SNAP OF BALL WHEN THEY'RE ON THE FIELD. You know what I mean?"**

**"When they're SCORING, I'm sitting there and I'm like, HECK, I HAVE NO IDEA WHAT HAPPENED. MY WIFE WILL TALK TO ME ABOUT IT. I'm sitting there looking at the IPAD trying to focus on the NEXT SERIES."**

**"So it's ALL ABOUT US. Just again — STACKING CONSISTENCY. CONFIDENT EXECUTION."**

Raider Nation, **his wife has to tell him what the offense did. That's a man who watches his iPad while 60,000 people watch the game.**

**And "it's all about us" is the right answer with a rookie quarterback in the building. He's not sitting there hoping Fernando Mendoza bails him out. He's planning the next series.**

## AND NOW SUNDAY'S PROBLEM: JAXON SMITH-NJIGBA

Raider Nation, this is the matchup, and Leonard did not sugarcoat it.

Asked how much stress he puts on his defense to stop him, and whether he's reassuring them it won't be a one-person job:

**"He's UNBELIEVABLE. He's NUMBER ONE IN THE LEAGUE across the category of — all things RECEPTIONS."**

**"But yeah, it's VERY CRITICAL. YOU START WITH HIM DAY ONE. We're STILL TALKING ABOUT HIM — all the way: EARLY DOWN, THIRD DOWN, RED ZONE."**

**"And NOT JUST COVERAGE-WISE. DIFFERENT THINGS THAT WE HAVE TO DO."**

Raider Nation, **"you start with him day one" means the entire week's game plan was built around one man before they looked at anything else.**

**And "not just coverage-wise" is the interesting part. That's not two corners and a bracket. That's motion rules, that's disguising the safety, that's maybe pressure timed to take away the window he lives in. That's a whole plan.**

**And the honest answer to the reporter's question is yes — it CANNOT be a one-person job. The league's leading receiver in receptions does not get handled by one defensive back having a nice day. Not Hezekiah Masses, not Germad McCoy, not anybody.**

**It's a bracket. It's help. It's eleven men knowing where he is on every snap.**

## AND HE TOLD US EXACTLY WHAT HE'S SEEN FROM FERNANDO MENDOZA

Raider Nation, this is my favorite piece of intel of the week, and it comes from the one man uniquely qualified to give it.

**Rob Leonard has spent nine weeks coaching the defense that Fernando Mendoza has been throwing against on the scout team.**

Asked what he's seen that tells him the kid is ready:

**"TIGHT WINDOW THROWS. He's on the SCOUT TEAM — just... ANTICIPATION."**

**"And it's been FUN TO SEE HIM MATURE — and a guy playing with A LOT OF CONFIDENCE."**

Raider Nation, **TIGHT WINDOW THROWS and ANTICIPATION. From the defensive coordinator he's been beating in practice.**

**Those are the two traits that separate an NFL quarterback from a college one, and they're the two things you CANNOT teach. Arm strength you can build. Footwork you can fix. Throwing it before the receiver is open — knowing it's going to be there — that's wiring.**

**And remember what Klint Kubiak said: "I see him progress in his ACCURACY and in his ABILITY TO MAKE GOOD DECISIONS, and I've seen those TWO THINGS REALLY JUMP OUT."**

**Raider Nation, the offensive head coach and the defensive coordinator — two men with completely opposite incentives — independently said the same thing about this rookie. That's the most encouraging fact of the week.**

**And his daily practice reps have been against Maxx Crosby, Quay Walker, and a live pass rush. Kubiak confirmed it: "he's got a lot of GAME SPEED REPS."**

## AND THE YOUNG DEFENSIVE BACKS ARE ACTUALLY DEVELOPING

Raider Nation, this is the good news and there's real substance here.

Asked about Germad McCoy, who got a lot of action against San Francisco and had **a really nice pass break-up in the END ZONE:**

**"He's PLAYING FASTER. HIS EYES ARE IN THE RIGHT SPOT. He MADE A PLAY ON THE BALL, which was AWESOME."**

**"Him and ZEKE are BOTH GROWING. And that's what you're looking for — IMPROVEMENT, right? It's been fun to get him MORE REPS so he can feel A LITTLE BIT MORE CALM out there."**

Raider Nation, **"his eyes are in the right spot" is the highest compliment a secondary coach can pay a young player.** **A corner with bad eyes is guessing. A corner with good eyes is PLAYING.**

**And "more reps so he can feel more calm" — that's the whole development philosophy. You don't calm a young player down with a speech. You calm him down with repetition until the game slows.**

**And remember Kubiak on the same two: "We've got YOUNG GUYS PLAYING GOOD FOOTBALL that we're going to KEEP PLAYING — all of them. Zeke and DP and Germad. LET THE CREAM RISE TO THE TOP."**

## AND THEN HE TOLD US A PRACTICE STORY ABOUT GERMAD THAT I CAN'T STOP THINKING ABOUT

Raider Nation, a reporter brought up Germad McCoy's 2024 tape — **"one of the best players in the entire country"** — and said McCoy himself has said he's starting to feel like that again. One play, then three or four plays. Have you seen the flashes?

**And Leonard lit up: "YEAH. There's — I'LL GIVE YOU ONE."**

**"There's one in practice MAYBE THREE WEEKS AGO. He has a RED ZONE JUMP BALL in the end — I mean, HE JUMPED OUT THE GYM. I was like, 'OH, THAT'S DIFFERENT.'"**

**And then: "He's doing GOOD. I see the consistency. HE'S IMPROVING. HE'S IMPROVING."**

Raider Nation, **"he jumped out the gym — I was like, oh, that's different" is a defensive coordinator being genuinely surprised by his own player's ceiling.**

**And remember the context Klint Kubiak gave us about this young man on Monday: "There's a guy that WE ALL DIDN'T KNOW IF HE'S GOING TO PLAY FOOTBALL THIS YEAR. And he's NOT MISSING PRACTICE. He's PLAYING IN GAMES. He's STAYING HEALTHY. He's PROGRESSING."**

**A man nobody was sure would play a single snap this season just jumped out of the gym in a red zone drill and made his coordinator say "that's different."**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S the kind of thing that happens in a season like this. The record is 4-5 and somebody is quietly becoming a player.**

## AND HE PROTECTED HEZEKIAH MASSES EXACTLY LIKE KUBIAK DID

Raider Nation, a reporter went back to the Hezekiah Masses touchdown — the 37-yarder to Jordan Watkins — and framed it honestly: **he played it well, it was a great throw and a great catch. How do you encourage a rookie who did it the way you coached him, when the other people are getting paid too?**

**And Leonard's answer was the most human moment of the press conference:**

**"I GRABBED HIM RIGHT AFTER. HIS HEAD WAS DOWN. HE WAS UPSET. He upset himself."**

**"HE'S AWESOME. And HE WASN'T THE ONLY ONE AT FAULT during that play. We'll just put it that way."**

**"He's COMPETING HIS BUTT OFF. HE WAS IN GREAT POSITION. And just KEEPING HIS CONFIDENCE HIGH."**

Raider Nation, **"I grabbed him right after, his head was down" — that's a coordinator who went and found a 22-year-old on the sideline of a game he was losing by three scores, because the kid was hurting.**

**And "he wasn't the only one at fault on that play" is the second time this week a coach has quietly taken blame off a rookie without naming the man who actually missed.**

**And remember what Kubiak said about him: "Zeke is ONE OF OUR BEST MAN GUYS out there. He CAN make that play and he WILL — he'll be given OPPORTUNITIES to do so moving forward."**

**Raider Nation, with the league's leading receiver coming to town, that rookie is going to get tested on Sunday. And both of his coaches have told him in public that they believe in him. That's how you build a corner instead of breaking one.**

## AND HE WAS ASKED DIRECTLY ABOUT QUAY WALKER IN COVERAGE

Raider Nation, this was the most pointed football question of the day, and his answer was revealing.

Asked whether Quay Walker has struggled in pass coverage — **"that's what it looks like"** — and how he can help him:

**"Every time we go to game plan, we're talking about MATCHING UP and WHO we're matching up on — and then the ZONE-TO-MAN RATIO. If we're STRAIGHT MAN-TO-MAN, what do we feel comfortable about THIS MATCHUP? Or if we're in ZONE, ISOLATED on the BACK, or whoever it may be?"**

**"I think he's done, honestly, a REALLY GOOD JOB. It's hard with that question to think of SPECIFIC SCENARIOS off the top of my head with him. I know there are SOME."**

**"But QUAY WELCOMES ALL CHALLENGES. He WORKS EXTREMELY HARD at it. He was out there DETAILING IT TODAY. And HE WANTS TO COVER ANYBODY — AS GREAT PLAYERS WANT TO DO."**

Raider Nation, **"I know there are some" is him declining to pretend there's no issue — and that's honest.** **Because we all saw the McCaffrey touchdown, and the film says Walker went the wrong way on that snap.**

**But "ZONE-TO-MAN RATIO" is the real answer buried in there. That's the lever. If a linebacker is better in zone than in man, you change the RATIO — you give him more zone snaps and let somebody else take the man assignment.**

**That's an actual adjustment. Not a speech. A ratio. And he said it's part of the game plan conversation every single week.**

**And "he was out there detailing it TODAY" — Raider Nation, that's a sixth-year linebacker working on his coverage footwork on a Thursday in Week 10 of a losing season. Whatever else you say about this defense, those men are still working.**

## AND THE LAST-MINUTE DECISION — THE OPENING ELEVEN

Raider Nation, here's the part the whole season now hinges on.

Asked what's different early in a game as opposed to later:

**"How AGGRESSIVE you want to be. Based on WHAT THEIR PERSONNEL IS, what OUR PERSONNEL IS. And WHAT GIVES YOU THE BEST CHANCE OF STARTING FAST? Is it COVERAGE? Is it PRESSURE? Is it a DIFFERENT FRONT? There's a MILLION VARIABLES."**

**"And then WHAT PERSONNEL PACKAGE DO WE WANT TO START WITH? WHAT ELEVEN DO WE WANT TO PUT OUT THERE TO START THE GAME? And then HAVE A PLAN TO GET TO A DIFFERENT ONE LATER."**

**"How did we PRACTICE that week? What was the BEST THING we're executing? Alright — I LIKE THAT AS A LEAD CALL."**

**"Very INTERACTIVE — both CALL-WISE and PERSONNEL-WISE. And it's a WEEK-LONG CONVERSATION of that back-and-forth decision of what's best."**

Raider Nation, **this is everything, because starting slow is THE problem.**

**Kubiak: "You've got to START THE GAME A LOT FASTER. Go TOE-TO-TOE in the first half so the second half isn't a THROWING FEST."**

**Kubiak again on Monday: "The FRUSTRATING PART of yesterday is just THE WAY WE STARTED. Can't start that SLUGGISH versus an ELITE TEAM."**

**And Leonard today: "Especially to start the game, WE'VE GOT TO START FASTER. And that's REALLY ALL ABOUT US."**

**Three separate press conferences. Same diagnosis. So the lead call and the opening eleven ARE the last-minute decision of Week 10 — and he just told us it's being argued about all week long.**

**Raider Nation, my ask is simple: whatever eleven he picks, put SEVEN OF THEM NEAR THE LINE OF SCRIMMAGE. Because the one thing we know for sure is that six or fewer in the box against a running team is an invitation.**

## AND YES — THEY DO SETTLE IN. AND HE TAKES CONFIDENCE FROM IT.

Raider Nation, the last question was a good one: how much do you take from the fact that you settle into games? You seem to FIND IT in the second half. Do you take confidence in that?

**"YEAH. YEAH. I mean, IT MAKES YOU FEEL GOOD. Guys have kind of a QUICK TRIGGER LIKE ME."**

Raider Nation, **and the numbers back him up completely.**

**San Francisco scored TWENTY-ONE before halftime and only TEN after it — and the other seven came off a pick-six the OFFENSE gave up.**

**Ten points allowed in the second half against the most efficient offense in football.**

**And Kirk Cousins said it on his way out the door: "Our defense did a NICE JOB in the second half getting us the ball back, creating TURNOVERS, TURNOVERS ON DOWNS."**

**So the unit CAN do it. Kubiak's words: "I SEE IT WORK. JUST NOT FOR FOUR QUARTERS."**

**Raider Nation, this defense is a very good second-half defense attached to a very bad first-half defense. If Rob Leonard can move whatever makes them good in the third quarter into the FIRST quarter, this team is a different football team in about ninety minutes of game clock.**

**That's the whole job. That's the last-minute decision. That's Week 10.**
