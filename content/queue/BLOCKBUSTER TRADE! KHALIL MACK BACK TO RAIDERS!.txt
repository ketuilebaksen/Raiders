## WHO PUT THIS ON THE TABLE

Adam Schefter put Khalil Mack on his list of possible trade candidates heading into this season's deadline.

That alone is worth paying attention to. Schefter does not throw names out for fun. When he's compiling a list of players who could actually move, those names come from somewhere.

But here's what makes this specific name hit different for us.

Mack isn't just some rental pass rusher on an expiring deal. He's the guy who wore silver and black from 2014 to 2018. The first-round pick who turned into Defensive Player of the Year. The player a lot of longtime fans still consider the best defender this franchise has had in over a decade.

And right now, he's sitting on a Los Angeles Chargers team that is 0-4.

## WHY 0-4 CHANGES EVERYTHING

Zero and four in the AFC West, on a roster that was supposed to be competing for this division. Instead the Chargers are staring down a lost season just four weeks in.

And that changes everything about how a front office thinks.

Teams that start 0-4 don't usually finish 0-4 and still hang onto every veteran contract on the books. They start making calls. They start asking themselves which 35-year-olds are going to be part of 2027 and which ones are worth converting into draft capital while somebody still wants them.

That's exactly the environment this rumor is dropping into. And I want to be blunt about something: without the Chargers collapsing like this, we are not even having this conversation. The entire premise of a Mack reunion is built on another team's disaster.

## AND THE PIECE THAT MAKES IT REAL FOR US

At the same time, ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported that the Raiders could be **buyers** before the trade deadline. Not sellers.

Buyers.

Raider Nation, stop and appreciate that distinction, because it's a notable thing to say about a franchise that's had its share of teardown years recently. For the last decade the deadline has been the time of year when this organization answered the phone instead of dialing it.

Combine that reporting with the Mack rumor and you've got a storyline that writes itself — for debate, for every Raiders fan who has wondered out loud what a reunion might look like.

So let's break it down the right way. Not just the emotion of it, but the math, the realistic trade cost, and whether this is something the front office should seriously consider or just something fun to argue about on a Tuesday.

## WHAT MACK ACTUALLY IS RIGHT NOW

Let's start with the player on the field today, not the highlight reel in your memory.

He's 35 years old. He's playing on a one-year deal worth $18 million with the Chargers. Through this season he's logged 14 tackles, five tackles for loss, and five quarterback hits.

That's not empty production. That's a man who can still get after the passer and set an edge, four years removed from his absolute peak. Tackles for loss and quarterback hits are the two categories that tell you an edge player is still winning his individual battles rather than just accumulating snaps.

For a defense that has needed juice off the edge for years, those numbers alone make this worth a conversation.

## THE CONTRACT MATH, WHICH ACTUALLY WORKS

Now the money, because this is where most trade rumors fall apart the second somebody looks at the numbers.

If Mack were traded to the Raiders this week, the reported breakdown is that Las Vegas would owe him roughly $6.2 million of the remaining $8 million on his deal, with the Chargers eating around $10 million.

Think about what that means. The Raiders would not be taking on some massive long-term burden. This would be a short, cap-friendly rental cost for a player who is still productive. Six million dollars for the back half of a season for a player of Mack's caliber is not expensive in a vacuum.

If you're purely looking at cap math, this isn't the problem.

The problem is what you give up to get him.

## THE PRICE FOR EVERYONE ELSE

A Chargers beat voice who covers that locker room daily put a number on it.

For a team outside the AFC West, a standard Mack trade would probably cost around a fourth-round pick.

And Raider Nation, I want you to remember that number, because it's the entire hinge of this video. A fourth-round pick is reasonable for a 35-year-old rental pass rusher who's still playing winning snaps. If that were our price, I'd be on the phone before the end of this sentence.

But the number gets stranger when you're talking about a trade between division rivals.

## THE PRICE FOR US

Because the Raiders and Chargers play twice a year, every single season, for as long as both franchises exist in this division. You don't usually hand a rival your good players for cheap, even in a lost season.

So the division-rival price tag, according to that same source, looks very different.

The speculated cost for the Raiders specifically would be sending **a sixth-round pick and a 2027 third-round pick** to the Chargers, while receiving Mack back along with a seventh-round pick.

Stop and look at that.

A sixth and a future third for a 35-year-old on a rental who turns 36 before next season even starts.

That's a real cost. That is not cheap just because he's one year away from free agency.

## WHY DIVISION TRADES COST MORE

And I want to explain the logic, because "division tax" gets thrown around without anybody defining it.

When you trade a player inside your division, you're not just handing over a player. You're handing over information. Familiarity. Reps against your own quarterback and your own scheme, twice a year, going forward. Mack would walk into our building, learn exactly how Rob Leonard communicates, exactly which protections give Cousins trouble, exactly what our tendencies are on third down — and then in eleven months he might be standing on the other sideline again as a free agent.

The Chargers know that. That's precisely why the speculated cost for a Raiders-specific deal is higher than what a non-division team would pay. They're not being difficult. They're pricing in a real risk.

## MY HONEST TAKE, AND IT'S NOT THE FUN ONE

Here's where I'll give you my real verdict, because I think this is where fans let nostalgia run over football logic — and this channel doesn't do that to you.

I would absolutely love to see Khalil Mack finish his career in silver and black. There's a storybook angle here that is genuinely hard to ignore. The guy who was drafted here. Who became Defensive Player of the Year here. Who got traded away under circumstances that still sting a lot of you to this day. Coming back one more time to close the loop.

That's a great story for highlight packages and nostalgia videos.

But building a roster isn't about nostalgia.

Giving up a future third-round pick, even with just a sixth attached, for a rental who's 35 years old — and handing it to a division rival that would now also be stronger on tape prep against us for two more games a year — that is a tough sell on pure roster-building logic.

This front office has been trying to build something sustainable. Mortgaging future draft capital for a short-term rental at a position where we already have pieces doesn't fit that plan unless the price comes down significantly from what's being speculated.

And look, this is consistent with what I told you yesterday when I graded that Kansas City film. Maxx Crosby earned an A-minus. Adam Butler earned a B-plus. The Kelce coverage earned a B-plus. **The pass rush is not what lost that football game.** The receiver room and the interior offensive line are what lost that football game. You don't spend a future third on your strongest unit.

**My verdict: at the speculated division price, I pass. At a fourth-round pick, I run to the phone.**

## WOULD MACK EVEN WANT US?

There's one more piece, and it's the one that keeps the dream alive.

That same source speculated that if the Chargers keep losing, ideal landing spots for Mack could include the Buffalo Bills, the Baltimore Ravens, and yes, the Las Vegas Raiders.

Again — that's speculation about preference, not a report of actual trade talks happening.

Understand why that matters mechanically. A 35-year-old on an expiring deal has real leverage in these situations, because a team won't trade for a player who doesn't want to be there, and a player can make a destination difficult if he's unhappy about it. If Mack ever says publicly that he wants to finish this in Las Vegas, the Chargers' leverage over the division tax starts to shrink.

## THE SMARTER BLOCKBUSTER: JOSH SWEAT

Out of that group, I think Josh Sweat is a far more realistic and more exciting target for this team than Mack. And let me explain why.

Sweat doesn't carry the same age concern. He's not on a one-year rental situation where you're paying future draft capital for half a season of production before he likely walks in free agency. You're buying a player who could still be on this roster when Fernando Mendoza is taking snaps.

If the Raiders are going to be buyers — and Fowler's reporting says they might be — then spending assets on a player who fits the longer-term timeline makes a lot more sense than spending them on a 35-year-old rental, no matter how much sentimental value that rental carries.

And there's a quieter advantage. Sweat isn't walking straight back into a familiar building full of players who used to be his teammates, ready to tell the Chargers everything about how the Raiders do business eleven months from now.

That's the version of "blockbuster" I'd actually sign off on.

## WHY WE MIGHT NEED TO BE BUYERS AT ALL

Let's talk about why this team is even in this position, because the injury attrition this year is a real factor heading into the deadline.

A third-round pick from this draft class is done for the season with an Achilles injury. That's confirmed, that's real, and it hurts a class that was supposed to be adding depth up front.

Jackson Powers-Johnson has already missed more than a dozen games across his first three seasons, which tells you durability has been an issue there regardless of talent level — and we spent an entire video yesterday arguing he should still start at guard the moment he's cleared.

And receiver Dante Thornton Jr. just returned to practice this week for the first time since landing on injured reserve back on August 31st with a hamstring injury. So there's been real attrition at that position group too.

None of those situations are directly solved by trading for Khalil Mack. But together they paint the picture of a team that's had to manage injuries all year and might look to the deadline to patch holes rather than make one emotional splash.

And that's actually where I think the real Raiders deadline story is headed: **depth additions, not reunion storylines.**

So where does this leave the actual Mack question? Let me line it up honestly.

The sentiment is real. The nostalgia is real. His production this season, even at 35, is real — 14 tackles, five tackles for loss, five quarterback hits for a player on a bad team. Schefter put him on the list, and Fowler says we could be buyers.

But the price being speculated — a sixth plus a future third, to a division rival, for a 35-year-old pending free agent — that's where it breaks down for me.

My honest take, and this is opinion, not reporting: **I don't think this trade happens.** Not at that price. Not with a division rival involved. Not for a rental who turns 36 before the Raiders would even see a full offseason with him.

That said, I completely understand why this rumor has legs. Mack is the kind of name that creates emotion instantly for anyone who watched him dominate in this uniform.

## THE ONE THING THAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

And here's the scenario where I flip completely.

If the Chargers truly collapse further — if the losses keep stacking and that front office decides to blow it up entirely — the price could come down. A fourth-round pick, as speculated for a non-division team, is a *very* different conversation than a third and a sixth going to a rival.

If Mack's actual market ends up closer to that lower number, I'm not writing another word of caution. At a fourth, you take the Hall of Fame talent, you put him opposite Crosby, you let him rotate with Kwity Paye, Malcolm Koonce and Nolan Smith, and you let this fan base have the moment it's wanted for eight years.

So for now, treat this exactly as what it is: a name on a list, a plausible scenario given the Chargers' record, and a rumor that makes for great debate but doesn't have enough concrete traction yet to call it likely.
