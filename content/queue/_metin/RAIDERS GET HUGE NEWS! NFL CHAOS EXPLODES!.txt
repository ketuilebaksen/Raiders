## WHERE OUR BLOOD PRESSURE STARTED

Let's start where every Raiders fan's Sunday ended.

**The Raiders dropped a 33-30 heartbreaker to the Buffalo Bills at Allegiant Stadium.** And just like that, the shiny 3-0 start is a distant memory. **Three straight losses** — a New England beatdown, a Kansas City nail-biter, and now a Buffalo thriller that slipped through the fingers.

**The Raiders are 3-3.** And if you're feeling emotionally whiplashed, Raider Nation, you are in very good company.

This team looked like world-beaters against Miami, the Chargers and the Saints. Then the schedule got a lot meaner, and the mistakes got a lot louder.

**But for the first time in a while, Monday brought honest-to-goodness good news out of that building** — and I want to walk you through all of it, because it matters.

## THE HUGE NEWS: TONKA HEMINGWAY IS ON HIS WAY BACK

Here's the one I know you've been waiting for.

**Tonka Hemingway is on his way back.** The second-year defensive tackle — **the Raiders' top interior defensive lineman and their snap leader up front** — suffered a concussion against the Patriots and sat out the Bills game.

**On Monday, Klint Kubiak said he expects Hemingway to return to practice this week.**

Now let's be careful and honest here, the way we always are with this: **practicing and playing are two different things, and the concussion protocol has the final say.** Nobody should be rushing this and nobody should treat a practice report as a guarantee.

**But the target is clear. If he clears every hurdle, he's suiting up Sunday at Allegiant Stadium against the Los Angeles Rams.**

## AND YOU COULD FEEL HIS ABSENCE

Raider Nation, this matters more than the headline suggests, and here's why.

**Adam Butler and Jonah Laulu stepped up and did their jobs, and real credit to them** — Butler especially has been the most consistent interior player on this roster. But **Hemingway is the guy who makes the middle of that defense feel like a locked door.**

And look at what happened without him. **The Bills ran for more than you'd like. Josh Allen did Josh Allen things. And Buffalo scored 20 points after halftime.**

Twenty points in the second half, with the best interior lineman in street clothes.

**Now consider who's coming to Allegiant: a Los Angeles Rams team that wants to play physical football.** Getting your best interior defensive lineman back before that game is a legitimate boost, and a badly needed one — because the one thing this defense absolutely cannot do is let another opponent control the line of scrimmage for four quarters.

## THE ROSTER MOVES, WITH TWO NEW DETAILS

The second piece of Monday news arrived the way the best Raiders news always does — in real time, with all of us refreshing the same feed.

**The Raiders signed offensive tackles Amari Kight and Darian Kinnard to the practice squad.** Kight is 25, and he **played for Klint Kubiak in Seattle last season when Kubiak was the Seahawks' offensive coordinator** — so this is not a random name pulled out of a hat. **This is a coach calling a man he already trusts, who already knows the language of this offense.** Kinnard is 26 and **has been part of three Super Bowl championship teams.** Three rings of locker room experience, now sitting on the Raiders' scout team.

When you're 3-3 and trying to stabilize an offensive line, you take championship pedigree wherever you can find it.

**To make room, the Raiders cut tight end Chris Mieric and offensive lineman Evan Neal.** And here are the two details I didn't have yesterday.

**Mieric had been elevated twice this season and played a handful of snaps** — so this wasn't a man who never got a look. With **Carter Renan working his way back from injured reserve**, he simply became the odd man out at a position where this team is already deep.

**And Neal started 27 games for the Giants** after being taken seventh overall in 2022. Twenty-seven NFL starts. A top-ten pick. Signed here in early September hoping a change of scenery would spark something — **and he never got elevated to the active roster once.**

**That experiment is officially over.** Is it a massive story? No. **But it tells you exactly how this front office is thinking: they would rather add men who fit the system than collect famous names.**

## NOW THE PART I FOUND MOST ENCOURAGING — AND IT WASN'T A TRANSACTION

Raider Nation, the best thing about Monday had nothing to do with the roster. **It was accountability.**

We hear that word thrown around every week in this league like confetti. On Monday, you could actually watch it happen.

**Start at the top.** Kubiak stood at the podium and pointed the finger at himself. The Raiders went two for five in the red zone, and he called it **unacceptable** and said **it starts with him.**

Think about how rare that is. A head coach saying: *I own this one. Not the players. Not the refs. Not the weather. Me.*

**Then there's Tyler Linderbaum, and I have to give the man credit, because he didn't hide.**

In the second quarter the Raiders faced **fourth-and-one at the Buffalo 13-yard line**, ran their version of the tush push, and **Linderbaum was flagged for a false start.** They had to settle for a field goal. **And that's the third time this season he's drawn a flag in that exact kind of situation.**

**The Raiders finished with seven flags for 62 yards on the day**, and Kubiak was blunt about it: seven penalties is seven too many.

**Linderbaum took full blame.** No excuses, no deflecting — just a man who knows he's paid like one of the best centers in football and knows he has to play like it.

## AND HERE'S WHAT I LOVE ABOUT THE KUBIAK ERA SO FAR

**He didn't throw his center under the bus.**

When he was asked about it, the tone from that coaching staff was **support, belief, and a clear expectation that it gets cleaned up.** He called Linderbaum a leader and a major bright spot and said a man who's hard on himself usually cares.

Raider Nation, I've said it a hundred times on this channel: **teams take on the personality of their leaders.** If the head coach owns his mistakes and the veterans own theirs, you build a room where **nobody is looking for somebody else to blame.**

**That's how you go from a team that says "here we go again" to a team that says "let's go get this next one."**

And Kubiak said it himself: everything is still in front of them. You know what? **He's right. It's October.**

## BUT I'M NOT HERE TO SUGARCOAT IT

We have real problems, and they showed up again Sunday.

**The third quarter continues to be a graveyard.**

The Raiders opened the second half with a three-and-out. **Then Kirk Cousins threw an interception to Buffalo linebacker Dorian Williams on a throw intended for Michael Mayer — and the Bills turned that into a touchdown.**

**Another scoreless third quarter.**

Raider Nation, that's the new detail: the pick was to a **linebacker**, undercutting a throw to the tight end. That's a defender reading the quarterback's eyes and jumping an intermediate route — exactly what Kubiak described when he said Buffalo "stepped underneath the check-down to Mike."

**This team has now made a habit of coming out of the locker room like they forgot something in the car.** Good teams adjust at halftime. Great teams win the third quarter. **We've been neither** — and remember the season number we broke down yesterday: outscored by 26 points in the third quarter, worst margin in the NFL.

## THE RUN GAME IS STILL SEARCHING

**The Raiders ran 20 times for 65 yards.** Ashton Jeanty punched in a one-yard touchdown on the opening drive, which was a beautiful way to start — **but the ground game never took over.**

One honest note here, because I want you to have the full picture: **the reports disagree about how good Buffalo's run defense actually is.** One account says the Bills came in allowing the second-fewest yards per contact in the league; another says they usually struggle to stop the run. I'm not going to pretend to settle that for you — but either way, **65 yards on 20 carries is 3.2 a crack, and that's the same number this offense has produced against everybody.**

Kubiak said the backs ran harder than they had in recent weeks, and **I agree with that.** They broke tackles, they moved the line when the lanes weren't there.

**But running hard and running smart are two different things. And let me say it plainly: hard running behind a line that can't open lanes is just a very athletic way to get tackled.**

## THE OFFENSIVE LINE: MY HONEST READ

Kubiak said Caleb Rogers was **both good and bad** against Buffalo — the most accurate scouting report you'll ever hear — and made it clear that competition up front isn't going away.

**Here's my read: the group that played Sunday is probably the group we're going to see for a while.** The Raiders just added two more tackles to the practice squad, so the depth is coming, **but I don't expect a wholesale shakeup.**

**If a bigger move happens, it's more likely to come in the form of a trade.** And the deadline is coming up fast — which is going to matter a lot in about five minutes when we get to the chaos around this league.

## NOW GIVE KIRK COUSINS HIS FLOWERS

Before anybody decides this offense is all doom and gloom, let's be fair to the quarterback.

**He threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns, finished with SIX SCORING DRIVES, and was never sacked. Not once. Forty-one pass attempts and nobody laid a hand on him.**

**He hit Michael Mayer on a dart for 28 yards on third down.** And after Buffalo stretched the lead to **33-23**, he found **Tre Tucker on a gorgeous 44-yard touchdown to make it a one-score game.**

Raider Nation, that's a 38-year-old man standing in there and giving this football team a chance.

**And the flip side, which he was honest enough to admit himself:** after James Cook's go-ahead touchdown, **he threw three straight incompletions.** He walked through all three at the podium — the dagger route, leading Bowers too far, and getting to Tucker too late — and blamed himself for each.

**That's the kind of quarterback accountability you want.** The man who threw for 313 spent his press conference apologizing for three throws.

## THE STAT THAT SHOULD KEEP EVERY RAIDERS COACH UP AT NIGHT

Now here it is, Raider Nation. The number that explains this entire season better than anything else I can show you.

**In the first three games, this defense forced TEN takeaways.**

**In the three losses since: ONE.**

**One.**

Ten, then one. The turnover machine that fueled the 3-0 start has gone **completely dark.**

And you can talk about scheme. You can talk about the quality of the opponents — Kansas City, New England and Buffalo are all legitimate contenders. **But the simple truth is this: the Raiders live and die by making the other team cough up the football.**

Remember what Kubiak himself admitted two weeks ago when a reporter asked whether the takeaways had been hiding this defense's weaknesses. He didn't deny it. He said the numbers say what they say — **and that opponents now protect the ball better specifically because of this defense's reputation.**

**So until the takeaways start again, every single game is going to be a coin flip that keeps landing on the wrong side.** That's not pessimism. That's ten versus one.

## THE OFFICIAL PUNISHMENT FOR WHAT HAPPENED TO BOWERS

Now the moment that lit up the internet, with the full sequence.

**Right before halftime, Bills safety C.J. Gardner-Johnson and Brock Bowers started jawing after a play. And then, with Bowers' back turned, Gardner-Johnson spit in his direction.**

Officials were right there. **Flagged. Ejected. Gone.** The cameras caught all of it.

**And the NFL didn't mess around afterward. On Monday the league suspended Gardner-Johnson for ONE GAME WITHOUT PAY, ruling that he violated the sportsmanship rule by spitting at an opponent.**

**Because Buffalo is on its bye this week, he'll sit out the Bills' game against the Baltimore Ravens on November 1st.**

**He apologized on Instagram**, saying there was no excuse for what he did, that he takes full accountability, and that he has to be a better veteran. **Bills coach Joe Brady said there will be no additional punishment from the team beyond the league suspension.**

And in a twist that surprised absolutely nobody, **49ers fullback Kyle Juszczyk publicly claimed Gardner-Johnson had spit on him last season too.** Raider Nation, that is an allegation and I'm going to leave it exactly there — unverified, from another player, about a separate incident. But you can see why the league wanted to make an example.

## MY TAKE, AND YOU CAN DISAGREE IN THE COMMENTS

**Spitting on another grown man is a bush-league move. Period. There's no gray area.**

But here's the part I actually find interesting. **You'd better believe that Raiders locker room noticed. When a man resorts to that, it tells you he's rattled.**

And I think it lit a small fire under this team. They went toe-to-toe with one of the best offenses in football and nearly pulled it out. It doesn't change the final score — **but it tells you something about the character in that building.**

**And Brock Bowers, for what it's worth, kept his cool.** The best player on this offense had somebody spit toward his back and he didn't take the bait — exactly as Kubiak told them before kickoff, when he said anybody who got into it would be standing next to him on the sideline.

## NOW LET'S ZOOM OUT, BECAUSE THE LEAGUE HAS GONE UNHINGED

And this matters for the Raiders more than you'd think.

**We'll start with the sad one.**

**Philadelphia Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts was ruled out of Sunday's game against Carolina because of a superficial blood clot in his calf.** The Eagles explained that the problem was found in an ultrasound after their trip to London, and a follow-up scan showed movement in the clot. **Tanner McKee got the start in his place.** No one knows exactly how long he'll be out, though reports suggest he could miss the rest of the season.

Raider Nation, whatever happens on the field, **I'm sending nothing but good thoughts his way.** Health first, always. And it's a reminder that every snap in this league is a gift — this is a man's circulatory system, not a depth chart question, and the only right response is to hope he's fine.

## THE FALCONS ARE PLAYING MUSICAL CHAIRS

**The Atlanta Falcons signed veteran Andy Dalton** — who had been released by the Eagles earlier this month after throwing an interception on his lone pass attempt — **and released Cooper Rush.**

Rush had started the first two games of the season while **Michael Penix Jr. and Tua Tagovailoa** were hurt, and Atlanta lost both. **Dalton now joins Penix and Tagovailoa in that room**, with the Falcons clearly wanting one more veteran arm after **Penix left the game with a rib injury.**

That's the NFL's version of musical chairs, and nobody wants to be standing when the music stops.

## AND THEN THE EARTHQUAKE IN INDIANAPOLIS

**The Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard on Monday — one day after losing 26-20 to the previously winless Tennessee Titans at Lucas Oil Stadium, a game in which they blew a 13-point halftime lead.** Fans were chanting for his firing from the stands.

**Ballard's record in Indianapolis was 72-82-1, with just two playoff appearances in a decade on the job.** Owner **Carlie Irsay-Gordon** said a change in leadership was necessary to meet the standard of competing for championships. **The Colts are 2-4.**

**Assistant general manager Ed Dodds will run the personnel department for the rest of the season.**

Raider Nation, losing to a winless team by blowing a halftime lead, and getting fired the next morning — that's as brutal as this business gets. Ten years, two playoff appearances, and a Sunday that ended it.

## AND THIS IS THE PART THAT MADE ME SIT UP

**Ed Dodds got his start in the NFL as an intern with the Las Vegas Raiders in the early 2000s — back in the Al Davis days.**

He has a long relationship with this organization. **And that doesn't hurt when you're picking up the phone to talk trades.**

Now let me be completely clear, because I don't do fiction here: **I am not saying a deal is coming.** There is no report connecting these two teams on anything. What I'm saying is that **the November 10th trade deadline is not far away, the Colts just hit the panic button at 2-4, and the man now answering the phone for their personnel department has Raiders roots.**

**It takes two to tango — and sometimes the dance partner already knows the steps.**

File it away. Nothing more than that.

## THE REAL LESSON IN ALL OF IT

And here's the bigger picture, Raider Nation, because I think it's genuinely worth appreciating.

Look at the carousel. **The Eagles don't know when their quarterback is coming back. The Falcons keep reshuffling veterans at the most important position in sports. The Colts just blew up their front office in October.**

**Meanwhile, the Raiders know exactly who they are at quarterback.** Kirk Cousins is leading the offense — second in the NFL in touchdown passes, 313 yards and six scoring drives on Sunday. **And Fernando Mendoza, the number one overall pick, is learning behind him** — running the huddle, working through progressions and reading pressures in his first NFL action.

Say whatever you want about the state of this offense. **That is stability in a league that's starting to feel like a game of dodgeball.**

And I'll admit there's something satisfying about Raiders fans watching other franchises scramble for answers at the most important position in sports **while we've got our guy.**

**The good news is real:** Tonka Hemingway, the snap leader and best interior lineman on this defense, is expected back at practice this week with the Rams coming to town. Nobody is pointing fingers in that locker room.

**My watch list for the Rams:** whether Hemingway clears protocol, the first play of the third quarter, the carry split in the backfield, and whether this defense takes the ball away even once.

Raider Nation, the league around us is on fire — a fired GM, a quarterback carousel, a suspension, and a health scare that puts all of it in perspective. **This team's best interior defender is coming back and its leaders are taking the blame instead of handing it out.
