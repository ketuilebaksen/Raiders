# RAIDERS TUNNEL — THREE UPDATES BEFORE NEW ENGLAND, AND THE ONE LINEUP DECISION THAT DECIDES THIS GAME 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, the Raiders just got three encouraging updates before facing the Patriots, and here's why I'm fired up about them: all three connect directly to the exact thing that frustrated me on the Kansas City film.

A thin receiver room. Shaky interior blocking. An offense that needs more room to operate.

Yesterday on this channel I laid out the central problem of this season: Las Vegas has faced the second-most stacked boxes in football, and you don't fix a numbers problem with effort — you fix it by making defenses pay for the gamble. Well, New England suddenly has a problem at the exact position we need to attack, and two developments on our side could let us take real advantage of it.

But there's a lineup decision I think we have to get right for any of this to work.

So today: the three updates, what each one actually means, and the guard decision that determines whether this plan is even possible. I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we chase 6,000 believers. Let's go.

## UPDATE ONE: NEW ENGLAND'S CORNERBACK ROOM

The first update is coming from the other side of the ball, and it's the one that opens the door.

Christian Gonzalez didn't practice, and his availability against us is in question.

If you're preparing this Raiders offense, you have to be watching that situation very closely. Gonzalez is their best cornerback and the player you'd assign to take away your opponent's most dangerous receiver. We have spent four weeks looking for ways to make defenses respect this passing game downfield, and this could be the opening.

And it goes beyond Gonzalez. Carlton Davis III missed practice along with other corners, and Marcus Jones was the only starting cornerback who actually practiced.

That is a significant amount of uncertainty in one position group.

## THE HONEST CAVEAT ON INJURY REPORTS

Now let me be responsible here, because this is where fan channels get themselves in trouble every single week.

I am not taking a Wednesday practice report and declaring New England's entire secondary unavailable. Wednesday is the day coaches rest veterans, manage workloads, and give nothing away. Players who don't practice on Wednesday play on Sunday all the time, and anybody telling you this game is already won because of a practice report is selling you a fantasy.

But here's what matters regardless of who ultimately suits up: if multiple corners can't go, New England has to adjust. They'll have to change coverage responsibilities, maybe play more zone to protect a replacement, maybe keep a safety closer to a young corner.

And I want our offense prepared to test those adjustments early instead of letting replacement players settle in comfortably. There's a real difference between a backup corner who gets challenged on the first drive and a backup corner who spends a quarter watching handoffs and realizing nobody's going to come after him.

## NEW ENGLAND HAS ITS OWN PROBLEMS

Their offense has concerns as well, which is worth knowing before anybody panics about a Patriots team that just beat Buffalo.

They're down a receiver from an already depleted room, which leaves them with fewer options. Rhamondre Stevenson has been their primary running back this season, while TreVeyon Henderson has struggled a bit.

So the Patriots have several situations to manage before Sunday too. This is not a fully healthy juggernaut we're flying into.

But for me, the cornerback injuries remain the most interesting part of the entire week, because they connect directly to something we're getting back. And this is the key principle: identifying a possible weakness matters a lot more when you have somebody who can actually attack it.

Which brings me to update number two.

## UPDATE TWO: DANTE THORNTON JR. IS BACK ON THE PRACTICE FIELD

Dante Thornton Jr. returned to practice. He's been dealing with a hamstring injury, and he says he's ready to play and feels confident about it.

Consider the timeline there. He hadn't practiced since August 31st and was on short-term injured reserve. So this is genuinely encouraging news. His return to the practice field opens the next step toward getting him available.

He still needs to be activated to the 53-man roster. But Raider Nation, we are finally talking about how he could contribute instead of just waiting for him to practice at all. That's a meaningful shift.

## REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS, STATED CLEARLY

Now I want to set expectations honestly, because overselling a young player does nobody any favors.

The athletic ability is there. The finished receiver is not there yet. He was taken in the fourth round and there was always development involved in that selection. That's not a criticism; that's what a fourth-round receiver is.

But here's what I don't want: I don't want us acting like a developing player can't help with a specific assignment. Those are two different things. Not being a complete receiver and not being useful are not the same statement.

We need outside speed. That is something he can provide right now without suddenly becoming our number one option.

The man runs in the 4.3 range. So make use of it. Put him outside, send him downfield, and make a cornerback turn and run.

Does that mean every deep route becomes a completion? Of course not. But it gives the defense another responsibility, and responsibilities are the currency of this entire conversation.

## WHY A DECOY CHANGES THE MATH

And this is where it ties straight into everything we broke down from the Kansas City tape.

Our running game has struggled, and the offensive line is part of that. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But when teams feel comfortable stacking the box, we have to give them a reason to reconsider. You cannot keep asking the ground game to operate in crowded space without threatening somewhere else.

Even his value purely as a decoy could matter.

Think about the mechanics for a second. A safety's job on every snap is to make a decision: do I come down and help against the run, or do I stay deep because somebody can beat me over the top? That decision happens before the ball is snapped, based on who's on the field and where they're lined up. If New England respects the possibility of Thornton getting behind the coverage, that safety's first step goes backward instead of forward. Maybe that creates more room underneath for Bowers and Mayer. Maybe the running game finally gets a little breathing room.

Those are the benefits I'm looking for. We don't need a huge receiving total from him for his presence to help this offense. But we do need a believable threat — because if we never throw his way, eventually the defense has less reason to worry about those deep routes at all. A decoy only works until the opponent figures out he's only a decoy.

## HE'S ALREADY DONE IT AGAINST THIS TEAM

And here's the detail that makes this matchup feel almost scripted.

That game-winning catch against New England last year was his best moment in a Raiders uniform. Geno Smith gave him an opportunity, and he delivered.

For a young receiver coming back from an injury, that experience matters more than people realize. Confidence isn't abstract for a 22-year-old — it's built out of specific memories, and he already has one against this exact opponent in this exact building.

So I'd like to see him get the chance to build some of that confidence back.

## MY ACTUAL PLAN FOR HIM

My starting point would be at least three targets. That's enough to give him a defined role without forcing the offense through him.

Take one shot early, preferably in the first quarter, so the Patriots understand immediately that we're willing to challenge them downfield. Then come back to it in the second half.

And please — don't wait until we're frustrated with crowded running lanes to finally try something. That's the mistake I keep seeing from offenses in this situation. They run into stacked boxes for twenty-five minutes, fall behind, then start throwing deep out of desperation when the defense is already comfortable and the score demands it. Establish the threat early and give their coverage something to consider throughout the entire game.

Klint Kubiak can help him with some pre-snap motion, too. Get him moving. Let him build momentum into his release instead of asking him to beat a jam from a standstill. And see whether a wheel route creates an opportunity — remember, Ian Thomas scored on exactly that design against Kansas City.

I'm not asking for a featured role. I'm asking for a few chances that fit his strengths. And if New England is missing corners, that's another reason to test it.

We already know what this receiver room needs. Give Thornton Jr. a chance to show whether he can supply some of it.

## THE DEPTH SITUATION MAKES THIS URGENT

And the depth picture makes those chances even more important.

Jack Bech is already on injured reserve, while Jalen Nailor missed practice after the concussion against Kansas City. So we could be missing two options, and the available players will have to handle more work.

That's exactly why Thornton's return feels useful right now, even with the development concerns attached to him.

## ON NAILOR, HANDLED THE RIGHT WAY

As for Jalen Nailor, I wouldn't count on him playing this week. And I want to be clear that this is my expectation, not a confirmed decision.

He took a big hit going over the middle. And concussion protocol isn't just about whether a player says he feels ready — there are specific steps he has to clear, administered by medical professionals, and that process exists for reasons that have nothing to do with Sunday's game plan.

We saw this with Trayden Stokes, who didn't return for the New Orleans game. Feeling capable of playing and actually getting cleared are different things.

And I expect the Raiders to be careful here, as they should be.

For context, reported figures suggest somewhere between roughly 38 and 60 percent of professional football players diagnosed with a concussion return within a week. That's an extremely wide range, and it tells us essentially nothing about whether Jalen Nailor specifically will be available. What it does explain is why I'm keeping my expectations cautious.

So prepare the offense with another plan. If he clears the process, great — you've got an additional option. But I don't want us reaching the end of the week with a receiver plan built around somebody who still can't play. That's how you end up improvising on Sunday.

## WHO'S ACTUALLY AVAILABLE

Let's take inventory, because this is the clearest way to understand why Thornton matters.

Tre Tucker leads our receivers with 23 targets entering Week Five. Cody White is available and has already converted two emergency opportunities into touchdowns this season. Rookie Malik Benson hasn't had much work. And Dariq Young isn't really a receiver option in the same way.

That's the room. That's it.

So if Dante Thornton Jr. gets onto the 53-man roster, I expect opportunities. And honestly, if he can't earn them in this particular group, that becomes a bigger conversation about how the organization views him long term.

Right now, I'd rather give him a defined job and see what he does with it.

## UPDATE THREE: JACKSON POWERS-JOHNSON PRACTICED

Of course, giving a receiver that job only helps if the protection lets the play develop. Which is where the third update comes in, and it might be the most important of the three.

Jackson Powers-Johnson practiced after missing Week Four against Kansas City with a groin injury.

To me, that's potentially a big step for this offense. If he's healthy enough to play, I want him starting. He is one of the five best offensive linemen we have, and our interior line has not been playing well enough for us to ignore a possible improvement.

## THE CONCERNS ARE REAL, THOUGH

I'm not going to pretend this is simple, because the hesitation around him is legitimate.

He's missed 12 games since being drafted in the second round out of Oregon in 2024. His conditioning has also been part of the discussion. You want him in better shape, available consistently, and able to build continuity with the players around him.

I understand that concern from the coaching staff completely. Continuity on an offensive line is a real thing — combination blocks and protection slides depend on five people reacting to movement the same way, and that comes from repetition together.

But here's my counterargument: keeping the same group together doesn't automatically solve anything. The interior has struggled for three weeks. At some point continuity stops being an asset and starts being an excuse. We need to evaluate whether the current combination is actually helping.

## THE DECISION IS AT GUARD

Let me be specific about what I'm proposing, because I'm not calling for a teardown.

I am not taking Kolton Miller, Tyler Linderbaum, or DJ Glaze out of this lineup. And I want to say something about Glaze: he is playing outstanding football and he deserves recognition for it. That young man has quietly become one of the most reliable players on this offense.

So the decision is at guard. Jackson Powers-Johnson, Caleb Rogers, and Spencer Burford all have experience on either side. Find the two guards who give this offense the strongest overall chance.

My choice would be JPJ over Caleb Rogers, assuming he's healthy.

## THE ROGERS TRADE-OFF, FAIRLY STATED

And I'm not dismissing what Caleb Rogers does well, because that wouldn't be honest.

His run blocking fits this offense. He might even be better in that specific area than the alternative. That's a real argument in a scheme built on wide zone and downhill running.

But his pass protection has been a major liability. That's the trade-off we have to address directly.

And think about what we're trying to do on Sunday. If we're planning to attack downfield against a depleted cornerback room, the quarterback needs time. A seven-step developing route against a defense playing a backup corner is worth nothing if there's pressure in Cousins' lap in two and a half seconds. A guard can help the running game and still create a problem that limits what we're able to do through the air.

That's the whole calculation. You can't select five guys based only on the plays where they look comfortable and then hope the passing downs take care of themselves. That's especially relevant if we're trying to stretch this secondary.

And on the analytics: PFF grades aren't the final answer for me. I said the same thing yesterday when those grades called Burford and Linderbaum our two best offensive players in a game where the run died. I want the tape too, along with other evaluations. When those things line up, the grades can support an argument. With Rogers, the concern is the gap between his run blocking and his pass protection — and that gap shows up on film, not just in a number.

We need a lineup that works across different situations.

## THE JPJ TRADE CHATTER NEEDS CONTEXT

And the trade discussion around Jackson Powers-Johnson needs that same context, because I've seen this one spiral online.

Talking about why teams might call doesn't mean I want him traded. Those are two different conversations and people keep mashing them together.

Here's the honest mechanism: if a talented young lineman isn't starting, other teams notice an opportunity, and he and his camp could reasonably start considering another situation. That's not disloyalty. That's how a career works when you've got a limited number of years to establish yourself. I understand completely why that conversation comes up.

But my preference is still to see him help the Raiders. If he's healthy and capable of improving this line, I'd rather use that talent here than watch somebody else benefit from it. We have spent all season identifying the interior offensive line as the single biggest structural weakness on this roster. Trading a second-round interior lineman while that's true would be difficult to explain.

## LET THE INTENDED OFFENSE ACTUALLY PLAY

And here's my bigger point, the one I keep coming back to.

I believe in Klint Kubiak and Rick Dennison, and I want another look at the offense they intended to put together.

Think about it. We have never actually seen this thing at full strength. Bowers missed time. Bech is on IR. Thornton hasn't practiced since August 31st. JPJ has been in and out. Nailor is in protocol. This offense is averaging over 28 points a game and we have genuinely not yet seen the version these coaches designed in the spring.

Get Brock Bowers, Jackson Powers-Johnson, and the preferred starting group working together, then evaluate how it functions.

That doesn't mean the lineup is beyond questioning. It means I want to see whether the intended combination gives us better protection and more room to operate before we start concluding that the plan itself is broken.

We've had enough uncertainty up front. A healthy returning starter gives these coaches a chance to address some of it.

Now, if they believe Powers-Johnson genuinely can't stay available, then I understand why Caleb Rogers remains important. Durability is part of evaluation and pretending otherwise is naive. But if he's ready now, I don't want the possibility of another injury automatically keeping a better option on the sideline. Evaluate what he can handle this week and make the decision from there.

For me, the highest upside comes with a healthy JPJ starting. Give him that chance and let's see whether the interior finally looks more settled.

## HOW THE THREE UPDATES FIT TOGETHER

So let me tie this up, because the connection between all three is the actual story of the week.

New England may be missing cornerbacks, which creates an opening downfield. Dante Thornton Jr. is back on the practice field with the speed to attack that opening and force a safety to stay deep, which is the only real cure for the stacked boxes that have been strangling our run game. And Jackson Powers-Johnson practiced, which is what makes any of it possible, because a deep shot requires protection and this interior line has not been providing it.

Weakness identified. Weapon returning. Protection potentially restored.

That's a complete plan, and it's the first time all season those three pieces have lined up in the same week.

For Sunday, here's what I want: an early deep shot, and protection that gives it time. If Dante Thornton Jr. is activated, give him meaningful work. Make New England defend the entire field instead of letting them crowd eight men into the box and dare us to beat them with a handoff.

Do that, and the Raiders walk out of Foxborough at 4-1 as legitimate buyers at the deadline. Don't, and we're a .500 team with a long list of names on a whiteboard.
