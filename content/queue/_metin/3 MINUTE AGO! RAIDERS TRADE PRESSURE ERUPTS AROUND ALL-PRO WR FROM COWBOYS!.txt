# RAIDERS TUNNEL — "ALL OPTIONS ARE BEING CONSIDERED": Kubiak Opens the Door, Damian Lillard Wants George Pickens, and Patrick Mahomes Just Said Something About This Team He's Never Said Before 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, the Raiders lost 30-27 and something strange happened afterward: this team got *more* respect, not less.

Kay Adams went on national television and predicted the Raiders are making the playoffs. Manti Te'o said he's completely invested in this roster and compared it to the New England teams of the 2000s. Patrick Mahomes, a man who just improved to 14-2 against this franchise, stood at a podium and called Maxx Crosby the leader of a good football team.

And inside the building, Klint Kubiak was asked whether the organization could add another wide receiver, and his answer was five words that open a very big door: "All options are being considered."

So today: the receiver crisis in full detail, the all-pro name a famous Raiders fan is demanding, the honest case for and against paying that price, exactly how those final 76 seconds unfolded, what the national media is suddenly saying out loud, and the message Mahomes delivered about Fernando Mendoza that nobody expected.

I'm Victor, and this is Raiders Tunnel. Hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and subscribe as we push toward 6,000 believers. Let's get into it.

## THE RECEIVER ROOM IS IN GENUINE TROUBLE

Let's start with the problem, because it's worse than most fans realize.

Jack Bech is out with a broken forearm. Jalen Nailor appeared to suffer a concussion against Kansas City and is now questionable for the Week Five road game at New England. And those two aren't the only ones unavailable: Dante Thornton Jr., Cory Rucker and Bech are all on injured reserve.

So count the healthy receivers this offense can actually line up on Sunday: Tre Tucker, Cody White, Malik Benson, and Dariq Young.

That's four. Four healthy wide receivers, one of whom was on the practice squad three weeks ago, one of whom is primarily a special teams contributor, and one of whom has barely played.

That lack of depth would be concerning against any opponent. It becomes genuinely serious for a team heading into a brutal stretch of schedule with playoff expectations growing around it.

And here's the structural problem underneath the injuries. The Raiders can keep leaning on Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer, and that two-tight-end identity has been the best thing about this offense. But a tight end duo cannot be the entire passing attack. At some point Kirk Cousins needs a receiver who can create separation on the perimeter, stretch coverage vertically, and punish defenses for sending extra attention at Bowers.

Right now that player does not exist on this roster with any consistency.

## "ALL OPTIONS ARE BEING CONSIDERED"

So when team reporter Levi Edwards asked Kubiak directly whether the organization could add another receiver, the head coach refused to eliminate any possibility and said all options are being considered.

Let's translate that, because coaches choose words carefully.

He didn't say "we like our guys." He didn't say "we'll get Nailor back and be fine." He said every option. That leaves both the trade market and free agency open to John Spytek, and it tells you the people inside that building see exactly what we see on the field.

Now, the quality and the cost of those options will determine whether Las Vegas actually acts, and the trade deadline is November 10th. That's the window. A month and change to decide whether this team is close enough to spend real capital on fixing its thinnest position.

## THE RUN GAME, AND KUBIAK TAKING THE BLAME AGAIN

Before we get to the shiny trade names, the other half of the offensive problem, because it's the half that actually decided the Chiefs game.

Las Vegas produced 72 rushing yards against Kansas City at 2.8 yards a carry.

Seventy-two yards. That's not a bad day, that's a non-functional run game, and it's a continuing slide since the opening win over Miami.

And here's the thing I keep being impressed by: Kubiak did not blame his running backs and he did not blame his offensive line. He put it on himself and his staff. He said the Raiders have solid backs and a solid offensive line, and that the coaching staff has to design better opportunities for those players. He pointed specifically at scheme, and he repeated that he has to put the offense in better positions.

That's the second straight press conference where this head coach has taken the hit publicly. After the Chiefs game he said "I blew it" about the late play call. Now he's saying the run game's failure is a design problem, which is the most uncomfortable thing a play-caller can admit.

Accountability matters. But accountability has a deadline, and the deadline is Sunday. New England is going to study exactly how Kansas City limited this offense and try to copy it. The answer has to show up on the field now, not in another press conference.

## DAMIAN LILLARD SAYS GO GET GEORGE PICKENS

Now the fun part, and the reason half of Raider Nation has been arguing in group chats since Sunday night.

Damian Lillard, the Portland Trail Blazers guard and one of the most recognizable Raiders fans in professional sports, reacted to this loss with a message on X that was about as direct as it gets: just go get Pickens.

He means George Pickens of the Dallas Cowboys.

Now, let me be clear about what this is and isn't. An NBA star tweeting is not evidence that the Raiders and the Cowboys are negotiating anything. There is no report of talks. But it reflects an obvious roster need, and when the need is this visible, these conversations start on their own.

So let's do the actual analysis, because the name is worth taking seriously.

## THE CASE FOR PICKENS

Start with what he was last season: 93 catches, 1,429 yards, and nine touchdowns. That is a legitimate number one receiver in a high-volume passing attack, a player who creates explosive plays and functions as the focal point of a real offense.

Through four games with Dallas, his production is down: 18 receptions for 225 yards, which is actually fewer receiving yards than Tre Tucker has.

But those numbers require context, and this is where it gets interesting. He's sharing targets with CeeDee Lamb, who currently leads the entire NFL in receiving yards. There is exactly one football being thrown in Dallas and a lot of it is going to the other guy.

In Las Vegas, the role would look completely different. Pickens outside, as the primary perimeter threat, while Bowers operates primarily from the slot and the middle of the field.

And that's the combination that would genuinely terrify a defensive coordinator, because it forces a choice nobody wants to make. Send extra coverage at Bowers over the middle and Pickens is isolated one-on-one on the perimeter. Devote safety help to Pickens and suddenly Bowers and Mayer have more space in the middle than they've had all season.

That's the whole reason I've been hammering the "second must-cover player" point for two weeks. It isn't about wanting a shiny name. It's about the geometry of the field.

## THE CASE AGAINST, HONESTLY

Now the other side, and I'm going to be fair rather than dismissive.

First, the fit question. Pickens created problems during his time with the Pittsburgh Steelers that contributed to the decision to trade him, and Las Vegas would have to decide whether that fits the culture Kubiak is building. And make no mistake, culture is the actual product in that building right now. This is a team that leads the league in discipline, whose head coach says "I blew it," whose star defender says the team has to earn a full crowd. You don't casually introduce a variable into that.

Second, the money. He's playing on the franchise tag, which means the team acquiring him would surrender draft capital and then immediately face a major contract decision. That's paying twice.

Third, the price. The analysis that accompanied Lillard's suggestion argues the Raiders shouldn't offer more than a second-round pick given the contract and fit questions. And honestly, even a second is difficult to justify for an organization still building a long-term foundation, when that same pick could address the interior offensive line.

So where do I land? If the price is a day-three pick or a conditional late selection, you make that call immediately. At a second-round pick, I hesitate, and I'd want to know what this coaching staff thinks about fit after doing real homework. Anything above a second and I walk away and spend it on a guard.

And there's a fourth path nobody mentions: patience. If Dallas doesn't use the franchise tag again, Pickens could reach free agency in the offseason, and you'd be bidding with money instead of picks.

## THE OTHER OPTIONS ON THE BOARD

The Pickens conversation isn't happening in isolation, and I want to lay out the full menu, because this is what Spytek is actually weighing.

Option one: pursue a premium name like Pickens and accept the cost. Option two: look at cheaper trade candidates, which is where Jerry Jeudy at a conditional fourth-round pick and Xavier Legette at a late pick have been floated all month. Option three: sign a free agent, which costs nothing in draft capital but rarely gets you a difference-maker in October. Option four: survive with the current group until the injured players come back.

That last one is more viable than it sounds, because the roster mechanics are favorable. Players on short-term injured reserve become eligible to return around this point in the season, which means reinforcements are theoretically arriving at the same time the schedule gets hard.

The honest truth is that the decision depends entirely on one question: how close does this front office believe this team is?

If four weeks have convinced them the window is already opening, adding an established receiver is worth real risk. If they believe the rebuild is ahead of schedule but incomplete, protecting premium picks is the disciplined choice. Both answers are defensible. Kubiak saying "all options" tells us they haven't decided yet.

And Tucker producing like a useful secondary receiver while Malik Benson hasn't yet shown he can handle a larger role, especially with Nailor possibly out, is exactly the kind of situation that pushes a front office to act.

## HOW THOSE LAST 76 SECONDS ACTUALLY WENT

Let me give you the exact sequence at the end of this game, because it's more painful and more encouraging than the final score suggests.

The decisive mistake came on a kickoff return, when Dariq Young fumbled and Kansas City turned it into an extended advantage, pushing the lead to 30-19 late in the fourth quarter.

At that point, this game is over for ninety percent of the teams in this league. Eleven points down, late, against Mahomes, after a self-inflicted turnover.

Instead, Las Vegas answered with a touchdown and then converted the two-point attempt, cutting the deficit to three.

Then the Raiders got one final possession: one minute and sixteen seconds on the clock, no timeouts, and the football at their own one-yard line.

Think about that assignment. Ninety-nine yards of field, no timeouts, 76 seconds, needing at minimum to reach field goal range against a defense coordinated by Steve Spagnuolo. The drive ended before they could finish it, with Cousins' interception coming on the final play.

Here's why I keep coming back to that sequence: this team was buried, and it refused to be buried. It scored, converted the two, and got the ball back with a real if desperate chance. That is not what the 3-14 Raiders did. Those teams were finished at 30-19 and everybody in the building knew it.

## KAY ADAMS: "I'M NOT FEELING SORRY FOR THEM"

Now the national reaction, and it surprised me.

On the October 5th edition of her show, Kay Adams said she didn't feel sorry for Las Vegas, because she was too impressed by what the team demonstrated. She acknowledged how difficult the remaining schedule is, and then predicted the Raiders will reach the playoffs.

She'd already been encouraged by the undefeated start and by how quickly Kubiak turned this thing around, but her point was that going toe to toe with Kansas City showed her what this roster is actually made of.

And she went out of her way to defend Cousins, describing him as a consistent perennial Pro Bowl quarterback and arguing that he proved he can still perform in exactly the kind of high-pressure game this team has to win to reach the postseason. Kansas City made him uncomfortable all afternoon and forced him to throw repeatedly, and he kept competing and gave his team a chance.

Her conclusion was the one I share: the fumbled kickoff may have been the difference between a loss and a completed upset.

Her prediction gets tested immediately, with New England, Buffalo and the Rams coming in order. None of those are easy. But her argument is that this team has demonstrated it can compete with every single opponent on its schedule, and after the last four weeks that's hard to dispute.

## MANTI TE'O: THE 2000s PATRIOTS COMPARISON

Then on Good Morning Football, the former NFL linebacker Manti Te'o reached a similar conclusion for a completely different reason, and his version is the one that got my attention as a football argument.

He said he's completely invested in this Raiders team because the roster construction reminds him of the successful New England teams of the 2000s. He pointed to four specific things: an efficient veteran quarterback, a capable running game, two quality tight ends, and a wide receiver group built largely from later-round draft selections.

Let me be careful here, because that comparison does not guarantee similar results, and Las Vegas is still searching for receiving talent and for consistency on the ground. Nobody is handing out dynasties in October.

But I think the comparison explains something important: why this team can stay competitive without a traditional superstar at receiver. Those New England teams won by being positionally sound, by having a quarterback who protected the football, by using tight ends as the structural center of the passing game, and by never beating themselves.

Look at this Raiders team through that lens. Cousins has 11 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead. Bowers and Mayer are the center of gravity on offense. The Raiders are one of the least-penalized teams in football. The defense leads the league in takeaways.

That's the formula. It isn't flashy and it travels.

## THE QUARTERBACK CONVERSATION IS OVER FOR NOW

Let's put a bow on something, because the national noise has gone quiet on it.

Cousins was not the reason Las Vegas lost this game. He performed effectively despite facing consistent pressure, and he has thrown 11 touchdown passes this season, which ties him for the league lead. His command of Kubiak's system gives this team no reason whatsoever to change quarterbacks.

And at 38 years old, he escaped pressure and created plays against Kansas City, which tells you that reduced mobility has not eliminated his ability to compete.

The analysis I read even floated him as a possible surprise MVP candidate if Las Vegas reaches the postseason. I'm not going that far today, but I'll say this: a quarterback tied for the league lead in touchdown passes on a team that was projected to win six games should be in the conversation about the best value signing of the entire offseason.

The calls for Mendoza have gone quiet because there's no argument left to make. Which brings us to the most unexpected moment of the week.

## MAHOMES' MESSAGE ABOUT MENDOZA

Patrick Mahomes left Allegiant Stadium with another win, improving to 14-2 against this franchise. And then he said something I genuinely did not expect.

He called Kirk Cousins a true professional, and said he believes Fernando Mendoza can learn a great deal by watching how a veteran like that prepares, communicates and operates an offense.

Then he added the line that made me laugh out loud: he joked that he hopes Mendoza doesn't learn too much, unless Mahomes is no longer around.

Understand why that's meaningful. Mahomes lived this exact situation. He spent his rookie season learning behind Alex Smith before becoming Kansas City's full-time starter, and that process was followed by three Super Bowl championships and two MVP awards. When the best quarterback of this generation looks at the Raiders' plan and says that's how you develop a young quarterback, that's the strongest possible endorsement of what Kubiak and Spytek decided in April.

And it reinforces exactly what this franchise is doing. Mendoza does not need to be rushed onto the field simply because he was the first pick in the draft. As long as Cousins keeps producing and this team stays competitive, the rookie develops without the pressure of rescuing a franchise, standing on the sideline in his helmet, listening to the play calls, reciting them back to the quarterbacks coach.

## AND THEN HE TALKED ABOUT CROSBY

Mahomes also had significant praise for Maxx Crosby after another physical chapter of this rivalry.

He said Crosby competes at maximum intensity throughout the entire game, delivering tackles and hits while maintaining mutual sportsmanship between plays. He mentioned that Crosby helped him back to his feet several times, and said he'd do the same if their positions were reversed. And then he described Crosby as the leader of a good football team, and said he expects more battles between them.

I want to sit on that phrase: the leader of a good football team.

Kansas City has rarely had to treat Las Vegas as an equal during Mahomes' career. Fourteen and two is a decade of dominance. For him to walk out of that building describing this as a good football team and anticipating more battles is the kind of acknowledgment you don't get for free.

Crosby, for his part, was visibly frustrated with the defense late in that game, and his effort never dropped off. That's the part that matters. A player who is angry about the result and still giving maximum effort in the fourth quarter of a game slipping away is the reason this locker room doesn't fold.

## WHAT STILL HAS TO IMPROVE

Let me keep it honest, because respect from Mahomes doesn't win you anything in the standings.

The Raiders still allowed too many explosive plays, and they have to clean that up before these two teams meet again in Week 18. The run game is at 2.8 yards a carry and the head coach has publicly called it a scheme problem. The receiver room is four deep and one more injury from a crisis. And the interior offensive line still needs Jackson Powers-Johnson healthy and Cesar Ruiz integrated.

The loss did not prove that the first three victories were fraudulent. What it showed is that competing with elite opponents and defeating them are two different challenges, and this team has only solved the first one so far.

## WHERE THIS LEAVES US

So here's the picture heading into New England.

The head coach has opened the door to adding a receiver and taken responsibility for the run game himself. A Hall of Fame-level NBA star is campaigning for George Pickens, and the price is the only real question. Kay Adams is predicting a playoff berth. Manti Te'o is comparing this roster's construction to the Patriots of the 2000s. Patrick Mahomes is calling this a good football team and endorsing the way this franchise is developing its first overall pick.

That's a lot of validation for a team that just lost. And it all becomes noise if this group doesn't go win a road game on Sunday.

If Kubiak restores the run game and Spytek strengthens the receiver room without damaging the future of this roster, this first defeat becomes the pressure that forced the offense to evolve. That's the version of this story I believe in.
