Here's exactly what happened. On the Gruden Family Football Show, Jon Gruden said this:

**A second-round pick. For Kirk Cousins. In the middle of a season.**

Now let me repeat the disclaimer, because the internet is going to run with this and strip all the context: **this is Jon Gruden's opinion. It is not a report. Philadelphia hasn't called. Minnesota hasn't called. Nobody has offered anything.**

## WHY THE IDEA ISN'T AS INSANE AS IT SOUNDS

Look around this league right now.

**Jalen Hurts is out for the season with deep vein thrombosis and clots that traveled to his lungs** — and I'll say it again, our thoughts are with him, because that's a serious medical issue and football could not matter less in comparison. **The Falcons are on their fourth veteran arm.** **The Giants just benched Jameis Winston for JJ McCarthy.** Quarterback injuries are tearing through this league.

**And almost nobody has what the Raiders have: the number one overall pick sitting on the bench as a backup.**

That's the uncomfortable math. **Fernando Mendoza is going to be the starter here eventually** — the only question anybody disagrees on is whether that's this season or next. **And it is genuinely hard to picture a front office turning down a second-round pick for a 38-year-old stopgap quarterback** if this team's season is already gone.

**So the question is brutally simple: is this team's season gone?**

## AND HERE'S WHY I DON'T WANT IT — NOT EVEN A LITTLE

Raider Nation, I'm going to make the case against, and I'm going to make it hard.

**First: if Kirk Cousins gets traded, it has NOTHING to do with his play.** He has been one of the better quarterbacks in the National Football League this season. **He is tied for the league lead with 14 touchdown passes.** Against Buffalo he threw for **313 yards and two touchdowns, put up six scoring drives, and was never sacked on 41 attempts.** He threw a 28-yard dart to Michael Mayer on third down and a **44-yard touchdown to Tre Tucker** to make it a one-score game.

Is he an athlete? No. **That limits what this offense can ask of him** — and it's part of why Kubiak admitted this week that he's at least thinking about using Mendoza in short yardage. **But he is making plenty of big plays down the field, and he's in sync with his head coach.**

**Second: you cannot replace what he gives that quarterback room with a draft pick.** Listen to what Andrew Janocko said about him this week:

**"He has a different base of knowledge that we can bounce ideas off of each other."** He said Cousins **thrives on accountability**, that he **wants to be pushed and coached**, and that when young guys see a man who's been in this league that long be the first to say **"I've got to step up"** — **"that's the type of stuff that young guys should mimic."**

**That's leadership, Raider Nation. And a second-round pick does not teach Fernando Mendoza how to be a professional.**

Remember how that room actually works? **Fernando sees something, asks a question, and Kirk loves talking about the WHY behind it. Then Kirk turns around and asks HIM: "What did you see there?"** You trade Cousins and that room goes quiet.

**Third: even the opposition is complimenting him.** **Aaron Donald said of Cousins: "He looks good. He's playing well."** And about this team: **"They're doing a lot of good things as a team."**

**Fourth, and simplest: this team is 3-3 with six total points separating it from 5-1.** You don't sell a quarterback who's tied for the league lead in touchdowns while you're still mathematically in everything.

**My verdict: if this team falls out of the playoff picture, then yes — you listen, and you'd be negligent not to.** But if the Raiders win a game or two before November 10th? **You do not shake this up. You ride him.**

**And honestly? I think all of it comes down to Sunday.**

**And on Friday, Kubiak made it clear he will not be activated for Week 7.** Asked directly if they were activating him, the answer was: **"We are not. No."**

**Closer.** That's the word. **Not here. Closer.**

## AND HERE'S WHY THAT MATTERS SO MUCH

Raider Nation, take an honest inventory of this receiver room.

**Tre Tucker. Jalen Nailor. Cody White.** And then the tight end duo, **Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer.**

**That's it. That's the firepower.**

And here's the sentence that should make every one of us uncomfortable: **the team's best receiver this season is Tre Tucker.** Now, I love Tucker — Kubiak called him **"the best conditioned athlete I've ever been around,"** he's been producing since April, and he took a 44-yard touchdown to the house against Buffalo. **But a team whose leading receiver is its third-best option has a problem.**

And look at what this offense has been forced to do. **Brock Bowers is a TIGHT END, and the Raiders are effectively using him as a wide receiver** because they don't have a real one. That's not creative scheming. **That's improvising around a hole.**

**That is why this offense faces the second-most stacked boxes in football. That is why Ashton Jeanty ran 10 times for 22 yards against Buffalo. That is why the red zone was two-for-five.**

**It all comes back to the same empty chair.**

## STORY THREE: THE AFC RECEIVER — JERRY JEUDY

So let's talk about the name that keeps popping up, and it's a good one: **Jerry Jeudy of the Cleveland Browns.**

Here's the situation. **Jeudy is getting pushed aside in Cleveland because the Browns spent two top-40 draft picks on receivers — KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston — and both are emerging.** When your franchise invests that kind of capital in two young receivers and they hit, **the veteran's role evaporates.**

**And it has. Jeudy has eight catches for 81 yards. That's it. That's his whole season.**

**But two years ago, Jerry Jeudy put up 1,229 receiving yards and made the Pro Bowl.** A Fox Sports column argued he's a receiver the Raiders should trade for, and said he could be a **solid number two on another team.**

**And then came the connection that genuinely stuck with me.**

**Jeudy's career-best six touchdowns came in 2022, in Denver — when he overlapped with Klint Kubiak.**

Raider Nation, **there is history there.** And we have seen this front office lean on exactly that kind of history all season. **Amari Kight got signed off Kubiak's Seattle year.** When this staff knows a man and knows he already speaks their language, **they move on him.**

## BUT DOES HE MOVE THE NEEDLE? MY HONEST ANSWER

I'm not sure. And I'm going to give you both sides.

**The case against him is real.** He came out of Alabama with enormous hype, and while he's played well across his career, **he hasn't fully lived up to it. One season over 1,000 yards. That's the whole resume.** If a guy's best season came three years ago in a different system, **you're betting on a bounce-back, not buying a certainty.**

**The case for him is also real.** He's **27 years old** — plenty left in the tank. He has legitimate production on his resume. And critically, **he could step in as a legitimate number one option on the OUTSIDE**, which lets Bowers go back to being a tight end and lets Tucker settle in where he belongs. **That's three problems solved by one player.**

**And then there's the contract, which is the whole reason I'd be careful: he has one year left on his deal.** That means **he's going to want an extension**, and that is exactly why **I would not pay a lot.**

**The figure being floated is that the Raiders shouldn't offer more than a FOURTH-ROUND PICK — and I completely agree.** He's not going to command a huge return given his current role, and **a fourth is the price where this makes sense.** If Cleveland wants a second, you hang up the phone and go home.

**So is Jeudy the answer, or is this a band-aid?** Honestly, Raider Nation? **I think he's a smart band-aid.** And at 3-3, a smart band-aid for a fourth-rounder is a very good piece of business.

## THE CHEAPER OPTION: HOLLYWOOD BROWN

There's another name in the mix, and it comes from the Eagles side of the trade board: **veteran receiver Marquise "Hollywood" Brown.**

Let me be fair about where he's at. **He may not be the same player he once was** — but he's a **former 1,000-yard receiver.** **Last season in Kansas City he played 16 games and put up 587 yards and five touchdowns.** And this year in Philadelphia? **One game played, and he has not caught a single pass.**

**So a change of scenery might be something he's very open to.**

The honest assessment is that he's probably **not the best option on the trade block** — **but the Raiders might be able to get him without giving up much.** And Raider Nation, **that kind of end zone threat would be genuinely welcome for Kirk Cousins.**

**My position hasn't changed from earlier this week: I like the idea of a cheap veteran. I don't love the idea of overpaying for anybody.**

**The smart move here is something small and smart. Not splashy.** New England traded a **2028 first-round pick** for a star receiver, and seven games into the year that pick has had **zero impact** on their football team. **That's the warning sign flashing right in front of us.**

Everything I just said runs through one football game.

**And look at the opponent.** The Rams are **2-4**, coming off that ugly loss to Arizona, **and they are very banged up.**

He means a lot to our defense."**

**And here's why it matters even more this week: if the Rams' top two receivers are unavailable, they're going to have to lean on the RUN.** Which means the game comes down to the middle of that defensive line — **exactly where Hemingway lives.**

Remember Rob Leonard's confession: **great run defense plus one 76-yard explosive still equals a loss.** You don't prevent explosive runs without your interior anchor. **He's back.**

## THE BIG INJURY TO WATCH: MIKE WASHINGTON JR.

Now the one that worries me, and it's bigger than people realize.

**Mike Washington Jr. — a rookie running back — is on the injury report and has not been able to practice.**

And here's the brutal line about this run game: **the only running back who has been able to gain yards this season is Washington.** That's the state of it. **The sixth overall pick is grinding into stacked boxes, and the rookie behind him is the one actually moving the ball.**

**The good news: he's not ruled out.** Kubiak said **"we're expecting him to be ready,"** and wouldn't get into whether there'd be a pre-game workout. He also said the staff is **"still counting on all three guys that are activated to go"** — and if Washington can't, **Roman Hemby is right there: "that'd be a good option, he's been practicing pretty well."**

**Watch that status before kickoff. It might be the most important name on the injury report.**

## AND NOW THE PART THAT MADE ME PROUD

Raider Nation, here's something I found fascinating that nobody is talking about.

**Klint Kubiak knows this Rams team extremely well. He coached against them THREE TIMES last season as the offensive coordinator in Seattle.** And the last time he faced them, **his offense put up 31 POINTS — in the NFC Championship Game.**

**Thirty-one points on that defense, with the season on the line.**

**And listen to how the Rams are talking about him this week.**

**Rams defensive coordinator Chris Shula said Kubiak "does one of the best jobs in the league at matching the offense to the personnel and the skill sets that they have."** He called him **one of the best in the league at what he does** and said facing him is **"a huge challenge."**

**And it's not just the coordinator. Sean McVay said: "Really good team. They've done a heck of a job. Kubiak's done a great job."** He said the Raiders are **well coached in all three phases** and that **you can see Klint's influence on the scheme.**

Raider Nation, **stop and think about that.** We are 3-3 on a three-game skid, the knives are out, and **the opposing head coach and defensive coordinator — from one of the best organizations in football — are praising this staff unprompted.**

**When the other team respects you like that, you're doing something right.** And it fits everything we've watched this week: a head coach who says the red zone **"starts with me,"** a defensive coordinator who named his own 76-yard failure, a special teams coach admitting **"it's confidence and learning,"** and a quarterback apologizing for three throws after a 313-yard day.
