## WHERE WE ARE, FOR CONTEXT

**The Raiders are 3-3 after three straight losses.** New England, then Kansas City by three, then Buffalo by three. **Six total points separating this team from 5-1.**

And the Rams are coming to Allegiant Stadium with **Davante Adams on injured reserve, Poona Ford on injured reserve, and a brand new kicker in Brandon McManus** after theirs tore an ACL. Klint Kubiak still called them **"probably the best offense in the NFL,"** and he's right — but they are walking in shorthanded.

**An opportunity. Not a gift.**

Oh, and the good news from earlier in the week stands: **Dont'e Thornton Jr. is in line for a possible activation** — Kubiak's exact words when asked if there was a chance: **"Yeah. Now. To Rob Leonard.

## THE DECISION: HOW DO YOU RUSH A MAN WHO DOESN'T RUN?

Here's the football question that defines Sunday, and Leonard answered it in detail.

**This defense has spent the entire season chasing mobile quarterbacks.** Patrick Mahomes. Josh Allen. Guys who turn a broken play into a first down with their legs. **And Leonard explained exactly what that forces you to do: you fill up rushing lanes so the quarterback can't buy time.** Your rush isn't about getting there first, it's about keeping him in a box.

**Matthew Stafford is a completely different animal. He's a traditional pocket passer.** He's not escaping you. He's standing in there and carving you up.

**So what changes?** Leonard laid out the thinking, and this is the most revealing thing he said all day:

**"Where do you want to allocate your defenders? Can we rush with four and get home? Can we get to the spot with three, four, five? That's at least the way I think."**

And then, on a quarterback who doesn't move: **"It's just the same conversations. Where do you want to allocate your resources? Do we need to double a guy? Is the quarterback a priority over our matchups?"**

Raider Nation, **that last sentence is the whole ballgame, and I want you to understand why.**

**Rob Leonard just told you he's weighing whether to prioritize the QUARTERBACK over the MATCHUPS.** That's the fork in the road. **Option one:** you commit extra bodies to the rush, you get home, you hit Stafford — but you're playing fewer men in coverage against the best receiving corps left standing in football. **Option two:** you drop more defenders, you double Puka Nacua, you protect your young corners — but now you're asking four men to beat five blockers and get to a quarterback who's seen literally everything.

**You cannot have both. He has to pick.** And he just told the league he's thinking about it.

**That's the decision in this title — and it's not a gimmick. It's the single most consequential call of Sunday afternoon.**

## AND HERE'S WHY THIS IS SCARY: THE TIME-TO-PRESSURE PROBLEM

Now connect it to something Kubiak admitted earlier in the week, because the two things fit together like a lock and a key.

A reporter pointed out that this defense is **still generating pressures and sacks — but the TIME to pressure is long.** It's taking them a while to get home. **Kubiak's honest answer: the deeper you get into a season, teams know what you do.** More tape, more preparation. **"That's that cat-and-mouse game of the NFL."**

**So now put that against Leonard's question.** *Can we rush with four and get home?*

**Raider Nation, if the answer were a confident YES, he wouldn't be asking it out loud.**

And look at the number that proves it: **this defense forced TEN takeaways in the first three games. In the three losses since, it has forced exactly ONE.**

**Ten, then one.** The rush arrives half a second late, the quarterback gets the ball out, and the takeaways vanish. **Against Stafford, half a second late isn't late. It's a touchdown.**

## THE SURPRISE NOBODY SAW COMING: THEY'RE STILL TACKLING IN PRACTICE

Okay, this is the part that genuinely made me sit up, and it's why I think Leonard deserves the headline.

A reporter framed it perfectly: **a third of the way through the season, you can't practice tackling. That's just not conventional in the modern NFL.** Bodies are precious, the injury risk is real, and most staffs in this league stopped doing it in September.

**And then he said it: "You still do today."**

**Rob Leonard's defense is still drilling live tackling in Week 7.** And when he was asked to walk through how, he didn't give a vague answer. He gave a coaching clinic:

**Angles.** **Contact entry** — which he said is what matters most to him. **Same arm, same leg.** Where are you striking on his body? **Where are your eyes?** Are you squeezing? **Are you finishing all the way through?**

Then the method: **"We work a lot with rugby tackling bags, and as much as you can get on a body and actually do it in real life."** And the line that tells you exactly who this coach is:

**"I don't care if they've got pads on or not — you can still get in a proper fit."**

Raider Nation, **that is a coaching philosophy disguised as a practice note.** Most coordinators protect their bodies and hope technique holds up on Sunday. **Leonard is drilling the fundamentals in October like it's August, in shells, with bags, because he believes fit and finish are habits, not talents.**

**And here's why it matters RIGHT NOW.** The Rams run three-tight-end personnel. They have backs who break tackles. Leonard's entire run-defense philosophy — which we'll get to in one second — is built on **not letting one missed tackle turn into 76 yards.**

**You don't fix missed tackles with a speech. You fix them with reps.** I love this. **I love this.** It's the most encouraging thing I've heard from this coaching staff in a month.

## NOW THE CONFESSION — AND THIS CHANGED HOW I SEE THIS DEFENSE

Asked about the key to slowing down a running attack, Leonard didn't talk about yards per carry. **He talked about explosives — and then he threw himself under the bus to make the point.**

His words, and I want you to really hear them: **"I used to hate when people say, 'Hey, we're really good run defense, we don't have this play, that play.'"**

**And then he named the game. "I keep thinking of the Chiefs week."**

**"We played great run defense — and then a 76-yard explosive. It's hard to win doing that."**

Raider Nation. **Seventy-six yards.**

That's Kansas City. **That's the game we lost by three points, 30-27.** And the defensive coordinator of the Las Vegas Raiders just stood at a podium and told you that his defense played great run defense that day **and gave up a 76-yard explosive anyway, and that's why they lost.**

**He's refusing to hide behind a good average.** That's exactly the kind of honesty that's become the signature of this coaching staff — Kubiak saying the red zone starts with him, Cousins apologizing for three throws after a 313-yard day, and now Leonard saying a strong run-defense stat meant nothing because of one snap.

**And he told you the fix: "It's something we're really focused on — the explosives, in the run AND the pass."**

**So here's my Sunday rule, Raider Nation: do not watch the Rams' rushing total. Watch their LONGEST run.** If Los Angeles' longest play from scrimmage is 18 yards, the Raiders probably won this game. **If it's 50-plus, we're going to be sick about it on Monday.**

## AND HE WASN'T DONE BEING HARD ON HIMSELF

A reporter gave him an easy one — *you must be at least pleased with the physicality this defense is bringing* — and Leonard refused to take the compliment.

**"There's areas I have to be better. I have to be better helping them with the level of detail."** He said **these guys want to do right, they're playing hard,** and that his job is **putting them in better situations.** He said he's proud of the way they play — and then immediately went back to the problem: **eliminating "good play, bad play," especially in those critical moments.**

Raider Nation, **"good play, bad play" is the most accurate four-word description of the 2026 Raiders defense I have ever heard.** A sack, then a 76-yard run. A third-down stop, then a touchdown drive. **Ten takeaways, then one.**

**Consistency is the entire gap between 3-3 and dangerous.**

## MATTHEW STAFFORD: "HE'S SEEN IT ALL"

Then Leonard got asked about Stafford specifically, and he gave him the respect he's earned.

**"The guy's seen it all, right? He's seen it all."** Great at identifying coverage. **"They're great at IDing what you're doing and adapting."**

And a reporter who covered Stafford early in his Detroit days added the detail that should worry every corner on this roster: **his ability to put the ball anywhere on the field, from any angle, is extremely unique.**

Leonard agreed — **"he's a gifted thrower, with the no-looks and all those things"** — and then gave us the actual coaching point he's hammering all week:

**"It doesn't matter what it looks like in the pocket. Finish through. Stay with your guy if the play extends."**

**That is the warning.** Against a mobile quarterback, your coverage breaks down because the quarterback runs. **Against Stafford, your coverage breaks down because a corner glances at the pocket, sees a body in Stafford's face, assumes the play is dead — and Stafford flicks a no-look dart into the void that corner just vacated.**

**"He's a special talent,"** Leonard said. He's right. And this is a young secondary.

## PUKA NACUA, AND THE PROBLEM WITH KNOWING WHERE THE BALL IS GOING

Here's the one that keeps defensive coordinators awake.

A reporter laid it out: the Rams usually have Davante Adams too — **and Puka Nacua still took 25 targets in a single game even when Adams was out there.** Twenty-five. In one game. **So what do you do when the whole stadium knows where the ball is going?**

**Leonard's answer was beautifully simple and completely terrifying: you still have to win the matchup.**

**"He's really strong at the top of the route. You can be there to make the play, and he has a unique skill set. So even if you know the ball's going there, you've got to win the matchup. That's what makes him a great player."**

Raider Nation, **that's the Nacua problem in one paragraph.** Knowing isn't stopping. He's not beating you with deception — **he's beating you with strength at the top of the route, which means he gets open even when you've guessed right.**

**And with Davante Adams on IR, every single one of those targets is about to get funneled somewhere.** Which brings us right back to Leonard's fork in the road: **do you double Nacua and rush four, or trust your corner and send five?**

**That's the game. That's the whole game.**

## THE GOOD NEWS IN THAT SECONDARY

Now let's give the young guys their due, because Leonard did.

**He was asked about Drayton, who had one of his better games against Buffalo.** Leonard's take: **he's just more confident** — **"it's easy to be more confident when you're making plays."** But the real reason, and this is the coaching nugget: **the play-in, play-out consistency of being in good position even when the ball ISN'T coming his way.** That's what makes him able to finish the play when it finally does.

**And it showed up where it counts: there were three third downs in particular where Buffalo went after him and he responded.** Leonard's reaction was pure joy: **"It's great for the team. It's great for everybody. It was big."**

Raider Nation, **a young corner winning three third downs against Josh Allen is a real building block.** Hold on to that.

## DARIEN PORTER AND THE PHRASE "THICK SKIN"

Kubiak had used the phrase earlier in the week, and Leonard was asked to expand on it.

**His answer: "Emotional consistency. A guy that's been up, been down, and he's the same guy every day. The same work ethic."**

Then he said something that I think explains how coaches actually evaluate character, and it stuck with me: **"You're around each other more than your own families, right? So you get to see the way a guy goes about his business."** And Porter is the same man **whether he's starting or not** — in practice, in preparation, in all of it.

**"He's just never down on himself, and he'll be one of the first guys out there at practice today going through his routine. I think that's what sticks out about great players — it's just the same."**

And when he was asked about Porter competing with another young corner in that room for starting reps — is that a good problem to have? — **Leonard said yes, and then said the thing that tells you about this locker room:** they both want to play, **"but you wouldn't feel that by the way the room operates. They're trying to make each other better."** He said Kubiak talks about the same thing in the quarterback room — **"and that's real. Those guys want to see the other guys succeed."**

Raider Nation, **a 3-3 team on a three-game skid either fractures or hardens.** Every single coach who's stepped to that podium this week has described a room that's hardening. **I'm choosing to believe them.**

## HE ALSO TOOK THE BLAME FOR THE COMMUNICATION BREAKDOWNS

Asked how the communication between a young group of corners and safeties is developing, Leonard said **it's improving — definitely improving.** But then he didn't let himself off the hook:

**It's hard not to think about the communication errors that have hurt them — "with me being a part of that, making sure the calls are in early and doing my part as well."**

**That's the third coach this week to say "that one's partly on me."** Raider Nation, whatever else is wrong with this football team, **nobody in that building is pointing fingers sideways.** That matters more than you think in the back half of a season.

## NOW THE OFFENSE — AND ANDREW JANOCKO GAVE US A LOT

Let's switch sides, because the offensive answers were just as revealing.

**First up: Washington is earning reps.** Kubiak had already confirmed it — purely on performance, with no controversy involving Ashton Jeanty. So Janocko was asked what Washington has actually done to earn that trust.

**His answer was a roadmap for every young player on this roster:** it starts by **proving it on the practice field** — taking it from the meeting rooms to the practice field, then from the practice field into a game, **and doing specific things well in a game.** Step by step. **That's how a young player earns more reps.**

Raider Nation, I've been banging this drum for weeks: **snaps should follow production, not salary.** Washington has gone from one carry a game to a career-high 23 snaps. **That's the system working the way it should.**

## THE CHASE FOR "EXPLOSIVES" — ON BOTH SIDES OF THE BALL

Then they asked about the Cousins-to-Tre Tucker connection — **the trust where Kirk just throws it and lets Tre go get it.**

**Janocko: "You're always looking for different ways to create explosives."** Every game is different, every series is different. **If there's a way to exploit something a defense is doing, they try to get it** — and the goal is putting players in the best position to make plays using their specific skills, run game and pass game.

He also gave real credit to his position coaches — **the receivers room and the tight ends room** — for the day-to-day development that makes that possible. **"Those guys maximize their players' talents, and Klint and I get to put the guys in the best position possible."**

Raider Nation, **listen to the word that just came out of both coordinators' mouths: EXPLOSIVES.**

**Leonard is obsessed with preventing them. Janocko is obsessed with creating them.** And you know what the Raiders' problem has been all season? **This offense doesn't generate enough of them, because defenses stack the box and dare them to throw it deep.** The 44-yard touchdown to Tucker against Buffalo was the exception, not the rule.

**Which is exactly why a possible Dont'e Thornton Jr. activation matters so much. He is an explosive-play specialist who costs nothing.**

## THE RUN GAME: HE REFUSED TO SAY "CLOSE"

Somebody asked the question we've all been asking — **how close is the running game to getting going?**

**And Janocko wouldn't bite.** He said it's something they look to improve every week — **and that even in a game where they rush for 150 or 200 yards, they'd go back and ask how to get better.** **"I don't know that we're looking at it in terms of 'it's close.' We're looking for incremental improvement every week in everything we do."**

**My honest read:** that's a coach who doesn't believe in one magic fix, and there's wisdom in it. **But Raider Nation, this offense is averaging about 3.2 yards a carry and ran 20 times for 65 yards against Buffalo.** At some point incremental has to become visible. **"One percent better" is a philosophy, not a result.**

## THE BEST FOOTBALL EDUCATION IN THE WHOLE PRESSER: RUNNING BACK PASS PROTECTION

And then came my favorite exchange of the day, because it's the kind of thing you never hear explained properly.

A reporter asked him to contextualize the nuance of pass blocking from the running back position. **Janocko's answer:**

**It's a skill they train at all times. It develops over time. "It's building callus."** It's **understanding patterns of defenses and how their eyes are working**, and it's **gaining experience** — a skill built through reps. **And then the want: "A want to be great at that is something you're always looking for in a back."**

Then the follow-up nailed it: **your running backs are giving up size to blitzing linebackers.** What about the physical toughness required?

**"It starts with a want-to. A want to physically put your face in there and take a bite — because your receiver is blocking for you downfield, so you've got to give him that extra tick to get open. It's still that eleven-as-one mentality and how it all fits together."**

Raider Nation, **"building callus"** and **"put your face in there and take a bite"** are two of the best football phrases I've heard all season. **And here's what it means practically: this is the hidden half of the zero-sack paradox.**

Remember: **Cousins went 41 attempts without being sacked against Buffalo.** We broke down how much of that was quick throws, play action and tight ends chipping. **But part of it is a running back stepping into a blitzing linebacker who outweighs him by 30 pounds, and absorbing it so his receiver gets one more tick to break open.** Nobody claps for that. **It's the most thankless job on the field.**

## MICHAEL MAYER: "HE SITS IN THE FRONT ROW"

Asked what Michael Mayer means to this team and why he's a key piece of long-term plans, Janocko started where good coaches always start: **with the person.**

**"You start with an ultimate competitor. Somebody who comes to work each and every single day."** Then the football skill set, how he's developed as a player, **and how he's developing in a leadership role.** **"That's something that's going to be a part of our success going forward."**

And when he was asked whether there was a moment he realized Mayer could be a key contributor in the pass game, he said no — **you just watch Mike work every single day.** Run game, pass game, protections, **"and how he takes nothing lightly."**

**Then the detail that tells the whole story: "Mike sits in the front row of the meeting rooms."** Watching him take notes, watching how he pays attention, and then watching it show up on the field. **"That's what you look for in a consummate pro."**

Raider Nation, **that's the guy who caught a 28-yard dart on third down against Buffalo. That's also the guy whose route got picked off by a linebacker — through no fault of his own.** The front row of the meeting room. **I'll take a whole roster of that.**

## AND BROCK BOWERS, WHO EVERYBODY PLANS FOR

Asked whether they feel the need to take shots at Brock Bowers even when teams aren't completely taking him away, Janocko was measured: **they go through their plan each week, and they know Brock's a tremendous talent that teams are going to have plans for.** So they try to **find ways to get him open, get him rolling** — **just like every other target in the offense.**

**Translation: we're not going to force it, but we know he's the first name on every defensive game plan in America.**

**And THAT, Raider Nation, is the argument for a real outside threat in one sentence.** When a defense can build its whole week around taking away Bowers, somebody else has to make them pay.

## WHY KIRK COUSINS IS BETTER NOW THAN HE WAS IN MINNESOTA

Janocko coached him in Minnesota, so this answer carried weight. **What's the biggest improvement?**

**Experience.** He's seen good things, he's had success in different systems since they were apart, **so he has a different base of knowledge they can bounce ideas off each other with.**

**But then came the real answer, and it's about character: "He thrives on accountability. He wants to be pushed. He wants to be coached."**

And this is the part I want every young player in that building to tattoo on their arm: **when other guys see a man who's been in this league that long WANT to be coached — and be the first one to say "I've got to do this, I can make this throw, I've got to step up" — and then when it's successful it's "we, it's us, it's together"** — **"that's the type of stuff young guys should mimic."**

Raider Nation, that is **exactly** what we watched on Monday. The man threw for **313 yards, two touchdowns, six scoring drives, zero sacks** — and spent his press conference walking through **three incompletions he blamed himself for.** The coordinator just told you that's not an accident. **That's who he is.**

## AND WHAT HE'S GIVING FERNANDO MENDOZA

This one made me smile.

Asked what Cousins has imparted on Mendoza through six weeks, Janocko said it's **the little nuances — things you only gain from playing in this league that long.**

**And here's how it actually works in that room: Fernando sees something, and he asks a question. "And Kirk loves talking about that, and what's the WHY behind things."**

**Then it goes the other way: Kirk asks HIM a question — "well, what did you see there?" — and they bounce ideas off each other.**

**"That's how growth is made for everybody involved."**

Raider Nation, **the number one overall pick is in a room with a 38-year-old who's top of the league in multiple categories, and they're quizzing each other on the why.** And remember what Kubiak told us: when everyone else has cleared out of the building, **Mendoza is still out on the practice field by himself.**

**The Eagles don't know when their quarterback is coming back. The Falcons are on their fourth veteran. The Colts just fired their GM.** And our quarterback room is two guys asking each other *what did you see there?* **I'll take it.**

## THE OFFENSIVE LINE, AND THE "ONE PERCENT" ANSWER

A reporter made the point that anybody can find mistakes, **but this offensive line is improving.**

**Janocko's answer was pure process:** it's every meeting, every snap, every play, every series, building those things together. **"Can we get one percent better with our techniques, our hand placement, how we come off the ball? What do our eyes look like? What's our pad level? What's our finish? All those things we can quantify. Can we get one percent better in that?"** And that's how they grow — **not just as an offensive line, but as an offense and as an organization.**

Raider Nation, I'll be fair: **there IS evidence.** Forty-one attempts, zero sacks against a Buffalo front. DJ Glaze's year-three jump. Burford's bounce-back game.

**And I'll be honest: there's also a false start on fourth-and-one at the Buffalo 13, seven penalties for 62 yards, and 65 rushing yards.** The arrow is pointing up. **It just hasn't gotten there yet.**

## AND THE SECOND-DOWN MYSTERY

Last thing, and it's a sharp question: **why is this offense struggling on second down?**

**Janocko's answer: you have to look at the big picture, and it's not the same thing every time.** What's the efficiency on **first** down? **Are we in second-and-long?** Do we have **tendencies** a defense is reading? They dig into all of it — first, second, third down. **"When we have success, what's the why? And when we don't, what's the why?"**

**Raider Nation, he just diagnosed it without saying it out loud.** Second down isn't a second-down problem. **It's a FIRST-down problem.** When you run it for two yards on first down, every second down is second-and-eight, and every second-and-eight tells the defense exactly what's coming. **That's how you end up two-for-five in the red zone and outscored by 26 points in the third quarter.**

**Fix first down, and second down fixes itself.** And fixing first down means — say it with me — **unstacking the box.**
