# A WAVE OF HUGE UPDATES BEFORE THE BILLS GAME! "We Were a Sloppy Team That Didn't Deserve To Win" — And Kubiak Just Promised a NEW APPROACH Up Front 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, a wave of news just came out of this building, and some of it is the most revealing stuff we've heard all season.

Klint Kubiak described his own football team in words most head coaches would never use out loud. He confirmed a **new approach** is coming to the offensive line — his words, not mine. He said flat out that he did a poor job of getting one of his best players involved and that it's going to change. He gave us a window into something happening to Brock Bowers that nobody has reported. And then he broke down a Buffalo team that just ran for a frightening number on Monday Night Football.

Plus the full roster picture: who's out, who's not being elevated, who's back, and exactly what the Mendoza tape showed.

So let's get through all of it. I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing 6,000 believers — hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and let's go.

## THE MONDAY MESSAGE: "A SLOPPY TEAM THAT DIDN'T DESERVE TO WIN"

Start with what he told his players when they walked back into the building after that long flight home.

**Kubiak: "The message was that there's lots to fix. We gave up a lot of yards. We didn't move the ball offensively. We had penalties. We were a sloppy team that didn't deserve to win. So we have a lot of work to do in order to go deserve to win this week."**

Raider Nation, read that again. **"We were a sloppy team that didn't deserve to win."**

That is not coach-speak. Coach-speak is "we didn't execute at the level we're capable of." This is a head coach using the word *sloppy* about his own team, in public, on the record.

And notice the construction of the last sentence, because it's the part I keep thinking about: **"in order to go deserve to win this week."** Not "in order to win." **Deserve** to win. He's telling his team that victory isn't something you hope for on Sunday, it's something you earn Wednesday through Friday — which is exactly the same philosophy behind "it takes great practices to win."

Four categories in one answer: yards allowed, no offensive movement, penalties, sloppiness. That's an honest audit, and it matches everything the film showed.

## "WHEN YOU GET DOWN THREE SCORES, IT BECOMES A THROWING GAME"

He was asked about the run game — specifically what it was like in-game, on the headset with his staff, when the deficit forced him away from it.

**Kubiak: "It's not where you want to be, but that was part of that game. When you get down to three scores, it becomes a throwing game. And that's not the formula for winning."**

**"That's not the formula for winning."**

This is the third different way he's now described the same failure, and each version has gotten more precise. First it was "I abandoned the run too early." Then it was "there's an art to it and it's not perfect." Now it's a flat statement of principle: a one-dimensional offense is not a winning formula, period.

And Raider Nation, this is the circular trap we've broken down all week. You fall behind, so you throw. You throw, so the defense plays pass. The defense plays pass, so you fall further behind. The only way out of that loop is to refuse to enter it — which means staying with the run through the ugly early carries instead of chasing points in the second quarter.

## THE BIGGEST NEWS: "A VERY CONCERTED EFFORT TO TAKE A NEW APPROACH"

Now the single most important thing he said, and I don't think anybody is going to lead with it.

He was asked how you improve an offensive line in-season — through preparation, film study, and possibly next man up.

**Kubiak: "I think you evaluate who's playing. You evaluate obviously what you're teaching each week and how you're teaching it, and trying to find ways to freshen things up for the players, get their bodies more fresh so they're hitting on all cylinders on Sunday. But there's a very concerted effort to take a NEW APPROACH in order to get what we want."**

**A new approach.** His words.

Raider Nation, let me unpack that answer, because there are four distinct admissions inside it.

**"You evaluate who's playing."** That's personnel — the guard competition we've been tracking, and the reason nobody has been named a starter four days out.

**"What you're teaching each week and how you're teaching it."** That's coaching. He's not just saying the players need to be better; he's saying the instruction itself is under review. That's an offensive line coach's curriculum being questioned, in public, by his boss.

**"Get their bodies more fresh."** That's workload management — fewer or lighter practice reps for linemen so they have legs on Sunday. It's a real lever that most fans never think about.

**And then "a very concerted effort to take a new approach."** That's the headline. After five games of the same group producing 2.1 yards a carry and 23 pressures from one guard, this staff is changing something structural — not just hoping for better effort.

**Watch the first six snaps on Sunday.** That's where "new approach" either shows up or it doesn't: a different guard, a different run concept, more gap scheme, extra bodies in protection. Something will look different, and now we know to look for it.

## THE BOWERS DETAIL NOBODY HAS REPORTED

Then came an exchange that gave us real information, and it's the kind of thing you only get when a coach is talking to someone he trusts.

He was asked what it's like watching the tape of Bowers getting hit at the line, or getting doubled, and still finding open space.

**Kubiak: "He demands a lot of attention. And the coverages that you see on Sunday are typically NOT what you think you'll see going into the game. I know that's been the case the last couple weeks. He's a unique player, and obviously he's a big part of us having success. With that being said, we've got to continue to find success for everybody so that we can keep moving the ball."**

Raider Nation, stop on the middle sentence. **"The coverages you see on Sunday are typically not what you think you'll see going into the game — and that's been the case the last couple weeks."**

That is a head coach telling you that opponents are now building **custom coverages for Brock Bowers** that don't appear anywhere on their previous film.

Think about what that means practically. A coaching staff spends all week preparing against what a defense has shown. Then Sunday arrives and that defense unveils brackets, double-teams and disguises specifically designed for one tight end — concepts they've never run before. Your game plan is partially obsolete at kickoff, and you have to solve it in real time, on a headset, between drives.

**That's the highest compliment in football and a serious problem at the same time.** It's the tight end version of what Mike Vrabel did to Maxx Crosby.

And it explains Kubiak's constant refrain this week — "we've got to find success for everybody." The answer to being double-teamed isn't for Bowers to beat two men. **It's for somebody else to make the double team expensive.** Which is why the receiver conversation and the Bowers conversation are the same conversation.

## "I DID A POOR JOB OF GETTING HIM INVOLVED"

And then the most direct self-criticism of the week, about Michael Mayer.

**Kubiak: "Mike's a leader for us. And I did a poor job of getting him involved in Sunday's game. And I expect that to change."**

**"I did a poor job. And I expect that to change."**

Raider Nation, earlier in the week he explained *why* Mayer disappeared — the protection demands against New England's blitzes swallowed his routes. That was the mechanism. This is the verdict, and he's handing it to himself.

And that last clause matters most: **"I expect that to change."** That's not an apology, it's a commitment. A tight end who caught eight passes for 82 yards against Kansas City went quiet in Foxborough, and the man who calls the plays just told the world he's fixing it at home.

If Mayer gets six or seven targets Sunday, this offense looks completely different — because a second tight end who can win underneath is exactly what punishes a defense that's bracketing Bowers.

## THE DYLAN LAUBE ANSWER, EXPANDED

I also want to come back to Dylan Laube, because he got a fuller description this time and it answers something I criticized.

**Kubiak: "Just so reliable. A guy that can play in the run, play in the pass protection game, play on special teams — just a constant every-week guy you can count on."**

Raider Nation, remember my complaint from the film review: Laube got four carries against New England while Washington Jr., averaging five yards a carry, got one. I called that the least defensible distribution of the afternoon.

Here's the answer, and it's in the middle of that quote: **"play in the pass protection game."**

Against a defense generating pressure on 44.8 percent of dropbacks, the back who stays in and blocks correctly is worth more on certain snaps than the back who gains more yards. If the staff trusts Laube's protection and is still developing Washington's, that's not favoritism — that's keeping your 38-year-old quarterback upright.

I still want Washington Jr. to get gap-scheme carries, and the coach has said he deserves more. But "reliable in all three phases" is a real reason, and you deserve to hear it instead of just my complaint.

## WHAT THE MENDOZA TAPE SHOWED

Now the quarterback update, and this is more specific than anything we've gotten.

Asked about the decision to put Fernando Mendoza in late and what the tape showed:

**Kubiak: "He ran the huddle. He got through his progressions. Saw coverage well, saw pressures well, communicated well. One-drive sample size was a positive start for him."**

Five separate competencies in one answer, Raider Nation, and I want to go through them because they're not the ones fans measure.

**"He ran the huddle."** That's command presence — eleven grown men in a loud stadium listening to a 22-year-old. It's the thing nobody can teach.

**"Got through his progressions."** He didn't lock onto one receiver. He read one, two, three.

**"Saw coverage well."** He identified what the defense was doing.

**"Saw pressures well."** This is the one I flagged last week — recognizing an unblocked rusher and knowing where the ball has to go.

**"Communicated well."** Protection calls, motion adjustments, alerts at the line.

None of that is in a stat line. Six-of-eight for 48 yards tells you nothing about any of it. And the coach's framing stayed honest: **"one-drive sample size."** He's not promoting anybody. He's confirming the processing is ahead of schedule.

## THE ROSTER WAVE

The hard news, all of it:

**Tonka Hemingway is out**, which thins the interior defensive line in the exact week this staff declared run defense its top priority.

**Thornton and Renan will not be elevated**, and Kubiak's words on Thornton were striking: **"He's got another week to practice here, and he's got a lot to prove to himself and to us. He's been working really hard, and I look forward to watching him compete again this week."**

"A lot to prove to himself and to us." That's a standard, stated twice over. And "getting closer" was the follow-up.

**Trey Zuhn is questionable**, which limits the heavy packages.

**And Jalen Nailor will be available.** Kubiak: "Jalen should be back this week." On his production: the catch numbers aren't necessarily there, but in terms of spacing and impact he's doing a great job — which, as we broke down yesterday, is a coach explaining that a receiver's job includes occupying defenders so somebody else gets open.

## NOW THE BILLS, AND THESE NUMBERS ARE SCARY

Let's get to Buffalo, because Kubiak's scouting report was detailed and sobering.

They played on Monday Night Football and **put up close to 400 yards of offense.** They are, in his words and by any measure, a very explosive team.

And he started where everybody has to start.

## JOSH ALLEN RAN FOR 121 YARDS — ON 12 COMPLETIONS

Here's the stat that defines the entire matchup. In that Monday night game, **Josh Allen ran for 121 yards and completed only 12 passes.** When he dropped back and nobody was open, he simply tucked the football and ran — and he was enormously productive doing it.

**Kubiak: "He's a competitor. He does what it takes to win. He's one of the elite players in the league, and a guy that we have to account for this week. Account for his arm and his legs."**

Raider Nation, 121 rushing yards from a quarterback is a nightmare for the specific defense we have.

Here's why. We just watched New England attack this secondary vertically while our pass rush produced one sack. The natural correction is to play more coverage and rush fewer men — keep everything in front of you, don't let anybody get over your head.

**Against Josh Allen, that correction is a trap.** Drop eight and he runs for twelve yards a carry. Blitz and leave your young corners on an island, and he throws it over their heads like Drake Maye did. There is no safe answer, which is why elite quarterbacks are elite.

The only genuine solution is disciplined rush lanes — contain, don't chase — and tackling in the open field. Which means Nakobe Dean and Quay Walker, both of whom had a difficult afternoon in Foxborough, have the hardest assignment of their season.

## JAMES COOK, AND A PERSONAL CONNECTION

Then the running back, and the number here is just as ugly for us.

**James Cook is averaging 5.6 yards per carry — the same as Josh Allen.**

Both of them. Five-point-six.

**Kubiak: "I know his family well. His brother played for us in Minnesota — Dalvin — and they're two very explosive backs. Five yards a carry says what it says. But his explosion, his ability to make plays in the pass game, make him a big threat."**

That's a nice human detail — Kubiak coached Dalvin Cook in Minnesota, so he knows this family's game intimately. But look at the scouting inside it: **"his ability to make plays in the pass game."**

That's the part that worries me most. A back who's a receiving threat forces linebackers to respect him in coverage, which means they can't simply crash downhill against the run, which opens the run. It's the exact same mechanism I described with Ashton Jeanty's 19 catches — except Buffalo has it working at 5.6 yards a carry.

And "five yards a carry says what it says" is the most Kubiak sentence imaginable. No spin. The number is the number.

## TWO TIGHT ENDS — A MIRROR OF US

Then the matchup that's almost poetic.

**Buffalo deploys Dalton Kincaid and Dawson Knox — two tight ends, similar to what Las Vegas has with Bowers and Mayer.** They didn't produce much on Monday because that game turned into broken plays and runs, but Kubiak was clear about the threat.

**"They've got two really good players. Their offense as a whole is very explosive. They score a lot of points. They move the ball well, on the ground and in the air. So it all starts with Josh."**

Raider Nation, a two-tight-end offense is a defensive coordinator's riddle, and we know it better than anybody because we run it. Do you play base personnel and risk getting beaten in coverage? Or do you go nickel and let them run at a lighter box?

Remember this team's biggest weakness from the Saints game — **Tyler Shough threw four touchdowns and all four went to tight ends.** That hole was identified in Week Three and it's still the thing opponents attack. Now here come Kincaid and Knox with Josh Allen throwing.

That's the single matchup I'd circle in red on Rob Leonard's board.

## AND THE DEFENSIVE FIX, STATED PLAINLY

On cutting down explosive plays:

**Kubiak: "It's a big challenge this week, and it's a big challenge every week. New England did a great job of getting the ball OVER OUR HEADS. So we have to adjust — as coaches, as players — and go make those plays. We have a lot to improve on, and that's our job as coaches to get fixed."**

**"Getting the ball over our heads."** That's the specific diagnosis, and it's the honest one. Not "we had some communication issues." The ball went over the top, repeatedly, which is what happens when young corners play without safety help and the quarterback has a clean pocket.

And once again: **"that's our job as coaches to get fixed."** I've lost count of how many times this man has pointed the thumb this month. The culture is not in question. The execution is.

## THE JOE BRADY FACTOR

One more piece of context, because Buffalo's sideline has changed.

**Joe Brady** has taken over a big spot in Buffalo as they open a new stadium — following a really successful head coach, which is one of the hardest jobs in sports.

**Kubiak: "Their team scores a lot of points. They do a great job of moving the football. They have really great players on both sides of the ball. Defensively, they get after the quarterback. Coach Brady has done a great job with that program, and we have a great team coming to our stadium that we're excited to play."**

"Defensively, they get after the quarterback." Raider Nation, that's the sentence that should make you nervous, and here's why: this offensive line just allowed pressure on nearly half of Cousins' dropbacks against a team that was good at pressuring. Now a second consecutive opponent with a real pass rush arrives — and this time there's no "new approach" excuse available, because the coach just promised one.

## THE MESSAGE TO RAIDER NATION

And he closed with something I want every one of you to hear, because Buffalo travels better than almost any fan base in football — and they're staying on the West Coast for a second game.

**Kubiak: "Oh yeah, we need the silver and black there. We need our fans. We need their support. We know there's going to be plenty of Buffalo fans — but the energy of being back in our building, getting to play in front of our fans on Sunday, is very exciting for our team. Our players are looking forward to giving them our best. But just to see our fans, and to see them tailgating there early before the game — it brings so much energy to myself and to our whole team."**

Raider Nation, this is the second time this season a Raiders figure has effectively asked for a blackout. Maxx Crosby said this team had to **earn** a full silver-and-black crowd. Now the head coach is asking for it outright, before a game against a fan base famous for taking over buildings.

Two home games in a row, a two-game losing streak to end, and a coach who just told you that seeing tailgates in the morning changes how his team feels at kickoff. That's a direct request. Answer it.

Let me put the wave together.

A head coach called his own team **sloppy** and **undeserving**, which tells you the message inside that building has been blunt. He confirmed a **new approach** is coming to the offensive line — personnel, teaching method, and workload all under review. He admitted he did a **poor job** involving Michael Mayer and promised it changes. He revealed that opponents have been showing Bowers **coverages they've never run before**, which is why everybody else getting involved isn't a luxury but a necessity. And he gave the Mendoza tape five specific checkmarks that have nothing to do with the box score.

Then he described Buffalo honestly: close to 400 yards on Monday, Josh Allen with 121 rushing yards on 12 completions, James Cook at 5.6 a carry with receiving ability, two tight ends that mirror our own personnel, and a defense that gets after the quarterback.

**My watch list for Sunday, four items.** Who lines up at guard on the first snap — that's where "new approach" becomes real. Michael Mayer's target count — that's where "I expect that to change" becomes real. Rush lane discipline on Josh Allen's first scramble — that's where the defensive adjustment becomes real. And the crowd at kickoff — that's on us.
