## WHAT WAS ACTUALLY REPORTED

Let me give you the reporting precisely, because the names attached to it are the heaviest in the business.

On October 10th, ESPN's Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport reported that the Arizona Cardinals were finalizing a trade involving Marvin Harrison Jr.

And here's the detail that tells you how far along this is: according to Schefter, Harrison was informed he would be **inactive against the Detroit Lions** while the details of the deal were being ironed out.

Raider Nation, that's not smoke. Teams don't sit a former fourth overall pick on game day for fun. When a club tells a healthy starter he's not playing while paperwork gets finished, the paperwork is close to finished.

So the question for us becomes very simple. Could Las Vegas be the destination?

## THE PRICE

Tom Pelissero reported what Arizona wants, and it's a number that matters enormously for us.

**The Cardinals are demanding at least a second-round draft pick, plus additional compensation.**

At least a second. Plus more.

And I have to be honest with you, because I've given you a specific line on this player for weeks: that is above my price. When Albert Breer floated Harrison as a Raiders fit, the structure I argued for was a conditional fourth-round pick with performance escalators — you pay for production you actually receive. A second plus additional compensation is a completely different transaction.

Now, Arizona's position is defensible. Harrison hasn't yet consistently lived up to expectations, but his physical talent and potential are going to attract teams looking to bolster an offense, and the Cardinals are not going to hand a top-four pick to a rival for day-three capital eighteen months after drafting him. They have to be able to justify it to their own fan base.

But a demand isn't a sale price. Asking prices come down as a deadline approaches, especially when the player is already being held out of games.

## THE COMPETITION

And this is why I'm cautious about getting your hopes up.

According to Schefter, Houston was in talks with Arizona regarding a potential trade, though a team source neither confirmed nor denied the interest. Meanwhile ESPN's Stephen Holder reported that the Indianapolis Colts are **not** involved in the negotiations.

So the field is narrowing in public, and Houston — a team whose own receiver situation we discussed on this channel just days ago when Nico Collins' name surfaced — is apparently in the mix.

That changes the leverage completely. If two teams want him, the price doesn't fall. It holds.

## KUBIAK'S ANSWER

Now here's the part that made me sit up.

Sam Warren of The Athletic asked Klint Kubiak directly whether Las Vegas would pursue another receiver.

The answer was straightforward: **"Everything is being evaluated."**

Raider Nation, that's the second time in a week this head coach has publicly left the door wide open. He said "all options are being considered" when asked about the receiver room before, and now "everything is being evaluated."

Coaches have a hundred ways to shut a question down. "We like our guys." "We're focused on this week." "That's John's department." He didn't use any of them. Twice.

And the reason is sitting on the injury report. Jalen Nailor is out and Jack Bech is on injured reserve. That's not a luxury upgrade conversation anymore — that's a team that walked into a Week Five road game with three healthy wide receivers.

And then the line that should make every Raiders fan lean forward. An NFL executive told Vacchiano that some talented receivers might no longer figure in their team's plans and could potentially be traded **for day-three draft picks.**

Day three. Rounds four through seven.

That is exactly the market I've been telling you to wait for. Jeudy has been at the top of my "most likely to actually happen" list for weeks, with five catches in four games and a Cleveland beat writer openly predicting a trade. Quentin Johnston comes off that 0-4 Chargers roster we've been monitoring for Khalil Mack. Godwin is a proven professional. And Brian Thomas Jr. is the kind of name that would genuinely shock this league if he moved.

With the deadline set for November 10th, the Raiders might be smart to wait for the more affordable opportunity rather than winning a bidding war against Houston.

John Spytek has to balance the need to bolster this offense against the preservation of draft capital. And right now there is no confirmation of a Raiders offer for Harrison at all — but the injuries at the position make the possibility genuinely intriguing.

**Ashton Jeanty is dealing with foot and ankle injuries.** Both. The ankle we've tracked all season, and now a foot.

Raider Nation, that's not a maintenance day. That's a player who tried, got worse, and stopped. His status against New England is genuinely uncertain, and it explains exactly why Kubiak stood at that podium and said "if he can't go, then we got to get the next man up."

## THE SEASON SO FAR, IN FULL

For the record, here's what the sixth overall pick of the 2025 draft has produced through four starts: **264 rushing yards on 78 carries, plus 19 receptions for 165 yards and two touchdowns.**

Do the division on the rushing line and you get about 3.4 yards a carry — the number we've been dissecting all week. But notice the receiving line, because people keep ignoring it. Nineteen catches in four games is a legitimate pass-game weapon. He's been useful even while the ground game has been strangled by loaded fronts.

## MEET ROMAN HEMBY

And here's the move Las Vegas just made in response, reported by Ian Rapoport on October 10th.

**The Raiders promoted rookie running back Roman Hemby from the practice squad.**

So let me introduce him properly, because most of you have never heard this name.

He's 24 years old. He joined the Raiders as an undrafted free agent in 2026 after finishing his college career at Indiana. In the preseason he played three games, recording 38 rushing yards on 11 carries and 22 receiving yards on three catches.

Limited NFL experience, obviously. But look at that final college season at Indiana: **864 rushing yards and six touchdowns on 163 attempts, averaging 5.3 yards per carry.**

Raider Nation, 5.3 a carry in major college football is a real number. That's a back who was finding creases against serious defenses. And the fact that he also caught the ball in the preseason matters in this specific offense, because Kubiak's scheme asks backs to be receiving threats out of the backfield.

He may now have a chance to prove his worth exactly when this team needs depth most. For an undrafted free agent, this promotion is the entire opportunity — the kind of moment Cody White turned into two touchdowns.

## THE BACKFIELD PLAN IF JEANTY SITS

The other option is rookie Washington Jr., who has tallied 120 rushing yards and a touchdown over the first four games.

So if Jeanty can't play or is limited, Las Vegas can split the workload between the two young backs — Washington as the primary with the experience and the pass-protection reps, Hemby as the fresh-legs change of pace.

And honestly? I'm not panicking about that. Here's why.

Remember what we established about the box count. Jeanty faced roughly twice as many stacked fronts as Washington did. Part of that is scheme-based — defenses specifically load up for the sixth overall pick. A backfield rotation nobody has film on removes one of the opponent's advantages, because New England's week of preparation was built around a specific back's tendencies.

That's not a reason to be happy Jeanty is hurt. It's a reason to believe this doesn't have to be a disaster.

## NOW THE PART WHERE I UPDATE MY OWN TAKE

And here, Raider Nation, I have to do something I think every honest analyst owes you.

Tyler Bischoff of Athlon Sports reported that Jackson Powers-Johnson is expected to return after recovering from his groin injury, which bolsters this line and helps Cousins in Week Five. He started the first three games and sat out Week Four, when Caleb Rogers stepped in at right guard against Kansas City. And Kubiak still has not confirmed the starter — only that "we'll see."

But buried in that report is a set of numbers that genuinely surprised me.

**Last season, Las Vegas allowed the most sacks in the NFL. This season, they've conceded only five in four games.**

Cousins deserves credit there too — he has the league's fourth-best ratio of pressures faced to sacks taken, which means he's getting the ball out instead of absorbing hits.

And then this, per ESPN figures cited in that report: **the Raiders lead the NFL in pass blocking success rate.** Kolton Miller, Tyler Linderbaum, Spencer Burford and DJ Glaze are all posting rates above 90 percent.

I have spent a month on this channel calling the interior offensive line the biggest structural weakness on this roster. I pushed back on PFF for grading Burford and Linderbaum as our two best offensive players. And I still believe my eyes on the Kansas City tape.

But I'm not going to hide from data this clear. **In pass protection, this line is not the problem. It's the best unit in football at that one job.** From worst in the league in sacks allowed to five in four games is one of the most underrated turnarounds in the NFL this season, and I haven't given it enough credit.

## SO WHERE DOES THE RUN GAME PROBLEM ACTUALLY LIVE?

Here's where the numbers get fascinating, and where everything we've discussed this week finally fits together.

**Las Vegas ranks last in the NFL in yards per carry. But the team sits 12th in run blocking success rate.**

Think about that gap. Twelfth in the league at winning run blocks. Dead last in actual yards per carry.

That is a statistical fingerprint, and it points directly at the thing Kirk Cousins described from the podium this week. Remember his words: it becomes the math of how many players are down inside the tight end box, and do we have angles, do we have numbers, or don't we.

Run blocking success rate measures whether blockers win their assignments. Yards per carry measures what actually happens. When you're twelfth at the first and last at the second, the losses aren't coming from blockers getting beaten — they're coming from defenders nobody was assigned to block, because the defense had more men in the box than the offense had blockers.

**You can win every block on the play and still get stopped at the line if you're outnumbered.**

So let me restate my position cleanly, with the correction included. The pass protection is excellent and I under-credited it. The run blocking is middle of the pack, which is below where a Kubiak scheme needs it to be but nowhere near the disaster the yards-per-carry number implies. And the real culprit is the box count — which is why the fix is a receiver who forces a safety to stay deep, not a different center.

That said, the data also leaves room for improvement from the backs themselves. Twelfth in run blocking success means creases are being created at a respectable rate, and at some point those creases have to be converted. A healthy, explosive Jeanty converts more of them than a compromised one. That's part of this too, and it's another argument for protecting that ankle and foot.

## ONE MORE INJURY NOBODY MENTIONED

And there's a quiet absence that makes the guard decision even more important.

Rookie Trey Zuhn is out for Week Five with a knee injury. He's played only 12 offensive snaps, so nobody's going to write a headline about it — but his versatility across tackle, guard and center is exactly what a staff uses in formations featuring extra linemen.

Translation: those heavy packages you deploy in short yardage and bad weather just got harder to field, and the staff's rotation options up front just shrank. Which makes Powers-Johnson's recovery more critical, not less.

So my answer to the guard question stays where it was. If he's healthy, start him. A unit already leading the league in pass blocking success, with a healthy second-round interior lineman added back into it, going into wind and rain against a defense that wants to pressure the quarterback and disrupt rhythm — that's the version of this offensive line I want to see.
