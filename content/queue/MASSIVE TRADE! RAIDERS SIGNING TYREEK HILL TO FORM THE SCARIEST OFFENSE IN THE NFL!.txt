THE EVAN NEAL RECLAMATION PROJECT

On September 3rd, the Raiders signed offensive lineman Evan Neal to the practice squad after the former New York Giants first-rounder was released at final cuts. And Raider Nation, I need you to remember who this player was SUPPOSED to be — because that's what makes this signing fascinating.

Neal went seventh overall in the 2022 draft out of Alabama. Seventh. Overall. The college résumé was pristine: a national title, All-American honors, a mountain of a man who looked like a decade-long franchise tackle. Then the NFL happened. Across his first three seasons he appeared in 29 games with 27 starts — but the injuries never stopped coming, the performance concerns kept mounting, and he missed the ENTIRE 2025 season, with a hamstring problem among the issues that kept him shelved. The Giants declined his fifth-year option in April 2025, brought him back on a one-year deal for one last look... and he still couldn't crack their final roster. That's how a seventh overall pick ends up on a workout list. The Raiders brought him in on September 3rd, liked what they saw, and added him to the practice squad the same day.

Now let's be adults about expectations: this does not make Evan Neal a starter, and it might never. But look at WHY this move makes so much sense for this specific roster. The Raiders have bet the entire franchise on the quarterback position — a $12.6 million bridge in Cousins and a number one overall pick in Mendoza — which means protecting the passer isn't a priority, it's THE priority. And the depth chart behind the starting five is thinner than anyone's comfortable with: Meredith was traded to the Jets, Mafi's spot is the flexible one, Powers-Johnson carries a real injury history — 23 of a possible 34 career games, remember — and Zuhn is being asked to cover three positions at once. Stashing a 24-year-old former top-ten pick on the practice squad costs you NOTHING and gives the staff months to rebuild his technique and confidence away from the spotlight. If injuries strike up front — and across eighteen weeks, they always do — you have a first-round talent to elevate instead of a street free agent.

And notice the pattern one more time, because it's becoming this front office's signature: Fatukasi off the veteran market. Luter off waivers. Cody White with the playbook familiarity. Sloan developed in-house. Now Neal, the classic buy-low reclamation. Spytek keeps shopping in the discount aisle for players with pedigree and something to prove — and in a hard-cap league, that's exactly how deep rosters get built. One of these lottery tickets doesn't need to hit for the strategy to be right. But if Neal ever rediscovers even 80 percent of what Alabama saw in him? That's a franchise-altering freebie.

COUSINS: NOW IT'S FROM THE COACH'S MOUTH

Quick confirmation on the quarterback front, because now we have Kubiak's own words. The head coach confirmed after practice that Kirk Cousins is the Week 1 starter against Miami — and his explanation matters: Cousins EARNED it, through what Kubiak called an excellent preseason and a strong training camp. DALLAS CALLS AGAIN: TWO FIRSTS AND RASHAN GARY?!

Now the story that made every Raiders fan's phone buzz. Imagine waking up to this headline: "Dallas wants Maxx Crosby — and might be willing to pay a steep price." According to a Bleacher Report trade projection published September 4th, the Cowboys would send edge rusher Rashan Gary PLUS first-round picks in 2027 AND 2028 to Las Vegas in exchange for Crosby.

Breathe. This is a hypothetical projection, not a confirmed offer. But before you dismiss it, understand the history, because Dallas' interest is documented and REAL. Back in March, the Cowboys reportedly offered the 12th overall pick in 2026 plus a 2027 second-rounder for Crosby. The Raiders said no — and instead agreed to send him to Baltimore for the 14th pick and a 2027 first. We all know how that ended: the medical exams reportedly flagged a suspected degenerative issue in Crosby's left knee, Baltimore backed out, and Maxx came home. But here's the part people forget: Jerry Jones said in JULY that Dallas was still willing to sacrifice a premium pick for an impact defender — and made it clear the Crosby conversation wasn't necessarily over. And remember, we covered the ESPN chatter connecting Crosby to Dallas back in August too. The Cowboys have been circling this water all year.

Why? Because the résumé speaks for itself: 15 games last season with 10 sacks, 73 tackles, 28 tackles for loss, 20 quarterback pressures, two forced fumbles, and an interception — on a torn meniscus. Career: 69.5 sacks in 110 games, five consecutive Pro Bowls, and at 29, still among the premier defensive players alive. Meanwhile Gary, who landed in Dallas from Green Bay in March, brings 46.5 career sacks and a 7.5-sack season of his own last year. So the theoretical swap is: an established elite edge with knee questions, for a very good (not elite) edge who's NFL-proven, plus two future firsts.

And I have to be honest with you: as a pure value proposition, this package is SIGNIFICANTLY better than the 49ers framework we broke down yesterday. One first plus an ACL-rehabbing Mykel Williams versus two firsts plus a healthy, productive Rashan Gary? It's not close. Two future firsts in drafts we already know are loaded — including that 2027 receiver class with Cam Coleman and Jeremiah Smith at the top — plus a plug-and-play edge so the defense doesn't collapse on day one? If you forced me to design the package that would actually make Spytek pick up the phone, it looks a lot like this one.

But my framework hasn't moved, and I'll die on this hill: Maxx Crosby is not "a defensive end." He's the identity, the grit, the heartbeat of this defense — the man who posted a farewell video, got un-traded by a physical, and responded by punching footballs out of teammates' hands in practice. You don't trade the heartbeat four days before the opener. And structurally you CAN'T: with Crawford's Achilles leaving four healthy edges, moving Crosby now would gut the entire front. The timeline I gave you yesterday stands — if this ever gets real, it gets real at the October deadline, with two months of standings and two months of Crosby health tape as evidence. Until then, every one of these projections is just the league telling us what we already know: we have the most valuable defensive player on the market that isn't for sale. Win early, and he never is.

THE WILSON DOOR SLAMS SHUT

And finally, the setback. The Raiders' dream future target for Fernando Mendoza — Cardinals receiver Michael Wilson — just signed a three-year, $75 million extension with Arizona. The deal can reach $78 million and includes $47 million guaranteed at signing. Translation: Wilson isn't going anywhere, and Arizona just told the league he's a foundation piece.

Why does this sting so much in Vegas? Because Wilson is EXACTLY the archetype this offense is missing. Around 6'2" and over 210 pounds — the size to win contested catches and terrorize the red zone. A refined route-runner who lines up everywhere. And last season he broke through with his first 1,000-yard campaign despite playing in an Arizona offense that couldn't consistently move the ball — we watched that chemistry with Jacoby Brissett up close in the preseason opener, where at times he looked more in sync with his quarterback than Marvin Harrison Jr. did. Now picture that player in Kubiak's system, with Bowers bending coverage toward the middle and Mendoza arriving at quarterback. That's the picture Spytek surely had pinned somewhere — a realistic deadline target if Arizona's season went sideways. Instead, the Cardinals made the decision for everybody. He's staying in the desert.

So it's back to the board, Raider Nation — but let's keep perspective, because the board is not empty. The cap flexibility is still there, roughly $59 million projected for next offseason. The trade targets we've profiled — Boutte, Mooney, the Hollywood Brown flyer — still exist. Tillman still sits one phone call away on the Saints' practice squad. That loaded 2027 receiver class looms, where two first-round picks (see the Dallas conversation above — funny how these stories connect) could buy Mendoza his running mate for a decade. And circle all the way back to where we started this episode: every door that closes at receiver makes the TYREEK door more interesting. Wilson off the board means the market for a true, proven, defense-terrifying WR1 just shrank to a handful of names — and the biggest one plays his home games in Miami, visits Allegiant on Sunday, and keeps getting whispered toward Vegas. Losing Wilson stings. It doesn't strand us. It might just be pointing us toward the bigger swing — and if Tucker seizes this contract year the way I believe he can, the WR1 conversation could even answer itself from inside the building.
