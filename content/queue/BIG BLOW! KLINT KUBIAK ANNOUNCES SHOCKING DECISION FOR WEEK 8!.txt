# BIG BLOW! KLINT KUBIAK ANNOUNCES SHOCKING DECISION FOR WEEK 8! "We're Going To Keep SHAKING IT UP" — And Five Words That Change This Offense 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, Klint Kubiak stood at that podium on Wednesday and said two things that genuinely change what this football team is going to look like in New York.

**The first one: "We're going to give other guys opportunities. We're going to keep SHAKING IT UP until we find the right mix."**

**That's the offensive line. Moving men to different sides. Switching positions. No sacred cows.** He confirmed it in plain English and he didn't flinch.

**And the second one is five words that I did not expect to hear from an offensive-minded head coach who just put 41 pass attempts on the board against Buffalo.**

Asked what needs to click after the opportunities this offense left on the field against the Rams, Kubiak said:

**"We need to take advantage of the run better. We've got to get better there. QUIT THROWING IT SO MUCH."**

**Quit throwing it so much.** His words. **That's a head coach publicly committing to change the identity of his own offense in the middle of a four-game losing streak.**

**And yes, there's a blow in this report too: Patrick Johnson did not practice today.** Kwity Paye was limited, though that one's good news — more on both in a second.

Plus: the Dont'e Thornton Jr. update is brutally honest, Jack Bech is probably playing Sunday, the Jets are a top-ten defense waiting on the road, **Kirk Cousins ran a quarterback sneak and converted it,** and Cousins himself gave the single best explanation of offensive identity I've heard all season.

## THE INJURY REPORT, AND THE GOOD BURIED IN IT

Kubiak opened with the practice list, as always.

**"Patrick Johnson did not practice today. Kwity Paye was limited."**

And when asked what Paye was dealing with, the answer was the best kind of answer: **"He has had a rest day."**

Raider Nation, **a rest day is not an injury.** And it's actually encouraging — **Paye finally got home for a sack in the Buffalo game** after weeks of us hammering that $48 million contract on this channel. **Giving your edge rusher a maintenance day in Week 8 of a long season is smart, not scary.**

**And then the update I was most relieved to hear.** Mike Washington went into Sunday's game banged up but played anyway — the rookie back who's been, in the honest assessment of the numbers, **the only Raiders running back consistently gaining yards this season.**

**Kubiak: "Mike practiced today. Mike looks like his OLD SELF."**

**That's a real win.** A banged-up rookie back who fought through the Rams game is already back to full speed on Wednesday.

## NOW THE SHOCKING DECISION: THEY'RE TEARING UP THE OFFENSIVE LINE

Here's the exchange that matters most for Sunday.

Asked whether, with the competition continuing on the offensive line, he's considered **moving guys to either side or switching their positions around**, Kubiak said:

**"Yeah, we'll probably — we're going to give other guys opportunities. We're going to keep shaking it up until we find the right mix."**

Raider Nation, **understand how unusual that is in Week 8.**

Most head coaches in this league would be clinging to continuity right now. Offensive line play is five men speaking one language — **you don't normally rip that up at the end of October.** But this group has produced **65 rushing yards against Buffalo, 20 carries, and Ashton Jeanty going 10 for 22.** Caleb Rogers **whiffed on a goal-line block that stopped Jeanty for a loss in the fourth quarter.** Jackson Powers-Johnson took **zero offensive snaps.** And Monday, when asked if Trey Zuhn or Bryce Cabeldue might get guard reps, Kubiak answered with one word: **"Yes."**

**He's not bluffing. The line is officially up for grabs.**

## AND TREY ZUHN MIGHT BE THE MOST INTERESTING MAN IN THAT BUILDING

Asked what he's seen from Zuhn's development and his readiness for more reps, Kubiak lit up:

**"Since the day he got here, he's been a SWISS ARMY KNIFE. He can play so many positions. Very intelligent, hardworking player. He's still ascending, still getting better — and he'll have his chance."**

Then it got better. Asked whether they'd honed in on one position for him, Kubiak basically laughed:

**"He can play tackle, he can play guard, he can play center. He might be able to play quarterback — I don't know, I haven't let him try yet."**

**And when a reporter pushed and asked whether they'd streamline it this week to just one spot, the answer was firm: "He HAS to play multiple positions."**

Raider Nation, **that's a 2026 third-round pick being used as the universal solvent on an offensive line that doesn't know what it is yet.** Is it ideal? No — **you'd love to park a young lineman at one spot and let him master it.** But when the mix is broken, **a man who can slide anywhere is how you find the right five.**

## AND HERE'S WHY THEY CAN ACTUALLY DO THIS IN WEEK 8

This was the smartest thing in the whole press conference, and nobody will clip it.

Asked about the challenges of moving linemen around, Kubiak was honest: **there are many, especially on the offensive line, because every scheme has its own unique calls and unique techniques.**

**"But the great thing about it is coach Dennison and coach Mario and Pete and Ben — they put those guys through all of that in TRAINING CAMP. So there are a lot of reps that we banked, that we're using now to our advantage."**

Raider Nation, **that is coaching foresight paying off in late October.** Rick Dennison and that offensive line staff cross-trained these men in August when it cost nothing. **Now, in a crisis, the Raiders can move a guard to tackle or a tackle to guard without teaching from scratch — because the reps are in the bank.**

**That's the difference between a staff that plans and a staff that reacts.**

## NOW THE FIVE WORDS: "QUIT THROWING IT SO MUCH"

Raider Nation, this is the real headline, and I want to put it in context so you understand how significant it is.

**Against Buffalo, Kirk Cousins threw 41 passes.** Against the Rams, this offense went **0-for-3 in the red zone in the first half** and got beaten 35-21. And the film analysts told us why: **nobody separating against man coverage.**

**And on Monday, Kubiak explained the mechanism better than anyone:** *"We're not going to run the ball until we STOP the run. You can't be in the fourth quarter chasing points and expect to be a good run team. So you need ATTEMPTS."* And: *"Teams can play that much man against us because we're not a threat running the football right now."*

**So it's a loop.** No run threat means man coverage all day. Man coverage all day means no separation. No separation means third downs and throwing. **Throwing means no carries. No carries means no run threat.**

**And Wednesday he named the place he's going to break it: "Quit throwing it so much."**

He also doubled down on the same diagnosis when asked about the run-stopping struggles:

**"It's tackling, it's ALIGNMENT, it's ASSIGNMENT. It's just playing a better brand of football and getting back to how we STARTED this season."**

Raider Nation, **"getting back to how we started this season"** — remember what that looked like? **Three straight wins and TEN takeaways in the first three games.** This team has forced **one** in the four losses since. **He's not asking for a new identity. He's asking for the old one back.**

## AND HE REFUSED TO SELL US A SILVER BULLET

I respect this answer a great deal.

Asked whether, evaluating the last four games, there's **one big overall thing** he wants to work on this week, Kubiak would not take the easy exit:

**"There's not one big thing. There's plenty of things we're working on that we're being intentional about. I WISH it was just one thing, but it never is."**

And then he said something that tells you how a professional staff actually operates:

**"Whether you're on a 3-4 team or you're on an undefeated team, you're always improving your weaknesses and always trying to get players better — and get the young players better who are going to play one day. That's part of the job: preparing for one week and preparing for the future all in the same day."**

Raider Nation, **that's the honest answer and it's the unsatisfying one.** There is no single fix. **There's tackling, alignment, assignment, run blocking, separation, slow starts and timeouts** — and all of it has to improve at once while you also develop the number one overall pick for a future that hasn't arrived yet.

**Preparing for one week and the future in the same day. That's the job.**

## THE THORNTON UPDATE — AND IT'S THE MOST HONEST ONE YET

Raider Nation, we've been tracking this activation for three weeks, and today Kubiak finally told us the actual obstacle.

Asked about Thornton's performance in practice: **"Dont'e's really working hard to come back. He's COMPETING FOR A JOB."**

And then the question — what does he need to do to take the next step? **And here's the answer:**

**"He's got to take the spot of another guy. So he's got to go beat out somebody."**

**There it is.** That's why **"Yeah, yes"** became **"We are not"** became **"that's a decision we don't have to make till Wednesday."**

**It was never purely medical.** A 53-man roster is a zero-sum game. **To activate Dont'e Thornton Jr., somebody else loses their spot** — and this staff isn't going to take a job away from a man who's been doing it just because a former draft pick is healthy again.

**And honestly? I respect it, even though I've been begging for the activation.** This is the same philosophy we heard about Dylan Laube: **provide value, wear a lot of hats, be someone people can count on, and the staff finds a place for you.** Nobody here gets handed anything. **Thornton has to take it.**

## JACK BECH IS PROBABLY PLAYING SUNDAY — AND HEAR WHY THAT MATTERS

Now the news that genuinely excited me.

Asked about the potential of getting **Jack Bech** back this week and what difference he could make in the run game as a blocker, Kubiak didn't start with the football:

**"I would just start with the ENERGY that he brings to our team. His love of the game. His work habits. He just has a whole other AURA about him when he's on the field, and his teammates feel it. Coaches feel it."**

**Then the football: "He's a really solid run blocker. He's got strong hands. Really happy to have him back there."**

**And the question Raider Nation wanted: is there a chance he's ready Sunday?**

**"I DO."**

Raider Nation, **a receiver who can block, on a team whose head coach just said "quit throwing it so much."** Think about the fit. **Perimeter blocking is how outside runs become explosive runs.** A receiver who will dig out a corner or seal a safety turns a four-yard run into a twelve-yard run — **exactly the margin Kubiak needs if this offense is going to commit to the ground.**

**And "aura" is a word coaches don't use lightly in Week 8 of a losing streak.**

## AND HE PROTECTED THE GUYS WHO'VE BEEN DOING IT

This is the part that shows you the character of this staff.

When the reporter framed Bech as maybe the best run blocker among the receivers, Kubiak agreed — **"that's a great point, I think he has a lot of value"** — **and then immediately defended the men who've been doing that job without him:**

**"But I would also say that the receivers who have been blocking before, they've been doing a really good job. Coach is as good as it gets with every detail of the game, especially the run game, and his guys are extremely well prepared."**

Raider Nation, **that's a head coach refusing to let a good-news story become an insult to the guys in the room.** Tre Tucker, Jalen Nailor, Cody White have been blocking their tails off in an offense that can't separate. **He made sure that got said.**

## THE ACCOUNTABILITY ANSWER — AND IT'S THE TOUGHEST THING HE'S SAID

After Sunday's loss, players in that locker room were talking about accountability — not just for themselves, but holding **each other** accountable. Asked how they'd responded after a day of practice, Kubiak gave an answer with some real steel in it:

**"That's the NFL. Every week you have to respond, and you've got to do it 17 weeks in a row. And you CAN'T SIT IN THOSE CHAIRS in those team meeting rooms if you're not an accountable human. If you're just a talented guy that does not have accountability, you don't last very long. And those are the guys we're looking for as we build our program."**

Raider Nation, **read that again, because that's a warning shot delivered with a smile.**

**"You can't sit in those chairs if you're not an accountable human."**

**That's the clearest statement of this regime's roster philosophy we've gotten.** It explains the sixteen rookies Spytek brought in — men Kubiak described as **"not only good players, but team-oriented individuals, guys that are about the Raider way."** It explains why Isaiah Pola-Mao stayed a leader after losing his job. **It explains "personnel and scheme."**

**Talent alone does not keep a chair in that room.**

## AND MICHAEL MAYER SAID SOMETHING THAT GOT TO HIM

A reporter relayed that **Michael Mayer talked last week about how badly he wants to win for Kubiak and this staff, and how much respect the players have for them.** What does it mean that that mentality survives a four-game losing streak?

**Kubiak's answer was beautifully undramatic: "Well, we're 3-4 and I've got a lot of season to play. That's nice of Mike to say — but shoot, we're RIDING THIS THING."**

**"We're riding this thing."** Raider Nation, that's not a man polishing his résumé. **That's a coach who intends to be here for a long time and isn't interested in being sympathized with.**

And on Mayer specifically — remember what Andrew Janocko told us: **"Mike sits in the FRONT ROW of the meeting rooms."** Of course he's the one saying it out loud.

## CONSISTENCY, AND THE QB SNEAK THAT CONVERTED

Asked to describe Kirk Cousins' demeanor and leadership through a losing streak, Kubiak went to the one trait that matters most:

**"Knowing him as long as I have — whether he's on a hot streak or on a losing streak, HE'S THE SAME. That's what you want from your leaders. You want consistency."**

**And then a fun one with real meaning.** A reporter noted they had Cousins do a **short-yardage sneak** this week and asked what gives him confidence Kirk can move the chains that way.

**Kubiak's entire answer: "Yeah. Converted."**

**That's it. "Converted."** Raider Nation, I love that. **No philosophy, no explanation. It worked.**

And remember the context — **two weeks ago a reporter asked whether Fernando Mendoza should get short-yardage snaps because of what he can do with the ball in his hands.** Kubiak called it **"a great point"** and **"definitely a valid point,"** but worried about a quarterback **"coming out off the field cold on the most important down of the drive."**

**So what did he do instead? He had his 38-year-old veteran run the sneak. And it converted.** That's the answer to a question we asked on this channel, delivered on a practice field.

And on what gives him confidence in Cousins going forward: **"I've seen the production. The production's there. But it's a team game — we've got to produce TOGETHER in order to win football games."** **Fourteen touchdown passes, tied for the NFL lead. That's production.**

## NOW THE JETS — AND THIS IS NOT A GIFT

Raider Nation, do not walk into Sunday thinking this is easy. **The Raiders are going ON THE ROAD to New York.**

**Kubiak on the Jets: "An extremely physical team. A very talented team. They have our FULL ATTENTION. They're very well coached."** And the discipline of a good staff: **no matter who we're playing, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday we better make sure we're focusing on ourselves and getting ourselves better.**

**And then the coaches' scouting report, which should worry you.**

**"Another top-ten defense. I think they're FIFTH IN THE LEAGUE IN YARDS AGAINST THE PASS, top ten overall. They're doing a great job forcing fumbles."**

Raider Nation, **stop and put that next to the problem this offense has.** This team cannot separate against man coverage, cannot run the ball, and has to throw it too much. **And it's walking into the fifth-best pass defense in football, on the road, against a team that takes the ball away.**

**That is the worst possible matchup for our worst possible weakness.**

**And there's real respect for the opposing head coach.** Our staff has **played against Aaron Glenn's teams going back to Detroit** — **"he did a great job there"** — and faced them last season while with Atlanta. **"A lot of respect for him and for their team and the scheme and the players. I know a few guys in that defense. Good challenge for us. And we've got to be able to go on the road and find a way to get a win."**

**He also refused to chase a stat.** Told the Jets have a documented weakness in the last two minutes of the first half, Kubiak brushed it aside: **"We're just trying to get ourselves better. Someone else's stats — you can identify things, but you've got to make sure you're worrying about yourself."**

**A 3-4 team doesn't get to scheme around somebody else's flaw. It fixes its own.**

## AND NOW KIRK COUSINS, WHO WAS THE CALMEST MAN IN NEVADA

Raider Nation, Cousins took the podium and gave a masterclass in professional indifference, and I mean that as the highest compliment.

Asked what it meant that Kubiak came out on Monday, before any questions, and declared him the quarterback:

**"I don't know. I just go out and do what my coach tells me and just go play. I don't have a big thought on it."**

And asked how he handles the narratives that inevitably come with having the **number one overall pick as his backup**:

**"Just go to work and stay the course. Nothing really changes."**

**That's it.** No drama, no gratitude theater, no shot at anybody. **A 38-year-old man who has heard every version of this conversation for a decade.** This is exactly what Kubiak meant by **"whether he's on a hot streak or a losing streak, he's the same."**

## HIS ANSWER ON HOW DEFENSES ARE PLAYING US WAS SMARTER THAN MINE

Asked whether he's seen a difference in how teams game-plan against the Raiders over these last four weeks, Cousins pushed back gently:

**"No, I wouldn't say that. Each team has had maybe a little bit different approach — each team has had their own flavor or wrinkle of how they wanted to defend us. Some of that is maybe specific to how they felt they want to defend us, but a lot of it is also their PERSONNEL and what they feel comfortable doing and how they feel they're built. So it isn't just us. Sometimes the team has to feel comfortable with what they're doing."**

Raider Nation, **that's a genuinely important correction to something I've said on this channel.** I told you the whole league was going to copy the man-coverage blueprint. **Cousins is saying it's not that simple: Kansas City, New England, Buffalo and the Rams all have personnel built to play man.** They didn't play man because of a scouting report on us — **they played man because that's who they are.**

**Which means the Jets might do something completely different.** And it means this offense's problem isn't only a league-wide conspiracy — **it's four straight opponents who happened to be built to exploit it.**

## AND HIS PHILOSOPHY ON IDENTITY VS ADJUSTMENT IS THE BEST THING IN THIS VIDEO

Asked — as a man who's been in this league a long time — when you know it's time to make adjustments versus getting back to your identity, Cousins gave the kind of answer you only get from twelve years of Sundays:

**"I don't think there's any science or formula. You never really know until you look back on the outcome and how it went. You've got to go with your gut, go with what you feel is best. And it's NEVER one or the other. I think THE EXTREMES ARE WHERE YOU CAN GET OUT OF WHACK."**

**"It's trying to find a balance of: we've got to stick to who we are, our identity, what we're comfortable with, what we OWN — not be too complicated. That's an important part of any offense being successful. But I think you also have to say, we need to have WRINKLES. We need change-ups. We need to throw curveballs. We need to keep the defense guessing. So you do BOTH. And finding that middle balance is the healthiest place to be."**

Raider Nation, **that is the answer to the argument this entire fan base has been having for a month.** Half of us want a total overhaul. The other half wants them to go back to what worked in September. **And the quarterback just told us the truth: the extremes are where you lose.**

**And when they asked what he wants the identity of this offense to be, he listed it:**

**"Disciplined. Run a great operation. Protect the football. Be explosive. Playmakers. How you get there can vary week to week — but no matter what, you want to be an offense that's explosive and can beat people a VARIETY of ways and be very multiple. Especially on a four-game losing streak."**

**Disciplined. Great operation. Protect the ball. Explosive. Multiple.** Raider Nation, **look at that list against what we've watched: seven penalties for 62 yards, a wasted timeout after a fake punt, 12 men on defense, an interception to a linebacker, and an offense that can only win one way.**

**He just described the exact opposite of the last four weeks. And he did it on purpose.**

## "TOUGH TIMES DON'T LAST, TOUGH PEOPLE DO"

Two last answers, and they're the heart of it.

Asked about accountability across position groups — he was once seen coaching up a defensive player in training camp — Cousins said:

**"Everybody's coachable. Guys have good habits. I don't think you get to this level unless you have good habits and are coachable and communicate well and listen well. Guys are coming into work every day ready to get better, and today was no different."**

And then a reporter brought up his own line — **"tough times don't last, tough people do"** — and asked whether he's learning that this is that type of locker room.

**"I think so. The game of football will WEED OUT people who aren't resilient. You've got a lot of years of Pop Warner and middle school and high school and college to weed people out. You get to the NFL, it's kind of the toughest of the toughest. So there are a lot of tough-minded guys in that locker room."**

Raider Nation, **that's your quarterback telling you that everybody in that building has already survived twenty years of cuts.** Four losses in October is not the hardest thing any of those men have been through. **Not even close.**

## AND THE DEADLINE LINE YOU SHOULDN'T SKIP

One last thing. A reporter noted there were **some trades around the league yesterday** and asked how active the Raiders anticipate being, one way or the other.

**Kubiak: "Yeah, we're talking EVERY DAY about those things. Spytek and his staff do a great job. And that's every day in the NFL — especially this close to the deadline, you're talking about MANY SCENARIOS."**

**"Many scenarios."** Raider Nation, that's not a denial and it's not a promise. **It's a front office doing its homework with twelve days left.** And after the Rams loss, Kubiak said the quiet part out loud himself: **"personnel, scheme — we've got to talk about all of it."**

**Beat the Jets and they're 4-4 into a bye week, and Spytek shops for help. Lose, and they're 3-5, and the scenarios get a lot colder.**
