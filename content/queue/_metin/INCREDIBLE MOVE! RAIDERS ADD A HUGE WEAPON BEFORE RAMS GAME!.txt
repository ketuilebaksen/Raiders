## OKAY. NOW THE UNCOMFORTABLE PART.

Here's the question somebody put to me this week, and I want to put it to you: **if the Raiders moved one of two offensive linemen before the Rams game, which one would surprise you MORE?**

**Name one: Kolton Miller.**

And Raider Nation, I'll be honest — this conversation makes me uncomfortable, and it should make you uncomfortable too.

**Kolton Miller has been here since the Oakland days.** He has played for different offensive line coaches, different head coaches, different offensive coordinators and a parade of quarterbacks. **He has been the one stable thing on this offensive line for the better part of a decade.**

**That history matters.** When you've watched a man handle that much change and keep showing up at left tackle, you don't casually say "let's move on." That's not how you treat a professional and it's not how you build a locker room.

**But I'm not going to pretend the conversation doesn't exist, because there are real reasons it's being had.**

**Reason one: there's a report worth noting.** A report out of The Athletic indicated that **Charles Grant took at least one starting rep at left tackle during warm-ups before the Buffalo game.** Now — **I am not treating one warm-up rep as a decision.** It isn't. It proves nothing. **But it is a reason to talk about who's standing behind Miller**, and that's a fair conversation.

**Reason two: the draft investment is real.** **Charles Grant was a third-round pick in 2025. Trey Zuhn came in as a third-round pick in 2026.** Those are not camp bodies. Those are players this organization spent real capital on and needs to develop. **If DJ Glaze stays on the right side, could Grant or Zuhn eventually be the answer on the left?**

**That's a fair question — and here's the honest answer: having young players available and knowing they're ready to start are two completely different things.** I've watched too many teams confuse the two and ruin a season over it.

**Reason three: the depth just changed.** On Monday the Raiders **signed tackles Amari Kight and Darian Kinnard to the practice squad and released Evan Neal.** Kight is 25 and **played under Kubiak in Seattle**, so he already speaks the language. Kinnard is 26 and **has been on three Super Bowl championship teams.**

**Does that prove the Raiders are preparing to trade Kolton Miller? No. Absolutely not.** Teams churn practice squads every single week — they're probably just improving depth. **But when you put those additions next to the young tackles already on this roster, you can see why the question comes up.**

**Reason four: age and health.** **Miller is 31.** That doesn't mean he can't play — he can, and he does. **It means a front office making decisions about the next three seasons has to ask how long they expect him to hold that position and who takes over when he doesn't.**

Let me plant my flag, because I don't like fence-sitting.

**I am not dismissing years of work because we're frustrated after a few losses.** That is exactly the kind of panic that turns a 3-3 team into a 4-13 team.

**And if you believe Kolton Miller is still your best option at left tackle — and I think he probably is — then trading him creates an immediate problem on Sunday.** You don't get to hope somebody figures it out. **Protecting the quarterback has to be part of the decision.** You can't hand a 38-year-old quarterback a brand new blind side in week seven and call it a plan.

Now, the money. **Here's where I'll be careful: I'm not going to assume he's demanding a new deal, and I'm not going to assume the front office has already decided against paying him down the road. **So what offer would get my attention?** This is me hypothesizing, nothing more. **A third and a fourth would make me stop and think. A second-round pick would make the decision genuinely hard.** Those are offers I'd listen to — **not confirmed bids, and no guarantee anybody would ever pay them.**

**Jacksonville is a team I'd look at as a theoretical fit** — again, my read of a depth chart, not a reported buyer. And remember: **you can make a trade sound brilliant from our side and still have the other team simply say no.**

**For me, the whole thing comes down to one question: do Klint Kubiak, Rick Dennison and Andrew Janocko trust the younger tackles to handle bigger roles?** If the answer is no, **I'm not moving Kolton Miller just because somebody waves a pick at me.** Glaze, Grant and Zuhn don't have to match everything Miller does — **but the staff has to believe it can put a dependable group on the field.** Otherwise you've manufactured a weakness and asked your offense to work around it every single Sunday.

**And this offense cannot afford another weakness.** Remember the zero-sack paradox we broke down this week: **41 attempts and nobody touched Cousins — but a chunk of that protection came from quick throws, play action and tight ends chipping.** This line is being helped. Take away your best tackle and that help stops being enough.

## NAME TWO: JACKSON POWERS-JOHNSON — AND THIS ONE BOTHERS ME DIFFERENTLY

Raider Nation, this is the one that actually hurts.

**JPJ is a player I've wanted to see become a long-term piece of this offensive line.** I love the physical edge he brings. You can hear him finish blocks. **But his role is changing, and I'm starting to wonder where he fits in this staff's plans.**

**Against Buffalo, Caleb Rogers started ahead of him. And JPJ took zero offensive snaps.** Zero. Rogers played every single one.

**And I'll admit something: that surprised me.** Going into that game I expected changes on the offensive line — **but I thought Spencer Burford might be the one to lose his spot.** The staff went in a completely different direction.

**And credit where it's due: I thought Burford responded well.** He gave me more reason to believe he can help this group than I had walking into that game. **Look, if we're going to criticize this staff when a decision blows up, we have to be willing to give them credit when we like the result.** That's the deal.

**But one lineup decision doesn't explain everything about Jackson Powers-Johnson's future.**

Let me be very clear about what I will NOT do here. **I don't know what's happening behind closed doors, so I'm not going to question his effort, and I'm not going to invent a story about teammates being unhappy with him.** That's garbage and this channel doesn't do it.

**What genuinely concerns me is whether these coaches see him as somebody they want to build around.**

And here's the uncomfortable structural fact: **he wasn't drafted by Klint Kubiak or John Spytek.** That doesn't mean they can't value him. It definitely doesn't mean a trade is inevitable. **But a new staff makes its own decisions about where players fit, and being a high pick under the previous regime doesn't guarantee you the same role under this one.** That's just how this league works.

**And there is real talent here.** **Jackson Powers-Johnson won the Rimington Trophy as the best center in college football.** That doesn't guarantee NFL success — plenty of Rimington winners never stuck. **But it's a very good reason not to be in a rush to give up on him.** Before I moved a young offensive lineman, I'd want to feel confident we'd actually given him a fair shot to find his place.

**Now here's the squeeze he's in.** **Tyler Linderbaum's arrival changed the entire picture at center.** The Raiders also added **Spencer Burford**, drafted **Trey Zuhn**, and brought in **Bryce Cabeldue.** That is a pile of competition across the interior.

**None of those moves proves this staff wants JPJ gone.** But stacked together, they make his long-term role a fair question.

**And I'm not turning a concern into a guaranteed trade.** Maybe he earns his way back into a bigger role — Kubiak said this week that **guys get disappointed, but you keep competing, you get another chance, and you make sure you're ready when it comes. "That's what a professional does."** Maybe the lineup changes again. Maybe the Raiders decide his value as interior depth beats anything another team offers. **Those possibilities are all still live.**

**But — and this is the part most trade videos skip — you cannot judge a player-for-player trade without knowing who's coming back.** I would not move Jackson Powers-Johnson **just because the return happens to be a receiver.** That receiver would need **a clear role in this offense and would have to make it genuinely better.** A name is not a plan. ## THE RISK NOBODY TALKS ABOUT: DEPTH DISAPPEARS IN ONE SNAP

Now let me play the other side hard, because I think this is the most important thing in the video.

**Offensive line depth vanishes faster than anything else in football.**

**One injury turns a backup into a man you need for every single snap.** That's not theory — we watched it happen to this team last season.

**Move Kolton Miller and you are asking a young tackle to handle the hardest job on the offense, in the middle of a season, against pass rushers who get paid to end careers.**

**Move Jackson Powers-Johnson and you have fewer answers inside** — and this interior has already given us Burford's five-pressure afternoon, a false start on fourth-and-one at the Buffalo 13, and a center who's drawn a flag in that same situation three separate times.

**And here's the deeper point: the Raiders need the players around Tyler Linderbaum to work well TOGETHER.** You can invest heavily at center and still struggle if the four men beside him can't communicate, can't pass off a twist, can't handle a late safety rotation. **Offensive line play is five men speaking one language. Every trade restarts that conversation.**

**I want to give Klint Kubiak a line he can build an offense around. Constant change makes that harder, not easier.**

So **before the Rams game, I'd be careful.** Kubiak said this week the line is going to **"keep competing"** — nothing locked, nobody safe. **Fine. Compete.** But I'd rather fix this group than keep shuffling it.
