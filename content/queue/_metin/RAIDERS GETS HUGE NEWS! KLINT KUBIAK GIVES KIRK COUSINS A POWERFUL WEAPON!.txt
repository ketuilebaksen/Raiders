## AND HERE IS WHY A SLOT RECEIVER SPECIFICALLY FIXES THIS OFFENSE'S DISEASE

Raider Nation, I want to go deeper on this, because "we need a receiver" has become a slogan on this channel and I owe you the actual football reason a man like Josh Downs is the right KIND of receiver.

**Remember the diagnosis from the film breakdown after the Rams game.** National analysts put it plainly: **teams are playing man-to-man against the Raiders and nobody is winning.** And they said the hardest part out loud — **"it's NOT the play design."** Kubiak's own answer completed the loop: **"Teams can play that much man against you because we're not a threat running the football."**

**So what actually beats man coverage?**

**Separation at the line and separation at the top of the route. And the slot is where that happens.**

Here's why, and it's pure geometry. **A receiver lined up outside has the sideline as a defender** — he can only break one direction without running out of real estate. **A slot receiver has grass on BOTH sides of him.** He can break in, he can break out, he can run a seam, he can sit in the hole. **A defender covering him man-to-man has to guess, and he has no help from the boundary.**

**That's why slot production is the single most reliable man-coverage beater in football.** And **Josh Downs is second in the NFL in slot yardage since he was drafted, behind only Amon-Ra St. Brown.**

**And now look at what this specific offense already does.** The analysts praised one thing about Kubiak's design: **the Raiders get into BUNCH FORMATIONS, because Kubiak knows teams want to play man against him — and the hardest thing about playing man is defending a bunch.** Receivers stacked tight, crossing each other, defenders having to fight through traffic.

**Who lives inside a bunch formation? The slot receiver.**

Raider Nation, **that's the fit.** This offense is already running the scheme that creates slot separation. **It just doesn't have the man who finishes it.** Tre Tucker is a vertical speed threat. Brock Bowers is a tight end being asked to do a receiver's job. **Nobody is winning the inside release on third-and-seven.**

**Put a top-two slot producer in that bunch, and Kirk Cousins — who's tied for the league lead with 16 touchdown passes and ranks third with 1,877 yards — finally has the one throw this offense can't make right now: the quick, open, in-rhythm completion against man coverage.**

**And that's also how you get the run game back.** Not with a bigger guard. **With a defense that can no longer leave eight men in the box because the slot will eat them alive.**
