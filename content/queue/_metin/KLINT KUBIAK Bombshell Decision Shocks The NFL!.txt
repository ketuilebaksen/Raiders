## THE BOMBSHELL: TREY ZUHN III STARTED AT RIGHT GUARD

Here's the decision, and it's the payoff of a three-week story we tracked every single day on this channel.

**Asked about Trey Zuhn at right guard, Kubiak said: "I have to watch the tape. But I was EXCITED for Trey to get that opportunity."**

And then — **unprompted — he said the thing that tells you why this locker room didn't fracture during a four-game losing streak:**

**"I also want to give a ton of credit to CALEB and to JPJ. Those guys are GREAT TEAMMATES. They continue to practice and get better, and it's going to take all of us to get through the whole season. They really SUPPORTED Trey — and that just speaks to their CHARACTER."**

Raider Nation, **stop and absorb that.**

**Caleb Rogers lost his starting job. Jackson Powers-Johnson — a former second-round pick and a Rimington Trophy winner — has now been passed by two different men at the same position.** And on the day a rookie took their snaps in a must-win game, **both of them helped him get ready.**

**And look at how long we watched this come together.** **"Yes"** when asked if Zuhn would get guard reps. **"We're going to keep shaking it up until we find the right mix."** **"He's a Swiss Army knife — he can play tackle, guard, center. He might be able to play quarterback, I don't know, I haven't let him try yet."** **"Trey had an injury a few weeks ago, he's battled back through it."** **"Could be."** First-team reps on Wednesday.

**And Sunday he started. And the Raiders won.**

## AND HE OWNED THE BACKFIELD DECISION AGAIN — WITH MORE DETAIL THIS TIME

Asked the reason behind Ashton Jeanty having only a few carries, Kubiak didn't dodge an inch:

**"That's my call, and Ashton's still going to be a huge part of our team. He had a HUGE CATCH. Huge catch. He's going to be a huge part of our RUN GAME going forward. We just wanted to give Mike a few more opportunities today. And I think BOTH of those guys are going to be a huge part of our rushing AND passing success moving forward."**

**And then the follow-up that mattered most: did Jeanty's lack of carries have anything to do with Kubiak's confidence in him?**

**"NO, IT DID NOT. IT DID NOT."**

Raider Nation, **he said it twice.** And he'd already said the pregame comments about the Jets' tackling weren't a factor either. **So that's two separate theories the head coach has personally shot down: it wasn't punishment, and it wasn't a loss of faith.**

**"He's going to be a huge part of our RUN GAME going forward."** That's not a man being phased out. **That's a man being rested and redeployed.**

## AND ON THE SPLIT GOING FORWARD, HE GAVE US THE KEY PHRASE

A reporter laid out the pattern perfectly: **Mike on early downs, Ashton on third down and passing situations.** Does that trend continue, or is it week to week based on matchup?

**Kubiak: "I think we just got to look at the film. And we know we have TWO RUNNERS THAT WE BELIEVE IN."**

**Two runners that we believe in.** Raider Nation, **that's the answer to the whole controversy.** Not a demotion. Not a competition with a loser. **A division of labor.**

**And on Washington specifically: "Mike did a good job. Ran hard. I think we took a STEP FORWARD in that aspect of our game — and it's something that's NOT A FINISHED PRODUCT."**

**"Not a finished product."** Raider Nation, I respect that enormously, because **25 carries for 83 yards is 3.3 a crack**, and Jeanty entered the game at 3.1. **The head coach is not pretending a different back solved the run game. He's saying the APPROACH took a step forward.** And that's true — it did. **The volume and the rhythm were the win, not the average.**

## AND THE OTHER DECISION THAT WON THE GAME: THE RED ZONE

Raider Nation, this is my favorite part of the entire breakdown, because it's a problem we identified on this channel last week and the Raiders fixed it in seven days.

**Remember the play that bothered me most against the Rams?** Third quarter, **fourth-and-two at the Rams' 13-yard line**, and the pass went to Cody White and fell incomplete. I said then: *"I don't know if Brock was open. I do know I'd rather give Kirk more ways to reach our best guys."*

**Well, the film panel found the answer, and it's even more specific than I realized.**

**"We had a similar play to this LAST WEEK, where we have Brock Bowers on a SLOT FADE — and we DON'T THROW IT, because we think he's covered."**

**They had the shot. They passed on it because he looked covered.**

**And this week? "Now we have him on a CORNER ROUTE."**

**Here's how it works, and it's beautiful.** **"This is what the TWO TIGHT END SETS do. You've got a DB on him. You've got a DB on BROCK."** When you put two tight ends on the field in the red zone, the defense has to answer with smaller, quicker bodies — **and now a defensive back is responsible for the best tight end in football.**

**"The PHYSICALITY that Brock plays with at the top — the DBs CAN'T DEAL WITH THAT."**

**And then the throw: "Kirk does a very good job of placing this ball where ONLY BROCK CAN TRACK IT. You see Brock's eyes, you see the DB's HEAD TURN. This is only where Brock Bowers can track it."**

**And the summary is the best football sentence of the week:**

**"It's just a BIG BOY FOOTBALL PLAY. Ball in the air, go get ball. One hand, two hands, one finger, two fingers — it don't matter. BROCK IS GOING TO COME DOWN WITH IT."**

**"And touchdowns in the red zone are bigger than field goals, for sure."**

Raider Nation, **THAT is the decision that shocked me.** Last week, in a one-score game, this staff looked at Bowers in the red zone and decided he was covered. **This week they decided covered doesn't matter.**

**Throw it to the best player. Let him go get it. That's the whole fix.**

## AND THEN THEY USED THE PASS GAME TO UNLOCK THE RUN — FINALLY

Raider Nation, this is the complementary football we've been begging for, and the film shows it clearly.

**"Once you set up the pass game — look at that. You've got Nailor up there, we've got our tight ends MOVING, we're moving people out of the way, because you've got to DEAL with these tight ends in the pass game."**

**"They've got THREE of them over here over TWO. And we've got NUMBERS IN THE RUN GAME."**

**Read that again, because it's the thesis of this entire channel, inverted and finally working.**

**For eight weeks, defenses have put eight and nine men in the box because nobody scared them.** **On Sunday, the Raiders used Bowers and Mayer as pass-game threats to pull defenders OUT of the box — and then ran into the numbers advantage that created.**

**"This is COMPLEMENTARY FOOTBALL on the offensive side of the ball, strictly because of what Brock brings and what Mayer brings. And now you get WASHINGTON COMING DOWNHILL AT YOU. Big time plays."**

**That's how you unstack a box without a trade. You do it with your tight ends.**

## AND THE THREE-TIGHT-END PLAY THAT PROTECTED COUSINS' BIGGEST WEAKNESS

One more design note, and I love the honesty in it.

**"Three tight ends. We're going to SEAL THE EDGE — because we know he don't run a 4.5 or nothing like that. So we've got to make sure our quarterback's SAFE getting out. But he goes through his progression, and you've got BROCK LEAKING OUT LATE with the creativity offensively. Once again — this is a touchdown in the red zone."**

Raider Nation, **that's a coaching staff designing around a limitation instead of pretending it isn't there.** Kirk Cousins is 38 and he is not escaping anybody. **So you line up three tight ends, you seal the edge so nobody touches him, you move the pocket on purpose, and you let Bowers leak out late while every defender is looking at the line of scrimmage.**

**Two red zone touchdowns designed specifically to turn field goals into seven. And last week they went 1-for-5 down there.**

## NOW THE TIGHT ENDS' DAY — AND KUBIAK GAVE THE CREDIT AWAY

**Brock Bowers and Michael Mayer combined for 12 CATCHES and roughly 130 YARDS.**

**Twelve catches between two tight ends.**

And look where Kubiak sent the credit: **"I give the credit to THOSE GUYS. Brock, Mike — IAN is fantastic. Carter Renan on the practice squad."** And then he went further: **"They have a TREMENDOUS COACH in Luke Steckel — one of the most detailed, prepared individuals. He deserves a TON of credit."**

Raider Nation, **that's the second time this month this head coach has publicly handed credit to an assistant** — he did it with Rick Dennison and the offensive line staff for banking cross-training reps in training camp, and he did it with Joe DeCamillis on special teams. **Head coaches who share credit get believed when they take blame. That's why "that's my call" lands.**

**And the best quote of the entire press conference came when he was asked whether the ridiculous things Bowers does at the catch point ever surprise him:**

**"Surprised me when he DOESN'T catch it. You know, that's the truth."**

**Perfect. Nothing to add.**

## AND GIVE JALEN NAILOR HIS MOMENT

Asked how pleased he was with Nailor, Kubiak lit up:

Raider Nation, this is the receiver Kubiak defended on Friday when the whole country said this offense can't separate. He said: **Nailor IS getting open down the field, it's "time on task."**

## AND DYLAN LAUBE HAD THREE MONSTROUS PLAYS — AT HOME

A reporter listed it: **Laube had THREE monstrous plays — forcing the ball out of bounds, a tackle, and the kick return.**

**"Dylan's a STUD player for us. He continues to make plays — and as you continue to make plays, the expectations continue to raise. And HE'S EARNED THAT."**

**Earned. That's the word.** Two-time captain. Four core special teams units. **And now a 248-game drought broken in front of his own people.**

## NOW THE HALFTIME STORY — AND THE NUMBER THAT DEFINES THIS TEAM

Asked what happened in a defensive first half and what adjustments were made, Kubiak was blunt:

**"They ran the ball — they ran the ball VERY WELL on us. And halftime-wise, our guys were NOT HAPPY. We made some adjustments, and the guys just PLAYED HARDER. We got an interception. We forced them into two punts. And it's a FOUR-QUARTER GAME — we talked about that last night. Our guys kept battling."**

**And now here are the numbers the panel put on the screen, and Raider Nation, they are stunning:**

**THE RAIDERS GAVE UP 140 RUSHING YARDS IN THE FIRST HALF.**

**AND 26 IN THE SECOND HALF.**

**One hundred and forty, then twenty-six.**

**And before you explain it away, the panel closed that door: it wasn't that the Raiders got so far ahead that the Jets had to abandon the run. "They COULD HAVE run the ball a little bit more."**

**So the adjustment was real. It was coaching. It worked.**

## BUT HERE IS THE QUESTION THAT HAS TO BE ASKED

And the panel asked it, and it's the right question:

**"Why aren't they doing that EARLIER? If they know how to adjust midway through the game — and this has been EVERY SINGLE GAME now — why can't they make the adjustments during the week and come out stronger early? They're giving up 146 RUSHING YARDS A GAME. One of the worst in the NFL."**

**One hundred and forty-six a game.** Raider Nation, that number keeps creeping up — **143.6 last week, 146 now** — and it's the single biggest thing standing between this team and the playoffs.

**And Kubiak didn't hide from it. Asked why run defense keeps being an issue to START games:**

**"It's a part of our team that we HAVE TO ADDRESS. We have to continue to find ways to improve that, because that's HURTING US. It hurt us in the first half. And that's up to US AS COACHES to put our heads together and get it fixed."**

**"That's up to us as coaches."** Six straight weeks of this man taking the bullet.

## AND THE PANEL'S EXPLANATION IS THE BEST FOOTBALL ANALYSIS I'VE HEARD ALL YEAR

Raider Nation, listen closely, because this finally explains WHY the first half is always worse.

**"You're not able to do it at the beginning of the game, because the score is even or you're behind. So the opponent is FREE to run the football. They can bring in that big package and you get MAN-TO-MAN ONE-ON-ONE. And for the most part, we've had real issues DEFEATING ONE-ON-ONE if you're an interior lineman."**

**There it is.** When the score is even, the other team can line up heavy and simply ask: **can your defensive tackle beat my guard, one man against one man?** **And for eight weeks, the answer has been no.**

**And here's why halftime changes everything:**

**"What happens with this football team after halftime — that's why it's so critical and important that after halftime you come out, you GET A DRIVE, YOU SCORE, and now you make the opposing offense have to score TOO. So now they're NOT FOCUSED ON THE RUN so much. So it's complementary."**

Raider Nation, **do you see what just happened on Sunday?** **The Raiders scored on their opening drive of the second half for the FIRST TIME ALL SEASON.** **They scored 14 points in the third quarter after managing three points across their previous four third quarters combined.**

**And the Jets' rushing yards collapsed from 140 to 26.**

**Those two facts are the same fact.** You don't stop the run with a scheme adjustment. **You stop it by scoring, so the other team has to throw.** **That's what Kubiak has been saying for a month: "We're not going to run the ball until we stop the run" — and the reverse is just as true.**

## AND THEN THE PANEL GAVE US THE SIMPLEST STANDARD IN FOOTBALL

**"But it really comes down to ONE-ON-ONE MATCHUPS all over the football field. When you've lost three or four games in a row, the coach says: we're going to keep it simple. And you come out game after game, you watch the film, and — DID YOU WIN YOUR ONE-ON-ONE MATCHUP? Yes coach, or no coach. And that's what it's about right now."**

**"Yes coach, or no coach."**

Raider Nation, **that is the entire season in four words.** And look at how many voices have now independently landed there: **Kubiak said the leaks are "tackling, alignment, assignment."** **Quay Walker said it starts with grown men defeating blocks and winning individual matchups.** **Nico Dean said "we can't all be individual mercenaries."** **A broadcast analyst said nobody trusts his brother on the A gap.** **Cousins' coordinator said the offense has to win one-on-one against man.**

**And the final prescription: "Offensively you can help the defense by scoring — but it ultimately comes down to our INTERIOR GUYS needing to do a better job of beating their one-on-one blocks and KEEPING THOSE INTERIOR GUYS OFF OUR LINEBACKERS."**

**Keep the interior linemen off the linebackers.** That's it. **That's the whole run defense.** When a guard climbs to the second level untouched, your linebacker is blocked before he can read the play. **When the defensive tackle holds his ground, the linebacker runs free and makes the tackle.**

**And that is exactly why DeForest Buckner — six sacks, 20 pressures, roughly $6.5 million against the cap — matters more than any receiver on that trade board.**

## THE THIRD DOWN ANSWER, AND THE "DREAM SCENARIO"

Asked what clicked on third down, Kubiak refused to take any of it himself:

**"It's TEAM. It's protection. It's halfbacks in protection. It's receivers getting open. It's Kirk doing a great job on third down of DISTRIBUTING the football."**

Then the coaching nugget: **"Usually when you're good on third down, your third downs aren't all super long. I don't know what they were — but when you're keeping them INSIDE OF SIX YARDS, that allows you to be more successful."**

**Inside of six yards. That's the number.** Third-and-four is a football play. **Third-and-eleven is a prayer.**

**And then the best strategic sentence of the day:**

**"The DREAM SCENARIO is NOT TO GET THE THIRD DOWN. Be a better FIRST AND SECOND DOWN team. And that's going to continue to help us when we run the football better. A step in the right direction — but a LEAP that we need to make."**

Raider Nation, **"a step in the right direction, but a LEAP that we need to make."** That is the most honest self-assessment of a winning team I've heard this season. **He won by five on the road and refused to call it a leap.**

## AND HIS ANSWER ON ROOKIE MISTAKES TELLS YOU WHO HE IS

Asked whether winning makes it easier to teach rookies through their mistakes, Kubiak rejected the premise entirely:

**"Whether we WIN or LOSE, it should be THE SAME. We should treat it the same way. And I think our guys know that's where our heart is when we're talking to them every week — because it's a HARD LEAGUE, and you've got to get the problems FIXED FAST."**

**Same standard, win or lose.** Raider Nation, that's the philosophy that produced **"you can't sit in those chairs if you're not an accountable human"** and **"when you're fighting for playing time, there is no better motivator."**

**This staff does not grade on the scoreboard. They grade on the tape.**

## SO WHERE ARE WE?

**Four and four. The streak is dead. And the trade deadline falls AFTER the San Francisco game.**

**Here's what did not get fixed:** **146 rushing yards allowed per game, one of the worst in football. 33 points allowed — the fifth straight game over 30 by my count. A first half where the Jets ran it at will. And interior linemen who are losing one-on-one.**

**Because "yes coach or no coach" is a standard, not a solution. At some point you have to go get a man who answers yes.**
