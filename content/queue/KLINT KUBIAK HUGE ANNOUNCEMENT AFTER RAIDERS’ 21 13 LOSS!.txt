# KLINT KUBIAK'S HUGE ANNOUNCEMENT AFTER THE 21-13 LOSS — BROCK BOWERS IS DAY-TO-DAY 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, Klint Kubiak opened that Monday press conference with the news this entire fan base has been holding its breath for since Sunday afternoon.

**"For BROCK BOWERS — he still has that ANKLE INJURY. We're still EVALUATING him. HE'S GOING TO BE DAY-TO-DAY."**

**"IT'S NOT AS BAD AS WE THOUGHT. But it STILL IS A SIGNIFICANT INJURY. So we'll keep MONITORING him."**

**And then, asked if there's a chance Brock could be available THIS WEEK: "DAY-TO-DAY. We'll keep going EACH DAY."**

**Raider Nation, that's the announcement. NOT AS BAD AS WE THOUGHT. Day-to-day. Not ruled out for this week.**

**For a man who has 658 receiving yards in seven games — the most of any tight end in the league — who has seven straight games over seventy yards, and who soaks up TWENTY-SEVEN PERCENT of this offense's targets, "not as bad as we thought" is the best sentence we could have heard this morning.**

**But Raider Nation, he also said the words "STILL A SIGNIFICANT INJURY." So nobody should be celebrating. We wait. We monitor. We don't speculate — and I'm not going to put a timeline on another man's ankle.**

Plus: the two off-script plays that lost this football game. The "safe place to ditch the ball" that Kirk Cousins knew and Fernando Mendoza doesn't yet. The right guard decision. The viral Aidan O'Connell moment. And Kubiak's answer about whether he's okay with his rookie calling it **"a dark day."**

## FIRST: HIS HONEST SUMMARY OF A 21-13 LOSS

Raider Nation, here's how he framed the whole game, and it's a genuinely balanced accounting.

**THE POSITIVES: "Obviously when you PROTECT THE FOOTBALL and you have NO PENALTIES — that GIVES YOU A CHANCE TO WIN THE GAME."**

**Raider Nation, stop right there and appreciate that.**

**ZERO TURNOVERS. ZERO PENALTIES.**

**One week after FIVE TURNOVERS in a single half in San Francisco — five! — this offense played a clean, disciplined football game against the best defense in the National Football League with a 22-year-old making his first career start.**

**No giveaways. No flags. Raider Nation, that is a REAL achievement and almost nobody is going to write about it.**

**THE NEGATIVES, and he named exactly two: "Just a LACK OF OFFENSIVE PRODUCTION IN THE SECOND HALF. And really TWO BIG OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS that we gave up DEFENSIVELY FOR TWO TOUCHDOWNS that really hurt us."**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S THE GAME. Right there. Two plays.**

**Twenty-one points. Two of their touchdowns came on OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS — meaning plays that broke down and a quarterback improvised. Not scheme. Not getting gashed on the ground. Two broken plays that turned into fourteen points.**

**Take those two snaps away and we're talking about a win.**

**And remember Kubiak's own answer on Jaxon Smith-Njigba's second half: "I thought they made some GREAT OFF-SCRIPT PLAYS. That was a BIG PART OF IT."** **Same diagnosis, two days running. It wasn't the game plan. It was two moments of chaos.**

## AND HIS VERDICT ON THE QUARTERBACK

Raider Nation, he put it on the record plainly.

**"I thought the QUARTERBACK SHOWED POISE in his first start — that was TO BE EXPECTED. SHOWED PLAYMAKING ABILITY. And he'll CONTINUE TO GROW from that game."**

**And later, even more directly: "I thought he PLAYED WELL ENOUGH FOR US TO WIN — ESPECIALLY IN THAT FIRST HALF. AND WE DIDN'T CLOSE IT OUT IN THE SECOND HALF."**

**HE PLAYED WELL ENOUGH FOR US TO WIN.**

Raider Nation, **that's the head coach saying the rookie wasn't the reason. "We didn't close it out" — WE. Not him.**

**And "showed poise — THAT WAS TO BE EXPECTED." He said the same thing Sunday: "That's NOT A SURPRISE. THAT'S WHO HE IS."**

**Six sacks, a 0-for-7 second half on third down, his best weapon gone at halftime, Mike Macdonald playing chess with his protection checks — and his head coach was not surprised that he kept his head up. That tells you what they saw in him since April.**

## AND NOW THE HONEST TRUTH ABOUT LOSING BOWERS

Raider Nation, asked how much the offense — and particularly Fernando's first start — was affected when Bowers went out:

**"When you LOSE ONE OF YOUR BEST PLAYERS, I think THAT AFFECTS THINGS."**

**"BUT — there's still LOTS OF PLAYMAKERS ON THE FIELD. There's MORE PRODUCTION TO BE HAD than what we did in that second half."**

Raider Nation, **I love that he gave the honest half AND the accountable half in the same breath.**

**Yes, losing a tight end with 658 yards in seven games wrecks a game plan. Especially for a rookie quarterback who needed him as a safety valve on third down.**

**But he refused to hide behind it. "There's more production to be had." That's not an excuse factory. That's a man who already knows he has to scheme somebody else open.**

**And he said it again when asked if the plan got stagnant: "I LOOK AT MYSELF. I have to do a MUCH BETTER JOB of MOVING THE BALL and SPREADING THE BALL AROUND."**

## AND HERE IS THE MOST IMPORTANT FOOTBALL ANSWER OF THE DAY

Raider Nation, this is the explanation of the entire second half, and it's a lesson in how football actually works.

Asked why the play-action game disappeared after the start — the same thing we saw against Kansas City:

**"This game in particular was VERY UNIQUE in the fact that it was FIRST, SECOND, THIRD DOWN, PUNT — FOR FOUR OR FIVE SERIES IN A ROW."**

**"If you're NOT GOOD ON FIRST DOWN, you put yourself in a DROP BACK POSITION on SECOND AND LONG. And then you're THIRD AND LONG and a DROP BACK POSITION."**

**"So when you're NOT HAVING SUCCESS ON FIRST DOWN — on FIRST AND TEN — I think THAT AFFECTS IT A LOT."**

Raider Nation, **three-and-out, three-and-out, three-and-out, three-and-out. FOUR OR FIVE SERIES IN A ROW.**

**And here's the chain reaction, and I want you to really understand it because it explains this entire season:**

**Play action REQUIRES a credible run threat and a manageable down. You can't fake a handoff on third and eleven — nobody in the stadium believes you, least of all a linebacker. So the second this offense falls behind the sticks on first down, half of the playbook becomes unusable.**

**And then what's left? DROP BACK PASSING. Against the best defense since 1999, playing SHELL COVERAGE, with DB BLITZES coming from angles a rookie has never seen.**

**That's SIX SACKS. That's 0-FOR-7. That's four or five straight punts.**

**Raider Nation, this is why I have been screaming about FIRST DOWN YARDAGE for weeks. It's not a detail. It is THE WHOLE THING. Four yards on first down and this offense gets to be itself. One yard and it's a drop-back-only offense against Mike Macdonald.**

**And the run game: 2.9 yards a carry on the season — the worst in the league.**

## AND THE SIX SACKS — HE SPLIT IT INTO THREE PARTS

Raider Nation, this is the fairest breakdown of a sack total I've ever heard a coach give.

Asked if he was concerned about the number of hits Mendoza took — as sacks, in the pocket, and running:

**"It WASN'T UP TO OUR STANDARD. For a quarterback, hits and sacks — THAT'S AN ENTIRE OFFENSIVE STAT."**

**"That's GETTING OPEN FAST. That's PROTECTING BETTER. And that's HIM GETTING RID OF THE BALL. THERE'S THREE THINGS THERE."**

Raider Nation, **three things: the receivers, the line, and the quarterback.**

**That's exactly right, and it's exactly why I don't want anybody scapegoating this offensive line today. A sack is almost never one man's failure. If nobody uncovers in two seconds, if the protection slides the wrong way, and if the quarterback doesn't pull the trigger — all three happened, and the quarterback gets buried.**

**And remember Mendoza's own version from Sunday: "A LOT OF THE SACKS TODAY — IF NOT ALL OF THEM — WERE ON ME. NOT ANY ON THE OFFENSIVE LINE. I thought they PLAYED GREAT."**

**The coach says it's three things. The quarterback says it's all him. Nobody in that building is pointing sideways. Ten games and it hasn't happened once.**

## AND THEN KUBIAK SAID THE MOST REVEALING THING ABOUT KIRK COUSINS

Raider Nation, this is the answer that explains the gap between zero sacks and six sacks in consecutive weeks.

Asked whether the tape exposed more issues on the line, given that Cousins was hardly ever sacked:

**"I'll LET YOU GUYS BE THE JUDGE of that. I think there IS AN OBVIOUS ANSWER — that a VETERAN QUARTERBACK KNOWS THE SAFE PLACE TO DITCH THE BALL."**

**"There's a part of his game that FERNANDO WILL LEARN."**

**"However — I still think a SIX SACK GAME IS NOT GOING TO WIN GAMES."**

**THE SAFE PLACE TO DITCH THE BALL.**

Raider Nation, **that is the single best explanation of the Cousins-to-Mendoza transition anybody has given.**

**Think about what that phrase means. A twelve-year veteran doesn't just know WHEN to throw it away. He knows WHERE — the spot at the receiver's back shoulder, or three yards in front of the checkdown, or into the ground at a lineman's feet — where the ball is dead, nobody gets hurt, no flag gets thrown, and you live to play the next down.**

**That's not talent. That's a THOUSAND REPS of accumulated scar tissue.**

**And Raider Nation, this answers the question I've asked twice on this channel: how much of that ZERO-SACK game against Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner was the offensive line, and how much was Kirk Cousins' internal clock?**

**The head coach just told us: a lot of it was the clock. There IS an obvious answer, he said. He let us be the judge, and the judge agrees.**

**And asked directly if that's still the biggest area of growth: "I THINK THAT IS A BIG AREA. YES. He's going to be GROWING IN ALL PHASES — and THAT ONE FOR SURE."**

## AND THE COACHING POINTS WHEN MENDOZA BECOMES A RUNNER

Raider Nation, this answer is going to define his rookie season.

**"Well — HE REMAINS A PASSER, NUMBER ONE. KEEP YOUR EYES DOWNFIELD."**

**"REMAIN A PASSER like you did to JACK BECH on that LEFT SIDELINE. Like he did to IAN, early in the game, GETTING THROUGH HIS PROGRESSION."**

**"Remain a passer. And then OBVIOUSLY PROTECT YOURSELF when you start to run down the field."**

Raider Nation, **"REMAIN A PASSER" — he said it THREE TIMES in one answer. That's a coaching point being hammered into a young man's skull.**

**And notice he gave him CREDIT with examples: the throw to Jack Bech on the left sideline, and the early throw to Ian Thomas where he worked THROUGH HIS PROGRESSION.**

**Raider Nation, that's not a scolding. That's "you already did it right twice — do it more."**

**And "PROTECT YOURSELF" — that's the third time in a week he's said it. Friday it was "we need you available for the WHOLE SEASON. You're NOT TRYING TO SELL OUT FOR A YARD ON FIRST AND TEN."**

**And Raider Nation, remember WHY that matters so much: this roster still has TWO QUARTERBACKS. Mendoza, Aidan O'Connell, and a Plan C the head coach won't describe.**

**And Mendoza himself said the same thing Sunday: "I NEED TO STAND IN THERE MORE. I WAS A LITTLE SPED UP. There were some times I should have STAYED IN THERE, STEPPED UP WITH ONE HITCH, AND DELIVERED A STRIKE — rather than ALREADY LOOKING TO SCRAMBLE."**

**One hitch. Then throw. The coach and the quarterback agree on the fix.**

## AND HE WAS GENUINELY IMPRESSED WITH SOMETHING NOBODY EXPECTED

Raider Nation, this is the part that should make you optimistic, and it surprised me.

Asked about Mendoza saying Mike Macdonald was playing chess with him and fooling him on his checks — what did you make of his operation at the line of scrimmage?

**"In his first start, I was VERY PLEASED with HIM GETTING OUT OF THE HUDDLE, him MAKING THE CORRECT CHECKS, THE CORRECT CANS."**

**"That's what he has SHOWN IN PRACTICE — his ability to do that and to be able to RUN THE SHOW that way."**

**"There's ROOM FOR GROWTH. But in his first start, I thought HE OPERATED THE GAME LIKE HE DOES IN PRACTICE."**

Raider Nation, **"HE OPERATED THE GAME LIKE HE DOES IN PRACTICE."**

**That is the single most important sentence of this entire press conference, and here's why.**

**Everything this organization said about Fernando Mendoza before Sunday was based on PRACTICE. Spytek: "he performed at a level that was IMPOSSIBLE TO IGNORE at practice." Kubiak: "it's CONSISTENCY — there's not ONE MOMENT, and that's a good thing." And on Friday: "usually it's those EMOTIONALLY STABLE INDIVIDUALS who are able to TREAT PRACTICE AND THE GAMES THE SAME WAY."**

**And the single biggest fear with any rookie is that the practice version doesn't show up on Sunday.**

**IT SHOWED UP. The head coach just confirmed the translation happened.**

**And the mechanical stuff — getting in and out of the huddle, making the right CANS and checks at the line against the best defense in football — that's the hard part. That's the part that makes rookies look lost on national television.**

**He didn't look lost. He got fooled by a man who's now beaten THREE STRAIGHT first overall picks. That's different.**

## AND THE RIGHT GUARD DECISION — TREY ZUHN STAYS

Raider Nation, this is real roster news.

Asked about right guard after reviewing the film — whether there are opportunities for other guys or whether it's simply going to be the same:

**"I LIKE WHERE TREY'S AT RIGHT NOW. TREY ZUHN AT RIGHT GUARD. YES."**

**TREY ZUHN III KEEPS THE JOB.**

Raider Nation, **that's the answer I wanted. Because the thing this offensive line has NEVER had is CONTINUITY.**

**JPJ was listed there. Zuhn played it against San Francisco. Then the concussion. Then Caleb Rogers moved in. Then Zuhn cleared protocol and started. And now he keeps it.**

**Raider Nation, a run game is built on semi-telepathy between a guard and a tackle. You cannot develop that with a new combination every Sunday. Same man, next week. Good.**

**And then he gave the honest assessment of the whole line, and it's the fairest thing he said:**

**"Overall as an offensive line, there's a SOLID FIRST HALF OF RUN GAME PRODUCTION — followed by a VERY POOR HALF OF PROTECTION."**

**"AND THAT'S NOT ALL ON THEM. That's on GETTING OPEN. That's on GETTING RID OF THE BALL. That's on PLAY CALLS. IT'S A TEAM THING."**

**"SO WE WIN TOGETHER AND WE LOSE TOGETHER."**

**Raider Nation, WE WIN TOGETHER AND WE LOSE TOGETHER. That's the thumb-pointing again. "That's on PLAY CALLS" — he put his own call sheet in the list of culprits.**

**Solid first-half run game. Poor second-half protection. Same explanation as everything else: good half, bad half, lose.**

## AND HE ADMITTED HE SHOULD HAVE USED TRE TUCKER MORE

Raider Nation, this is a concrete admission and it points to Sunday's fix.

Asked about the times Tre Tucker was open for explosives but Mendoza didn't have time to throw:

**"I think they're going to have a GREAT CONNECTION."**

**"I think I COULD HAVE DONE A BETTER JOB OF GETTING TREY MORE INVOLVED YESTERDAY."**

**And on the broader issue of explosive plays in general — he acknowledged it's a problem.**

Raider Nation, **that's the head coach saying he underused his fastest receiver in a game where the deep ball was taken away by shell coverage.**

**And remember what I said after San Francisco: the way you get Tre Tucker room is to make a defense spend an extra body on Brock Bowers and then take what's left over. Without Bowers in the second half, there WAS no extra body to pull. Tucker got covered straight up by the best secondary in football.**

**So this is the real question for this week: if Bowers is day-to-day and maybe out, who draws the extra defender? Because without an answer to that, Tre Tucker doesn't get open and we're running drop-back offense into shell coverage again.**

## AND THE AIDAN O'CONNELL MOMENT WENT VIRAL — AND IT DESERVED TO

Raider Nation, this is my favorite thing to come out of this loss.

A reporter brought up the **viral moment of AIDAN O'CONNELL ENCOURAGING FERNANDO MENDOZA throughout the game — even despite the loss.**

**And Kubiak said: "AIDAN'S BEEN ONE OF THE ELITE TEAMMATES ON OUR ROSTER. THAT HASN'T CHANGED SINCE APRIL."**

**"So I just see Aidan as HIM. That's WHO HE IS. He's a GREAT TEAMMATE. He's an EXCELLENT PLAYER."**

Raider Nation, **think about Aidan O'Connell's situation for one second.**

**He was in that quarterback room when it had three men and a culture. Then Kirk Cousins got traded. He got jumped by the first overall pick. And now he is ONE SNAP from the field with NOBODY dressed behind him.**

**And he spent an entire losing afternoon on that sideline coaching up the 22-year-old who passed him. On camera. Where everybody could see it.**

**And Mendoza named him unprompted in his own press conference: "AIDAN'S BEEN EXTREMELY HELPFUL, especially as HE'S GONE THROUGH A SIMILAR EXPERIENCE — teaching me and helping me with the ropes."**

**Raider Nation, Mendoza has now mentioned Aidan O'Connell in EVERY SINGLE press conference he's given. Every one.**

**"ONE OF THE ELITE TEAMMATES ON OUR ROSTER. THAT HASN'T CHANGED SINCE APRIL." Give that man his flowers.**

## AND THE "DARK DAY" QUESTION WAS HANDLED PERFECTLY

Raider Nation, this was the most delicate question of the day and I thought he answered it beautifully.

The question: Fernando called it **"a dark day"** right after the loss. When you have a young man who's that much of a competitor, are you OKAY with that — or do you have to talk to him about how he approaches things?

**"Obviously we're going to TALK TO HIM ABOUT HIS APPROACH WITH EVERYTHING — with how he TALKS ABOUT THE GAME WITH THE MEDIA."**

**"But the MOST IMPORTANT THING is we're talking about HOW HE'S PLAYING THE GAME. And we'll COACH UP EVERYTHING."**

**"But like I told Fernando — I WAS PLEASED, because of THE PREPARATION HE PUT IN, and THE THINGS HE DID IN PRACTICE. THEY SHOWED UP IN THE GAME. And he's going to have A LOT OF THINGS TO LEARN AND GROW FROM."**

Raider Nation, **look at the order of his priorities there.**

**Yes — we'll coach him on how he talks publicly. A 22-year-old calling his first NFL start "a dark day" on a podium is the kind of thing that becomes a headline and follows a young man around.**

**But he immediately said the most important thing is HOW HE'S PLAYING. And then he told us what he told the kid privately: I WAS PLEASED.**

**He told him in that room that he was pleased, before he told any of us. That matters.**

**And Raider Nation, I understand both sides. On one hand, you want a competitor who HATES losing — remember the kid also said "it's GOOD TO GET PUNCHED IN THE FACE," and "I'm the FUTURE HOPEFUL LEADER of this offense," refusing to even claim the job. And remember when the fans stayed seated to honor his mother, he said: "A DARK DAY THAT BRINGS A SMILE TO MY FACE."**

**On the other hand, "dark day" after game ONE of a career is a lot of weight to put on your own shoulders. And Kirk Cousins' entire farewell speech to that locker room was about this exact thing: "COMPARISON IS THE THIEF OF JOY. This league can steal your joy really quick if you let it. DON'T LET THIS LEAGUE STEAL YOUR JOY."**

**Raider Nation, I hope somebody plays that tape back for Fernando Mendoza this week.**

**And when asked if the kid learned more about himself playing an elite defense: "ABSOLUTELY. YES."**

## AND NOW THE DEFENSE — AND ROB LEONARD GOT REAL CREDIT

Raider Nation, this is the headline nobody is covering and it deserves to be.

Asked where he saw the defense take a step forward:

**"I just thought THE WAY THAT WE STARTED THE GAME."**

**"I thought ROB did a REALLY GOOD JOB THIS WEEK of GETTING OUR GUYS READY TO PLAY, GETTING THEM READY TO START FAST, GETTING TURNOVERS, EXECUTING THE GAME PLAN."**

**"There's PLENTY OF THINGS WE HAVE TO DO BETTER. But overall, as a defense, I SAW US PLAY BETTER FOOTBALL."**

Raider Nation, **GETTING THEM READY TO START FAST.**

**That is the exact thing that has been broken all season. Kubiak said it after San Francisco: "the FRUSTRATING PART is just THE WAY WE STARTED THE GAME. Can't start that SLUGGISH." And the play count was 38 to 17 in the first half.**

**And Rob Leonard told us Thursday exactly what he was going to fix: "Especially to START THE GAME, we've got to START FASTER. And that's REALLY ALL ABOUT US." And: "SENSE OF URGENCY TO GET LINED UP. PLAYING WITH PHYSICALITY. EVERYBODY RUN TO THE BALL. AND IT'LL HAPPEN."**

**HE SAID IT ON THURSDAY AND HIS UNIT DID IT ON SUNDAY.**

**Twenty-one points allowed. Two takeaways. A fast start. Against the defending world champions.**

**Raider Nation, this is the unit that was the FIFTH TEAM IN NFL HISTORY to allow thirty-plus in six straight games. The streak is over. It ended against the champions. And it ended in the week when the entire football world wanted the coordinator fired.**

**"EXECUTION FUELS EMOTION." He was right.**

## AND HIS ANSWER TO THE LAST QUESTION TOLD YOU EVERYTHING

Raider Nation, a reporter asked a genuinely kind question at the end: you led that offense to a Super Bowl last year, you know 21 points is above your threshold — but looking at what your defense just did, can't you take some comfort in that improvement against the world champions?

**"Well — like I said, WE'RE SUPPORTING OUR COACHES, WE'RE SUPPORTING OUR PLAYERS, AND WE'RE FOCUSED ON GROWTH AND WE'RE FOCUSED ON GETTING BETTER."**

Raider Nation, **he would not take the compliment.**

**He was handed a chance to say "see, I was right to keep Rob Leonard, I told you all so" — after a week of people demanding a head on a platter.**

**And he said: we're supporting our coaches, we're supporting our players, focused on growth.**

**No victory lap. No I-told-you-so. The same four sentences he's been saying since September.**

**Raider Nation, THAT'S why that locker room plays for him.**

## SO WHERE DOES THIS LEAVE US?

**4-6. Twenty-one points allowed and only thirteen scored. Zero turnovers, zero penalties. Six sacks. Four or five straight three-and-outs. Two off-script plays that became two touchdowns. And Brock Bowers DAY-TO-DAY with an ankle that's NOT AS BAD AS THEY THOUGHT.**

**Denver next. Then Cleveland. Then the Week 13 bye.**

**Raider Nation, here's the honest ledger from this press conference:**

**THE DEFENSE TOOK A STEP FORWARD AND BROKE THE WORST STREAK IN FRANCHISE HISTORY. The offense protected the football and took no penalties. A rookie quarterback OPERATED THE GAME LIKE HE DOES IN PRACTICE, made the correct checks at the line, and PLAYED WELL ENOUGH FOR US TO WIN. Trey Zuhn III keeps the right guard job. Aidan O'Connell is one of the elite teammates on the roster. And Brock Bowers might play this week.**

**AND WE STILL LOST, because first down went nowhere four or five series in a row, a veteran's knowledge of where to ditch the ball went to Minnesota, and two broken plays turned into fourteen points.**

**Six sacks won't win games. He said it himself. But this was the closest this team has come to playing complementary football all season — and it came against the champions, with a kid taking his first snap.**
