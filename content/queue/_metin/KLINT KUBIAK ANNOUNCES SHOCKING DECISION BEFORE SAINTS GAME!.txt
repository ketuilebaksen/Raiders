THE HISTORIC START NOBODY EXPECTED

First, understand what this offense has already done, because it's the reason the whole league is suddenly telling us to add to it.

Two games, at least 26 points in each. And for the first time since 2017, the Silver and Black have opened a season scoring 26 or more in consecutive games. Kirk Cousins is the first Raiders quarterback since Kenny Stabler to throw three touchdowns in each of the first two games. The Raiders are averaging 26.5 points a game.

Without Brock Bowers. Without a true number one receiver. With a run game that produced 2.3 yards a carry last Sunday.

That's the argument for adding a weapon: if this is the floor, what's the ceiling?

THE DREAM WR FILES, RANKED BY PRICE

So let's put the three names on the table, with the price and my verdict on each, because we've done the full breakdown on all of them.

Justin Jefferson. The best receiver alive, 27 years old, four-time All-Pro. Reported cost: multiple first-round picks, on a contract averaging about $35 million a year, against a cap where this team had roughly $8.26 million of room. Verdict: you make the call, you listen, and you almost certainly hang up. The dream is real, the math isn't.

Marvin Harrison Jr. The fourth overall pick from 2024, 24 years old, one catch for 33 yards in two games this season. Reported cost: possibly a third-round pick, because Arizona might not be able to get a second. Verdict: at a third, I take that swing. Klint Kubiak helped Jaxon Smith-Njigba make a major leap in Seattle, and a 24-year-old with that pedigree is worth a rotational-player price.

Jerry Jeudy. A Pro Bowler with a 1,229-yard season on his résumé, now squeezed out in Cleveland behind two young draft picks. Reported cost: a conditional 2027 fourth that becomes a third if he plays 65 percent of the snaps. Verdict: yes, and it's the safest bet of the three. Kubiak also knows him from Denver in 2022.

That's the honest board. One dream, two realistic swings, and a deadline on November 10th.

THE WEAPON THAT'S ACTUALLY ARRIVING

But here's why I titled this the way I did, and I mean it.

You want the scariest offense in the NFL? Start by giving this one its best player back.

Brock Bowers had a procedure on his knee five days before the season. He missed both wins. He went through pregame warmups at SoFi, and Kubiak said afterward that he's close. Now picture the group that's waiting for him.

Tre Tucker just went for five catches, 119 yards and a 40-yard touchdown, and he did it by running past an NFL secondary rather than finding a blown coverage. Cody White caught two touchdowns on seven snaps, both on third down. Michael Mayer blocks like a sixth offensive lineman and was just extended for three years and $45 million. Jack Bech works the slot. Ashton Jeanty caught six passes for 45 yards and two touchdowns in Week One out of the backfield.

Add Bowers to that and a defensive coordinator has a genuine problem: you cannot double the tight end, bracket Tucker, keep a linebacker on Jeanty, and still have enough bodies to stop the run. Somebody gets left alone. That's what "scariest offense" actually means. Not one superstar. Too many answers.

KIRK MORRISON'S INSIDE VIEW

Now the part of this week I enjoyed most. Kirk Morrison, who played nearly a decade at linebacker in this league and now works as the Raiders' radio color analyst, went on a national show and gave real insight into what's happening in this building.

His first point was about stability. This organization has cycled through head coaches and general managers for years, and he said it finally feels like there's stability since the Kubiak hire. Free agency, the draft, taking Fernando, adding quality players, a good training camp. They expected improvement on three wins. They just didn't expect it this fast.

Then he addressed the quarterback question directly, and I want you to hear this because it settles the debate in the comments. Cousins understands the arrangement completely. The job was his because he earned it, and Mendoza is the quarterback of the future. There's no looking over the shoulder. And as Morrison put it, if Cousins keeps winning, he's not giving the football up.

THE MENDOZA DETAIL I CAN'T STOP THINKING ABOUT

And then Morrison shared something I've never heard about any backup quarterback in this league.

Fernando Mendoza wears his helmet on the sideline during games.

Not for show. He wears it so he can hear the play call going into Cousins on the field. Then he recites that play call back to quarterbacks coach Tim Sullivan, and talks through what he expects to happen and what he's seeing from the defense. Every series. In real time.

Morrison said in all his years playing and covering this league, he's never seen a backup quarterback do that. It's essentially a live film session happening on the sideline while the game is being played.

Raider Nation, that's the most encouraging thing I've heard about this franchise's future all month. The number one overall pick isn't sulking because he isn't starting. He's treating every snap of Cousins' season as his own education. That's how a quarterback gets ready.

And there's a support system around him. Morrison said Tom Brady is a phone call away for everybody in the organization: Kubiak, Spytek, who was his college teammate at Michigan, Cousins, Mendoza, anybody. He'll give you his opinion, and the whole quarterback situation has been handled collaboratively.

THE OPPONENT: THE SECOND-BEST PASSING OFFENSE IN FOOTBALL

Okay, enough good feelings. Here's the test, and it's a serious one.

The Saints are 1-1, but that record is a lie. New Orleans enters this week with the second-ranked passing offense in the NFL, averaging 305.5 passing yards a game.

Tyler Shough leads the entire league with 662 passing yards. Four touchdowns. He's completing 68.9 percent of his throws. This is a quarterback playing at a high level, and the Raiders have to respect him.

His main weapon is Chris Olave, who already has 18 receptions for 268 yards and a touchdown. Those 18 catches are second in the NFL, behind only Amon-Ra St. Brown. So this isn't a passing game searching for an identity. It's one that knows exactly what it wants to do.

And this is the second straight road trip for Las Vegas. New Orleans is playing its home opener, and when that team is playing well, that building is one of the loudest in football.

THE NUMBERS THAT TELL THE REAL STORY

Now let me show you the comparison that explains this matchup better than anything else.

Total yards per game: the Raiders average 297.5, the Saints 383.5. Through the air: Las Vegas 204, New Orleans 305.5. Those are big gaps, and they don't favor us.

But look at the scoreboard. Points per game: Raiders 26.5, Saints 27.0.

Half a point. New Orleans is piling up yards. Las Vegas is turning fewer yards into nearly identical points. That's efficiency, red-zone execution, and a defense that produces short fields with takeaways. Three takeaways against the Chargers. Two interceptions from Hezekiah Masses. A forced fumble from Nakobe Dean that Jeremy Chinn scooped up.

That's the actual identity of this football team: win the margins. And it's why yardage rankings don't scare me the way they used to.

THE FOURTH QUARTER PROBLEM

Here's the number from this week that worries me most, and it isn't about yards.

Going back to Week 10 of last season, only one quarterback in the entire NFL has more fourth-quarter touchdowns than Tyler Shough. One. Josh Allen has eleven; Shough has four in that span.

That tells you exactly who New Orleans is. They fell into a 21-point hole against Detroit and nearly came all the way back. They went to Baltimore, fell behind, and won the game. They play their best football in the second half, and their quarterback finishes games.

So the Raiders can't just win three quarters here. If we're up ten going into the fourth, that's when this opponent is most dangerous, in front of their own crowd, with a quarterback who has been closing games since last November.

KUBIAK VERSUS HIS OLD OFFENSE

There's one more layer: Klint Kubiak spent the 2024 season as the Saints' offensive coordinator. He described that offense this week as explosive and well-coached with a lot of playmakers, many of whom he knows very well, and said the Raiders have their work cut out for them.

That knowledge cuts both ways. He knows their people and their tendencies. They know how he thinks. Kellen Moore has that team playing with real belief, and they arguably feel like they should be undefeated after how the Detroit and Baltimore games went.

Also, on the other sideline of last week's game, some context that makes our win look better rather than worse: the Chargers lost their starting center to a knee injury, their interior line has been a problem, their number one receiver was banged up, their first-round receiver hasn't lived up to that billing, and they've had multiple tight ends on injured reserve. Justin Herbert has been carrying a lot. That's a team with real problems, and we beat them on the road.

THE NATIONAL VERDICT: "NOT THE SAME OLD RAIDERS"

The panels are still split on us, and I'll give it to you straight.

One analyst said the Raiders are for real in the sense that other coaching staffs now think, "we'll probably win, but these guys are going to be a pain for four quarters." He added that he doesn't think we make the playoffs or win the division, but that this is a respectable football team, and he summed it up in a line I liked: these are not the same old Raiders.

Then came the honest follow-up question that I want every one of you to remember. If the Raiders go into New Orleans and beat a team that everybody on that panel picked to win the NFC South, at what point do they have to acknowledge this is a quality football team? One of them admitted the logic doesn't hold: you can't call the Saints great and then refuse to give credit to the team that beats them.

Their answer was Week Four. Kansas City at home. Then the Patriots, the Bills, and the Rams. That four-game stretch is where the league decides who we are.

But the door starts on Sunday.

MY PICK

So here's the plan. Win the first quarter against a team that struggles to start games. Attack their backup left tackle and the protection slides it creates. Keep feeding Jeanty even when the first carries are ugly, because abandoning the run is how Baltimore lost this exact game. Protect the middle of the field with whoever plays safety, because Shough completed all eight of his passes for 118 yards and a touchdown when safeties were the primary coverage defenders in Baltimore. And use Bowers to force decisions.

I'm staying where I was: Raiders 27, Saints 23. And I'm telling you now, the fourth quarter is going to be uncomfortable.
