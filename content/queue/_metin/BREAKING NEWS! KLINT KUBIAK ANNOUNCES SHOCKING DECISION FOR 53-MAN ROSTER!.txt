NEWS ONE: THE HONEST ROSTER X-RAY

The initial 53 tells us a lot about the team Klint Kubiak is taking into the regular season — and the biggest concern, no surprise, is wide receiver.

We knew this was the weak spot coming into camp. Kubiak himself called the room out publicly after the Houston game, practically daring somebody to step up. And when the roster was finalized, the composition told the story louder than any quote. Dont'e Thornton Jr. made the team despite struggling early in camp, missing roughly a MONTH with injury — including all three preseason games — and coming off an underwhelming rookie year as a 2025 fourth-rounder. Now, you know the follow-up from our recent coverage: days later he moved to injured reserve with his return rights preserved, the chess-move sequencing we broke down. But the fact that he made the initial 53 at all, with zero August tape, tells you exactly how limited the options were. Draft position shouldn't guarantee anybody a roster spot — there simply wasn't enough proven talent pushing him.

Same story with Dererick Young. One catch for ten yards this entire preseason — and he still made the team, because his special teams value carried him. Look, we called that battle with Shedrick Jackson correctly, and Young earned his spot the way fringe players are supposed to. But when a one-catch August wins you a receiver job, that's not a compliment to the player as much as it's a diagnosis of the room. Six receivers on the initial roster, one already on IR, and a head coach on record demanding more. I fully expect the Raiders to keep hunting at this position ALL season — the practice-squad market we've mapped, the trade targets we've profiled, maybe even the big swing. This story is not finished.

THE O'CONNELL MARKET: WHY HE'S STILL HERE

Now, Aidan O'Connell — because his situation is quietly fascinating. Fourth season, contract year, Cousins locked in as the starter, Mendoza anointed as the future. Every arrow pointed toward a trade... and yet he's still a Raider. Why? The honest answer: the preseason killed the market. His August line — 22 of 46 for 283 yards, no passing touchdowns, one rushing score — told the league a muddled story. We watched the full arc up close: the Houston comeback that had Detroit sniffing around and trade value talk everywhere, then the three-for-nine finale that cooled it all off. Meanwhile the QB carousel spun without us — Kyle McCord moving from the Packers to the Dolphins, Quinn Ewers from the Dolphins to the Jaguars — and nobody offered Vegas enough to move Aidan.

And you know what? I've said it since episode one of this channel, and I'll say it again: keeping him might be the RIGHT outcome. If Kirk Cousins gets hurt in October, do you really want to shove Fernando Mendoza into the fire mid-game, off the bench, before his development curve says he's ready? No. You want the proven backup who can operate an NFL offense while the franchise protects its investment. That's what O'Connell is. And here's the kicker — one quarterback injury anywhere in the league changes his trade market overnight. He's simultaneously our insurance policy AND a tradeable asset whose price can spike any Sunday. Holding was correct.

JEANTY: THE THREE-BACK TELL — AND KUBIAK'S SMILE

Now the story every Raiders fan cares about most. When Ashton Jeanty went down on August 23rd, it looked TERRIFYING — couldn't put weight on the leg, teammates in a prayer circle, the whole nightmare. The diagnosis of a sprained ankle was the exhale of the summer. But here's the roster-construction detail that tells you what the team really believes: the Raiders are carrying only THREE running backs — Jeanty, Washington Jr., and Laube. Dare Ogunbowale didn't make the roster. Think about what that means. If the building expected Jeanty to miss significant time, you'd see more insurance at the position — a veteran claim, a fourth body, something. Three backs is a team saying: our guy is coming back, and soon.

And then Kubiak went further. Asked DIRECTLY whether he believed Jeanty would play against Miami... the head coach smiled and said YES. Raider Nation, that smile is the single best piece of news of the entire week. The engine of this offense, the man with 1,321 scrimmage yards as a rookie behind a broken line, on track for opening night. And if the ankle has any late setback? Mike Washington Jr. is expected to start — the fourth-rounder Spytek reportedly pushed HARD to draft, the back who delivered three efficient preseason games in a row. Kubiak was careful not to pile pressure on a rookie who hasn't played a real NFL snap yet, but the staff's trust is obvious, and so is ours: we've been on the Washington train since that 53-yard run in August. Either way, this backfield walks into Week 1 in far better shape than we feared two weeks ago.

THE QUIET WARNINGS: UDFAs, SLOAN, AND THE INTERIOR LINE

A few more x-ray findings worth your attention. First: not a single undrafted rookie made the initial 53. With a ten-player draft class, the math was always brutal — even Brandon Cleveland, the final pick of the class, didn't survive. And of course Sloan, the camp darling, missed out with only four edge spots to give — though he cleared waivers and is back on the practice squad, exactly the outcome we rooted for. This is what a deep, drafted-and-developed roster construction looks like: ruthless at the margins.

Second, and this one concerns me: the interior offensive line. After the Meredith trade, center depth is a genuine question. Jackson Powers-Johnson starts at right guard, where he spent camp — but behind Linderbaum in the middle, the top option is rookie Trey Zuhn III... who logged all of SEVENTEEN preseason snaps at center, per PFF. Seventeen. And then there's Atonio Mafi, who surprised people by making the roster at all: promising run blocker, inconsistent pass protector — Mendoza wore some of those breakdowns in August, and we said then that Mafi's spot was the flexible one. I'd keep both eyes on guard and center over the next month. If the Raiders bring in veteran help early in the season — and the Evan Neal stash suddenly looks even smarter in this light — Mafi could be fighting for his roster life by October. Between the receiver room and the interior line, we know exactly where this roster's fault lines run.

NEWS TWO: COUSINS, OFFICIALLY — AND THE FULL PICTURE OF THE DEAL

And now the second big story: Klint Kubiak has officially named Kirk Cousins the starting quarterback for September 13th against Miami. Expected? Completely — Cousins ran the first team nearly all preseason while Mendoza worked with the twos. But the official word matters, and so does Kubiak's framing: he's happy with all three quarterbacks, and Cousins EARNED the job.

Here's something else that's come into focus: the fuller structure of Cousins' contract. The reporting now describes a two-year framework with $20 million guaranteed — with the Atlanta Falcons still responsible for $8.7 million of his salary this season. Read that carefully, because it changes the interpretation: the Raiders didn't bring Cousins to Vegas as a clipboard-holding mentor. You don't guarantee that money to a mascot. He's here to PLAY — at a discount subsidized by his old team, which is honestly beautiful business. And the résumé he brings into his fifteenth NFL season is that of a genuine professional starter: 88 wins, 77 losses, two ties across Washington, Minnesota, and Atlanta, with a 13-4 Vikings season in 2022 as the peak. None of that guarantees anything in silver and black. But experience plus system familiarity — remember, Kubiak praised how much Cousins' command has grown through their film work — is exactly the stabilizer a rookie-quarterback franchise needs.

So when does Mendoza play? The number one pick, the Heisman, the national title, the preseason flashes — the reason he went first overall is obvious, and so are the rookie inconsistencies. The development challenge gets harder now, because Cousins eats the first-team reps and Mendoza learns from the scout team, staying ready in case his number is called. When reporters pushed Kubiak on whether the rookie plays this season, his answer was seven perfect words: "We'll get to Miami first." And honestly? I LOVE that answer. One game in front of you. No promises, no timelines, no pressure — just Miami. But you know this channel's position, stamped and dated since episode one: the Mendoza era begins before Thanksgiving. Kubiak can take it one week at a time. We'll take it one prediction at a time.

One somber note to close the news: Kieran Crawford's season-ending Achilles is now fully confirmed in Kubiak's words too. The coach praised his work ethic, called his future bright, and expects him in every meeting while he rehabs. Losing a third-rounder who'd worked his way into the rotation still hurts — but the way this organization is keeping him plugged in tells you how they build people, not just rosters.

THE BIG PICTURE: TALENT, YES — DEPTH, WE'LL SEE

Put both stories together and the picture is crystal clear. Cousins leads the offense into Miami. Mendoza gets time to develop, with a smiling coach refusing to rush him. Jeanty is trending toward playing — and the three-back roster construction backs the optimism. Washington has earned real trust. And on the other side of the ledger: the receiver room needs help, the interior line depth is seventeen snaps deep, O'Connell's market never materialized, and the defense moves on without Crawford.

For me, the biggest question of this season was never "do the Raiders have talent?" They do — more than they've had in years. The question is DEPTH: whether this roster can survive the injuries and adversity that hit every NFL team eventually. My wishlist for this season is simple and I'm putting it on the record before kickoff: I want a real offensive identity. I want Cousins executing Kubiak's system cleanly. I want Mendoza developing without being rushed. I want Jeanty healthy and making plays. And I want this front office attacking the obvious weaknesses the moment opportunities appear — because they've shown us all summer that they will. If those boxes get checked, this team has a chance to surprise people. Ignore the warning signs, and 5.5 wins is exactly where the oddsmakers put us for a reason.
