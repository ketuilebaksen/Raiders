# "THAT WAS MY DECISION." KUBIAK OWNS THE LAST-MINUTE LINEUP CALL AFTER A 33-30 HEARTBREAKER 🏴‍☠️

Raider Nation, this one hurts in a completely different way than last week did.

**Buffalo 33, Las Vegas 30.** Three points. Kirk Cousins threw an incredible ball to Tre Tucker at the end and it still wasn't enough, because there were no timeouts left. The Bills walk out 5-1. **And the Raiders are 3-3.**

Then Klint Kubiak walked to the podium and did something I want to start with, because it sets the tone for everything else: a reporter asked him about the last-minute lineup change nobody saw coming — Caleb Rogers starting at guard over Jackson Powers-Johnson — and his entire answer was four words.

**"That was my decision."**

No explanation. No deflection. Four words.

So today we're doing the full press conference: the decision and whether it's permanent, the red zone number he called unacceptable, the pre-snap penalty he admits he has failed three times to fix, the receiver statistic that should end the deadline debate, the player who finally got his chance and delivered, the ugliest moment of the afternoon, and then JJ Watt's genuinely surprising assessment of this defense.

I'm Victor, this is Raiders Tunnel, we're chasing 6,000 believers — hit that like button, drop "I AM A RAIDER" in the comments, and let's get into it.

## THE OPENING STATEMENT: NO MORAL VICTORIES

**Kubiak: "Very disappointed. Very disappointed that we didn't get it done today. Credit to Buffalo, they played a great game. As a team, we did not close the game, and there's plenty of improvement. But we're not satisfied with any moral victories. We're a 3-3 football team with everything ahead of us. Avoided injuries today. So we're moving forward. But we didn't get it done today."**

Raider Nation, that's the right tone and I'll tell you why.

**"We're not satisfied with any moral victories."** Last week this team lost by 28 and he called them sloppy and undeserving. This week they lost by three to a 5-1 team, which is the kind of result a struggling organization is tempted to celebrate. He refused the compliment before anybody could offer it.

And notice the two things he chose to put in his opening statement. **"We did not close the game"** — that's the diagnosis. **"Avoided injuries today"** — that's the one genuine piece of good news, and after a month of this injury report, it matters more than it sounds.

But let's be honest about the record, because this channel doesn't do spin: **this is three consecutive losses.** A team that started 3-0 is now 3-3, and the schedule ahead includes the Rams. Close is not the same as good.

## THE DECISION: ROGERS OVER POWERS-JOHNSON

Now the headline.

**Reporter: "What went into the decision to start Caleb Rogers over Jackson Powers-Johnson today?"**
**Kubiak: "That was my decision."**
**Reporter: "Is that going to be permanent?"**
**Kubiak: "Let's go watch the film and see where we're at."**

Raider Nation, this is the **"new approach"** he promised on Friday, and it arrived without an announcement.

Remember the sequence. He refused to name a starter all week. He said telling someone he's playing "isn't fair to the Raiders or to the players." He said the uncertainty itself helps the team improve "until we kick the ball off." And then he kicked the ball off with Rogers at guard — the same player who replaced Powers-Johnson in Kansas City.

**"That was my decision"** is the most accountable four words available. He didn't say the staff decided, he didn't cite a competition, he didn't hide behind an injury. He put a lineup change on his own name.

And here's the part that makes it interesting: **it may have worked.**

## BECAUSE THE RUN GAME WAS BETTER

**Reporter: "Twice as many pass attempts as rushing attempts in this game. What do you attribute that to?"**
**Kubiak: "Honestly, I'm encouraged — I thought the run game was better. It has not been good enough. And at the end of the game, when it's a game like that, you've got to play to your strengths. So that was what we did."**

**"I'm encouraged. I thought the run game was better."**

Raider Nation, that's the first positive thing this man has said about his running game since Week One. And the pass-heavy split has a defensible explanation this time: in a three-point game in the fourth quarter, you throw. That's not abandoning your identity in the second quarter like New England — that's playing the situation.

I've hammered the run game for three weeks. If a guard change plus a better plan produced genuine improvement, then the "new approach" wasn't talk. **The film will tell us whether Rogers keeps the job, and I'd expect he does.**

## "TWO FOR FIVE IN THE RED ZONE — THAT'S UNACCEPTABLE"

Now the number that actually lost this football game.

**Kubiak: "That was the expectation, to bounce back. That's the type of football we need to play. But we've got to do it for four quarters, and we've got to get the ball in the red zone. We're two for five in the red zone, and that's unacceptable. And that starts with me."**

**Two for five.**

Raider Nation, do the arithmetic, because in a three-point loss it's brutal. Five trips inside the twenty, two touchdowns. If even one of those other three possessions becomes seven points instead of three, this team wins. **One red zone touchdown was the entire margin.**

And this isn't new. Cousins said after the Kansas City loss that the Raiders "kicked too many field goals" and didn't find enough points. The red zone has been a quiet problem for a month and it finally bit in the exact game where it mattered most.

"That starts with me." Again. The thumb, not the finger.

## THE PENALTY HE HAS FAILED TO FIX — AND HE SAYS SO

Then the single most striking exchange of the entire press conference.

**Reporter: "Tyler Linderbaum was called for a false start when you were trying to go for a fourth down, and it seems like that's been happening frequently."**
**Kubiak: "It's happened THREE TIMES and it's killing us. Absolutely killing us. And I have not fixed it. So shame on me. It's on me to get it fixed."**

**"Shame on me."**

Raider Nation, I have never heard a head coach use that phrase about himself in a press conference. And I want to be fair to him and hard on the problem at the same time, because both are warranted.

**Fair to him:** that is total ownership of a repeated, specific, correctable error. He named the frequency, named the consequence, and accepted the blame without a single qualifier.

**Hard on the problem:** a pre-snap penalty on fourth down, three separate times, is the cheapest possible way to lose football games. It costs nothing but focus. And it has now killed three fourth-down attempts — which in a season decided by three-point margins is likely the difference between 3-3 and 5-1.

And it fits the larger pattern, because the reporter in that room had the context ready.

## SEVEN PENALTIES — AND A THREE-WEEK TREND

**Reporter: "Is there any reason why these last three games you've committed far more penalties than you had in your first three? You were playing pretty disciplined football early on."**
**Kubiak: "I've got to go look at everyone individually, but seven penalties is seven too many."**

**"Seven penalties is seven too many."**

Last week it was eight. This week it's seven. And through the first three games, discipline was one of the genuine strengths of this football team — I listed it as a preserved advantage in my Kansas City film review.

Raider Nation, fifteen penalties in two weeks from a team that was among the league's cleanest is not variance. It's a trend, and it's the most fixable thing on this entire list. **You do not need a trade to stop jumping before the snap.**

## THE TURNING POINT HE NAMED HIMSELF

**Reporter: "Can you determine the turning point that frustrates you most?"**
**Kubiak: "I'm really upset with how we're starting the third quarter. Go three and out. That's disappointing. Every game has adversity — the game's not over in the third quarter when you go three and out, but it really kills momentum."**

And this is a pattern worth naming, because it's the third straight week the second half has been the problem.

Against Kansas City, the Raiders came out of halftime, drove into scoring position, made a field goal, erased it with an accepted penalty and got stopped on fourth down. Against New England, the third quarter was when the game disappeared entirely. And now against Buffalo, the first possession after halftime is a three-and-out.

**Whatever is being said in that locker room at halftime is not translating to the first drive after it.** That's a coaching question, and it's one I'd want answered before the Rams.

## THE CHALLENGE, AND AN HONEST ADMISSION

**Reporter: "Can you walk us through the decision to challenge in the third quarter?"**
**Kubiak: "Looking back, for a two or three-yard gain, whatever it would have been — probably not worth it."**

Short, and completely honest. A challenge risks a timeout, and in a game that ended with Cousins throwing without any timeouts left, that resource had real value.

Raider Nation, connect those two facts: he spent a challenge on two or three yards in the third quarter, and the game ended with no timeouts available on the final drive. I'm not going to claim that's the difference — but he isn't claiming it was smart either, and that honesty is worth something.

## THE INTERCEPTION

**Reporter: "What do you see happen on Kirk's interception?"**
**Kubiak: "They made a good play. They stepped underneath the check down to Mike. I have to watch it on film, but yeah — definitely gave points away there."**

So a defender read the check-down to Michael Mayer and undercut it. That's a Buffalo defender winning with anticipation, and it's the kind of play that comes from film study.

**"Definitely gave points away there."** In a three-point loss, a turnover that costs a scoring chance is the whole game. And notice he didn't blame his quarterback — he credited the defender and took the result.

## THE STATISTIC THAT SHOULD END THE DEADLINE DEBATE

Now the number that made me put my head in my hands.

**Reporter: "Six receptions from the receiving room, and five of them came from Tre Tucker. What needs to happen to get more production from the receivers?"**
**Kubiak: "There's guys that are making plays right now that we're getting the ball to. So those guys got to make plays."**

**Six catches. Total. From the entire wide receiver group. Five of them by one man.**

Raider Nation, that means every other receiver on this roster — with Jalen Nailor back from protocol, with Cody White active, with Malik Benson available — combined for **one reception.**

One.

And Kubiak's answer is as blunt as he's been all year. He didn't make excuses for them and he didn't promise more targets. He essentially said: *the ball is going to the players who are producing, and the rest have to produce.*

That's the harshest thing he's said about a position group this season, and he said it without raising his voice.

**This is the end of the argument.** For a month I've said buy small and only small — Ridley at a sixth, Godwin at a late pick, Jeudy at a fourth. After six catches in a three-point loss at home, with the deadline three weeks away, that isn't a preference anymore. **It's an obligation.** This offense scored 30 points today with one functional wide receiver. Imagine it with two.

## AND THEY TOOK BOWERS AWAY FOR TWO AND A HALF QUARTERS

**Reporter: "It was two and a half quarters before Brock got his first catch. What do you attribute that to?"**
**Kubiak: "Credit Buffalo. They did a great job of playing him. They had a good plan."**

Raider Nation, this is the exact thing he warned us about on Friday — that the coverages he sees on Sunday aren't the ones he expects going in, and that it's been happening for weeks.

**Two and a half quarters without a target for the best tight end in football.** Buffalo built a plan, executed it, and dared everybody else to beat them. And as the receiver number shows, nobody did.

That's the cost of having one threat. **When the opponent erases your best player and your second-best option is one catch, you score 30 and still lose.** Everything connects.

## THE VINDICATION: WASHINGTON JR. FINALLY GOT HIS CHANCE

And now the part of this press conference that genuinely made me happy.

**Reporter: "What did Washington Jr. give you today in the running game?"**
**Kubiak: "He gave us a spark. He did some good stuff. He's been practicing well and had some good runs for us. And between him and Dylan and Ashton — we're going to play all those guys."**

**"He gave us a spark."**

Raider Nation, I have argued for this for two straight weeks. One carry for five yards in New England while a back averaging five yards a carry sat was the least defensible decision of that afternoon. The coach admitted he deserved more. The staff told us his style fits gap schemes. And today he got his opportunity and **gave the offense a spark.**

"Between him and Dylan and Ashton, we're going to play all those guys." That's a three-man rotation, officially. And it's probably why the head coach is finally encouraged about his run game.

This is what it looks like when a staff identifies a mistake and corrects it in one week. Credit where it's earned.

## COUSINS: "GRITTY, GUTTY, SUPERB FOOTBALL"

**Reporter: "What did you make of Kirk's performance overall?"**
**Kubiak: "Gritty, gutty, just the same guy you guys have seen all season. He's playing superb football right now."**

**"He's playing superb football right now."**

And the evidence is on the final drive: an incredible throw to Tre Tucker with the game on the line and no timeouts left in his pocket.

Raider Nation, remember where we are. This is a 38-year-old quarterback, second in the NFL in touchdown passes, who just put 30 points on a 5-1 team **with six receptions from his entire wide receiver corps** and his best tight end erased for two and a half quarters.

Anybody still asking about trading him for a fourth-round pick has their answer. And anybody wondering why this staff isn't rushing Fernando Mendoza onto the field has it too.

## THE UGLIEST MOMENT OF THE AFTERNOON

Now the incident everyone is talking about.

**Reporter: "C.J. Gardner-Johnson looked like he was goading your guys into a lot, and then he gets kicked out. What did you see?"**
**Kubiak: "I really didn't see it. So I can't comment on that."**
**Reporter: "What does it say about your players that they didn't give in to his antics?"**
**Kubiak: "That's the expectation. We knew going in that there are certain guys that are going to try to do that to us — and the players knew before the game that if they got into it, they'd be standing by me on the sideline."**

Raider Nation, **that is the quote of the week** and I want to make sure you appreciate the coaching inside it.

He didn't deliver a speech about character after the fact. He **pre-empted it.** Before kickoff, he told his players that anybody who took the bait would be standing next to him instead of playing football. That's not discipline as punishment — that's discipline as preparation. He identified an opponent's tactic, named the consequence in advance, and his team didn't flinch.

And on the incident itself, JJ Watt described what happened: **"We've all been on that field and we know that's going to get you ejected. You spit on a guy, you're going to get ejected."** His view was that Joe Brady will now have to decide whether he can trust Gardner-Johnson going forward — and that while it didn't cost Buffalo today, a hot head making a decision like that will eventually swing a game.

I'm not going to pile on a player beyond what the officials already decided and what Watt said on the record. The ejection speaks for itself. What I'll say is this: **the Raiders were the provoked team and the Raiders kept their composure** — and given that this team took seven penalties, keeping composure in that specific situation is worth acknowledging.

## AND NOW THE SURPRISE: JJ WATT PRAISED THIS DEFENSE

Here's the part I did not expect, and it's the most encouraging analysis of the day.

Buffalo has now won two games in six days by a combined **70-64**, and Watt's honest read was that it's "tough to look at as a sustainable formula" — their defense still needs improvement, especially on the back end, while the offense is spectacular.

Then came the Josh Allen numbers, and they're remarkable: **he carried the ball five times for one yard, and this was the first game all season in which Josh Allen did not have a rushing touchdown.**

Five carries. One yard. Zero rushing touchdowns.

And listen to the explanation:

**JJ Watt: "I think the Raiders actually did a really good job containing him, and there were a lot of great examples today. They rushed with four a lot of the time, but they were so focused on keeping him in that pocket, not letting him out. And even sometimes when he did get out of the pocket, they had great pursuit to not let him run down the field. So that's what you have to do. The problem is you also have to have the guys on the back end who are locking people down left and right, so that when he does stay in that pocket, he can't be delivering it downfield. And then — oh yeah — you also have to stop the run in James Cook."**

Raider Nation, this is a massive piece of information, so let me translate it.

**The defensive plan worked.** I said on Friday that the only genuine answer to Josh Allen was disciplined rush lanes — contain, don't chase — and tackling in the open field. Rob Leonard did exactly that: **rushed four, kept him in the pocket, and pursued well when he escaped.** A quarterback who ran for 121 yards on Monday night finished with one.

That's a coordinator who was under real pressure this week answering with a plan that neutralized an MVP-caliber runner.

**And Watt named the exact remaining problem:** you also need the back end to lock people down so a contained quarterback can't throw over the top, and you still have to stop James Cook. That's the same two weaknesses we've identified for three weeks — the third level and the interior run defense, which was playing today without Tonka Hemingway.

So the diagnosis is now complete and public. **Contain the quarterback: solved. Cover the back end and stop the run: not yet.**

## "CLOSE GAMES OUT."

And then the two words that summarize this entire season.

**Reporter: "What have these last three games shown you about where this team needs to improve?"**
**Kubiak: "Close games out."**

That's it. That's the answer.

Raider Nation, look at the three losses. Kansas City 30-27. New England 45-17. Buffalo 33-30. Two of those were three-point games against teams that are 4-0 and 5-1. This is not a roster that can't compete with good teams — it's a roster that cannot finish against them.

And "closing" is made of exactly the things that went wrong today: red zone conversion, pre-snap discipline, a third-quarter three-and-out, a challenge that wasn't worth a timeout, an interception that gave points away. **Every single one of those is correctable, and not one of them requires new players.**

Asked about the big picture, he refused the frame: **"You don't look big picture. You look at the next game."** Correct. A 3-3 team in October has no business thinking about January.

## WHERE THIS LEAVES US

Let me put it together honestly.

**The good, and it's real:** 30 points against a 5-1 team. A run game the head coach is finally encouraged about. Washington Jr. providing a spark in a three-man rotation. Cousins playing superb football. A defensive plan that held Josh Allen to one rushing yard and earned praise from JJ Watt. No new injuries. And a locker room that refused to take the bait from an opponent trying to get them thrown out.

**The bad, and it's decisive:** three straight losses. Two for five in the red zone. Seven penalties. A fourth-down false start for the third time. A third-quarter three-and-out. An interception in a three-point game. Six receptions from the entire receiver room. And Brock Bowers erased for two and a half quarters.

**My fix list is now four items, and they're all internal.** Convert the red zone. Stop jumping before the snap, especially on fourth down. Script a better first drive out of halftime. And get the ball to someone other than Tre Tucker and Brock Bowers — which, with the deadline three weeks out, now means the front office has to help.

Raider Nation, this team is better than its record and worse than its potential, and both of those are true because of one phrase: **close games out.**
